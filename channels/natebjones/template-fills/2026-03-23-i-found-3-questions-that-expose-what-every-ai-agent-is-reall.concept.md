## TL;DR

OpenClaw's rise has forced every major AI company to make a distinct bet on the future of agents. These bets can be decoded along three axes: where the agent runs, who orchestrates the intelligence, and the interface contract. Understanding these axes lets you see the strategic logic behind any new agent product and decide if it's right for you.

## The argument

### Define the Three Axes
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:48']
- **Claim**: Every agent product makes a bet on three axes: where it runs (local, cloud, hybrid), who orchestrates the intelligence (single model, multi-model, model-agnostic), and the interface contract (messaging app, desktop, phone). These axes determine data privacy, cost, vendor lock-in, and product experience.
- **Role**: definition

### Profile OpenClaw as the Sovereignty Play
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:07']
- **Claim**: OpenClaw runs locally, is composable with any LLM and messaging platform, and gives users full control. This sovereignty comes with high technical complexity and security risks, as evidenced by 30,000 exposed instances and 800 compromised skills. It's for technical users who want maximum control.
- **Role**: evidence

### Contrast with Perplexity's Delegation Play
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:46']
- **Claim**: Perplexity Computer is the opposite bet: it runs in the cloud, handles orchestration, and promises long-running tasks. It trades user control for convenience and safety, but at $200/month and with full trust in Perplexity. This is the delegation play for non-technical users.
- **Role**: evidence

### Examine Manis and Anthropic's Distribution and Safety Plays
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:42']
- **Claim**: Manis, now owned by Meta, is a distribution play to capture the agent moment within Meta's ecosystem, trading data privacy for scale. Anthropic's Dispatch is a safety play, offering a secure, single-threaded Claude experience via messaging, reinforcing their brand as the safe option.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesize the Strategic Landscape
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:22:08']
- **Claim**: OpenClaw set the terms of the debate, and all competitors are playing on its graph. The question of 2026 is how many niches exist in this ecosystem. Products must either go deep with unique capability or be general enough to become a default delegation layer; the middle is where products die.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker profiles OpenClaw, Perplexity, Manis, Anthropic, and Lovable. OpenClaw has 250,000 users and 30,000 exposed instances; Perplexity claims long-running tasks but hasn't been around long enough to verify; Manis is a distribution play with unclear model mix; Anthropic's Dispatch is primitive but easy for non-technical users. The speaker notes that Lovable, despite being the most copied product, is pivoting to agent-first workflows, illustrating the pressure of relentless simplification. He also cautions that the framework is a simplification and that products may evolve, as seen with Perplexity's hybrid personal computer offering.
