## TL;DR

Most companies suffer from an 'apprenticeship gap' where individual AI proficiency remains invisible because work happens in private channels. Shopify solved this by mandating that its coding agent, River, only operates in public Slack channels. This forces the implicit reasoning, context, and review processes of senior engineers to become visible to the entire organization. The result is collective intelligence rather than isolated efficiency.

## The argument

### Define the Apprenticeship Gap
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:45']
- **Claim**: The core problem is not tooling but visibility. When AI work happens in private windows, the implicit reasoning and context of senior workers disappear. This creates an 'apprenticeship gap' where junior employees cannot learn the craft because they never see how experts frame problems or correct errors.
- **Role**: definition

### Expose the Four Layers of Work
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: To fix the gap, organizations must make four discrete parts of AI work visible: the task, the context loaded, the interaction pattern (including pushbacks), and the review standard. Sharing only the final output teaches nothing; sharing the messy process builds shared taste and reusable workflows.
- **Role**: definition

### Enforce Public Constraints
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:08:02']
- **Claim**: Voluntary sharing fails due to privacy concerns. The solution is binding constraints, like Shopify’s rule that River cannot be used in DMs. By forcing interactions into declared public channels, companies create a safe surface for learning without exposing protected data, turning individual insight into collective intelligence.
- **Role**: evidence

### Model Senior Judgment
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:15']
- **Claim**: The highest value comes from senior leaders running real, high-stakes work in public. When executives like Toby Licki model their agent interactions openly, they teach active supervision and judgment rather than just prompt syntax. This allows juniors to see how ambiguity is framed and how quality is assessed.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Shopify’s CEO Toby Licki models this by using River in public channels, allowing critique and questions. The speaker notes that while privacy is a serious objection, declared spaces with clear boundaries (e.g., anonymized data for healthcare/finance) allow compliance while enabling learning. The speaker hedges that this is not about dumping all chats into Slack but curating reusable workflows and useful failures. He cites the difficulty of digitizing tacit knowledge from manufacturing (John Deere) as a parallel, noting that physical expertise is hard to capture, making the digital visibility of AI reasoning even more critical.
