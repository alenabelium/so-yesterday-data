## TL;DR

Nearly half of US companies have cut management layers, but most haven't understood what they eliminated. Management bundles three distinct functions: information routing (automatable), sensemaking (deeply human), and accountability/feedback (human ownership). Leaders who decompose these roles before compressing them will build stronger organizations.

## The argument

### Define the Three Core Management Functions
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:41']
- **Claim**: Management bundles three distinct jobs: routing (information logistics), sensemaking (extracting signal from noise), and accountability/feedback (human ownership and coaching). These are not new; they trace back to Roman centurions and Prussian general staff.
- **Role**: definition

### Routing Is Automatable, Sensemaking Is Not
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:33']
- **Claim**: AI excels at routing—synthesizing and distributing information. But sensemaking requires years of business experience and human-to-human conversation to align rich context with strategic direction. AI can assist but not replace the manager's role in sensemaking.
- **Role**: evidence

### Accountability and Feedback Remain Human
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:51']
- **Claim**: Accountability is a human trait—managers feel ownership of goals over long periods, which agents cannot simulate. Feedback delivery is also human, though AI can assist by synthesizing data for insights. This function is unlikely to be fully automated.
- **Role**: evidence

### Three Companies, Three Bets on Decomposition
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['12:43']
- **Claim**: Kimi (flat, no hierarchy, agents route, founders sensemake, no accountability) shows speed but cultural casualties. Block (world model, DRIs, player-coaches) splits roles explicitly. Meta (compressed management, intensified accountability) keeps functions bundled but wider spans.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Decompose Before You Compress
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:29:43']
- **Claim**: Leaders must decompose management roles before assuming AI can fill gaps. Automate routing first, invest in feedback mechanisms, and name responsible individuals. The firms that decompose deliberately will be stronger long-term than those that just compress.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Kimi's routing example: a PM launches three agents that scan 3,000 user feedback items, interpret sentiments, and monitor competitors, producing a requirements doc and 70% of implementation by 11:30 AM. Sensemaking is concentrated in five co-founders, each with ~50 direct reports, causing cognitive strain. Accountability is absent, leading to anxiety and drift; employees cry in meetings. Block's model uses DRIs with 90-day ownership and player-coaches, but Jack Dorsey hasn't rolled it out yet—it's a proposal. Meta's compression shows stock growth (3x) but anecdotal reports of burnout and uncertainty about long-term retention. Caveats: AI may improve 10x, but sensemaking and accountability have human runway; agent-led companies may emerge but trust may require humans.
