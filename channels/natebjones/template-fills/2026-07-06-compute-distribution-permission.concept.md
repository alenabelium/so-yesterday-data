## TL;DR

The AI industry is shifting from a singular focus on model capability to a multi-layered competition. Value now lies in compute distribution, consumer engagement surfaces, and political permission. Builders must look beyond benchmarks to integration and societal adoption.

## The argument

### The Old Scoreboard Logic
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:55']
- **Claim**: For two years, the industry ran on a single question: who has the best model? This logic justified massive capex to own the best model, treating models as the sole product and winner-take-all asset.
- **Role**: definition

### Compute as Asset Class
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:27']
- **Claim**: Meta is monetizing infrastructure by selling excess AI compute, competing with AWS and Azure. This signals that compute is an asset class, not just a training resource, and model ownership is no longer the only moat.
- **Role**: evidence

### Distribution via Consumer Surfaces
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:30']
- **Claim**: Meta launched a consumer gaming app (Gizmos) to generate engagement with small models. This represents a distribution play, experimenting with consumer surfaces to build sticky habits rather than waiting for agent transformation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Political Permission as Infrastructure
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:30']
- **Claim**: OpenAI is offering the US government a 5% equity stake to secure regulatory headroom. With the government able to delay releases, political alignment is now critical infrastructure for getting models to market.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesis: The Multi-Layered Game
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:15']
- **Claim**: The market is repricing AI value across three contested layers: infrastructure margins, distribution bets, and political permissions. Success requires integrating models into society, not just building them.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Meta's stock jumped on the compute news, showing investor interest in infrastructure margins. OpenAI's equity offer is a proposal needing Congress, not a completed deal. Jersey Mike's IPO mentions AI 22 times, illustrating how cheap capital has migrated to peripheral hype. Anthropic focuses on enterprise distribution via forward-deployed engineers. The model race isn't over, but it's not the only race. CapEx is still up roughly a third; spenders are reallocating capital to maximize value across these new layers.
