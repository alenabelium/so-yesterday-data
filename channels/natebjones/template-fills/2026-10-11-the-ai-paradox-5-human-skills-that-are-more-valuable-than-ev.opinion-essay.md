## Thesis

Five human skills—taste, agency, focus, ingenuity, and follow-through—are becoming more valuable in the AI era, not less, and mastering them will let you take on projects that once required teams or experts.

## Argument

The mechanism is the expansion of individual capability. AI has lowered the cost of implementation, making it possible for one person to do work that previously required a team. This shifts the bottleneck from resources to human skills. Taste, defined as acting on what you care about, lets you identify problems and create solutions without waiting for permission. Agency means pushing boundaries and using AI to scale your initiative. Focus becomes critical as AI generates more opportunities and distractions; you must choose what to pursue and see it through. Ingenuity is the ability to navigate unfamiliar territory as tools change. Finally, follow-through—completing work that benefits someone—builds trust and creates real impact. These skills compound: taste finds the problem, agency starts, focus sustains, ingenuity adapts, and follow-through delivers. In 2027, the ability to execute the full cycle from idea to useful result will be more valuable than ever.

## Counterpoints

- The common narrative that AI is a 'coming wave' washing away skills is false; these five skills are actually growing in value.
- Some say you can try many things because AI enables it, but that doesn't maximize potential; focus is essential.
- AI can make distractions look productive, but many prototypes don't change anyone's life; follow-through matters.
