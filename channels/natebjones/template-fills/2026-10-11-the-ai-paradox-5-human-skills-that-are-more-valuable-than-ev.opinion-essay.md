## Thesis

In the AI era, five human skills—taste, agency, focus, ingenuity, and follow-through—are becoming more valuable, not less, and mastering them is the key to thriving in 2027.

## Argument

The dominant narrative says AI is washing away human skills, but that's false. The real shift is that AI expands the impact of these five skills, letting individuals do work that once required teams or experts. This is the opposite of the 'loss of skills' story, and betting on that story is betting on the wrong horse. [00:00:00]

Take taste: it's acting on what you care about, noticing nuances others miss. AI lets you act on that taste directly—create a guide, draft a better process—and learn faster from the feedback loop. [00:03:42] Agency is the 'said-done' ratio. AI removes the blockers that used to stop you, so your willingness to act today has a more direct impact. You don't need to wait for perfect clarity; the first useful attempt reveals what you really want. [00:07:25]

Focus is counterintuitive when everyone says 'try everything.' But focus and determination let you put more leverage into results. Two people with the same tools: one starts 15 projects and abandons them; the other focuses on one client problem and solves it. The second person builds understanding and feedback. [00:11:36] Ingenuity is finding a way forward when you don't know what to do. AI changes what it feels like to be a beginner—you can learn at the moment of need. The tools will change, but the ability to navigate unfamiliar territory stays valuable. [00:15:36]
