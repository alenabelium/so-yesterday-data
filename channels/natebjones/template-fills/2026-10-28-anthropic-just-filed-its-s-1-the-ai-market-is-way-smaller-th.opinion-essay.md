## Thesis

Today's AI budgets don't determine the market's size; the real question is how quickly enterprises will embed intelligence into core operations, making Anthropic's S-1 a preview of massive growth.

## Argument

The mechanism is the gap between winning a customer and becoming important to them. Anthropic counts half the Fortune 500 as customers, but the average one spends less than $1 per $1,000 of total spending. That's not a sign of a bubble; it's a sign of untapped opportunity. The top 10 clients spend eight times the average share, yet still under 1% of their budgets. This shows the biggest buyers have barely scratched the surface of potential use cases.

Anthropic's own filing says most enterprises deploy Claude in a small number of teams compared to what it could solve. The 900% net revenue retention suggests customers who start small expand rapidly as they discover value. Cowork, a non-technical product, is growing faster than Claude Code, indicating expansion beyond engineering into other departments. This is the next stage: moving from table-stakes adoption to deep embedding.

The investments are cumulative. Each deployment connects systems and makes knowledge useful, simplifying the next one. Anthropic's $100 million Frontier Academy to train 10,000 engineers is a bet on accelerating this learning curve. The goal is to help customers discover and create the next workflow, making each deployment a gateway to more.

Anthropic's revenue model is also broader than traditional SaaS. Customers pay directly, through cloud providers (48% of revenue), and via third-party products like Salesforce. ## Counterpoints

- Bear case: Customers see the limits of intelligence and won't overinvest, as value is hard to capture without intentionality.
- Skeptic: The 900% net revenue retention might be a one-time 2026 phenomenon, not a sustainable trend.
- Question: Is the $100 million Frontier Academy a temporary bridge or a permanent requirement for scaling?
- Uncertainty: The S-1 doesn't detail how much growth depends on Anthropic's direct deployment engineers.
