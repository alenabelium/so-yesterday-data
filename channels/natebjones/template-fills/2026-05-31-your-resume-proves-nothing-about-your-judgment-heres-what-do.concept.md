## TL;DR

AI has decoupled artifact quality from human competence, rendering traditional resumes and polished portfolios unreliable signals. True professional value now lies in demonstrating judgment under pressure through live, rigorous reasoning processes. Professionals must shift from showcasing static outputs to exposing their decision-making logic, risk assessment, and ability to learn in public.

## The argument

### The AI Evidence Problem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: AI lowers the barrier to high-quality artifacts, making polished resumes and prototypes poor signals of competence. The core issue is an 'evidence problem': we can no longer trust static work samples to prove human judgment because AI makes production easy while hiding the thinking process.
- **Role**: definition

### Whiteboarding as Judgment Proof
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:30']
- **Claim**: Live whiteboarding sessions provide the necessary evidence by forcing private judgment into visible work. This format reveals what a person noticed, believed, rejected, and how their thinking held up when challenged by a serious peer, exposing the 'live reasoning' that static artifacts conceal.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Four Judgment Layers
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:23']
- **Claim**: Effective judgment demonstration requires exposing four discrete layers: 1) Situation (context/constraints), 2) Decision (plausible paths and rejected options), 3) Risk (prevented losses and accepted dangers), and 4) Change (what gets clearer or safer). This structure moves beyond diary-like recording to connect judgment to tangible work changes.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Talent Board Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:28']
- **Claim**: The 'Talent Board' is a digital artifact that preserves the evidence of live reasoning for hiring and promotion. It shifts the career strategy from 'generation' (which AI solves) to 'comprehension' (which is scarce), allowing professionals to showcase their ability to learn in public and improve their point of view over time.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Microsoft data shows 86% of users treat AI output as a starting point, not a final answer, and 58% of users produce work they couldn't have a year ago. The speaker notes that while building a portfolio is still valuable for showing you can ship, it is incomplete because it points to the part AI makes easier. The 'talent board' is not about proving you arrive fully formed, but showing you can learn in public. Formats can vary (shared docs, Loom, annotated prototypes) as long as the discipline of making reasoning visible is maintained. The speaker hedges that this is especially important in the first month of a new role to form a point of view early.
