## TL;DR

Using AI to speed up routine work you don't enjoy is a trap. Instead, follow interesting people, run small experiments, and engage with communities to build a learning flywheel. This turns AI into a tool for expanding choices, not just optimizing your current path.

## The argument

### Follow People Whose Work Opens Doors
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:57']
- **Claim**: Riley Brown's path shows the power of following people who are actively experimenting with AI. By joining Discord groups and later collaborating on X, he built a feedback loop that turned into apps and videos. This combination—seeing others' experiments and making your own public enough for help—is powerful in the age of AI.
- **Role**: definition

### Try Small Experiments to Build Meta-Skills
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:05']
- **Claim**: Use AI to generate concrete activity ideas that combine work and hobby, like recording family stories. This builds meta-skills—logical thinking, visual analysis, voice, pattern-making, and search—that transfer across domains. It also teaches you to craft meaningful prompts and push back on AI's generic suggestions, a crucial meta-skill of denial.
- **Role**: evidence

### Learn With a Community by Sharing Early
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:41']
- **Claim**: Swyx's example of analyzing Dan Abramov's React demo the next day shows how publishing your learning attracts engagement. Choose something recently released to increase conversation chances. In AI, everything is new, so you can join discussions without being an expert—just understand your small experiments well enough to describe them clearly.
- **Role**: evidence

### Reflect on What the Experience Teaches You
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:16:15']
- **Claim**: Project success isn't the measure of learning. You can learn what you don't want to do even from a successful project, or meet helpful people from a failure. Simone Hirtz discovered she preferred creating things and programming, but also needed the right environment. Write a one-page explanation of something you know to uncover what you enjoy.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Expand Choices, Not Just Optimize
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:22:29']
- **Claim**: AI should expand your choices, not just make you faster at work you don't want. The tyranny of the text box makes it feel demanding, but you can break free by choosing one thing to try this week. This moves you from a technology 'for everything' to something you choose yourself, with more influence over your direction.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Riley Brown's story: started in Discord, built apps despite not being an engineer. Swyx's React demo analysis: posted the next day, built community. Ben Tossell built 50 products, none sold, but interest led to teaching and MakerPad. Simone Hirtz: iPhone case with guitar strings landed an internship, but she needed to change context. Caveats: You don't need a YouTube channel; you can follow threads unnoticed. You don't need to be an expert; just describe your small experiments clearly. Be honest about what you tried and didn't know. Don't delegate finding joy to AI—you must listen to your intuition. Hold on to your current income until you're convinced a new path is profitable.
