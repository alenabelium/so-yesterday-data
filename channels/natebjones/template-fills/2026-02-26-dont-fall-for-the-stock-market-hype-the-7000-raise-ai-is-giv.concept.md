## TL;DR

The stock market is swinging between doom and boom narratives about AI, but both ignore the real bottleneck: the gap between how fast AI capabilities improve and how slowly society actually adopts them. This 'capability-dissipation gap' is where the real economic opportunity lies for those who can bridge it.

## The argument

### Define the Capability-Dissipation Gap
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:04']
- **Claim**: Both doomer and boomer narratives assume AI capabilities translate rapidly into economic impact. But capabilities are not deployment, deployment is not adoption, adoption is not deep integration, and deep integration is not economic impact. Social inertia is a massive, underrepresented force.
- **Role**: definition

### Four Inertias Keep the Gap Wide
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:13:23']
- **Claim**: Regulatory, organizational, cultural, and trust inertia slow adoption. Regulatory approvals take years; companies are not rational actors; cultural change is slow (even Shopify needed a mandate); and enterprises don't trust AI output without costly verification systems.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Gap Creates Asymmetric Returns
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:22:00']
- **Claim**: The gap between the fast capability curve and the slow dissipation curve is where we live. It explains why AI is stunning but disruption is modest, and why the market is confused. More importantly, it creates outsized, compounding returns for those operating at the capability frontier while others move at the dissipation rate.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Speed Is the Small Player's Advantage
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:24:53']
- **Claim**: Large firms have capital, data, and distribution but carry the full weight of organizational inertia. Small firms and individuals lack those but have speed. The gap creates an asymmetric advantage for anyone who can collapse the integration timeline, making speed the key differentiator.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Practical Playbook: Build Eval Frameworks
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:31:15']
- **Claim**: The career move is to become the person who can test AI in real workflows, build evaluation frameworks, and cross the gap between technical understanding, business workflows, and implementation. This skill set is rare and increasingly valuable.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The bear case (Catrini's fictional memo) is well-constructed but relies on extreme assumptions: no policy response, no consumption rebound, and rapid labor displacement. Economist Alex Emsis models these conditions and finds them unrealistic. The bull case (Michael Bloke) argues AI agents could compress service costs by 40-70%, returning $4,000-7,000 per household annually, and cites record business formation (532,000 new applications in January 2026). However, both narratives assume fast conversion from capability to impact, which the four inertias (regulatory, organizational, cultural, trust) prevent. The speaker notes that even Toby Lutke needed a company-wide mandate to get AI adoption at Shopify, and that most organizations lack the capital or stomach for verification infrastructure. The gap persists and compounds, but the speaker cautions that this is not 'learn AI and you'll be fine'—it's structural and specific.
