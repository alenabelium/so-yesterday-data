## TL;DR

As AI commoditizes software production, app builders risk becoming thin wrappers around model APIs. The future web will organize around five durable verticals of value that models cannot replicate: trust, context, distribution, taste, and liability. Understanding these layers reveals where to build defensible businesses.

## The argument

### The Middleware Trap
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:16']
- **Claim**: AI app builders like Lovable and Replit are functionally thin wrappers around base models. Their differentiation is a UI layer that can be replicated in a week, so better models from Anthropic or OpenAI can instantly commoditize their products.
- **Role**: definition

### Trust as the Verification Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:07']
- **Claim**: With millions of AI-generated apps flooding the web, trust becomes the critical layer. Companies that verify legitimacy and back it up—like Stripe, Shopify, or Apple's App Store—become the routing layer for responsible web traffic, especially for autonomous agents.
- **Role**: evidence

### Context as the Choke Point
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:23']
- **Claim**: The most valuable asset is specific situational data—company data, customer relationships, medical records. Companies that own the authoritative store of context and its permissioning layer become the choke point every agent must flow through.
- **Role**: evidence

### Distribution and Curation
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:00']
- **Claim**: When supply is infinite, curation becomes the scarcest resource. Distribution gatekeepers like Google, Apple, and TikTok get stronger as the flood grows. For agents, a new discovery layer is emerging—an agent-native app store—that will determine which businesses agents transact with.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Taste and Liability as Human Anchors
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:16:00']
- **Claim**: When production is free, taste—human judgment on what to build—becomes the entire game. And liability, the accountability for agent actions, becomes a governance layer. Both are human skills that AI cannot replace, and they become more critical as agents act autonomously.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites specific examples: Lovable's 100,000 projects per day, Replit's 25 million developers, Vercel's hosting of production apps for OpenAI and Nike. Notion's model picker and context play, Salesforce, Epic, and Palantir as context owners. For distribution, Google, Apple, TikTok, YouTube, and Amazon are named. For liability, Deloitte and McKinsey repositioning as AI assurance providers, 11 Labs offering voice agent insurance. Caveats: The speaker admits some distribution plays are 'guesses' and that the trend toward auto-research may evolve agentic taste, but human accountability remains. He also notes that most wrapper companies will die, but some like Lovable may become platforms.
