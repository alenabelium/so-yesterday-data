## TL;DR

AI labs are paying millions for corporate work archives—emails, chats, documents—to train agents. But these records capture the chaotic imitation of work, not its true value. This raises urgent questions about what work really is and who profits from its data.

## The argument

### The Market for Work Records
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: Google's $10 million bid for Spirit Airlines' emails and Teams messages reveals a market where companies sell work archives to train AI agents. The seller gets paid, the broker gets a commission, but the employees whose work created the data get nothing.
- **Role**: definition

### The Imitation Problem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:21']
- **Claim**: Work archives mix valuable interventions with mundane coordination. An agent trained on this data learns to imitate the process, not the judgment. The example of resolving an invoice dispute shows that the crucial insight—spotting a misapplied credit—is lost among 30 messages and three meetings.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Data of Last Hope
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:40']
- **Claim**: The companies selling work records are often on the brink of bankruptcy, like Spirit Airlines. This means the data agents learn from is mostly from failing companies, reflecting their chaos and mistakes, not successful work practices.
- **Role**: counter

### The Complexity of Intellectual Work
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:00']
- **Claim**: Reducing intellectual labor to optimization problems for agents misses the nuances that make work effective. Point-by-point optimization forgets the context, judgment, and coordination that are essential to real work, which is why AI has yet to show a sustainable impact on the labor market.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites Mercor's approach of paying experts to define success criteria for agent tasks, which transforms an expert's interpretation into a training goal. However, he notes this is a complex problem and that the resulting evidence may not reflect true work value. He also mentions employee resistance, with leaders estimating up to a third of teams passively sabotaging AI tools. The speaker acknowledges that agents can be useful for specific tasks like presentations or spreadsheets, but only if the human worker understands and creates real work with them.
