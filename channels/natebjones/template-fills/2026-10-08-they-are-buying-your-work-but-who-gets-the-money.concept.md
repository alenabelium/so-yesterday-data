## TL;DR

AI labs are paying millions for corporate work records—emails, Slack messages, meeting transcripts—to train agents. But these archives capture the chaotic imitation of work, not the judgment and tacit knowledge that create real value. This gap between data and work is reshaping who gets paid and what counts as labor.

## The argument

### The Data-Buying Boom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: Google's $10 million bid for Spirit Airlines' work emails and Teams messages reveals a market where labs buy archives of corporate communications to train agents. The prevailing theory is that these records—Slack, Zoom, documents—can teach AI to do intelligent work.
- **Role**: definition

### Records Capture Imitation, Not Work
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:32']
- **Claim**: Work records capture the visible coordination—meetings, messages, documents—but miss the tacit knowledge and judgment that actually solve problems. The invoice example shows that the valuable intervention (noticing a misapplied credit) is buried in 30 messages and three meetings, indistinguishable from the noise.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Buyer's Dilemma
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:21']
- **Claim**: When buyers like Mercor train agents on these archives, they must decide what counts as 'work done.' This transforms an expert's interpretation into a training goal, and the agent learns to imitate the process, not the underlying judgment. The risk is that agents learn to close tickets without resolving the real issue.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Last-Resort Data Problem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:40']
- **Claim**: The data sold to labs mostly comes from failing companies like Spirit Airlines, which are desperate for cash. This means agents are trained on the chaotic records of bankrupt businesses, not on successful work patterns. The data is a 'last hope' for sellers, not a representative sample of good work.
- **Role**: counter

### The Real Work Is Coordination
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:41']
- **Claim**: The true value of work lies in coordination—getting people on the same page, making judgment calls, and driving business outcomes. Agents that imitate Slack messages produce 'simulations of work' that are entertaining but low-value. The path forward is structuring data so agents augment human judgment, not replace it.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The Spirit Airlines auction is the central example: 100 million emails and 500 million Teams entries, with Google winning at $10 million over Mercor's $7.5 million. The deal is pending a hearing on October 14. Mercor's acquisition of Deep Tune and its paid 'task success criteria' experts illustrate the attempt to define 'work done.' The speaker notes that every example is 'a somewhat anonymized situation that I have personally experienced,' and he acknowledges that some coordination is necessary, but much is 'the price of working within that particular company.' He also cites employee resistance, with leaders estimating up to a third of teams passively sabotaging AI tools. The speaker hedges that agents can be useful for repetitive tasks, but he is skeptical about scaling laws capturing company-specific judgment.
