## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting power from sellers to buyers as AI agents become the primary purchasing interface. Stripe, Visa, and Microsoft are building infrastructure for this agentic commerce, where intent forms before a user visits a seller's site. Businesses must become programmatically usable and trusted by agents or risk being left behind.

## The argument

### The old funnel was a machine for making human intent visible
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:07']
- **Claim**: The funnel wasn't just a marketing diagram; it was an institutional arrangement for observing human intent. Websites, checkout pages, and pricing pages were controlled environments where sellers could watch demand take shape and optimize around it.
- **Role**: definition

### Payment authority travels with the task, not inside checkout
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:29']
- **Claim**: In the agent model, the buyer's agent brings payment authority to the seller. Payment methods are attached to the task, bounded by amount, currency, or approval state, and can expire. This relocates the point where intent and payment meet, away from the seller's surface.
- **Role**: definition

### The next competitive question is whether your business can be called by agents
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:27:25']
- **Claim**: Success depends on being programmatically usable by software acting on behalf of humans. This means exposing structured data, clear policies, and reliable fulfillment so agents can understand, compare, and transact with your business without human babysitting.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Stripe's agentic commerce suite includes Links wallet for agents, shared payment tokens, and the machine payments protocol. Visa and Mastercard are building agent payment systems. Microsoft is pushing shopping inside Copilot, and Meta is moving checkout closer to ads. However, instant checkout experiments like Walmart's converted poorly, suggesting the future isn't a single buy button but a broader shift in how commerce begins. The transition will be uneven, with cards serving as adapters for the existing web and stablecoins enabling machine-native transactions.
