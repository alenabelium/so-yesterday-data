## TL;DR

Power in the internet economy is shifting from sellers to buyers as AI agents form intent before visiting a seller's website. Success requires businesses to expose structured data and payment authority programmatically, moving from persuasive marketing funnels to being 'usable' by software acting on behalf of consumers.

## The argument

### The Shift from Funnel to Agent Usability
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00', '00:03:07']
- **Claim**: The traditional marketing funnel was an institutional arrangement for making human intent visible within a seller-controlled environment. In the agentic economy, intent is formed by the buyer's agent before it reaches the seller, requiring businesses to be programmatically usable rather than just discoverable.
- **Role**: definition

### Structured Data as the New Brand
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:41', '00:09:27']
- **Claim**: Agents require structured metadata—prices, policies, inventory, and constraints—to reason about purchases. Brand loyalty shifts from emotional persuasion to a 'ledger' of reliable preferences and trust history that the agent carries as a constraint.
- **Role**: evidence

### Relocation of Payment Authority
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:29', '00:18:35']
- **Claim**: Payment authority moves from the seller's checkout flow to the buyer's agent via tools like shared tokens and one-time cards. This enables complex transaction types like streaming payments and usage-based billing that traditional checkout pages cannot support.
- **Role**: evidence

### Trust and Fraud as Infrastructure
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:21:31', '00:23:41']
- **Claim**: As automation increases, fraud becomes a systemic risk that can poison the agentic experience. Trust must be guaranteed through network-wide risk signals and programmable primitives, making the infrastructure of trust as critical as the transaction itself.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Examples include Stripe's 'Links' wallet for agents, shared payment tokens, and the 'Machine Payments Protocol'. The speaker notes that 'instant checkout' inside chatbots may convert poorly compared to directing users to merchant sites, suggesting the future is a mix of discovery and transaction surfaces. Caveats include the reality that cards will remain useful as adapters for the existing web, while stable coins suit machine-native transactions. The transition is uneven, and brands must survive by becoming reliable data points in the buyer's memory rather than just persuasive visuals.
