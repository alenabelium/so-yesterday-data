## Thesis

NVIDIA is building more AI capacity than the market will pay for, as customers shift to cheaper models and token spending growth lags behind infrastructure commitments.

## Argument

The mechanism is economic. Customers are learning to buy intelligence more cheaply, and the data shows it. RAMP reports that token spending is growing slowly, while the most expensive models only account for about 70% of costs. Companies are deliberately routing work to cheaper models, and Google's new Orchestrator feature in Gemini automatically switches between models to cut costs by 24% and speed up by 19%. This is a rational response: for routine tasks like customer support, you don't need deep reasoning. The result is that token usage grows, but the bill doesn't. Labs have ordered computing power years in advance, but if customers pay less per token, the labs need far more volume to justify their valuations. The Wall Street Journal reports Jensen Huang's own concerns about the build-up of liabilities and the risk of capacity reallocation. This is not a .com bust—token demand is still growing—but it's a mismatch between capacity commitments and willingness to pay. The question is whether the revenue multipliers labs need will materialize if overall token spending stays flat. The answer is uncertain, but the combination of customer price cuts and cash-flow concerns makes this the key issue to watch.

## Counterpoints

- Demand for tokens is still growing rapidly, unlike the .com bust.
- The most powerful models still command a significant market, about 70% of costs.
- Cheaper models can still be profitable if operating costs are low enough.
- AI usage will increase dramatically by 2027, even if cost growth lags.
