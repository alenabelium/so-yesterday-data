## TL;DR

NateBJones argues that AI agents are ending the app era, shifting software value from applications to data layers and agent gateways. He illustrates this with examples from Meta, Salesforce, Microsoft, and DoorDash, showing how companies reposition to remain indispensable. The video emphasizes that individuals and buyers must understand the new three-layer stack—data, application, and agent—to navigate the changing landscape and avoid vendor lock-in.

## The argument

### Agents Disintermediate Traditional Apps
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:00']
- **Claim**: The core shift is that agents take over the information component of a role, making the traditional application less indispensable. In the recruiting example, the agent pulls candidate profiles, prepares a shortlist, and the user stops opening the recruiting tool. The company still gets paid because it sells the data, but the app's role diminishes.
- **Role**: definition

### Data and Trained Agents Become the New Moat
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:50']
- **Claim**: The trained agent, filled with personal context and knowledge, becomes harder to replace than the software. This creates a new form of lock-in: the agent learns the unwritten rules of the job, making it difficult to switch AI vendors. This is the battle awaiting teams in 2027.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Three-Layer Stack: Data, Application, Agent
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:43']
- **Claim**: To navigate this, NateBJones introduces a three-layer framework: the data layer, the application layer, and the agent layer. Every company, from Meta to Microsoft, is positioning itself within this stack. For example, Meta's Muse for Small Business connects to the data layer, while Salesforce aims to be both a data layer and a place where agents can use tools.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Counter: The Application Layer Still Matters
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:22:27']
- **Claim**: Despite the shift, the application layer is not dead. Structured workflows, like those in payroll, still require human maintenance, configuration, and approval. Agents help people use these systems more effectively, and companies that provide structured workflows will offer value with confidence.
- **Role**: counter

### The New Imperative: Sell the Future, Not the Present
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:31:55']
- **Claim**: The final step is that software vendors must sell a vision for 2027, not just today's product. They need to position their workflows as plug-and-play for AI, with accessible data layers and multi-tenant agents. Buyers are buying into the belief that the vendor will stay on top of AI and remain relevant when the next model comes out.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

NateBJones uses the DoorDash example (September 30th announcement) to illustrate the future of software ordering via text and MCP. He also cites Crust Data's case study of a two-person recruiting business building its own skill on their API, and Workday's payroll agent. Caveats: He notes that the recruiting story is anonymized, and that not every employee will have their AI usage reviewed by the CFO. He also acknowledges that some workers are 'scared to death of the command line,' so AI will be woven into traditional interfaces.
