## TL;DR

Software is shifting from app-centric to agent-centric, forcing a new three-layer stack: data, application, and agent. Buyers and sellers must understand their position in this stack to navigate the changing landscape and avoid vendor lock-in. The future belongs to those who can articulate value across all three layers.

## The argument

### The Agent Era Redefines Software Value
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:51']
- **Claim**: As agents take over tasks like reading contracts and compiling plans, the traditional app becomes less indispensable. The real value shifts to the data and the trained agent, which are harder to replace than the interface.
- **Role**: definition

### Data and Distribution Are the New Moats
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:43']
- **Claim**: Meta, Salesforce, and Microsoft are all positioning around their data layers and distribution. Salesforce, for example, is building an agent gateway and a reasoning model (Koa) to serve all agents, not just its own, leveraging its data and synthetic training.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Application Layer Isn't Dead—It's Decomposed
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:27:17']
- **Claim**: Applications split into interfaces and workflows. While interfaces may change, structured workflows (like payroll compliance) remain valuable. The key is to deliver extraordinary value in workflows that buyers don't want to touch.
- **Role**: counter

### The Three-Layer Stack as a Mental Model
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:58']
- **Claim**: To navigate this shift, everyone—employees, buyers, and sellers—must understand their position in the data, application, and agent layers. This framework helps articulate value, make informed purchases, and avoid being trapped by vendor lock-in.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Examples: DoorDash's text ordering and MCP server show that losing the interface doesn't mean losing the transaction. Crust Data's case study shows a two-person recruiting firm building its own skill on top of a data API, bypassing traditional SaaS. Workday's payroll agent demonstrates the value of structured workflows. Caveats: The speaker notes that not everyone will use command lines, and that the application layer isn't dead—it's decomposed. He also warns that individual AI usage patterns are idiosyncratic and must be shared to avoid losing productivity when vendors change.
