## TL;DR

NateBJones argues the app era is ending as AI agents become the primary interface, forcing a shift from selling applications to selling data layers, workflows, and agent gateways. He illustrates this with examples from Meta, Salesforce, Microsoft, and DoorDash, showing how companies are repositioning to remain indispensable. The video emphasizes that individuals and buyers must understand the new three-layer stack—data, application, and agent—to navigate the changing software landscape and avoid vendor lock-in.

## The argument

### Define the Three-Layer Stack
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:58']
- **Claim**: The common thread across all examples is the need to understand your position on the data layer, the application layer, and the agent layer. These are three different levels, and each of us—whether employee, leader, or builder—must be able to talk about our work in these terms.
- **Role**: definition

### The Agent Makes the App Dispensable
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:51']
- **Claim**: When an agent takes over the information component of a role, the additional tool that previously created that plan becomes less indispensable. The trained agent, with all its acquired knowledge, becomes harder to replace, posing a risk to the SaaS industry.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Counter: Data and Workflows Remain Valuable
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:22:27']
- **Claim**: Even as agents replace interfaces, the underlying data and structured workflows remain critical. For example, payroll systems like Workday still require human maintenance and configuration, and agents help people navigate this complexity, making the system more valuable.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: The New Value Proposition
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:31:55']
- **Claim**: The winning proposition for software vendors is to set up workflows as a plug-and-play system, easy to plug AI into, with a data layer accessible from different interfaces and agents that can read it effectively. This is how companies can sell the future and accelerate business as buyers increasingly purchase results.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

NateBJones uses several real-world examples to illustrate the shift. DoorDash's September 30th announcement allows users to message an agent to order food, and they also announced a limited beta of their MCP, showing they want to be available through any assistant. Salesforce, at Dreamforce, announced Koa, a reasoning model based on the Neumotor stack, trained on synthetic data to avoid using real customer data. Microsoft positions itself with Autopilot, a persistent agent in preview, alongside its data layer and productivity suite. A recruiting story shows a user who stopped opening the recruiting tool, but the company still gets paid for data. Crust Data's case study shows a two-person recruiting business building its own skill using the API instead of buying an off-the-shelf tool. The speaker notes that these are based on true stories with details changed for anonymity. He also cautions that agents are constantly improving, making buying decisions more complex, and that individual workflows are idiosyncratic, so sharing how you use AI is crucial to avoid losing productivity if the company changes vendors.
