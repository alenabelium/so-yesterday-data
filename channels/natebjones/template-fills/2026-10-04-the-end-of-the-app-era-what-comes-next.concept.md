## TL;DR

Software is shifting from selling applications to selling data layers, workflows, and agent gateways. The new three-layer stack—data, application, and agent—requires buyers and sellers to rethink value. Individuals must share their AI workflows to avoid vendor lock-in and stay productive.

## The argument

### Define the Three-Layer Stack
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:58']
- **Claim**: In the agent era, software value is defined by three layers: the data layer, the application layer, and the agent layer. Each layer has distinct value and must be understood separately to navigate the changing landscape.
- **Role**: definition

### Evidence: SaaS Companies Reposition
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:43']
- **Claim**: Meta, Salesforce, and Microsoft are all repositioning around the data and agent layers. Meta's Muse for Small Business connects to data, Salesforce uses its data advantage with Koa, and Microsoft leverages its data and productivity suite to offer a control plane for agents.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: The Risk of Vendor Lock-In
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:51']
- **Claim**: As agents become indispensable, switching AI vendors becomes difficult because the agent holds accumulated knowledge and workflows. This creates a new form of vendor lock-in, making it hard for companies to change providers even if costs or flexibility become issues.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: The New Value Proposition
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:31:55']
- **Claim**: Software vendors must sell plug-and-play workflows that are easy to integrate with AI, with accessible data layers and multi-tenant agents. The winning proposition is one that clearly explains how the layers combine to accelerate business results.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Examples include DoorDash's text ordering and MCP integration, showing how even consumer apps are becoming agent gateways. Crust Data's case study illustrates a two-person recruiting firm building its own skill on top of a data API instead of buying a full SaaS tool. Workday's payroll agent uses customized data and rules to improve payroll operations. The speaker notes that agents are still in pilot stages (e.g., Koa) and that individual AI usage patterns are idiosyncratic, making it hard to generalize. He emphasizes that not everyone will adopt command-line interfaces, but AI will be woven into traditional interfaces.
