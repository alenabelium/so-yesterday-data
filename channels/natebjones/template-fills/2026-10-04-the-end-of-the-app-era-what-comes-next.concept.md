## TL;DR

The app era is ending as AI agents become the primary interface for software. This forces a shift from selling applications to selling data layers, workflows, and agent gateways. Buyers and sellers must understand the new three-layer stack—data, application, and agent—to navigate the changing landscape and avoid vendor lock-in.

## The argument

### Agents Disintermediate Traditional SaaS
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:51']
- **Claim**: As agents take over tasks like reading contracts, reviewing sales notes, and creating launch plans, the traditional software tool that previously performed these functions becomes less indispensable. The agent, trained with company-specific knowledge, becomes harder to replace than the software, threatening the SaaS business model.
- **Role**: definition

### The Data Layer Becomes the Moat
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:00']
- **Claim**: In the recruiting example, the agent pulls candidate profiles from a data provider, prepares a shortlist, and the user stops opening the recruiting tool. The company still gets paid because it sells the data, not the interface. This shows that the data layer retains value while the application layer is commoditized.
- **Role**: evidence

### Incumbents Reposition Around the Stack
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:43']
- **Claim**: Meta, Salesforce, and Microsoft are all repositioning: Meta connects its agent to business data, Salesforce leverages its data and distribution with a custom reasoning model (Koa), and Microsoft positions itself as a control plane with Azure data and Copilot/Autopilot. Each is trying to secure a place in the new stack.
- **Role**: evidence

### The New Stack: Data, Application, Agent
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:58']
- **Claim**: The common thread across all examples is the need to understand your position on three layers: the data layer, the application layer, and the agent layer. This framework applies to individuals, buyers, and sellers, and is essential for navigating the future of software.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Risk of Vendor Lock-In and the Need for Choice
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:16:26']
- **Claim**: Buyers want to avoid being locked into a single AI vendor. This is why Salesforce partners with Claude and offers a control panel for multiple models. Sellers must offer choice to remain attractive, and buyers must understand their own unique stack to avoid being trapped.
- **Role**: counter

## Evidence and caveats

The video uses several real-world examples: DoorDash's agent-based ordering and MCP integration, Salesforce's Koa reasoning model, Microsoft's Autopilot, and a case study from CrossData about a recruiting firm building its own skill. The speaker notes that agents help navigate corporate complexity, as seen with Workday's payroll agent. Caveats include the risk of vendor lock-in and the difficulty of changing AI vendors due to idiosyncratic usage patterns. The speaker emphasizes that individuals should share their AI workflows to avoid losing productivity if the company changes suppliers.
