## TL;DR

AI labs are hiring humans to bridge the gap between general model capabilities and specific enterprise workflows. This role combines product judgment with engineering to find high-leverage bottlenecks rather than just writing code. The last mile of AI adoption requires this hybrid skill set to realize measurable business value.

## The argument

### Define the FTE as a Translator
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:30']
- **Claim**: FTEs bridge the gap between general AI capabilities and specific enterprise workflows by translating vague business goals into technical implementations. They identify high-leverage bottlenecks, such as missing documents in insurance claims, to maximize impact with minimal risk.
- **Role**: definition

### Identify Leverage Points
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:18']
- **Claim**: The core skill is finding points of leverage where a small build moves the largest amount of work without giving the model dangerous authority. This involves analyzing real data to find delays, like incomplete intake files, and measuring the potential time savings before building.
- **Role**: evidence

### Master Technical Delivery
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:13:24']
- **Claim**: Technical delivery requires understanding system architecture, permissions, and constructing rigorous evals. Domain expertise matters significantly; experts achieve verified success more than twice as often as novices in tasks like code generation when using AI tools.
- **Role**: evidence

### Own Deployment & Iteration
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:16:13']
- **Claim**: FTEs must own the system after launch, measuring real-world performance and iterating based on user feedback. This ownership mindset ensures the AI capability delivers extraordinary value rather than just incremental improvements, requiring a cycle of measuring, learning, and building.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites OpenAI paying up to $280,000 for this role and Anthropic training tens of thousands of engineers. A key caveat is that industry knowledge cannot substitute for technical requirements in roles demanding production software engineering; however, non-technical users can achieve near-parity with engineers by using AI to build small projects and focusing on system understanding over line-by-line coding. The speaker also notes that while the role is hybrid, specific job titles vary widely in their coding expectations, so candidates must read descriptions carefully.
