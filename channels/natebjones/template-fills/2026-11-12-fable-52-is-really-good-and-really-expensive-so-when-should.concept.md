## TL;DR

Fable 5.2 is a powerful but expensive AI model that excels at complex, high-stakes tasks. Its value is task-dependent, and businesses must identify high-return problems to justify its cost. This leads to a K-shaped economy where some workers and companies benefit greatly while others see little improvement.

## The argument

### Define the K-shaped economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:53']
- **Claim**: The K-shaped AI economy splits into two groups: labs and advanced users on the upper slope, and regular businesses on the lower slope. The divergence is driven by the ability to find tasks where the model's improvement is worth its cost.
- **Role**: definition

### Evidence: e-ink display success
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:03']
- **Claim**: After months of failed attempts with other models, Fable 5.2 succeeded by analyzing previous attempts and inferring the user's design intentions. This demonstrates the model's ability to handle complex, ill-defined tasks where the user knows what they want but can't articulate it.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: task-level saturation
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:35']
- **Claim**: Many business tasks are already saturated by existing models, so upgrading to Fable 5.2 yields minimal benefit. Even when improvement is real, the high cost (e.g., $250 for one API dialogue) makes it hard to justify, leading to a 'task-level saturation' that limits adoption.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: high-return tasks and human judgment
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:21']
- **Claim**: To justify Fable 5.2's cost, businesses must identify high-return problems where spending a few hundred dollars yields $10,000 in value. This requires higher-order cognitive skills—recognizing useful problems, evaluating solutions—and a shift from AI as assistant to intelligent partner, where humans act as critical judges.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Anthropic reports Fable 5.2 performed 41% of freelance projects at least as well as a human professional, vs. 21% for Opus 3.5. The author's e-ink display project succeeded after three failed attempts, highlighting the model's ability to infer intent from context. However, the cost is significant: $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, with users reporting stalled runs due to credit limits. The author notes that task-level saturation means many business tasks won't benefit from the upgrade, and that human judgment remains crucial for evaluating outputs. The author also admits that his design success was partly due to his own extensive thinking about the problem, not just the model.
