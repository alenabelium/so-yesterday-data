## Thesis

AI leaderboards and benchmarks are dead; the new test for any AI product is customer obsession and real-world usefulness, not raw model capability.

## Argument

The mechanism is the lab-to-client map. Anthropic owns the corporate sector, OpenAI spans enterprise and consumer, Meta is deep in consumer AI—but Google's Argon tops a benchmark while almost no one can use it, so nobody can tell you if it's useful. That disconnect is the failure mode. The best AI products come from teams that obsessively use them, notice when things go wrong, and fix real problems. I saw this firsthand: I used Dots to connect all my subscription sources, build a financial model, then handed it to Muse to cancel subscriptions. Dots is slower but great with connectors; Muse is faster and better at web tasks. I use both because each solves a different real problem. That's the customer experience you only get by using the product. The bottleneck is no longer the models—it's the product experience and customer care. We're stuck in the tyranny of chatbots, copying ChatGPT's design. We need broader design and more empathy. The next year in AI will be the year of the product, not benchmarks.

## Counterpoints

- Some say you can just cancel subscriptions yourself, but delegating to AI saves time when you're short on it.
- Apocalyptic discussions about AI prevent us from hoping for the best for our clients.
- People worry about trusting only Anthropic or OpenAI; we need more players like Google for safety and consumer choice.
