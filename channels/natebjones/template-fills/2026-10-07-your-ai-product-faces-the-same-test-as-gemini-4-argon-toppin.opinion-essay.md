## Thesis

AI leaderboards and benchmarks are dead; the new test for AI products is customer obsession and real-world usefulness, not raw model capability.

## Argument

The mechanism is the lab-to-client map. Anthropic targets the corporate sector, OpenAI splits between enterprise and consumer, and Meta is deeply immersed in the consumer segment. Google, despite making money from computing and serving search, has Gemini — but Argon, its new model topping a leaderboard, has no clear customer-facing use case. Why do we need such an advanced model if no one can use it? That's the disconnect.

Customer obsession is a business discipline as much as an engineering one. The best AI products come from teams that use them obsessively, notice when things go wrong, and fix real problems. I saw this firsthand with Dots and Muse. Dots runs slower but handles connectors and large data well — it processed 20,000 likes and bookmarks from my X account to build a reading list. Muse is faster and better at delegating tasks like canceling subscriptions. Each tool has strengths and weaknesses, and you only discover them by using them.

A business needs to use the products it sells more often to be confident in specific usage scenarios. There's room for many winners — eight billion people, countless use cases. We need more players like Google, but also outside Google, providing AI in a clear and accessible form. The next year in AI will be the year of the product, not the year of benchmarks.

## Counterpoints

- Some might say you can just go to the website and do it yourself, but when you're short on time, delegating to AI is valuable.
- Apocalyptic discussions about AI prevent us from hoping for the best for our clients and what intelligence can do if it's everywhere.
- People might worry about trusting AI for medical advice, but it can provide an outside perspective and empathy without being a doctor.
