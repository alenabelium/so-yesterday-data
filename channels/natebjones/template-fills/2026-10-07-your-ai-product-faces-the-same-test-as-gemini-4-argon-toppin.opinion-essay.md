## Thesis

AI leaderboards and benchmarks are dead; the new test for AI products is customer obsession and real-world product experience, not raw model capability.

## Argument

The mechanism is customer obsession. Google's Argon model tops a leaderboard, but almost no one can use it right now, so no one can tell you if it's useful. The AI that wins is created by humans who obsessively use it and fix bugs. This is the same test for the AI your company is creating.

Look at the lab-to-client map: Anthropic focuses on the corporate sector, OpenAI has enterprise and consumer arms, Meta is deeply immersed in consumer AI. But Google? Google makes money from computing and serves people through search, yet Argon's use cases are unclear. Why do we need such an advanced model? The close relationship between customer and product is what matters in the age of AI.

I want Google to succeed because more players make things safer and better for consumers. But I don't see Argon interacting with customers yet. For companies, the challenge is: in a world where intelligence evolves every week, how do you make money with AI when the underlying model keeps changing? Development plans don't work now. The bigger challenge is creating a product with ever-evolving intelligence that helps users understand what will happen to their world when AI changes for the better.

The best AI products are created by people who use them obsessively, notice when things go wrong, and give meaning to those mistakes. It's a business discipline as much as an engineering one. ## Counterpoints

- Some might say you can just go to the website and do it yourself, but when short on time, delegating to AI is nice.
- People worry about trusting Anthropic or OpenAI, but more players like Google make things safer and better for consumers.
- Some might argue benchmarks still matter, but they don't reflect real-world usefulness or customer experience.
- There's a concern that AI is apocalyptic, but we need hope for what intelligence can do if it's everywhere.
- Some might say AI isn't perfect, but the imperfections are now due to product experience, not models.
