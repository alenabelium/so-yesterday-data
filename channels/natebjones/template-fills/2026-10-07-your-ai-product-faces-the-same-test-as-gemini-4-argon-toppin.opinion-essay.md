## Thesis

AI leaderboards are dead; the new benchmark is customer obsession, and the AI products that win will be built by teams who use them obsessively and fix real problems.

## Argument

The mechanism is the lab-to-client map. Anthropic targets the corporate sector, OpenAI has enterprise and consumer arms, Meta is deeply consumer-focused, but Google's Argon tops benchmarks with no clear customer-facing use case. Why do we need such an advanced model if no one can use it? The answer is we don't — not without a product that serves a real need.

This is a business discipline as much as an engineering one. The best AI products come from people who use them obsessively, notice when things go wrong, and give meaning to those mistakes. I've lived this with Dots and Muse: Dots processes 20,000 likes and bookmarks from my X account, building a reading list of 741 books; Muse handles subscription cancellations and even offered empathetic advice to a friend whose child injured an eye. These are the real-world tests that matter.

The bottleneck has shifted. In 2024 and 2025, model imperfections were the problem. Now, the imperfections in our AI experiences are due to product design, not models. We've fallen into the tyranny of chatbots — every product borrows ChatGPT's design philosophy. We need a broader design approach, more empathy, and more customer focus.

So when I see Argon's test results, I ask: where will it appear, and for which customers? Google, please release it. I want it to succeed because more players make AI safer and better for consumers. The next year in AI will be the year of the product, not the year of benchmarks.

## Counterpoints

- You can just go to the website and do it yourself — but when you're short on time, delegating to AI is valuable.
- AI isn't a doctor — but it can provide an outside perspective and empathy in a moment of need.
- Benchmarks still show capability — but they don't tell you if a model is useful in practice.
