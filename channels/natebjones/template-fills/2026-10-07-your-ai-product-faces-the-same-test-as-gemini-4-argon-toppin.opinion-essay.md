## Thesis

AI leaderboards and benchmarks are dead; the real test of an AI product is customer obsession and real-world product experience, not topping performance charts.

## Argument

Benchmarks like those topped by Google's Argon model no longer indicate success because they fail to answer the fundamental question: which customers will use this and for what? The best AI products emerge from teams that obsessively use them, notice failures, and fix real problems. I've seen this firsthand with tools like Dots and Muse, where practical tasks—managing subscriptions, processing bookmarks, or getting parenting advice—reveal true value. The bottleneck has shifted from model capability to product experience and customer care. As models evolve weekly, companies must focus on creating products that evolve with intelligence and serve specific users, not chase leaderboard scores. The year ahead is the year of the product, not benchmarks.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue you can just do tasks yourself, but delegating to AI saves time and provides results when you're short on time.
- Skeptics worry about AI trust and safety, but more players like Google in the game makes things safer and better for consumers.
- Apocalyptic discussions about AI prevent us from hoping for the best for clients and seeing the positive potential of intelligence.
