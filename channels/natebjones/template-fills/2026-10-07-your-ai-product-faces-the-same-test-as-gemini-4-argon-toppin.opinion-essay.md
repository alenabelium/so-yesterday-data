## Thesis

AI leaderboards and benchmarks are dead; the new test for AI products is customer obsession and real-world usefulness, not raw model capability.

## Argument

The mechanism is the lab-to-client map. Anthropic owns the corporate sector, OpenAI spans enterprise and consumer, Meta is deep in consumer AI, but Google's Argon tops a leaderboard with no clear customer-facing use case. That's the tell: a model that leads a benchmark but has no product home fails the only test that matters now.

When intelligence evolves weekly, the old product discipline of shipping a fixed release and updating monthly breaks down. The real challenge is building a product with ever-evolving intelligence that helps users understand what changes for the better. That starts with knowing who your customer is and what they do — not with a score.

The best AI products come from teams that use them obsessively, notice when things go wrong, and fix those bugs. That's a business discipline as much as an engineering one. I live this: I used Dots to connect Mercury and other financial sources, pull together all my subscriptions, build a financial model, then uploaded it to Muse to act as my hunting drone. Dots is slower but great with connectors; Muse is faster and can delegate tasks like canceling a subscription. I even use Dots to process 20,000 likes and bookmarks on X to build a reading list — it's already found 741 books. That's the customer experience you get when you actually use AI.

A business must use the products it sells to know which scenarios work. There's room for many winners — eight billion people, endless use cases. We need more players, like Google, but also outside Google, who deliver AI in a clear, accessible form.

## Counterpoints
