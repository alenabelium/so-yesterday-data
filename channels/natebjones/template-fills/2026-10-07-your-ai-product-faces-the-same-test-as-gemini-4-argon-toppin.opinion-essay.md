## Thesis

AI leaderboards and benchmarks are dead; the new test for AI products is customer obsession and real-world usefulness, not topping a performance chart.

## Argument

The mechanism is product experience. Google's Argon model tops a leaderboard, but almost no one can use it, so no one can tell you if it's useful. The labs map to clear customer segments—Anthropic to corporate, OpenAI to enterprise and consumer, Meta to consumer—but Google's Argon lacks a clear customer-facing use case. This is the same test your company's AI faces: in a world where intelligence evolves weekly, you can't rely on development plans; you must start by understanding who your customer is and what they do.

The best AI products come from teams that use them obsessively, notice when things go wrong, and fix real problems. I use Dots and Muse for practical tasks—consolidating subscriptions, building a reading list from 20,000 likes, delegating cancellations. These tools work because they solve specific problems, not because they score well on benchmarks. The imperfections in AI experiences now come from product design, not models. Models are no longer the bottleneck; product experience and customer care are.

We've fallen into the tyranny of chatbots, copying ChatGPT's design. We need broader design approaches and more empathy. The next year in AI will be the year of the product, not benchmarks. We need more players like Google, but also outside, providing AI in clear, accessible forms. Everyone must become an AI expert, and the opportunity is to create something you'd use yourself, that evolves with AI and serves specific customers.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue you can just do tasks yourself on a website, but delegating to AI saves time when you're short on time.
- Apocalyptic discussions about AI prevent hope for what intelligence can do if it's everywhere.
- People worry about trusting Anthropic or OpenAI, but more players like Google make things safer for consumers.
