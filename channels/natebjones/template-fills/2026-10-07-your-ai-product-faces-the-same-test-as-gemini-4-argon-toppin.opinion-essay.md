## Thesis

AI leaderboards and benchmarks are dead; the new test for AI products is customer obsession and real-world product experience, not raw model capability.

## Argument

The mechanism is the lab-to-client map. Anthropic owns the corporate sector, OpenAI splits enterprise and consumer, Meta is deep in consumer AI, but Google's Argon tops a leaderboard with no clear customer-facing use case. That disconnect is the tell: benchmarks no longer predict usefulness. The real work is building products with ever-evolving intelligence that help users navigate change, and that starts with knowing who your customer is and what they do. The best AI products come from teams that use them obsessively, notice when things go wrong, and fix them — a business discipline as much as an engineering one. My own workflow proves it: I used Dots to pull together scattered subscription data and build a financial model, then handed it to Muse to sort and cancel subscriptions. Dots handles long, complex jobs like processing 20,000 likes and bookmarks into a reading list; Muse is faster and better at web tasks. Each tool has strengths and weaknesses, and I only know that because I use them. That's the customer experience you get when you actually try AI. Businesses need to use their own products more to be confident in what works. There's room for many winners — eight billion people, countless use cases. We need more players like Google, but also outside Google, who deliver AI in clear, accessible forms. Everyone now has to be an AI expert, and the opportunity is to create something you'd want to use yourself, that evolves with AI and serves specific customers. ## Counterpoints

- You can just go to the website and cancel the subscription yourself — but when you're short on time, delegating to AI is genuinely nice.
- Some worry about trusting Anthropic or OpenAI and want other players — more players makes things safer and better for consumers.
- Apocalyptic discussions about AI prevent us from hoping for the best for our clients and seeing what intelligence can do.
- Models were imperfect in 2024 and 2025, but now the bottlenecks are product experience and customer care, not the models.
