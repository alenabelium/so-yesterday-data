## Thesis

AI leaderboards and benchmarks are dead; the new test for AI products is customer obsession and real-world product experience, not raw model capability.

## Argument

The mechanism is the shift from model capability to product experience. Google's Argon tops a leaderboard, but almost no one can use it, so no one can tell if it's useful. The AI that wins is created by humans who obsessively use it and fix bugs. This is the new benchmark: customer obsession. The lab-to-client map shows Anthropic focused on corporate, OpenAI on enterprise and consumer, Meta on consumer, but Google's Argon lacks a clear customer-facing use case. The bigger challenge is creating a product with ever-evolving intelligence that helps users understand what will happen to their world when AI changes for the better. This is a tough product challenge, and if you don't start by understanding who your customer is and what they do, you won't get far. The best AI products are created by people who use them obsessively, notice when things go wrong, and give meaning to those mistakes. It's a business discipline as much as an engineering one. I share my own experience using Dots and Muse to manage subscriptions, showing where AI performs best and where it doesn't. Dots is slower but great with connectors and processing large amounts of information; Muse is faster and better for delegation. A business needs to use the products it sells more often to be confident in specific usage scenarios. There will be room for many winners in AI, and we should not complain about useful agents that actually work. We need more players like Google, but also outside, who can provide AI in a clear and accessible form. We've fallen into the tyranny of chatbots; we need a broader design

## Counterpoints

- Some might say you can just go to the website and do it yourself, but when you're short on time, it's nice to delegate the work to AI and just get the result.
- Apocalyptic discussions about AI often prevent us from hoping for the best for our clients, but we need more customer optimism.
- People might worry about trusting AI with personal tasks, but the value is in the outside perspective and delegation, not in replacing human judgment.
