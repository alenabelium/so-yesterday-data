## Thesis

Microsoft's Autopilot, built on OpenClaw, will reach far more workers than any other AI this year, and its value depends on data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft's 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, and Autopilot is embedded in Excel, Outlook, and Teams—where you already work. It's not smarter; it's everywhere. The value comes from the data it can use and how well you know how to use AI. This creates a K-shaped future: people with the same tools get wildly different results. The five practices that determine which slope you're on are: define what 'good' looks like, provide context and verify evidence, make tasks repeatable, match reasoning to complexity, and obsess over improving the next run. Microsoft's advantage is trust and existing relationships, not model quality. They monetize trust, not intelligence. The real competition is about who understands the job well enough that we keep coming back to delegate work to them.

## Counterpoints

- The strongest argument against Microsoft winning is that labs like Anthropic are serious about improving the model itself, which could redesign workflows and eliminate the need for Microsoft's processes.
- A sufficiently powerful model can find more efficient ways to prepare updates, potentially making weekly reports and meetings obsolete.
- Meta is a contender with huge data, distribution, and AI talent, targeting small businesses with its enterprise platform.
- Autopilot is still in preview and not rolled out to half a billion people overnight, so its impact is gradual.
- The cost of AI agents and token usage is a concern, and routing models by cost may not reflect the value of rework.
