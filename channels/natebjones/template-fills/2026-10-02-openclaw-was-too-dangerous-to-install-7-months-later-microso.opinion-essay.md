## Thesis

The value of enterprise AI comes from data access and user skill, not model intelligence, and Microsoft's distribution advantage makes it the default AI for most companies.

## Argument

Microsoft's 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, giving it a distribution advantage that matters more than model smarts. Autopilot, based on OpenClaude, is an agent with identity, memory, and workspace, but it's not very intelligent. The real value is in the data it can access and how well you use it. This creates a K-shaped future where people with the same tools get wildly different results. To land on the upward slope, follow five practices. First, define what 'good' looks like for a specific task. Second, put the right information in front of the model and check if it used it. Third, make a successful task repeatable with triggers and accountability. Fourth, match the model's reasoning to the complexity of the task, especially with Microsoft's router. Fifth, improve the next run by fixing what caused the mess and sharing what works. The competitive landscape is expanding beyond OpenAI vs. Anthropic, with Meta entering the enterprise space. But for most workers, the AI you're allowed to use at work is Microsoft, and learning these skills now is your chance to deliver more useful work.
