## Thesis

Microsoft's Autopilot, built on OpenClaw, will reach far more workers than any other AI this year, and its value depends on data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft's 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, and Autopilot is embedded in the tools people already use—Excel, Outlook, Teams. This is why it matters more than any model release this year. The value isn't in intelligence; it's in the data the agent can access and how well you use it. The future is K-shaped: same tools, wildly different results.

To stay on the upward slope, you need five practices. First, define what 'good' looks like for a real task—tell the agent what a useful outcome is, and evaluate its output against that standard. Second, put the right context and data in front of the model, and verify it actually used them. Check the evidence tree. Third, make good tasks repeatable—set triggers, schedules, and limits, so the agent works while you do something else. Fourth, match reasoning to complexity—use stronger models for hard problems, and give the router the context it needs to distinguish trivial from complex requests. Fifth, obsess over improving the next run—fix the cause of failures, save what works, and share it with colleagues.

Microsoft's advantage is trust and existing relationships. It doesn't need to win every model comparison; it just needs to be useful and manageable. But the strongest counterargument is that better models will eventually redesign the processes you automate, making many steps obsolete. That's Anthropic's play. Still, for most workers, the AI you're allowed to use is Microsoft's, and learning these skills now is your best bet.

## Counterpoints

- Microsoft doesn't have its own frontier model, so it may lose to labs like Anthropic that can redesign workflows more efficiently.
- Autopilot's router may waste tokens on context, making it cheaper to just use a smarter model.
- The agent might reproduce reports no one needs, just because you taught it to.
- Meta is entering the enterprise with its own platform, targeting small businesses with customer data.
