## Thesis

Microsoft's Autopilot, built on OpenClaw, will reach far more workers than any rival AI this year, and the value it creates depends on data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft has 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats, versus OpenAI's 9 million business users, and it's embedding Autopilot into Excel, Outlook, and Teams—the tools people already use. This means the AI you're allowed to use at work is about to start completing tasks, not just answering questions. The value isn't in the model's intelligence—it's not very smart—but in the data it can access and how well you use it. That's why the future is K-shaped: people with the same tools get wildly different results.

To get on the upward slope, you need five practices. First, define what 'good' looks like—give Autopilot a specific task and tell it what a useful outcome is. Second, put the right information in front of the model and check if it actually used it; most people don't do this. Third, make a good task repeatable—set a trigger, schedule, and limits. Fourth, match reasoning to complexity—routers that pick models based on cost can't distinguish between a simple syntax error and a three-hour coding problem if they only see the same words. Fifth, obsessively improve the next run—fix the cause of failures, save what works, and share it.

Microsoft's edge is trust and existing relationships, not model superiority. They can monetize by calling existing customers, and they don't need to win every benchmark. But the strongest counterargument is that better models, like Anthropic's, could redesign workflows entirely, making Microsoft's processes obsolete. The competition is now about who understands the job well enough that we keep coming back to delegate

## Counterpoints

- A sufficiently powerful model can find a more efficient way to prepare updates, potentially eliminating the need for weekly reports and meetings that exist only to coordinate human activity.
- Microsoft doesn't have its own frontier model, so it may lose the intelligence race to labs like Anthropic, which could redesign corporate processes from scratch.
- Automatic routing can be inefficient: you might spend more tokens figuring out which model to use than just sending the task to a smart model.
- Meta is entering the enterprise with its own platform, leveraging its customer relationships and data, which could challenge Microsoft's workplace dominance.
