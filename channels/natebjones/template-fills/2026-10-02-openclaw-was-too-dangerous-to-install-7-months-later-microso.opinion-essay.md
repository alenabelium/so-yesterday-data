## Thesis

Microsoft is bringing OpenClaw-derived agent technology to the enterprise as Autopilot, and the value it creates depends not on model intelligence but on data access and user skill.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft's 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, and Autopilot is embedded in the tools people already use—Excel, Outlook, Teams. This isn't about being smarter; it's about being everywhere. The real differentiator is the data an agent can access and how well you use it. 

First, define what 'good' looks like. Give Autopilot a specific task tied to a real outcome, like identifying outstanding customer obligations before a renewal. Then, provide the right context and verify the evidence. Most people don't do this, but it works across all models. 

Second, make tasks repeatable. Set up scheduled prompts or agents that run continuously, with clear triggers and limits. This is where the economics get interesting: Microsoft's router selects models based on cost and accuracy, but it needs context to distinguish between a trivial request and a complex problem. You must match reasoning to complexity. 

Third, obsess over improvement. When a briefing misses a commitment, find out why—missing source, unclear instructions, or model failure. Fix the cause, save the fix, and share it. This learning cycle is what separates the 5% who get value from the rest. 

Microsoft's advantage is trust and distribution, but Anthropic and Meta are competing on intelligence and new entry points. The future is K-shaped: those who master these practices rise, others fall.

## Counterpoints

- A sufficiently powerful model can redesign workflows, eliminating the need for reports and meetings that exist only to coordinate human activity.
- Microsoft doesn't have its own frontier model, so it may lose the intelligence race to labs like Anthropic.
- Router systems can't distinguish between tasks with identical text but different complexity, leading to wasted tokens or errors.
- Meta's enterprise platform and customer data could disrupt Microsoft's workplace dominance.
