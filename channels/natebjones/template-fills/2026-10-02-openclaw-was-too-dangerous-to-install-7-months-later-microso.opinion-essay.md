## Thesis

Microsoft is bringing OpenClaw-derived agents to 450 million seats, and the value comes from data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft's 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, and in one company after another, Microsoft is the AI you're allowed to use. An agent your employer approves of matters more to your workday than the model everyone argues about online. Autopilot isn't very smart, but that's the point—value comes from the data it can access and how well you know how to use AI. This creates a K-shaped future: people with the same tools get very different results. The first practice is to define what 'good' looks like. Give Autopilot a specific task, like identifying outstanding customer obligations before a renewal conversation, and tell it what a useful result would be. Most people leave the standard unspoken, so they get average briefings. The second practice is to put the correct information in front of the model and check if it actually used it. Provide the account plan, client commitments, and service data, and tell it which source takes priority. Then ask for an evidence tree and open the links. When responses are weak, check what information the tool had before blaming the model—often it's an access or permissions issue. The third practice is to make a good task repeatable. Once you get a result you'd actually use, decide what triggers the agent to start again and what it's responsible for between runs.
