## Thesis

Microsoft's Autopilot brings OpenClaw-derived agent technology to 450 million enterprise seats, but the real value comes from data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution, not intelligence. Microsoft's 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, and in most companies, Microsoft is the AI you're allowed to use. Autopilot, built on OpenClaw, is entering this ecosystem, and while it's not very smart, that doesn't matter. The value comes from the data it can access and how well you use it. This creates a K-shaped future: people with the same tools get wildly different results. The first practice is to define what 'good' looks like for a specific task. For a contract renewal, tell the agent to identify outstanding obligations and what needs to happen before the conversation. Then, check if the output actually helps you act. The second practice is to put the right information in front of the model and verify it used it. Give it the account plan, commitments, and service data, and ask for an evidence tree. If the response is weak, check what data it had before blaming the model. The third practice is to make good tasks repeatable. Set a trigger, define responsibilities, and monitor the agent's periodic reports. The fourth practice is to match reasoning to complexity. Separate routine prep from consistent interpretation, and give hard problems the context they need. The fifth practice is to obsessively improve the next run. Find out why a commitment was missed, fix the cause, and share what works. Microsoft's advantage is its existing trust and distribution, but the real competition is about who understands the job well enough to delegate to.

## Counterpoints

- A sufficiently powerful model can find more efficient ways to do corporate work, potentially eliminating the need for the processes you automate.
- Anthropic's guided agents and Claude's Excel/PowerPoint add-ins could make another company the place we start when we want the job done.
- Meta's Enterprise Platform and Muse target business customers, leveraging its customer relationship data to compete with Microsoft.
