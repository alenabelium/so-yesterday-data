## Thesis

Microsoft is bringing OpenClaw-derived agent technology to the enterprise as Autopilot, and the value it delivers depends on data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft's 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, so Autopilot reaches workers where they already are—in Excel, Outlook, Teams—without requiring them to become engineers. The agent, though not the smartest, gains power from the data it can access and how well you use it. Five practices determine which side of the K-shaped future you land on: define what 'good' looks like, provide the right context and verify evidence, make tasks repeatable, match reasoning to complexity, and obsess over improving the next run. Microsoft's advantage is data access, not intelligence, but the real competition is about who understands the job well enough to keep you delegating to them.

## Counterpoints

- A sufficiently powerful model can find more efficient ways to do corporate work, potentially eliminating the need for processes you planned to automate.
- Automatic routing may cost more tokens to figure out than just using a smart model, especially for complex new problems.
- Meta's enterprise platform and Anthropic's guided agents are contenders that could shift where work gets done.
