## Thesis

Microsoft's Autopilot, built on OpenClaw-derived technology, will reach far more workers than any rival AI this year, and its value hinges on data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft's 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, and Autopilot is embedded in the tools people already use—Excel, Outlook, Teams. This isn't about being smarter; it's about being everywhere. The K-shaped future means the same tool yields wildly different results based on how you use it. The first practice is defining what 'good' looks like: give Autopilot a specific task, tell it what a useful outcome is, and evaluate its output against that standard. Second, feed it the right context—account plans, commitments, service data—and verify it actually used them. Third, make successful tasks repeatable: set triggers, schedules, and boundaries. Fourth, match reasoning to complexity: separate routine prep from nuanced interpretation, and be explicit about when a task is hard. Fifth, obsess over improving the next run: diagnose failures, fix instructions or data, and share what works. Microsoft's advantage is data access and trust, not model quality. Even if the model is mediocre, the agent's ability to pull from your company's internal systems—customer records, meeting notes, permissions—creates real value. The counterpoint is that a sufficiently powerful model could redesign workflows entirely, eliminating the need for reports and meetings that exist only to coordinate humans. That's Anthropic's and Meta's opening. But for now, the 5% who master these practices will race ahead, regardless of which vendor wins.

## Counterpoints

- A sufficiently powerful model could redesign workflows, making current processes obsolete—Anthropic's guided agents and Claude's Office add-ins threaten Microsoft's lock-in.
- Automatic routing may cost more tokens than just using a smart model, especially for complex new problems that require reasoning to even classify.
- Meta's Enterprise Platform and business tools could leverage its customer relationships to compete with Microsoft's workplace dominance.
