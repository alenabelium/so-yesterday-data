## Thesis

Microsoft is bringing OpenClaw-derived agent technology to the enterprise as Autopilot, and the value it creates depends on data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft's 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, and Autopilot is embedded in the tools people already use—Excel, Outlook, Teams. This means the AI you have access to is about to start doing a different kind of work: completing tasks while you do something else. The value comes from the data it can use and how well you know how to use AI, not from raw intelligence. The future takes a K-shape: people with the same tools get very different results. To land on the upward slope, you need five practices. First, define what 'good' looks like—give Autopilot a specific task and tell it what a useful result is. Second, put the correct information in front of the model and check if it actually used it; build an evidence tree and verify sources. Third, make a good task repeatable—set triggers, schedules, and limits, and test the next run. Fourth, match reasoning to complexity—separate routine preparation from consistent interpretation, and give hard problems the context they need. Fifth, obsessively improve the next launch—fix the cause of failures, save what works, and share it with colleagues. This is a skill problem, not an intelligence problem, and it travels with you regardless of which AI your employer adopts.

## Counterpoints

- A sufficiently powerful model can find more efficient ways to do corporate work, potentially eliminating the processes you planned to automate—this is the strongest argument for Anthropic's enterprise scaling.
- Microsoft doesn't have its own frontier model, so it may lose the intelligence race to labs like Anthropic, even if it wins on distribution.
- Automatic routing can waste tokens figuring out which model to use, and complex new problems may require significant reasoning just to identify what they are.
- Meta is entering the enterprise space with its own platform, leveraging its customer relationships and data to compete with Microsoft.
