## Thesis

The AI you have access to is about to start doing a different kind of work, and the value comes from data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

Microsoft's 450 million paid seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, giving it a distribution advantage that matters more than model smarts. The K-shaped future means people with the same tools get wildly different results. To land on the upward slope, you must define what 'good' looks like, give the model the right context and data, make tasks repeatable, match reasoning to complexity, and improve the next run. This is a skill problem, not an intelligence problem. Even if Autopilot isn't the smartest, it has access to your company's data—customer records, permissions, meeting notes—which is where the real value lies. Microsoft's Work IQ ad shows them thinking about this too, connecting sales records with service data to spot problems. The launch of Autopilot, based on OpenClaw, brings agentic workflows to non-engineers. But the strongest counterargument is that better models, like Claude, could redesign workflows entirely, making some processes obsolete. The competition is now Microsoft vs. Anthropic vs. Meta, each leveraging their existing relationships—Microsoft with workplace, Meta with customer reach. For you, the question is what you learn to do with the tools you have. Don't wait for the perfect product; start learning now.
