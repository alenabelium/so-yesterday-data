## Thesis

Microsoft is bringing OpenClaw-derived agent technology to the enterprise as Autopilot, and the value comes from data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft has 450 million paid Microsoft 365 seats, dwarfing OpenAI's 9 million business users. Autopilot is embedded in Excel, Outlook, and Teams, so it reaches workers where they already are. The value isn't in the model's intelligence—it's in the data it can access and how well you use it. This creates a K-shaped future: people with the same tools get vastly different results. To stay on the upward slope, you need five practices. First, define what 'good' looks like for a specific task. Second, provide the right context and data, and check if the model used it. Third, make successful tasks repeatable. Fourth, match reasoning to complexity, especially with automatic routing. Fifth, obsessively improve your next use. Microsoft's advantage is its existing relationship with IT and its data access, not model superiority. But Anthropic and Meta are competing, and the real competition is about who understands the job well enough that we keep coming back to delegate work.

## Counterpoints

- Microsoft doesn't have its own frontier model, so labs like Anthropic could redesign workflows more efficiently.
- Autopilot's router may not have enough context to distinguish between simple and complex tasks, leading to wasted tokens.
- Meta's enterprise platform and data on customer relationships could challenge Microsoft's workplace dominance.
