## Thesis

Value in enterprise AI comes from data access and user skill, not model intelligence, and Microsoft's distribution advantage will shape the market.

## Argument

Microsoft's 450 million paid seats dwarf OpenAI's 9 million business users, giving it a massive distribution advantage. The real value isn't in the model's intelligence—it's in the data it can access and how well you use it. This is why Microsoft is bringing OpenClaw-derived agents to the enterprise via Autopilot.

To leverage this, you need five practices. First, define what "good" looks like for a specific task. Second, provide the right context and data, and check if the model actually used it. Third, make successful tasks repeatable. Fourth, match reasoning to complexity—don't use a cheap model for a hard problem. Fifth, improve the next run by fixing what went wrong.

Microsoft's advantage is its existing relationships and trust, not model superiority. But the strongest counterargument is that better models will eventually redesign workflows, making processes like weekly reports unnecessary. This is why Anthropic's guided agents and Claude's Excel/Word add-ins matter.

The competitive landscape is expanding beyond OpenAI vs. Anthropic. Meta's Enterprise Platform targets small businesses with its customer data. For you, the question is what you learn to do with the tools you have. A small group (5%) figures out how to get useful work done, while others get a fraction of the value. Don't wait for the perfect product—start learning now.
