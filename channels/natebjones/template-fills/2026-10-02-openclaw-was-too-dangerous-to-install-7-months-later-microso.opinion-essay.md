## Thesis

Microsoft is bringing OpenClaw-derived agent technology to the enterprise as Autopilot, and the value it delivers will depend on data access and user skill, not model intelligence.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Microsoft already covers about 450 million paid Microsoft 365 commercial seats, compared to OpenAI's 9 million paid business users. That installed base means Autopilot, even in preview, reaches workers who never touch a terminal. The agent's intelligence is secondary; what matters is the data it can reach and how well you direct it. The first practice is defining what 'good' looks like for a real task, like a contract renewal. You tell the AI what a useful briefing contains, then check whether it distinguished a promise from a client request. The second is feeding it the right context and verifying it used it—give it the account plan, the commitments, the service history, and ask for an evidence tree. When the response is weak, check what information the tool had before blaming the model. The third is making a good task repeatable: set a trigger, define accountability, and test the next run. The fourth is matching reasoning to complexity—separate routine preparation from consistent interpretation, and give hard problems the context they need. The fifth is obsessively improving the next launch: find out why a commitment was missed, fix the cause, save the fix, and share it. This is a skill that travels with you, regardless of which vendor wins.

## Counterpoints

- A sufficiently powerful model can find a more efficient way to prepare updates, potentially eliminating the need for weekly reports and meetings, which is the strongest argument for Anthropic's enterprise scaling.
- Microsoft doesn't have its own frontier model, so it may lose the intelligence race even if it wins distribution.
- Routing models by cost and accuracy is tricky: the same query 'What is this?' can mean a trivial syntax error or a complex coding problem, and the router may not distinguish them.
- Meta is entering the enterprise with its own platform, leveraging its customer relationships and data, creating a new competitive front.
