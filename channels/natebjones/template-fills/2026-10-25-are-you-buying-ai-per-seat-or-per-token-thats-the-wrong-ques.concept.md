## TL;DR

The debate over AI pricing—per seat, per token, or per outcome—misses the real question: how to turn intelligence into economic value. Intelligence is a new economic resource alongside people and capital, and companies must develop the capability to apply it productively. Individuals can become 'intelligence managers' who decide where and how to deploy AI, shifting focus from cost-cutting to value creation.

## The argument

### Intelligence is a new economic resource
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: Tokens measure computation, jobs measure SaaS, but AI is intelligence. Since companies have existed, we've had two economic resources: people and capital. Now we have a third: intelligence. This changes how we value AI and what corporations do.
- **Role**: definition

### The burden of value creation falls on the buyer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:59']
- **Claim**: Token pricing fits budgets but doesn't guarantee useful thinking. The contract places the burden on the buyer to extract value. Companies struggle to make real profits from intelligence at scale, so they limit spending—but that's underinvesting. The real question is how to empower employees to make the right bets.
- **Role**: evidence

### The engineer as intelligence manager
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:27']
- **Claim**: In a hypothetical equipment company, an engineer uses AI to analyze recurring faults, but must decide when to stop and talk to a human. This judgment—when to use AI and when not—is the essence of intelligence management. It creates new products and revenue streams, turning intelligence into value.
- **Role**: evidence

### Partnerships and performance pricing as deeper collaboration
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:16']
- **Claim**: AI providers may have expertise that exceeds token value, leading to performance-based pricing or joint ventures. OpenAI's stake in Thrive and Anthropic's joint venture with Blackstone illustrate this. It's about both parties committing to figure out how to use intelligence to generate disproportionate value.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The counter: not all work fits outcome pricing
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:59']
- **Claim**: Outcome pricing works for closed loops like customer service, but not for complex, open-ended work like redesigning a manufacturing service program. Token pricing will persist. The variety of models reflects different levels of AI proficiency, and companies must instill ownership of intelligence to see ROI.
- **Role**: counter

## Evidence and caveats

Cedar's AI agent Cora resolves medical bills at 2 AM, showing outcome-based value. Shopify's COO Jess Hertz reports healthy ROI with revenue up 34% while headcount stayed flat. Block saw gross profit up 25% after downsizing and embracing AI. However, the speaker notes that effective pricing is difficult to measure, and not every company is AI-proficient. He cautions that outcome pricing works best for closed loops, and that front-end engineering as a service alone won't teach enough—balance is needed.
