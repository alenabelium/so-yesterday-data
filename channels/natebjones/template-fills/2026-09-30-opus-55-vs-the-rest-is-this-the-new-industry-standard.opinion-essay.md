## Thesis

Opus 5.5 is the new industry standard because it delivers unprecedented task-level efficiency, making complex work dramatically cheaper and faster than any previous model.

## Argument

The real measure of a model isn't token price but task efficiency: how many tokens, attempts, and corrections are needed to complete a job. Opus 5.5 shines here. My 514-piece LEGO build, with full instructions and parts list, consumed only 1% of my weekly Claude usage — about 50 cents of my subscription. At API prices, that same task would cost $44. This is the kind of efficiency that matters for real work.

Anthropic's pricing reinforces this: Opus 5.5 is 20% cheaper per token than Opus 5 and 60% cheaper than Fable 5.1. Combined with fewer tokens used, Anthropic claims typical workloads cost 40% less. That's not just a price cut; it's a fundamental improvement in how the model works.

This efficiency extends to writing. Opus 5.5 is far more controllable, preserving your intent when you ask for edits. It doesn't strip nuance or soften your stance. It responds to direction immediately, without the back-and-forth that plagued earlier versions.

Anthropic is also using AI to build AI: over 80% of code merged into their codebase is now Claude-authored. This recursive improvement lets them respond to user feedback faster, shortening the gap between releases and making models like Opus 5.5 more attuned to what users actually need.

To see if Opus 5.5 is right for you, run your own tasks with the same inputs and track token usage, API cost, and weekly usage. Compare against previous models. That's how you'll know if the efficiency gains are real for your work.

## Counterpoints

- Your tasks are not my tasks; a model that's efficient for one job may not be for another.
- Counting tokens too early can mislead; a great screenshot doesn't mean the whole task was efficient.
- Opus 5.5's persistence can lead to long, token-wasting runs without clear stopping conditions.
- Writing quality with AI is often assumed to be poor, but good AI writing preserves your intent.
- The 40% cost reduction claim is plausible but requires checking token usage across all segments.
