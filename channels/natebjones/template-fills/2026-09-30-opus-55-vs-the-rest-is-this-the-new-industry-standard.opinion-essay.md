## Thesis

Opus 5.5 is the new industry standard because it delivers task-level efficiency that makes advanced AI practical for everyday work, not just benchmarks.

## Argument

The real metric isn't token price—it's how many tokens a model burns to finish a job. Opus 5.5 turned a complex logo into a 514-piece LEGO model with full instructions, parts list, and inspection files using only 1% of my weekly Claude usage. At API rates, that same task would cost $44, but I paid about 50 cents. That's the efficiency story: fewer retries, fewer explanations, fewer wasted attempts.

Anthropic's pricing is 20% lower than Opus 5 and 60% lower than Fable 5.1, but the bigger win is that typical workloads cost 40% less because the model uses fewer tokens to complete the same work. GitHub, Lovable, and Spotify report similar gains. This isn't just about cost—it's about headroom. When a task uses 1% of my weekly budget, I can iterate, refine, and take on bigger projects without watching my usage meter.

The other breakthrough is controllability. Opus 5.5 listens. I can ask for a top hat instead of a cap, or more assembly steps, and the model adjusts without fighting me. That's the difference between sloppy AI and useful AI: preserving my intent. And because the model codes well, it can handle visual work—3D scenes, animations, even autonomous overnight tasks—with clear stopping conditions.

This efficiency is why I'm optimistic. Labs are using AI to build AI—Anthropic says Claude writes 80% of its merged code. That feedback loop means faster releases and better responses to user complaints. The result: a model that beats my LEGO benchmark from two months ago while using a fraction of the resources. That's the new standard.

## Counterpoints

- Your tasks are not my tasks; a model that's efficient for one job may be wasteful for another.
- Complex scenes like Lower Manhattan will naturally use more tokens than a simple LEGO build.
- Counting tokens too early misses the point—you need to measure the whole task, not just the output.
- Long autonomous runs can waste tokens without clear stopping conditions and a defined 'done' state.
- Writing quality is subjective; some users find AI edits polished but less clear, erasing intended nuance.
