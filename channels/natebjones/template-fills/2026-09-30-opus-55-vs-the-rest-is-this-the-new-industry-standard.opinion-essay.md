## Thesis

Opus 5.5 is the new industry standard because it delivers task-level efficiency that makes advanced AI dramatically cheaper and more controllable, and this efficiency is what users should measure.

## Argument

The real cost of AI isn't the token price—it's how many tokens the model burns through to finish a job. My LEGO logo build, a 514-piece model with full instructions and parts list, consumed 89 million tokens, which at API rates would be $44. But on my subscription, it used just 1% of my weekly usage—about 50 cents. That's the efficiency that matters. Anthropic's own numbers back this up: typical workloads on Opus 5.5 cost 40% less than on Opus 5, thanks to both lower prices and fewer tokens per task. GitHub, Lovable, and Spotify all report similar savings. This efficiency isn't just about cost; it's about room to iterate. Because Opus 5.5 gets things right faster, I can push a design further, ask for changes, and still have budget left. It's the difference between a tool that feels like a luxury and one that feels like a workhorse. And this efficiency extends to writing. Opus 5.5 finally restores the controllability that was lost in 4.7, 4.8, and Fable. I can ask for a specific tone or to remove ambiguity, and it complies without a fight. That's the line between sloppy AI and useful AI. The broader story is that labs are using AI to build AI—Anthropic says Claude writes 80% of its merged code—which shortens the feedback loop between user complaints and model fixes. That's why Opus 5.5 feels like a response to the community's frustrations, not just a spec bump. So stop counting tokens and start measuring tasks. ## Counterpoints

- Your tasks are not my tasks; a model that's efficient for one job may be inefficient for another, so you need to measure your own workloads.
- Some scenes, like a full city build, will naturally use more tokens than my LEGO example, so efficiency isn't universal.
- Opus 5.5's persistence can lead to long, token-wasting runs without clear stopping conditions, so you must define 'done' explicitly.
- Writing improvements are subjective; what feels controlled to me might feel bland to you, so you need to test for your own intent preservation.
