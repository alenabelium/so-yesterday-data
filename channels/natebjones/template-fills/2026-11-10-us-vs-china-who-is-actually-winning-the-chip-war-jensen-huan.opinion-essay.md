## Thesis

The Jensen Huang scandal is a compute constraint arriving at precisely the worst possible time, threatening the chip supply needed for AI-driven cyber defense and economic growth.

## Argument

The mechanism is geopolitical and temporal. China is holding up 600,000 H200s in Hong Kong while allegedly courting the man who sells the chips it can't get. This isn't about one CEO's affair; it's about who gets access to scarce advanced chips, which determines who builds AI faster. The H200 vs GB300 gap shows how chip generation translates into research leverage. Mega Speed's 17,000 GB300 imports in a month, rented to Alibaba, illustrate how overseas compute still benefits Chinese companies. Meanwhile, Washington opened a route to H200s while keeping Blackwell restricted. The timing is catastrophic because recursive self-improvement is starting. METR's assessment, based on 11 weeks inside Anthropic, shows models improving twice as fast as before, with AI-assisted research as the main driver. OpenAI and Google report similar acceleration. This means labs with the strongest models get a head start, widening the gap with everyone else. The Binance hack, where attackers used an internal AI tool to reach wallet engineers, shows how access to frontier intelligence is now a cyber defense necessity. The takeaway: compute availability for non-lab companies over the next 6-12 months is existential. A long investigation could stall chip sales, leaving defenders without the models they need during a wave of attacks. We need as many chips as we can get, and we need them now.

## Counterpoints

- Jensen's argument: selling abroad keeps developers on Nvidia's stack, preventing them from building on alternatives.
- Nvidia says Mega Speed's overseas cloud business is permitted under export rules and disputes diversion allegations, citing zero evidence of smuggling.
- China's reluctance to import H200s stems from concern about dependence on American technology, not just US restrictions.
- The board says it has confidence in Jensen and no reason to believe Nvidia secrets or national security were compromised.
- METR's findings are uncertain (1.3x to 3x speedup) and based on redacted evidence, with models too secret for direct testing.
