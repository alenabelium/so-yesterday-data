## Thesis

Cybersecurity's fundamental asymmetry—attacking is far easier than defending—means the internet will remain dangerously fragile until defenders adopt AI-driven simulation and recovery, not just patching.

## Argument

The asymmetry is structural, not incidental. Attackers using open-source frameworks like Artex can coordinate hundreds of AI agents to find and exploit a single vulnerability in minutes—Google documented one actor going from first compromise to deep access in 15 minutes. Defenders, meanwhile, are stuck patching decades of accumulated complexity. Microsoft's own fix for a dormant bug sent traffic to healthy servers, crashing them too, because the system's interdependencies are too tangled to predict. ConnectWise's response to an Artex attack shows the same pattern: revoking seven credentials took minutes, but restoring service took weeks of per-customer reauthorization. The patch itself becomes a liability. The Java advisory from CISA illustrates the inventory problem: finding every instance of Java in a sprawling enterprise is a project in itself. This is why Bank of America hires armies of contractors just to identify vulnerabilities. The hope lies in using AI for defense—simulating recovery scenarios, building defensive profiles, and automating detection—but that's a harder problem than attack, and it will lag by months. Until then, we're in a bumpy period where the internet is more vulnerable than ever, and basic preparation—password managers, family plans—is essential.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue these incidents are one coordinated attack; the evidence is more nuanced, showing separate but interconnected failures.
- Skeptics say AI is already used for defense; the host counters that defense is inherently more complex than attack, not just slower.
- Critics might claim patching is straightforward; the host points to Microsoft's fix that crashed healthy servers and ConnectWise's weeks-long recovery.
- Optimists see browser security and CDN protections as sufficient; the host notes these are valuable but not comprehensive.
- Some believe the internet's fragility is overstated; the host cites the FAA, NSA, and bank outages as proof of systemic risk.
