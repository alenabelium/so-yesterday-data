## TL;DR

AI makes it cheap to see inside a company, but that visibility is a trap. Leaders must choose between investing in AI-powered dashboards and metrics or in AI-powered execution through small, empowered tiger teams. The real value comes from the messy, illegible work of trusted teams, not from Potemkin villages of fake legibility.

## The argument

### Define Legible vs. Illegible Work
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: Legible work is what shows up in Jira, OKRs, and roadmaps—planned, trackable, explainable. Illegible work is the harsh reality: favors, back channels, shared intuition, quick fixes, and tiger teams. It's the illegible work that keeps companies going, especially in emergencies.
- **Role**: definition

### AI Amplifies Both Real and Fake Legibility
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:08']
- **Claim**: AI drops the cost of making work visible to near zero, but it also makes fake legibility—the belief you can see everything—even cheaper. This is what vendors sell as a single pane of glass. The old cost of legibility constrained it; AI removes that constraint, leading to Potemkin villages of dashboards and metrics.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Danger of Overconfidence in the Wrong Map
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:34']
- **Claim**: AI-generated dashboards can look empirical but aren't debugged; risk scores appear but no one knows what they mean; productivity metrics correlate to ticket-turning tricks, not outcomes. The real danger is not blindness but overconfidence in an AI-generated map that obscures where value is created.
- **Role**: counter

### Tiger Teams Are the Real Production Engine
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:19']
- **Claim**: Real progress comes from small, empowered teams with shared context, taste, and trust. AI gives them leverage—faster coding, debugging, synthesis—so a five-person pod can produce what 20-30 people did before. Leaders must not mistake AI-driven omniscience for control or apply AI to the wrong part of the org.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites Amazon firing middle managers whose primary job was to curate information, showing AI's impact on legibility. He acknowledges an upside: less ceremony, more clarity, less pressure to force everything into rigid processes. But he warns that AI makes it trivial to generate fake legibility—vibecoded dashboards, meaningless risk scores, and productivity metrics that don't reflect real outcomes. He notes that AI-powered teams make a bigger mess than before, so teams will hide their work if messiness isn't encouraged. The speaker shares a personal story of engineers who did valuable work on weekends, outside OKRs, and emphasizes that good leaders preserve messy spaces and use AI to translate work, not dictate it.
