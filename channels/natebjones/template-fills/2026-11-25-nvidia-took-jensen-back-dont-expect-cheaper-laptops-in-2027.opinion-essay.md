## Thesis

AI's infrastructure demand will keep laptop and phone prices high through 2027-2028, forcing users to adapt to tighter AI limits and prioritize efficiency skills.

## Argument

The mechanism is memory. AI's shift to agent workloads has made general-purpose memory like DDR5 a scarce resource, competing directly with consumer electronics. Memory prices have surged—a 16-gigabit DDR5 chip went from $8.70 to $48 in 11 months—and memory now accounts for 54% of a budget smartphone's component cost. This isn't a temporary blip; Micron expects the shortage to worsen through 2027-2028. Meanwhile, the financing picture has darkened. Big tech's capex has outpaced cash flow, leading to over $680 billion in debt for advanced computing, with off-balance-sheet liabilities exceeding $3 trillion. This debt burden will make AI tools more expensive and usage more restricted, as companies seek to justify every dollar. The winners will be those who can extract maximum value per gigabyte, per query, and per dollar—efficiency becomes the new competitive advantage.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue the tech sell-off signals a bubble burst, but demand data (Open Router token volumes up 25x, Anthropic revenue up 12x) shows real, paid demand.
- Skeptics question the sustainability of AI infrastructure debt, but the scale of investment (3% of GDP) exceeds historical booms, and lenders are becoming selective.
- Optimists hope for a quick supply response, but memory fabs take years to build and are already at capacity, so prices will stay high.
