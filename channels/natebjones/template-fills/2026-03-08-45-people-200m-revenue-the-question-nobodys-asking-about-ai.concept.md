## TL;DR

The explosion of meetings since 2020 isn't a meetings problem—it's a team-size problem. AI has raised per-person output by an order of magnitude, making coordination overhead catastrophically expensive. The structural unit of the AI era is the five-person strike team, optimized for correctness, not volume.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate: The Number Five
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:16']
- **Claim**: The human brain caps deep coordination at about five people, as shown by Dunbar's research, military effectiveness data, and Brooks's Law. At five people, there are 10 communication pathways; at 20, there are 190, overwhelming shared context.
- **Role**: definition

### AI Raises the Penalty for Exceeding Five
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:24']
- **Claim**: AI-native companies generate $2-3 million per employee, 5-10x the traditional SaaS benchmark. When each person produces that much, the coordination cost of adding a sixth person becomes a catastrophe, not a minor tax.
- **Role**: evidence

### Volume Is Free; Correctness Is Scarce
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:25']
- **Claim**: AI makes volume cheap, so optimizing for volume is optimizing for the wrong thing. A Harvard study found AI teams were 3x more likely to produce top-10% quality ideas. Five-person teams optimize for correctness; 20-person teams optimize for volume, which leads to pseudo-work.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: The Solo Scout Model Has Limits
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:32']
- **Claim**: Scouts—one person with full AI toolkits—excel at exploration, as Peter Steinberger showed with OpenClaw. But solo models fail when correctness requires multiple perspectives or sustained production. The five-person strike team is the minimal surface area for a complete decision.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: The Force Multiplier, Not Cost Reduction
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:13:07']
- **Claim**: AI is a force multiplier, not a cost-reduction tool. A 500-person company now has the capacity of 2,500-5,000 people. The right response is not to shrink but to restructure into strike teams and pursue a mission 5-10x larger. This requires executive mandate and a focus on taste and judgment.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Evidence: AI-native companies like Lovable, Midjourney, and 11 Labs show revenue per employee 5-10x the SaaS benchmark. A 2025 Harvard Business School field experiment with 776 P&G professionals found AI teams were 3x more likely to produce top-10% quality ideas and broke functional silos. Peter Steinberger built OpenClaw in 60 days with 4-10 coding agents, demonstrating the scout model. Caveats: The speaker notes that most organizations still need human judgment on the path to production, and that the solo scout model has limits—OpenClaw shipped with holes, and Steinberger joined OpenAI to scale his vision. The speaker also acknowledges that some people will not want to transition to AI, and that the honest answer for some companies will be fewer people if they lack ambition.
