## Thesis

Mark Zuckerberg's pivot from the metaverse to Muse proves that AI rewards bold risk-taking, and you don't need billions to emulate his success.

## Argument

The story of Muse is a story of subjectivity, not inevitability. Zuckerberg lost $19.2 billion in 2025 on the metaverse, yet he didn't retreat into caution. He tried again, and 10 million people set up Muse in six weeks. The key isn't his money—it's three emulatable behaviors.

First, he hired a small, focused team. Alexander Wang led the effort with fewer than 200 people, deliberately kept small to avoid consensus-driven compromises. This allowed sharp, decisive product choices, unlike the bureaucratic tradeoffs that plague larger teams. You can do this too: find your Alexander Wang, your Nat Friedman, and let them act.

Second, obsessive attention to detail. The team tested over a hundred specific behaviors before launch, from the avatar's facial expressions to the tiny laptop Marvin types on. This polish signals that everything is under control, which matters for a personal assistant you'll live with daily. In the age of MVPs, this craftsmanship is a differentiator.

Third, the courage to try again after failure. Zuckerberg didn't let embarrassment or sunk costs trap him. He admitted defeat, changed his approach, and made another bet. For the rest of us, AI lowers the technical and financial barriers to trying, but the emotional barrier—fear of public embarrassment—remains. That's the real obstacle.

So, stop being timid. Build a prototype, show it to someone, and iterate. You don't need billions—you need the willingness to make a bold bet and the discipline to polish it.

## Counterpoints

- Meta has advantages most of us lack: distribution, compute, talent. But that's not an excuse to see Muse as inevitable fate.
- Muse isn't the smartest agent on the market, but intelligence isn't the point—polish and user experience are.
- Muse is expensive and needs to scale to billions, but that's a future problem, not a reason to dismiss the lesson.
- Some might say changing course after the metaverse is an admission of failure, but Zuckerberg proved it's a strength.
