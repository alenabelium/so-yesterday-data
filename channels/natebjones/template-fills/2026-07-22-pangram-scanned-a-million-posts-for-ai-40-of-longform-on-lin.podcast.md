## Key arguments

### The 40% AI Slop Crisis
- **Timestamp**: [01:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m_ZyTNmCDeY&t=98)
- **Summary**: Pangram's data reveals that 40% of long-form LinkedIn posts are AI-generated. This isn't just about bad content; it's a structural shift where the share of human-written text is dropping dramatically, forcing platforms and users to navigate a new reality where authenticity is harder to verify.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0]

### Intent Over Tooling
- **Timestamp**: [04:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m_ZyTNmCDeY&t=289)
- **Summary**: The dividing line is intent, not the tool. Using AI as a paintbrush with human belief is valid; using it to game systems cynically is a denial-of-service attack on the public square. Substack integrates Pangram for transparency, not censorship, allowing readers to see if text went through an LLM.

### High-Variance Ideas as Alpha
- **Timestamp**: [21:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m_ZyTNmCDeY&t=1304)
- **Summary**: AI favors a central distribution of ideas (e.g., always mentioning ethics). True value lies at the edges of the distribution—high-variance, original perspectives. We need tools to map human ideation diversity against LLM averaging to protect the 'alpha' of new, non-consensus thinking.

### Human Attention as the Scarce Resource
- **Timestamp**: [43:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m_ZyTNmCDeY&t=2638)
- **Summary**: As AI generates 99% of content, human attention becomes the only non-inflationary, scarce resource. The goal is to ensure that when humans pay attention, it is worth it. This requires new cultural norms around video and interaction to prevent betrayal of expectation, similar to catfishing.
