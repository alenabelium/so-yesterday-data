## TL;DR

The fundamental unit of computing is shifting from the instruction to the token—from deterministic human-written code to purchased intelligence. This shift redefines software engineering, creating three distinct developer tracks and reshaping organizational structures around token budgets. Understanding this paradigm is crucial for anyone building software or planning a career in tech.

## The argument

### The Unit of Work Shifts from Instruction to Token
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: For 60 years, software's unit of work was the deterministic instruction—a human writes code, a machine executes it. Now, the unit is the token: a unit of purchased intelligence. You describe what you want, feed context, and buy enough intelligence to get a result. The human's job shifts from writing logic to specifying outcomes and managing the intelligence budget.
- **Role**: definition

### Token Economics Reshape Organizations
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:08:59']
- **Claim**: In the old paradigm, the scarce resource was developer time, so management focused on headcount. In the token economy, the bottleneck is the ability to convert tokens into economic value—knowing how to aim tokens, structure context, route tasks, and measure outcomes. This creates a new organizational capability: token management, which is measurable and becoming a core competency.
- **Role**: definition

### Three Developer Tracks Emerge
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:13:00']
- **Claim**: The developer role differentiates into three tracks: the orchestrator (specifies outcomes, manages intelligence), the systems builder (builds agent frameworks and evaluation pipelines), and the domain translator (non-developers with deep domain expertise who can now build tools). The middle of the old distribution—competent application coders—is most exposed.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Token Economics Can Backfire
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:15']
- **Claim**: Cursor, a billion-dollar AI coding editor, faced a structural trap: it sent all its revenue to Anthropic in API costs. When Anthropic raised prices, Cursor's costs exploded, forcing it to gut its unlimited plan and introduce a $200/month tier, sparking user revolt. The lesson: token economics is a core business competency; without it, you're one supplier pricing change away from crisis.
- **Role**: counter

- **Claim**: The market splits along an axis: enterprises compete on token volume, building horizontal platforms with capital moats; specialists win on specificity, niche markets, and customer relationships. ## Evidence and caveats

StrongDM's CTO disclosed a three-person team targeting $1,000/day in token spend with no handwritten code. Cursor's AWS costs spiked from $6M to $12M between May and June 2025, coinciding with Anthropic's priority service tiers. Anthropic spent $2.66B on AWS against $2.55B revenue, and Perplexity spent 164% of its 2024 revenue on AI infrastructure. Per-token costs are falling 10-200x per year; GPT-4-equivalent performance dropped from $20 to $0.40 per million tokens. Jevons paradox explains why cheaper tokens lead to more consumption. Average enterprise LLM spend hit $7M in 2025, up from $4.5M two years prior. Caveats: organizational change is slow and path-dependent; enterprises that fire 90% of engineers may not immediately become productive. CLA's initial AI rollout was disastrous, but they still see revenue per employee scaling into seven figures.
