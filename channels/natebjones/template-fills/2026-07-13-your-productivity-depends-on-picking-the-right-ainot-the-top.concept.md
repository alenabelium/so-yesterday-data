## TL;DR

Productivity hinges on matching AI models to individual cognitive styles rather than chasing top benchmark scores. Models exhibit distinct 'family resemblances'—OpenAI favors precise, task-oriented execution while Anthropic excels in ambiguous, high-level conceptual thinking. Selecting the right tool accelerates your personal workflow loop.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: Productivity depends on picking the model that works for you, not the one with the highest benchmark score. The goal is to provide tools for users to select models aligned with their specific work patterns.
- **Role**: definition

### Contrast Engineering vs. Conceptual Styles
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:48']
- **Claim**: OpenAI models like 5.6 Soul excel at precise, task-oriented knowledge work and long-running agentic coding through reinforcement learning. In contrast, Anthropic's Fable 5 leverages pre-training for general-purpose, ambiguous conceptual thinking and 'big model smell'.
- **Role**: evidence

### Adopt the Family Resemblance Framework
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:37']
- **Claim**: Models should be treated as a family with distinct personalities rather than benchmarkable commodities. The 5.x family prefers explicit instructions and execution, while the Mythos lineage thrives on ambiguity and philosophical depth.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Align with Personal Cognitive Loops
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:09']
- **Claim**: Users should analyze their own best work processes first. If you prefer lengthy, technical prompts, 5.6 fits. If you wrestle with high-level ambiguity, Fable is better. The right model accelerates your specific loop.
- **Role**: evidence

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker uses his private 'Dingo' benchmark to show Soul scoring 93 on knowledge work, yet still preferring it for specific tasks. He notes that while OpenAI focuses on engineering ergonomics (CodeX), Anthropic connects technology with philosophy (JSpace study). Caveat: He acknowledges ChatGPT Work is a 'first stab' at non-engineer knowledge work but feels it lacks the sophistication of coding tools, often dumbing things down based on an engineer's perception of non-technical users. He also mentions Ringer as a tool to orchestrate between these families.
