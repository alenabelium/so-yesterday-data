## Thesis

OpenAI's 700 math papers prove narrow superintelligence in formal domains, but that doesn't mean your job is next because engineering and business still require human judgment, context, and the ability to ask the right questions.

## Argument

The mechanism is the gap between verifiable domains and real-world work. Math offers clear success criteria and verifiable rewards, which is why OpenAI's unreleased model can solve open problems in about three hours each. But engineering, despite being testable and heavily invested in, hasn't seen a superhuman engineer emerge. Instead, engineers hedge against AI weaknesses with zero-trust environments, reviews, and bug-finding—human contributions that create the main value. A simple example: AI generates a document search feature that passes tests, but an engineer must ask what happens when permissions are revoked, tracing the system's dependencies and writing the right test. That ability to ask the right question is part of the intelligence required. In business, the gap widens further: finding conflicting documents for a client reveals a deeper problem—who owns the information, which version is current—that no initial prompt could have anticipated. The person who discovers this makes an extremely valuable contribution. So, AI's usefulness lies in generation, not in systems-level design or business intuition. The lesson is to focus on your experience, passion, and drive, using AI as a tool, and to recognize that we have unexpectedly large freedom in choosing where to apply it. This fragmented superintelligence should give us more peace of mind than feared.

## Counterpoints

- Labs claim it's just a matter of time before superintelligence transfers to engineering, but the host asks: why hasn't it happened yet?
- Some might say code is verifiable, so superintelligence should appear there first, but the host points out that engineers still need to hedge against AI weaknesses.
- The host acknowledges AI is extremely useful in engineering, but clarifies its value is in generating code, not in designing maintainable systems.
- The host notes that AI is not getting better at questioning your assumptions, despite expectations, because it's trained to be useful.
- The host sympathizes with mathematicians whose life's work was solved, but argues this doesn't imply transfer to other domains.
