## Thesis

The upcoming IPOs of SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic will exploit a structural flaw in index funds to force massive buying of artificially scarce shares, transferring retirement savings to insiders.

## Argument

The mechanism is a structural flaw in index funds, not a conspiracy. When a company goes public, it typically sells 15-25% of its shares, but SpaceX plans to sell only 3.3%, just enough to raise $50-75 billion while keeping the rest of the company private. This tiny float creates a scarcity premium, as seen when a fund with tiny stakes in SpaceX and Anthropic traded at 16 times its net asset value. The problem escalates because NASDAQ's new rules, effective May 1st, allow companies like SpaceX to be added to the NASDAQ 100 after just 15 trading days, weighted by their total valuation, not just the float. This forces every index fund tracking the NASDAQ 100 to buy SpaceX stock immediately, competing for a pool of shares that represents only 3% of the company. Bloomberg Intelligence estimates that if all three companies sell 5% of their shares and get fast-tracked, funds would need to buy roughly $39 billion worth of stock, and the total wave of buying could swallow more than half of all available shares in a few days. This is your 401k buying at whatever price the market offers during a period of historically limited supply. The insiders, who got in at valuations like $46 billion for SpaceX, will sell into this inflated market once lock-up periods expire, making a 38x return, while index funds are forced to keep buying. The public market is becoming the lender of last resort, as OpenAI's $500 billion Stargate project couldn't get traditional lenders, and now it's turning to public equity markets.
