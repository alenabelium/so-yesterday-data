## TL;DR

As AI-generated media becomes indistinguishable from human output in low-attention environments, the primary risk shifts from technical perfection to structural trust. The Creator Trust Stack framework decouples the binary 'AI vs. Human' question into five distinct layers of verification. This distinction is critical because audiences can no longer rely on visual or auditory cues to determine authenticity.

## The argument

### Define the Core Threat
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The danger is not perfect AI, but 'good enough' AI in low-attention environments where audiences cannot distinguish synthetic media from human performance. The threshold for deception is whether content creates enough ambiguity to break the audience's sense of relationship to the creator.
- **Role**: definition

### Decouple AI Questions
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:26']
- **Claim**: The binary question 'Was this made with AI?' is a crude primitive that mashes together five distinct inquiries: synthetic voice, synthetic face, synthetic script, synthetic idea, and human approval. These must be separated to understand where human judgment actually operates.
- **Role**: definition

### Map the Trust Stack
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:33']
- **Claim**: The Creator Trust Stack provides five discrete layers: Disclosure (what is synthetic), Provenance (source consent), Control (approval rights), Judgment (who made the argument), and Accountability (who owns the result). This structure replaces the blunt AI/no-AI binary.
- **Role**: definition

### Identify the Scarce Asset
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:16']
- **Claim**: Content and polish are no longer scarce; judgment and accountability are. The future belongs to those who use AI for leverage while preserving legible human responsibility. Trust is the only defensible moat against synthetic ambiguity.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker demonstrates voice cloning at 00:01:34, noting it is 'impressive and frankly a little creepy' but distinct from full human presence cloning. He argues that full cloning is harder because 'micro expressions are missing' and 'hands don't have weight' (00:02:21). He notes that 'human weirdness is going to start looking like machine weirdness' (00:06:20), citing examples like mispronunciations or wearing a beanie to avoid hair styling. He hedges that 'we are going to use these tools' (00:05:24) and that 'being human is no longer enough' (00:09:18); one must be 'legibly human' or 'legibly synthetic.'
