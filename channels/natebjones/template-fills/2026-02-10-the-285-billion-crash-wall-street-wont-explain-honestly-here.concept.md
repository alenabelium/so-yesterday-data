## TL;DR

A 200-line open-source prompt from Anthropic triggered a $285 billion sell-off in enterprise SaaS stocks, but the crash wasn't about the prompt—it exposed the per-seat licensing model's structural fragility. The real signal is KPMG using AI as leverage to cut audit fees, showing how AI changes fee negotiations across professional services. Incumbents with data and accountability edges can survive, but only by pivoting to agentic-first architectures.

## The argument

### The Markdown File Revealed the Cracks
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: Anthropic's open-source Claude Co-work plugin for legal contract review—roughly 200 lines of structured markdown—triggered a $285 billion market crash. But the file itself wasn't the cause; it made visible that the per-seat SaaS licensing model was already cracking. The market hadn't priced in the structural problem.
- **Role**: definition

### Per-Seat Pricing Breaks When Agents Don't Log In
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:03']
- **Claim**: Enterprise software from Thomson Reuters to Salesforce runs on per-seat licensing: every human user pays a fee. That model works when humans are the bottleneck, but breaks when AI agents do the work without logging in. The crash repriced this risk, compressing software P/E ratios to levels not seen since the dot-com bust.
- **Role**: evidence

### Jensen Huang's Counterargument Misses the Point
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:35']
- **Claim**: Nvidia's CEO argued AI doesn't replace software—it runs on software, so AI should increase software demand. But the market isn't attacking the product; it's attacking the pricing model. Confusing the two is how incumbents lose transitions they should have survived, like print media did with the internet.
- **Role**: counter

### The KPMG Precedent: AI as Negotiating Leverage
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:08:10']
- **Claim**: KPMG used AI's existence to force a 14% fee cut from its auditor Grant Thornton. This is an operating event, not a market event: a real company using AI as leverage in a real negotiation. The playbook—'we both know AI changes the economics'—will cascade through legal, consulting, and every professional services fee.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Survival Path: Agentic-First, Not Bolt-On AI
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:40']
- **Claim**: Incumbents have real edges: proprietary data and accountability (the 'ringable neck'). But they must pivot from UI-first to agentic-first architecture, charging for data and accountability rather than per-seat access. Bolting AI onto existing UIs is a death sentence; the window is closing fast.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The crash: Thomson Reuters down 16%, RELX down 14%, LegalZoom down 20%, and PE firms like Ares, KKR, TPG down ~10%. The KPMG-Grant Thornton negotiation: fees dropped from $416k to $357k (14% cut). Jensen Huang's counterargument at Cisco AI Summit: 'AI doesn't replace software; AI runs on software.' Bank of America's Vivek Arya called the sell-off 'internally inconsistent'—two contradictory theses (AI capex overbuilt vs. AI adoption destroying software) can't both be true, yet the market rotates between them. Caveats: The speaker admits the sell-off may be an overreaction, and the articulation problem—AI's difficulty understanding vague human needs—remains a bottleneck. The data and accountability edges are real, but the transition is risky and time-sensitive.
