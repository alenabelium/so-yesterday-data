## TL;DR

Prompt engineering has become table stakes, necessitating a shift to a 'questioning' methodology for advanced AI agents. By treating AI as a senior partner rather than a junior assistant, users can leverage complex agentic workflows more effectively. This approach involves framing interactions through strategic questions that define intent, explore boundaries, and synthesize diverse data inputs. Adopting this mental model allows for deeper collaboration and higher-leverage outcomes in heavy knowledge work.

## The argument

### Define the Senior Partner Mental Model
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:41']
- **Claim**: AI is no longer a junior partner requiring specific task definitions; it is a senior partner capable of deep partnership thinking. This shift requires moving from defining tasks to forming questions that enable the agent to do its best work.
- **Role**: definition

### Principle 1: Flashlight Intent
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:08:22']
- **Claim**: Questions must convey a 'center of the flashlight' intent—a clear thesis or perspective—while also defining hard edges or boundaries. This directs the AI's focus without being overly open-ended or closed-off, allowing for targeted exploration.
- **Role**: evidence

### Principle 2: Open-Ended Synthesis
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:56']
- **Claim**: Instead of relying solely on rigid evals, ask questions that invite the AI to explore what 'good' looks like. This forces the AI to synthesize across complex, ambiguous outcomes rather than just executing a predefined standard.
- **Role**: evidence

### Principle 3: Data and Opinion Wrestling
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:16:17']
- **Claim**: Effective questioning requires invoking both hard data inputs and implicit opinions/theses. By providing a folder of diverse files and a specific thesis, you force the AI to engage with the entire breadth of context, not just a subset.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesis: The Question Method
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:49']
- **Claim**: The core of this shift is expressing intent through a sharp series of questions that help the AI explore the problem space. This moves beyond prompt engineering's focus on words to a focus on intent, enabling meaningful creative work.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The host provides a marketing attribution example where a manager asks an AI to turn CSVs into a deck and doc, guiding the thinking with questions. A PR FAQ example illustrates asking the AI to synthesize customer experience across software and hardware. A caveat is that this method requires cutting-edge models (like 4.7 or 5.5) and heavy knowledge work; it may not work for free accounts, older models, or defined agentic pipelines (like customer service ticket processing) which are predictable workflows rather than deep partnership thinking.
