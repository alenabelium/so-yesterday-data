## TL;DR

Prompt engineering has become table stakes, rendering traditional task-based prompting obsolete for advanced agents. The speaker introduces the 'AI Question Method,' a framework for treating AI as a senior partner rather than a junior assistant. This shift enables higher-leverage outcomes in heavy knowledge work by leveraging complex agentic workflows through strategic inquiry.

## The argument

### The Senior Partner Mental Model Shift
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:41']
- **Claim**: AI capabilities have accelerated with models like 4.7 and 5.5, making it 100x more powerful than agents from six months ago. Users must shift from treating AI as a junior partner requiring specific task definitions to treating it as a senior partner capable of deep partnership thinking and high-leverage work.
- **Role**: definition

### Principle 1: Flashlight Intent and Boundaries
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:23']
- **Claim**: Effective questioning requires conveying a 'center of the flashlight' intent—a clear thesis or perspective—along with hard edges or boundaries. This guides the AI’s exploration without being overly open-ended or closed, ensuring the agent targets the specific problem space while knowing what to exclude.
- **Role**: evidence

### Principle 2: Inviting Synthesis Over Eval
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:13:28']
- **Claim**: Instead of relying on rigid evaluations, users should ask open-ended questions that force the AI to contend with complex outcomes and synthesize across multiple directions. This allows the agent to explore what 'good' looks like in nuanced scenarios, such as drafting a PR FAQ, rather than just checking boxes.
- **Role**: evidence

### Principle 3: Wrestling with Data and Implicit Inputs
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:31']
- **Claim**: Questions must invoke both formal data artifacts and the user’s implicit opinions or theses. By framing questions that challenge the AI to examine all provided files through the lens of the user’s perspective, users ensure the agent engages with the full breadth of context rather than mirroring a single narrow angle.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesis: Intent Over Instruction
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:49']
- **Claim**: The core of this shift is that intent matters more than instruction. By using a series of sharp questions, users enable the AI to perform meaningful creative work and push back as a senior partner would, moving beyond the flat, task-listing prompts of the past.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker provides concrete examples, such as using a marketing attribution problem to illustrate 'flashlight intent' and a fictional Prime Video 3D soccer launch to demonstrate synthesis. He notes that this method requires cutting-edge models (like 5.5) and heavy knowledge work contexts; it is not suitable for free accounts, token-limited users, or older models. He also distinguishes this 'heavy knowledge work' from 'agentic pipelines' used for predictable tasks like customer service ticket handling.

Caveats: The speaker acknowledges that not everyone has had a 'good manager' to learn from, and that this approach requires a significant mental model shift. He also notes that while prompt engineering is 'dead' as a differentiator, it remains 'table stakes'—you still need to be good at it, but it no longer earns credit.
