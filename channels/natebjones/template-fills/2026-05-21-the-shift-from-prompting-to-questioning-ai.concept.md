## TL;DR

Prompt engineering has become table stakes, rendering it insufficient for advanced AI agents. To leverage tools like Claude Code and CodeEx for heavy knowledge work, users must adopt a 'questioning' methodology. This approach treats the AI as a senior partner, using strategic questions to define intent, explore boundaries, and synthesize complex data inputs.

## The argument

### Define the Mental Model Shift
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:41']
- **Claim**: The core shift is treating AI as a senior partner rather than a junior assistant. Junior prompts require specific task definitions; senior partner interactions require strategic questioning that invites deep partnership thinking and high-leverage outcomes in heavy knowledge work.
- **Role**: definition

### Principle 1: Flashlight Intent
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:23']
- **Claim**: Questions must convey a 'center of the flashlight' intent—a clear thesis or perspective—while also defining hard edges or boundaries. This prevents overly open-ended or closed-ended responses, giving the AI a target to explore within defined constraints.
- **Role**: evidence

### Principle 2: Synthesize Outcomes
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:06']
- **Claim**: Instead of rigid evals, ask questions that invite the AI to explore what 'good' looks like for an outcome. This forces the AI to synthesize across multiple complex directions and contend with the desired result, rather than just executing a static instruction.
- **Role**: evidence

### Principle 3: Engage Full Data Breadth
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:21:17']
- **Claim**: Questions must explicitly invoke all data sources and file inputs, ensuring the AI engages with the entire context window. By framing a thesis that spans multiple artifacts, you prevent the AI from latching onto a single file and force a comprehensive analysis.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesize the New Standard
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:49']
- **Claim**: The 'question method' replaces prompt engineering as the primary interface for advanced agents. Intent is now best expressed through sharp, sequential questions that enable the AI to act as a collaborative partner, exploring problem spaces and pushing back with its own thesis.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites updates in AI agents (specifically '4.7 and 5.5') as the catalyst for this shift, noting that agents are now '100 times more powerful' than those from six months ago. He distinguishes 'heavy knowledge work' (deep partnership) from 'agentic pipelines' (predictable workflows like customer service tickets). Caveats include: this method requires cutting-edge models and sufficient context windows; it may not work on free accounts or older models. The speaker also notes that while prompt engineering is 'table stakes,' it no longer earns credit for advanced work. He references his Substack for 'quick start guides' and 'question prompter' tools to help users practice this new muscle.
