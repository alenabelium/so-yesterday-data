## TL;DR

AI is unblocking extraordinary people who were never limited by ability but by overhead. The key skills are conviction, speed of control, and judgment density—not tool fluency. Leaders must remove coordination overhead and foster these skills or risk losing their best talent to solo founding.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate: Overhead, Not Ability
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:17:11']
- **Claim**: Extraordinary people are blocked by overhead, not ability. AI removes coordination burdens, acting as a proxy for team perspectives, enabling individuals to match two-person team performance.
- **Role**: definition

### Evidence: Solo Founders and the P&G Experiment
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:09']
- **Claim**: Solo founders like Ben Sira ($2.5M ARR) and Sarah Gilliam (potential one-person unicorn) demonstrate AI's power. A Harvard experiment at P&G showed individuals with AI were 3x more likely to produce top-10% ideas, breaking down silos.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: The 'Greenfield' Objection
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:09']
- **Claim**: Skeptics argue solo founders lack enterprise complexity. However, the P&G experiment shows AI's coordination value applies inside large companies, making the objection invalid.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: The Skills That Matter
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:59']
- **Claim**: The real differentiators are conviction (acting before validation), speed of control (high-quality sequential decisions), and judgment density (pattern recognition). These are learnable skills, not innate traits.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Ben Sira hit $2.5M ARR solo with Pulsia, going from $1M in four days. Mauromo built Base44 to 300k users and $3.5M ARR. Sarah Gilliam, profiled by the Economist, is a potential one-person unicorn. Carta data shows solo founders now a third of new US ventures. Caveats: Solo founding carries risk and heartbreak; not everyone should do it. Leaders should upskill existing talent, as they know the domain. The skills are learnable through practice, not reading.
