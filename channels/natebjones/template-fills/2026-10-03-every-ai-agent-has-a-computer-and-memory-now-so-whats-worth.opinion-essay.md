## Thesis

The agent war is won by utility, not form factor: everyone can copy a computer and memory, but the quality of work an agent does for you is what justifies a $100/month subscription.

## Argument

The mechanism is utility, not form factor. Every agent now ships with a computer, memory, and background work—OpenAI's Dots, Meta's Muse, and even Grok's bot look the same. But the usefulness diverges because the underlying model and task-specific competencies differ. OpenAI's bet is that context is the moat: with 1.2 billion users, they have the most context, and Dots is the first step in making that context intelligent. The real value is in proactive, context-aware work—like Dots fixing a recurring weekend meeting or adjusting launch materials when a feature slips. This is where the flywheel spins: a cheaper model like GPT-6.1 Soul lets you feed more work into the system, and Spaces and Pages turn solo ChatGPT usage into a collaborative workspace, directly challenging Microsoft Office. The strategic implication is that small teams become more ambitious: with agents handling the grunt work, the bottleneck shifts from engineering capacity to taste and ambition. The future is multi-agent, and the skill is knowing which agent to delegate what. So, when deciding on a $100 plan, don't pay for the form factor—pay for the utility, and make sure you have a plan to get that $100 back.

## Counterpoints

- If everyone can copy a computer and memory, why pay? Because the quality of work differs based on the model and configuration.
- Spaces assumes AI-native organizations, which are rare outside Silicon Valley; adoption may require significant change management.
- Microsoft has a natural advantage with Copilot due to trust, even if not cutting-edge; the 'no one got fired for choosing Microsoft' argument applies.
- The $200 plan is getting a lower limit for the same price, which may upset users despite the efficiency of GPT-6.1 Soul.
- Dots' proactive suggestions currently over-index on recent events, so it needs to learn what's truly important in your context.
