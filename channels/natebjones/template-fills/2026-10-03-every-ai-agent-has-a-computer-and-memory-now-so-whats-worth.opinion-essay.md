## Thesis

The agent war is won by utility, not form factor: while every AI agent now has a computer, memory, and background work, the real differentiator is the quality of work, which varies by model and task-specific competencies.

## Argument

The mechanism is utility. Form factors are converging—OpenAI's Dots, Meta's Muse, and Anthropic's Instinct all offer computers, memory, and background work—but the usefulness diverges because the underlying models and configurations differ. Muse excels at practical, low-intelligence tasks like email and calls; Instinct focuses on travel and restaurant transactions; Dots is built for serious work, leveraging the context you've accumulated in ChatGPT. This is why Dots is the first step in a long game, and why OpenAI's bet on making context intelligent is huge. The value emerges when an always-on agent can read a product launch delay, understand the cascading changes to promises and presentations, and proactively prepare adjustments. This is where Dots shines, and it's why OpenAI paired it with their most powerful model, Astra. The broader trend is outcome-focused AI: everyone—from Dreamforce to Dev Day to Muse—realizes we need results, not just activity. For individuals, the question is whether the $100/month Pro plan pays for itself. My advice: don't upgrade just for Dots, but if you use ChatGPT frequently, it's a worthwhile investment in your future. The real opportunity is for small teams to become more ambitious, using Dots, Spaces, and Pages to tackle projects that previously required huge engineering investment. The future belongs to those who master the meta-skill of delegating tasks to AI and finding ambitions that match our intelligence.

## Counterpoints

- Why pay if anyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work? The answer is the quality of work, which differs by model and configuration.
- Spaces and Pages assume AI-native, AI-literate organizations, which are rare outside Silicon Valley; Microsoft has a natural advantage with trust and IT familiarity.
- The $200 plan is getting a lower limit for the same price, which is unpopular; but the efficiency of GPT-6.1 Soul may compensate.
- Dots doesn't instantly know everything from ChatGPT; it only gets memories you choose to share, which is a privacy choice but limits its context.
- The $500 plan is for advanced users; if you're not confident in managing AI, you won't benefit from the speed and may get wrong answers faster.
