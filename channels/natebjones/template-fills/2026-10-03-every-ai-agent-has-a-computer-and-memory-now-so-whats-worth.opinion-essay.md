## Thesis

The agent war is won by utility, not form factor, so $100/month is worth it only if you can make the model pay for itself.

## Argument

The mechanism is economic. OpenAI's Dots gives you a computer, memory, and background work, but Meta's Muse and Anthropic's Claude offer the same form factor for less or free. The real differentiator is the quality of work: Dots runs on Astra, the most powerful model, and uses your accumulated ChatGPT context to fix real problems like zombie meetings and scheduling conflicts. That's utility you can't copy.

This is why I keep coming back to the distinction between form factor and utility. All agents look the same now—they have a computer, memory, and background work—but the underlying model and task-specific competencies differ. Muse is great for practical chores like making calls, Instinct is focused on travel and restaurants, and Dots is built for serious work. You need to track your real habits and understand which agent excels at which task.

For OpenAI, the strategic bet is on context. With 1.2 billion users, they have a massive advantage in making your AI context intelligent. Spaces and Pages are the first step toward a Microsoft Office competitor, letting agents and people collaborate in real time. But this assumes an AI-native organization, which is rare outside Silicon Valley. Microsoft has a natural advantage with trust, but the 'no one got fired for choosing Microsoft' argument won't hold here.

On pricing, the Soul 6.1 model is the unsung hero. It's approaching Astra's quality at a fifth of the cost, so you can do complex work without burning your weekly limit. For current $200 subscribers, there's a catch: limits drop after October 29th. My advice: don't
