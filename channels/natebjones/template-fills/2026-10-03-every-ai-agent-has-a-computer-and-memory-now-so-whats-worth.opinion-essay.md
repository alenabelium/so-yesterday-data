## Thesis

The agent war is won not by form factor but by the quality of work an agent does, which is why OpenAI's Dots, despite being copyable, is worth $100 a month.

## Argument

The form factor for agents is converging—everyone has a computer, memory, and background work—but utility diverges based on model quality and task-specific competencies. OpenAI's Dots, built on Astra, excels at work-related tasks because it leverages the context you've built in ChatGPT. This context, combined with a powerful model, allows Dots to proactively fix a zombie meeting or resolve a DMV dispute, saving real time and hassle. The utility is not just in the agent's features but in the intelligence applied to your specific work context. This is why OpenAI's bet on making context intelligent is so powerful: it turns accumulated AI context into proactive, valuable actions. The efficiency of the new GPT-6.1 Soul model further enhances this by allowing more work to be done without exhausting plan limits, making the $100 Pro plan a sound economic investment for heavy ChatGPT users. The real value is in the outcome—the time saved, the problems solved—not in the agent's form factor.

## Counterpoints

- Why pay if anyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work? The answer is the quality of work, which depends on the underlying model and configuration.
- Some argue that all agents are becoming the same, but utility differs because different tasks and competencies emerge for different models, even with the same form factor.
- The $200 subscription change, where subscribers get a lower limit for the same price, is unpopular, but the efficiency of GPT-6.1 Soul mitigates the impact.
- Spaces and Pages assume an AI-native, AI-literate organization, which is rare outside Silicon Valley, raising questions about adoption and change management.
