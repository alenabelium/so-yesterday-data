## Thesis

The agent war is won by utility, not form factor: every AI agent now has a computer and memory, but what justifies a $100/month subscription is the quality of work the agent does for you.

## Argument

The mechanism is utility. OpenAI's Dev Day launched Dots, an agent with a computer, memory, and background work, but Meta's Muse and other agents offer the same form factor. The difference is the model and task-specific competencies. Dots is built on GPT-6.1 Astra, which is approaching Astra's capabilities at a fifth of the cost, making it efficient for high-volume work. The real value is in the context you've built with AI: Dots can proactively fix your calendar, resolve conflicts, and prepare launch materials because it has access to your work context. This is the flywheel: more context leads to better proactive actions, which leads to more value. OpenAI is betting that more of your work will stay in ChatGPT, and they're building Spaces and Pages to make it a collaborative workspace, directly competing with Microsoft. The key is to focus on outcomes, not activity. Agents are becoming a commodity, but the ones that deliver results will be worth the price.

## Counterpoints

- If everyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work, why pay? The answer is the quality of work, which depends on the model and task-specific competencies.
- Dots doesn't instantly know everything you did in ChatGPT; it only gets memories you choose to share, which is a privacy choice that builds trust.
- Spaces and Pages assume an AI-native, AI-literate organization, which is rare outside Silicon Valley; adoption may require significant change management.
- Microsoft has a natural advantage with Copilot because of trust, but the 'no one got fired for choosing Microsoft' argument won't work here.
- Current $200 subscribers will get a lower limit after October 29th, which is unpopular, but the efficiency of GPT-6.1 Soul may compensate.
