## Thesis

The agent war is won not by form factor—everyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work—but by the quality of work an agent does for you, which is why OpenAI's Dots is both interesting and not the most important Dev Day release.

## Argument

The mechanism is utility, not form. All agents are starting to look the same—Dots, Muse, Grok—but the usefulness does not become similar. Just because you have a computer, memory, and background work, it doesn't mean you have the same model or configuration behind the scenes. Dots is built for work, leveraging the context you've accumulated in ChatGPT, and it's powered by Astra, the most powerful model. That's the differentiator: the quality of work, not the packaging.

OpenAI's strategy is to make context intelligent. They have 1.2 billion users, and they're betting that more of your work will stay in ChatGPT, giving AI more context for the next task. The new GPT-6.1 Soul model is the unsung hero—it's approaching Astra in some estimations at a fifth of the cost, so you can give it more work without exhausting your plan limits. This spins the flywheel: more work, more context, better results.

Spaces and Pages are the long-term play, a direct challenge to Microsoft Office. They're shared digital spaces where agents and people collaborate in real time. But this assumes AI-native, AI-literate organizations, which I don't see outside Silicon Valley. Microsoft has a natural advantage with trust, but the 'no one got fired for choosing Microsoft' argument won't work here. The question is whether OpenAI can communicate this new way of working and anchor it at the enterprise level.

For you, the practical takeaway: don't upgrade to $500 unless you're confident in your AI skills. The $100 Pro plan gives you your first Dots, and Soul 6.1 is so efficient you won't miss the higher

## Counterpoints

- All agents look the same—Muse, Grok, Dots—so why pay? Because utility differs based on model quality and task-specific competencies.
- Spaces and Pages assume AI-native organizations, which are rare outside Silicon Valley; Microsoft has a trust advantage.
- The $200 plan change is unpopular: existing subscribers get lower limits after October 29th, but Soul 6.1's efficiency may compensate.
- Privacy concerns: Dots only accesses memories you choose to share, which builds trust but limits context.
