## Thesis

Every AI agent now has a computer, memory, and background work, so the only thing that makes you keep one is the quality of work it does for you.

## Argument

The agent war has begun, and OpenAI's Dots, Meta's Muse, and Anthropic's Claude all offer the same basic form factor: a computer, memory, and background work. But form factor is converging while utility is not. Just because you have the same mechanism doesn't mean you have the same model or the same configuration for your tasks. Dots is built on Astra, the most powerful model, and it's designed for work, not just personal tasks. The real differentiator is the context you've built with AI. OpenAI has 1.2 billion users, and they're betting that more of your work will stay in ChatGPT, giving AI more context for the next task. That's why they launched Spaces and Pages, a shared workspace that competes with Microsoft Office. The efficiency of the new GPT-6.1 Soul model is the unsung hero, letting you do complex work without exhausting your plan limits. The future is about outcomes, not activity. Agents are becoming a category of persistent intelligence in context, and the question that matters is: what kind of work can be done well and get results?

## Counterpoints

- Why pay $100 a month when anyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work?
- Spaces and Pages assume AI-native, AI-literate organizations, which are rare outside Silicon Valley.
- Microsoft has a natural advantage with Copilot because it's trusted, even if not cutting-edge.
- The $200 plan is getting a lower limit, which is unpopular despite the efficiency of Soul 6.1.
- Dots doesn't instantly know everything you did in ChatGPT; it only gets memories you choose to share.
