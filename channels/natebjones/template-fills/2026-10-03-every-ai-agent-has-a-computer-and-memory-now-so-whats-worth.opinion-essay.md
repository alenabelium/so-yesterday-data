## Thesis

The agent war is won by utility, not form factor: every AI agent now has a computer and memory, but the quality of work and task-specific competencies determine which agents are worth paying for.

## Argument

The form factor for agents is converging—everyone has a computer, memory, and background work—but utility diverges sharply. OpenAI's Dots, Meta's Muse, and Anthropic's Claude each excel at different tasks: Muse handles practical chores like calls and small purchases, Instinct focuses on travel and restaurant transactions, while Dots is built for serious work, leveraging the context you've accumulated in ChatGPT. The real differentiator is the model behind the agent and how it's configured for specific scenarios. OpenAI's bet is that context becomes intelligent: Dots uses your memories and connected apps to proactively fix your calendar or prepare launch materials when a feature slips. This is where the value lies—not in the generic capability, but in the quality of work delivered. The efficiency of GPT-6.1 Soul, at a fifth of Astra's cost, makes it a workhorse for high-volume tasks, while Astra remains for complex, high-stakes problems. The strategic play is Spaces and Pages, a collaborative workspace that competes with Microsoft Office, but its success hinges on AI-native organizations, which are rare outside Silicon Valley. The real opportunity is for small teams to become more ambitious, using agents to deliver outcomes, not just activity. The market is shifting from efficiency to results, and the winners will be those who master the meta-skill of delegating tasks to the right AI.

## Counterpoints

- If everyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work, why pay? The answer is the quality of work, not the form factor.
- Spaces and Pages assume AI-native, AI-literate organizations, which are rare outside Silicon Valley; Microsoft has a trust advantage.
- The $200 plan's limits are being reduced for existing subscribers, which is unpopular despite the efficiency of GPT-6.1 Soul.
- Dots doesn't instantly know everything from ChatGPT; it only accesses memories you choose to share, which is a privacy choice but limits proactivity.
