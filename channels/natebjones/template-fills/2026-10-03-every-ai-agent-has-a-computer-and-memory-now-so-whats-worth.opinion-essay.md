## Thesis

The agent war is won by utility, not form factor, so the $100/month question is really about which agent delivers the best work quality.

## Argument

The mechanism is utility. While every agent now has a computer, memory, and background work, the real differentiator is the quality of work delivered. OpenAI's Dots, built on Astra, excels at proactive, context-aware tasks like fixing calendar conflicts or preparing launch materials. This is a step beyond Meta's Muse, which handles practical but low-intelligence chores, or Instinct, which focuses on travel and restaurants. The value of Dots lies in its integration with ChatGPT's 1.2 billion users and the context you've built, making it a persistent intelligence that understands your work. This is why OpenAI pairs Dots with Spaces and Pages, creating a collaborative workspace that competes with Microsoft. The efficiency of GPT-6.1 Soul, at a fifth of Astra's cost, makes this accessible. The real opportunity is for small teams to become more ambitious, using these tools to deliver outcomes, not just activity.

## Counterpoints

- Anyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work, so why pay?
- The form factor is converging, but utility differs based on model quality and task-specific competencies.
- Spaces and Pages assume AI-native organizations, which are rare outside Silicon Valley.
- Microsoft has a natural advantage with trust and enterprise distribution.
- The $200 subscription limit reduction is unpopular, but Soul 6.1's efficiency mitigates the impact.
