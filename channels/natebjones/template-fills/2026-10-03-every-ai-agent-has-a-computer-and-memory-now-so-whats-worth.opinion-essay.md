## Thesis

The agent war is a form-factor tie; the real differentiator is the quality of work each agent does for you.

## Argument

**The mechanism is utility, not form factor.** Every agent now ships with a computer, memory, and background work, so the surface looks identical. But the usefulness diverges because the underlying model and its task-specific competencies differ. Muse excels at practical chores like calls and small purchases; Instinct is proactive around travel and restaurants; Dots is built for serious work, leveraging the context you've accumulated in ChatGPT. This is why OpenAI pairs Dots with its most powerful model, Astra, betting on scenarios that demand advanced intelligence. The value isn't in the packaging—it's in the outcome. For me, that meant Dots fixing a zombie meeting and resolving a DMV dispute without me lifting a finger. That's the promise of 'intelligence in context' becoming proactive. The real strategic play is Spaces and Pages, which extend this into a collaborative workspace, directly challenging Microsoft. But this assumes an AI-native, flat organizational structure that's rare outside Silicon Valley. The 'no one got fired for choosing Microsoft' argument won't apply here. The question isn't the feature set; it's which organizations will adopt this new way of working. For individuals, the economic case is clear: if you can't make back the $100, don't upgrade. But for builders, the plugin marketplace and 'log in via ChatGPT' are the sleeper opportunities—a way to sell outcomes without token costs. The future isn't about one agent; it's about a multi-agent company where you master the meta-skill of delegation.
