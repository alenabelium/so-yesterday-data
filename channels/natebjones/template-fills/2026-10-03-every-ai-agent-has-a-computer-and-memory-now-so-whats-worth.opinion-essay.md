## Thesis

The agent war is won by utility, not form factor: every AI agent now has a computer and memory, but the real differentiator is the quality of work, which is why OpenAI's $100/month Dots is worth it for serious AI users.

## Argument

The agent war has begun, and OpenAI's Dots—an agent with a computer, memory, and background work—is the latest salvo. But the real battleground isn't the form factor; it's utility. All agents are starting to look the same, but the usefulness doesn't converge. The quality of work depends on the model behind the agent and the specific tasks it's configured for. Muse excels at practical, low-intelligence tasks like making calls; Instinct is proactive and travel-focused; Dots is built for serious work, leveraging the context you've built in ChatGPT. This is the key: context is the moat. OpenAI has 1.2 billion users, and Dots uses that context to fix your calendar, resolve DMV disputes, and prepare for product launches. The value is in the outcome, not the activity. This is why I use Dots, and why I'm willing to pay $100/month. The efficiency of the new GPT-6.1 Soul model, at a fifth of the cost of Astra, makes it even more compelling. The future is multi-agent, and you need to master the meta-skill of delegating tasks to the right AI. The $100/month is an investment in your future productivity, not just a subscription.

## Counterpoints

- Why pay if anyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work? The answer is the quality of work, which depends on the model and configuration.
- Dots doesn't instantly know everything from ChatGPT; it only gets your memories and what you connect. This is a privacy choice, but it limits its proactive capabilities.
- Spaces and Pages assume an AI-native, AI-literate organization, which is rare outside Silicon Valley. Microsoft has a natural advantage with trust and enterprise integration.
- Current $200 subscribers will get a lower limit after October 29th, which is unpopular. The efficiency of Soul 6.1 may not compensate for the perceived loss.
- The $500 Pro Max plan is only worth it if you have the skills to manage the speed and avoid costly mistakes. It's an economic proposition, not a status symbol.
