## Thesis

The agent war is won not by form factor—computers, memory, background work—but by utility: the quality of work an agent does for you, which is why Dots is worth $100 a month.

## Argument

The mechanism is utility, not form factor. All agents now have a computer, memory, and background work—Dots, Muse, Instinct, Grok—but the usefulness diverges because the underlying model and task-specific competencies differ. Muse excels at practical, low-intelligence tasks like calls and small purchases; Instinct targets travel and restaurant transactions; Dots is built for serious work, leveraging the context you've accumulated in ChatGPT. This context is the key: OpenAI's 1.2 billion users generate a flywheel where more work in ChatGPT leads to more context, which makes Dots more proactive and valuable. The GPT-6.1 Soul model is the unsung hero, delivering near-Astra performance at a fifth of the cost, letting you scale work without exhausting limits. Spaces and Pages extend this into collaborative territory, challenging Microsoft Office, but they assume an AI-native organizational culture that's rare outside Silicon Valley. The real opportunity is outcome-focused: agents that proactively adjust promises when features slip, or that help small teams become more ambitious. The future is multi-agent, and the meta-skill is knowing which agent to delegate to. For $100, you're not just buying a tool; you're investing in a persistent intelligence that works for you, saving time and hassle, as Dots did for me with a zombie meeting and a DMV conflict.

## Counterpoints

- Why pay if anyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work? The answer is the quality of work, not the form factor.
- Dots doesn't instantly know everything from ChatGPT; it only accesses memories you choose to share, which is a privacy choice that builds trust.
- Spaces and Pages assume an AI-native, AI-literate organization, which is rare outside Silicon Valley; adoption may require significant change management.
- Microsoft has a natural advantage with trust and existing enterprise relationships, but the 'no one got fired for choosing Microsoft' argument won't apply here.
- The $200 plan is getting a lower limit for the same price, which is unpopular; but GPT-6.1 Soul's efficiency may compensate.
