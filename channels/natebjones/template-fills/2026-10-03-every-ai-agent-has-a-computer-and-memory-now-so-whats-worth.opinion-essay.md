## Thesis

The agent war is won not by form factor—computers, memory, background work—but by utility: the quality of work an agent does, which depends on the model and task-specific competencies.

## Argument

The mechanism is utility, not form factor. All agents now offer the same basic capabilities—a computer, memory, background work—but that doesn't mean they deliver the same value. The difference lies in the model behind the agent and the specific tasks it excels at. Muse, for example, is great for practical, low-intelligence tasks like making calls or planning dates, while Instinct is more proactive and travel-focused. Dots, on the other hand, is built for serious work, leveraging the context you've built in ChatGPT to handle complex tasks like fixing calendar conflicts or resolving DMV disputes. This is why I argue that utility diverges even when form factors converge. OpenAI's bet is that context becomes intelligent: the more you work in ChatGPT, the more context your agent has, and the better it can serve you. This is the flywheel they're spinning with the new GPT-6.1 Soul model, which offers near-Astra performance at a fifth of the cost, allowing you to do more work without exhausting your limits. The real opportunity, though, is in the ecosystem: Spaces and Pages for collaborative work, the Decisions API for classification tasks, and plugin extensions that let you build on ChatGPT's infrastructure. The future belongs to those who master the meta-skill of delegating tasks to AI effectively, and to small teams that can now be more ambitious because they have access to intelligence that was previously out of reach.

## Counterpoints

- If everyone can copy a computer, memory, and background work, why pay for an agent? The answer is the quality of work, which depends on the model and task-specific competencies.
- Some argue that agents are all the same because they share form factors, but utility differs based on the underlying model and configuration for specific use cases.
- Spaces and Pages assume AI-native, AI-literate organizations, which are rare outside Silicon Valley, raising questions about adoption and change management.
- Microsoft has a natural advantage with Copilot due to trust and enterprise integration, but the 'no one got fired for choosing Microsoft' argument won't apply here.
- There are limits to what Dots knows; it doesn't automatically have access to all your ChatGPT context, which is a privacy choice but also a limitation.
