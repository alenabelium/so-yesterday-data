## TL;DR

OpenAI and Anthropic’s IPO valuations hinge on a strategic pivot: making raw intelligence cheap while building proprietary 'harnesses' that capture the workflow layer. This bet transforms labs from API suppliers into operating systems for knowledge work. The real question is whether they can build these systems faster than enterprises can build their own.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:30']
- **Claim**: The thesis rests on two simultaneous capabilities: making intelligence cheap enough for massive scale and building a proprietary layer around it fast enough that companies rent the system rather than building it themselves.
- **Role**: definition

### Reframe API Pricing Logic
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:57']
- **Claim**: High API prices are retail markup, not internal cost. Labs can subsidize heavy users while racing the inference cost curve down through efficiency gains, turning cheap tokens into a strategy to make intelligence abundant.
- **Role**: evidence

### Identify the Value Shift
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['01:57']
- **Claim**: As raw intelligence becomes a commodity, value moves to the 'harness'—the layer that defines context, permissions, and workflow. Labs want to sell the work surface, not just the model, to avoid being routed or substituted.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Analyze the Context Asymmetry
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:11']
- **Claim**: Enterprises hold an information asymmetry advantage through private context. Labs counter this with forward-deployed engineering to map workflows and create sticky, company-specific harnesses that lock in customers beyond model performance.
- **Role**: counter

### Define the Strategic Fork
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:38']
- **Claim**: The critical decision is whether companies own their harness (making labs suppliers) or rent it (making labs the operating layer). Owning the harness requires controlling context, evals, and routing logic, not just using the model.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Codeex serves as the primary example of a harness, combining model intelligence with repo access, file editing, and test running to participate in knowledge work. The speaker notes that while labs have speed and infrastructure, companies have private context (Salesforce fields, approval steps, internal history). Forward-deployed engineering is the bridge to overcome this. Caveats include the risk that if companies learn to own their harnesses, labs become mere suppliers with falling token margins. The speaker also hedges that almost no company should train frontier models; the goal is owning the layer above them.
