## TL;DR

Meta's Muse app is disrupting the 'inattention model' where companies profit from consumer inertia and forgotten subscriptions. By autonomously managing these tasks, Muse saves users significant money but threatens Amazon's $68.6B advertising revenue by intercepting the purchase journey. This forces a shift in platform power from search-based discovery to agent-mediated commerce.

## The argument

### Define the Inattention Model
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:17:47']
- **Claim**: The 'inattention model' is a technical tool where companies profit from subscriber inattention. Economists found that card replacement forces active confirmation, increasing cancellation rates. Inattention boosts operator revenue by an average of 87%, proving inertia is highly profitable.
- **Role**: definition

### AI as the Inertia Breaker
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:44']
- **Claim**: Muse breaks this inertia by providing personal context and proactive review. Unlike raw intelligence, Muse uses memory to distinguish duplicates and family needs, turning passive spending into informed decisions. This utility forces consumers to act on forgotten subscriptions.
- **Role**: evidence

### Amazon's Defensive Blockade
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:05']
- **Claim**: Amazon blocked Muse to protect its $68.6B advertising business. If an agent recommends a product, Amazon loses the sponsored spot and the ability to shape the purchase path. This is a battle for platform ownership over the customer relationship.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: The New Commerce Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:13:53']
- **Claim**: Muse captures value through transaction fees, not ads. By connecting with Shopify and Stripe, it creates a new commerce layer where businesses pay for customer acquisition via agents. This shifts power from search engines to the assistant that knows the user.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Muse saved the host $1,285 in one year and helped users save thousands on insurance (00:03:44). Walmart partnered with Muse because it competes with Amazon's ad dominance, whereas Amazon defends its status quo (00:16:37). Caveat: ChatGPT's checkout feature had lower conversion rates than site visits, so results are not guaranteed (00:16:37). Meta lacks transparency on human contractor involvement in calls (00:02:22). Trust is a barrier for some users wary of Meta's data practices (00:31:04).
