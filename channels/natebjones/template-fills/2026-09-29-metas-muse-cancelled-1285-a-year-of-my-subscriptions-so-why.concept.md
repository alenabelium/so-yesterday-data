## TL;DR

Amazon blocked Meta's Muse not for technical reasons, but because it threatens the 'inattention economy'—a model where companies profit from consumer inertia and forgotten subscriptions. While Amazon protects its $68B ad revenue by controlling the purchase journey, competitors like Walmart partner with Muse to capture new traffic. This shift proves that tangible personal utility, delivered through intuitive agents, is displacing raw intelligence as the primary driver of AI adoption.

## The argument

### Define the Inattention Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:17:47']
- **Claim**: The 'inattention model' allows companies to increase revenue by an average of 87% through subscriber inertia, where users forget to cancel services. This passive profit stream is the core asset being threatened.
- **Role**: definition

### Amazon's Defensive Blockade
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:59']
- **Claim**: Amazon blocked Muse to protect its $68.6B advertising business, which relies on owning the 'moment' of purchase decision-making. By intercepting this journey, Muse shifts value from Amazon's ad platform to the agent.
- **Role**: evidence

### Competitor Alignment with Meta
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:00']
- **Claim**: Walmart and Shopify joined Muse Connect because they compete with Amazon's dominance. They view the agent as a new starting point for shopping, offering a chance to capture customers who would otherwise start on Amazon.
- **Role**: evidence

### Utility Over Intelligence
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:44']
- **Claim**: Success depends on 'personal context' and memory, not just model smarts. Muse wins by handling mundane coordination (subscriptions, schedules) with an intuitive avatar, making AI invisible and accessible to non-technical users.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Muse saved the host $1,285/year in cancelled subscriptions. Other users reported saving thousands on auto insurance and phone bills. However, Meta initially used human contractors for calls, raising privacy concerns that were later addressed by autonomous implementation. Walmart previously abandoned ChatGPT checkout due to low conversion rates, suggesting Muse must prove its own efficacy. Amazon's objections cite credential security, but the host argues the real motive is protecting the 'customer purchase journey'.
