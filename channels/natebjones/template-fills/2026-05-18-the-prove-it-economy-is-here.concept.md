## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from attention to interpretation, where AI agents mediate decisions. To succeed, individuals and marketers must build a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that allows AI to accurately understand and recommend their products or skills. Simultaneously, maintaining strong human connections and brand loyalty remains critical, as humans ultimately apply preference to AI-mediated options.

## The argument

### Shift to Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The internet economy is moving from an attention model to an interpretation economy where AI agents filter consumer and hiring decisions, requiring a new positioning strategy.
- **Role**: definition

### Build a Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: Entities must provide a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (like JSON schemas) that allows AI agents to extract specific, provable facts about products or skills, avoiding being flattened into the internet average.
- **Role**: evidence

### Leverage Offline Memory
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:38']
- **Claim**: Strong offline human connections create brand loyalty that 'seats prompts,' allowing humans to ask AI for specific brands by name, thereby bypassing the agent's neutral comparison logic.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesize Dual Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05']
- **Claim**: Success requires reinforcing both agent-facing clarity (structure/evidence) and human-facing memory (trust/preference); neglecting either leads to being averaged out by agents or ignored by humans.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via AI chat as a concrete example of the interpretation economy in action. He warns against 'AI washing' and emphasizes that while back-office automation (like AI writing resumes) is table stakes, it doesn't provide leverage. The speaker hedges that while agents are doing the shopping, humans still apply the final preference, making offline memory increasingly precious. He also notes that marketing is becoming more technical but doesn't require being an engineer, just understanding AI logic.
