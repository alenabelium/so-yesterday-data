## TL;DR

The internet economy has shifted from an attention-based model to an interpretation economy where AI agents mediate decisions. To succeed, individuals and marketers must build a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that allows AI to accurately understand and recommend their products or skills. Simultaneously, maintaining strong human connections and brand loyalty remains critical, as humans ultimately apply preference to AI-mediated options.

## The argument

### The Shift to Interpretation
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The internet economy has moved from an attention model (shouting for eyeballs) to an interpretation economy where AI agents filter all consumer and hiring decisions. Success requires positioning for AI as the first port of call.
- **Role**: definition

### Building the Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: Marketers and individuals must create a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (like JSON schemas or specific technical specs) that agents can read. Without opinionated, provable details, entities are flattened into the internet average.
- **Role**: definition

### The Offline-Online Feedback Loop
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:38']
- **Claim**: While agents handle comparison, humans apply preference. Offline events and emotional connections create brand loyalty, which constrains agent searches to specific brands. Human memory becomes more valuable as transactions become more mediated by AI.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via chat with Claude and ChatGPT as a personal example of AI-mediated purchasing. He notes that 'AI washing' is dangerous because it deceives buyers/hirers who are then disappointed. He also mentions the 'Talent Board' project as a tangible step for individuals to prove AI skills. Caveat: The speaker acknowledges that back-office automation (like using ChatGPT for resumes) is now 'table stakes' and provides no competitive advantage.
