## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from attention to interpretation, where AI agents mediate hiring and purchasing. Success requires building a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that agents can read and trust. Simultaneously, human brand loyalty remains critical as the final arbiter of preference.

## The argument

### Define the Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:53']
- **Claim**: The economy has moved from attention (shouting for eyeballs) to interpretation (AI filtering decisions). Marketers and candidates must position themselves for AI agents, not just humans.
- **Role**: definition

### Build a Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: To avoid being flattened into the internet average, you must provide a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (like JSON schemas) that proves your claims with technical detail, allowing agents to map intent to your product or skills.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: Human Loyalty Still Matters
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:13']
- **Claim**: While agents interpret, humans apply preference. Offline events and emotional connections create brand loyalty that constrains AI choices, proving that human-facing work is not obsolete but complementary to agent-facing clarity.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesize: The Two-Internet Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05']
- **Claim**: The future belongs to those who refuse to choose between the two internets. You must simultaneously build agent-legible truth layers for retrieval and memorable human narratives for preference, avoiding 'AI washing' in favor of honest differentiation.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via AI chat as a consumer example where AI filtered options based on room dimensions and budget. For individuals, he references his 'Talent Board' project, which requires proving AI skills via MLOps pipelines rather than just LinkedIn profiles. He warns that 'AI washing' is dangerous because it deceives buyers/hiring managers who are disappointed by the lack of substance. Caveat: The speaker acknowledges that back-office automation (like using ChatGPT for resumes) is 'table stakes' in 2026 and no longer provides competitive advantage.
