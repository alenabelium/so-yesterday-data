## TL;DR

The internet economy has shifted from an attention model to an interpretation economy where AI agents mediate decisions. Success requires building a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that allows agents to verify claims, while simultaneously cultivating strong offline human connections to drive brand loyalty and preference.

## The argument

### Shift to Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The economy has moved from attention-based advertising to an interpretation economy where AI agents filter consumer and hiring decisions, making visibility dependent on AI trust rather than human eyeballs.
- **Role**: definition

### Building the Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: Entities must provide a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (e.g., JSON schemas) that proves specific claims, preventing them from being flattened into the internet average by agents.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Two-Internet Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05']
- **Claim**: Success requires reinforcing two distinct layers: agent-facing clarity for retrieval and human-facing emotional connection for memory, as agents mediate options but humans apply final preference.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via AI chat as evidence of agent-mediated shopping. He warns against 'AI washing' and emphasizes that back-office automation is merely table stakes. Caveat: Agents may not always find the truly best option, only what seems best based on available data. Offline events are crucial for seating prompts in human memory.
