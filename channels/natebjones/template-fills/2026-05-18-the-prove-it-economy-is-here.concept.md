## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from attention to interpretation, where AI agents mediate hiring and purchasing. Success requires building a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that allows agents to verify claims. Simultaneously, human brand loyalty remains critical as the final arbiter of preference.

## The argument

### Define the Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The economy has moved from attention (shouting for eyeballs) to interpretation (AI filtering decisions). Agents are the first port of call for trust and verification.
- **Role**: definition

### Build the Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:42']
- **Claim**: Marketers and individuals must provide high-fidelity, structured data (JSON schemas, technical details) so agents can extract reliable signals. Without this, you are flattened into the internet average.
- **Role**: definition

### Maintain Human Brand Loyalty
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:38']
- **Claim**: Agents mediate, but humans apply preference. Offline events and emotional connections create brand loyalty that constrains agent choices, preventing them from suggesting competitors.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesize the Two-Internet Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05']
- **Claim**: The winning strategy rejects the choice between human and agent. It combines memorable human-facing work with agent-legible technical truth layers. AI washing fails because it lacks the substantive proof agents require.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via AI chat as a personal example of agent-mediated purchasing. He notes that hiring managers trade prompts for candidates, confirming this applies to talent markets. He warns that 'AI washing' is dangerous because it deceives buyers who are then disappointed by a lack of substance. The caveat is that while agents do the comparison, the human still applies the final preference; thus, emotional connection remains valuable.
