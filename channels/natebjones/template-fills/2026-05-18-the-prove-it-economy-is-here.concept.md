## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from attention to interpretation, where AI agents mediate decisions. Success requires building a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that agents can read, while simultaneously cultivating offline human brand loyalty to drive specific intent.

## The argument

### Shift to Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:53']
- **Claim**: The economy is moving from attention-based ads to an interpretation economy where AI agents filter all consumer and hiring decisions, making AI the first port of call for trust.
- **Role**: definition

### Build the Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: To avoid being flattened into the internet average, individuals and brands must provide a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (like JSON schemas) that allows agents to extract provable, opinionated signals about value.
- **Role**: evidence

### Seating Prompts Offline
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:38']
- **Claim**: Strong offline brand loyalty creates 'seated prompts,' allowing humans to ask AI for specific brands by name rather than generic comparisons, bypassing the agent's neutral aggregation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesize Two Internets
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05']
- **Claim**: The future is a 'two internet economy' where agent-facing clarity and human-facing memory must reinforce each other; failing to provide distinct opinions leads to being averaged out by AI.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via AI chat as a personal example of the interpretation economy in action. He notes that 'AI washing' is dangerous because it deceives buyers and leads to disappointment. Caveat: Back-office automation (like using AI to write resumes) is 'table stakes' and does not provide competitive leverage; it is merely a baseline requirement. The speaker also warns that if marketing only touches content without clarifying product truth, the role becomes weak.
