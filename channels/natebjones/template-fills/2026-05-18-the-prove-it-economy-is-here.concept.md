## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from attention to interpretation: AI agents now mediate how we shop, hire, and get hired. Success requires building a 'truth layer' of structured, provable data that agents can read and trust, while also cultivating human brand loyalty. The future belongs to those who are memorable to people and legible to machines.

## The argument

### Define the Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:53']
- **Claim**: The internet economy is moving from an attention model—where you shout to get eyeballs—to an interpretation economy where AI filters the web and forms opinions about you, your product, or your company. This shift applies equally to individuals seeking jobs and marketers selling products.
- **Role**: definition

### Agents Need a Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:42']
- **Claim**: To be included in AI-mediated consideration sets, you must build a 'truth layer': structured, factual, provable data about your product or skills that agents can extract and trust. Without it, you get flattened into the internet average and miss the transaction entirely.
- **Role**: definition

### The Sound System Example
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:18']
- **Claim**: The speaker's recent sound system purchase was entirely mediated by AI—he gave room dimensions, budget, and preferences to Claude and ChatGPT, and they recommended a system. The marketers had no influence, proving that agents now do the shopping and the consideration set is decided by AI interpretation.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Counter: Brand Loyalty Still Matters
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:13']
- **Claim**: One might think agents make brand irrelevant, but the opposite is true: human memory becomes more precious as more of the transaction is mediated. If a brand builds a strong emotional connection, the human will ask for it by name, constraining the agent to that choice.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: The Two-Internet Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05']
- **Claim**: The best marketers and candidates must operate on both internets: human-facing work that creates memory and preference, and agent-facing work that provides clarity, structure, and evidence. If your story is memorable but your truth layer is incoherent, agents flatten you; if your data is clean but your brand is forgettable, humans won't choose you.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker's sound system purchase is the primary worked example: he used Claude and ChatGPT to shop, and the marketers had zero influence. He also cites hiring managers trading prompts to find candidates, and the 'talent board' project as a way to prove AI skills. 

Caveats: The speaker admits he doesn't know if he got the truly best option—only what AI told him. He acknowledges that back-office AI automation is 'table stakes' and won't earn credit. He warns against 'AI washing'—overstating capabilities—because it leads to disappointed buyers or employers. The argument assumes agents will increasingly mediate transactions, but the speaker notes that humans still apply final preference.
