## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from an attention model to an interpretation economy where AI agents mediate decisions. To succeed, individuals and marketers must build a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that allows AI to accurately understand and recommend their products or skills, while simultaneously maintaining strong human connections to drive brand loyalty.

## The argument

### The Shift to Interpretation
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The internet economy is transitioning from an attention-based model to an interpretation economy where AI agents filter consumer and hiring decisions. Success requires positioning oneself for AI agents as the first port of call, rather than just seeking human attention.
- **Role**: definition

### Building the Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: Entities must create a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (like JSON schemas) that proves specific claims. Without this technical detail, AI agents will flatten the offering into the internet average, making differentiation impossible.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Offline/Online Feedback Loop
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:13']
- **Claim**: Human memory and offline interactions become more valuable as transactions are mediated by AI. Strong brand loyalty allows humans to bypass agent comparisons by asking for specific brands by name, creating a powerful feedback loop between offline presence and online retrievability.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites his own experience buying a sound system via AI chat as evidence of the shift. He warns against 'AI washing' and emphasizes that back-office automation is merely table stakes. The argument holds that while agents handle comparison, humans apply preference, making emotional connection critical. The speaker also notes that marketing must become more technical to be legible to agents without requiring full engineering expertise.
