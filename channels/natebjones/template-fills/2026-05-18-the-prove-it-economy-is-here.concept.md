## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from attention to interpretation, where AI agents mediate hiring and purchasing. Success requires building a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that agents can read and trust. Simultaneously, humans still apply final preference, making offline brand loyalty critical to anchor agent-mediated decisions.

## The argument

### Define the Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The economy has moved from an attention model (shouting for eyeballs) to an interpretation model where AI agents filter all consumer and hiring decisions. Individuals and marketers must position themselves for this new reality.
- **Role**: definition

### Build the Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: To avoid being flattened into the internet average, entities must provide a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (like JSON schemas or specific technical specs) that allows agents to map customer intent to specific product features or skills.
- **Role**: definition

### Anchor with Offline Memory
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:38']
- **Claim**: While agents interpret data, humans apply preference. Strong offline brand loyalty allows humans to ask for specific brands by name, constraining the agent's search and preventing it from recommending competitors.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesize the Two-Internet Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05']
- **Claim**: The winning strategy combines agent-facing clarity (structured data) with human-facing memorability (offline connection). AI washing fails because it lacks the substantive truth layer agents require, while pure attention marketing fails because it lacks the data agents need to interpret.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites his own purchase of a sound system via AI chat as evidence of the shift. He notes that marketers often resist technical detail, calling it 'too technical,' but argues it is the new leverage. A key caveat is that this does not eliminate the need for human connection; rather, human memory becomes *more* precious as transactions become mediated. The speaker also warns against 'AI washing'—deceptive claims of AI-native status—which leads to disappointment when agents cannot verify the underlying truth layer. He advises against being 'the candidate for everyone' without opinions, as agents will average out undifferentiated signals.
