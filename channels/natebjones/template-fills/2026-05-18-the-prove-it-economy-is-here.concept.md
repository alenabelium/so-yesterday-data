## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from an attention-based model to an interpretation economy where AI agents mediate decisions. To succeed, individuals and brands must build a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that allows AI to accurately understand and recommend them, while simultaneously maintaining strong human connections to drive brand loyalty.

## The argument

### Shift to Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00', '00:00:53']
- **Claim**: The internet economy has moved from an attention model (selling ads to eyeballs) to an interpretation economy where AI agents filter consumer and hiring decisions. Success requires positioning for AI as the first port of call.
- **Role**: definition

### Build a Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51', '00:08:50']
- **Claim**: Entities must provide a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (e.g., JSON schemas, specific technical details) that enables AI agents to extract reliable signals. Without this, products and candidates are flattened into the internet average.
- **Role**: definition

### Maintain Human Brand Loyalty
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:38', '00:14:13']
- **Claim**: Human memory and offline connections become more precious as transactions are mediated by AI. Strong brand loyalty allows humans to ask for specific products by name, constraining the agent's options and preventing the brand from being averaged out.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesize Dual Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05', '00:16:49']
- **Claim**: The optimal strategy rejects choosing between human and agent focus. Marketers and individuals must refuse to AI wash, instead combining agent-legible technical clarity with memorable human storytelling to create a distinctive wedge in the market.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via chat with Claude and ChatGPT as evidence of the interpretation economy in action. He notes that 'AI washing' is dangerous because it deceives people who are then disappointed, leading to a bad situation for everyone. He also hedges that while agents are doing the shopping, the shift is happening fast but is not yet fully visible in all survey data. He emphasizes that being 'AI native' requires being a little bit technical but not necessarily an engineer.
