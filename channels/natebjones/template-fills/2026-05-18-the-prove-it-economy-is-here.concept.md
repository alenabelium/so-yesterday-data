## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from attention to interpretation, where AI agents mediate hiring and purchasing. Success requires building a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that agents can read, while simultaneously cultivating offline human loyalty to drive specific brand intent.

## The argument

### The Shift to Interpretation
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The economy has moved from an attention model (shouting for eyeballs) to an interpretation model where AI agents filter all decisions. Individuals and marketers must position themselves for AI agents as the first port of call.
- **Role**: definition

### Building the Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: To avoid being flattened into the internet average, entities must provide a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (e.g., JSON schemas) that allows agents to extract specific, provable facts about products or skills.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Offline Anchor
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:13:19']
- **Claim**: Human memory and offline events become more valuable as transactions become AI-mediated. Strong brand loyalty allows humans to bypass agent comparisons by asking for specific brands by name, constraining the agent's output.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesis: The Two-Internet Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05']
- **Claim**: Success requires reinforcing both the human-facing side (memory, trust, offline presence) and the agent-facing side (clarity, structure, evidence). AI washing is dangerous; clear, opinionated differentiation is the only way to stand out in both layers.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via chat with Claude and ChatGPT as a personal example of agent-mediated shopping. He notes that while back-office automation (like using AI for resumes) is 'table stakes' in 2026, it doesn't provide competitive leverage. A key caveat is that agents need 'opinionated' signals; vague or averaged-out claims get flattened. The speaker also warns that if a company's human brand contradicts its agent-readable truth layer, it weakens in both directions. He advises against 'AI washing' because it deceives buyers and leads to disappointment when the product doesn't match the hype.
