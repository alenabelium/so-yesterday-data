## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from attention to interpretation, where AI agents mediate decisions. Success requires building a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that allows agents to verify claims, while simultaneously cultivating strong human brand loyalty to drive specific intent.

## The argument

### Define the Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The economy has moved from an attention model (shouting for eyeballs) to an interpretation model where AI agents filter all consumer and hiring decisions. Individuals and marketers must position themselves for this new reality.
- **Role**: definition

### Build a Truth Layer for Agents
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: To avoid being flattened into the internet average, entities must provide a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data (e.g., JSON schemas, technical specs) that agents can extract and verify, proving claims with facts rather than emotional marketing.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: Human Memory Remains Critical
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:13']
- **Claim**: Contrary to the idea that agents replace humans, human memory becomes more valuable as transactions are mediated. Strong offline brand loyalty ensures humans ask for specific brands by name, constraining the agent's options and preventing the 'average' trap.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesize the Two-Internet Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:05']
- **Claim**: The optimal strategy rejects choosing between human-facing and agent-facing work. Marketers and candidates must refuse to 'AI wash' and instead build a distinctive 'wedge' of opinionated, technical clarity that satisfies both agent legibility and human preference.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via chat with Claude and ChatGPT as evidence of the shift. He notes that 'AI washing' is dangerous because it deceives buyers who may be disappointed by unproven claims. Caveat: The speaker acknowledges that back-office automation (like using AI for resumes) is now 'table stakes' and offers no competitive advantage; the leverage lies in the truth layer and brand loyalty.
