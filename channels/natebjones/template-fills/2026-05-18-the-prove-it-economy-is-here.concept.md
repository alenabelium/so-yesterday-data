## TL;DR

The internet economy has shifted from an attention model to an interpretation economy where AI agents mediate decisions. To succeed, individuals and marketers must build a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that allows AI to accurately understand and recommend their products or skills, while simultaneously maintaining strong human connections to drive brand loyalty.

## The argument

### Shift to Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The internet economy has moved from an attention model to an interpretation economy where AI agents filter consumer and hiring decisions, making trust and interpretation the new currency.
- **Role**: definition

### Build the Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: Marketers and individuals must create a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data that enables AI agents to extract clear, opinionated signals about products or skills, avoiding being flattened into the internet average.
- **Role**: evidence

### Reinforce with Human Memory
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:13']
- **Claim**: Human memory and offline brand loyalty become more valuable as transactions are mediated by AI, requiring a dual strategy where agent-readable clarity reinforces human-facing emotional connection.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via AI chat as a concrete example of the interpretation economy in action. He notes that back-office automation (like using ChatGPT for resumes) is now 'table stakes' and offers no competitive advantage. A key caveat is that 'AI washing' is dangerous; if a brand's story is memorable but its truth layer is incoherent, the agent will flatten the brand, and if the PR is structured but the brand lacks memory, the human won't choose it. The speaker emphasizes that you must have opinions and specific data to stand out, as agents average out generic content.
