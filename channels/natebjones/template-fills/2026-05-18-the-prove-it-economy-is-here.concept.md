## TL;DR

The internet economy is shifting from attention to interpretation, where AI agents mediate consumer and hiring decisions. To survive, individuals and brands must build a 'truth layer' of structured, factual data that allows agents to accurately understand and recommend them. Simultaneously, maintaining strong human connections remains critical, as humans ultimately apply preference to AI-mediated options.

## The argument

### Define the Interpretation Economy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The economy is moving from an attention model (shouting for eyeballs) to an interpretation model where AI agents filter all decisions. Success requires positioning for AI as the first port of call, not just for humans.
- **Role**: definition

### Build the Truth Layer
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:51']
- **Claim**: Agents need a 'truth layer' of high-fidelity, structured data to avoid flattening brands into the average. Without opinionated, provable details (e.g., specific materials, energy savings), agents cannot map customer intent to your product or skills.
- **Role**: definition

### Counter AI Washing with Specificity
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:55']
- **Claim**: AI washing is dangerous because it deceives and disappoints. The alternative is leaning into specific expertise and opinions. Agents need clear differentiation to stand out; being 'for everyone' leads to being averaged out by algorithms.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesize the Two-Internet Strategy
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:13']
- **Claim**: Human memory becomes more precious as transactions are mediated by AI. The best strategy reinforces both: offline human connection drives brand loyalty, while online truth layers enable agent legibility. Refusing to choose between these two internets is key.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker cites buying a sound system via AI chat as a personal example of the interpretation economy in action. He notes that while back-office automation (like AI writing resumes) is table stakes, it doesn't provide leverage. He warns that if marketing only touches decoration rather than product truth, the role is weak. He also highlights that human preference still applies to AI-mediated options, making offline events crucial for seating prompts.
