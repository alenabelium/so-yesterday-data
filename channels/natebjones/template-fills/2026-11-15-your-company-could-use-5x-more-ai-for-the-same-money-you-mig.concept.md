## TL;DR

Businesses are hitting task saturation: cheaper AI models are good enough for most work, so buying more intelligence adds little value. This shifts procurement to price-sensitive evaluation, flattening AI spending even as token usage grows. The result is a buyer's market where value moves from models to applications built on top.

## The argument

### Define task saturation
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:30']
- **Claim**: Task saturation means a few models already do a job well enough, so buying more intelligence at a higher price adds little or nothing to results. Models improve, but the work doesn't need that much improvement.
- **Role**: definition

### Evaluation replaces model selection
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:51']
- **Claim**: Procurement now tests models against specific requirements rather than picking the smartest one. If a cheaper model passes the tests, it's a viable replacement, giving buyers evidence to negotiate lower prices and switch suppliers.
- **Role**: evidence

### Token growth decouples from spending
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:17']
- **Claim**: Ramp data shows AI spending flat for three months (Aug-Oct) while token usage grows. A billion-token bill at $5/M drops to $1/M, enabling 5x more tokens for the same cost—more value for the buyer, no extra revenue for the provider.
- **Role**: evidence

### Premium market persists but shrinks
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:58']
- **Claim**: Advanced models still capture ~70% of spending, but that doesn't mean they do most of the work. Task saturation applies to more processes, so expensive models justify their cost only where superior intelligence is truly needed.
- **Role**: counter

### Value shifts to applications above the model
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:32']
- **Claim**: As tokens get cheaper, pricing pressure hits all suppliers—even Infosys sees customers demand AI savings reflected in prices. The next trillion-dollar markets are in value-added services built on AI, not the models themselves.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Ramp's October report shows two months of near-zero token spending growth (Aug-Sep) despite rising usage; November 11 update shows 1.65% growth in October, with adoption at 56.8%. Customer success spending jumped from <1% to 13% of AI spend, a lagging indicator of simpler use cases. The speaker notes premium models still hold ~70% of spending, so the premium market isn't dead. He cautions that a month of low costs isn't proof labs can't succeed, and capacity shortages don't guarantee pricing power. He also warns that complex systems built to overcome model weaknesses may become obsolete after updates, so investments should focus on durable assets like evaluation methods and data integration.
