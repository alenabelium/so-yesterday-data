# Five Rules for Picking an AI Model That Actually Works

## Executive Summary

The video argues that selecting an AI model should be driven by the specific nature of the work rather than model popularity or cost alone. It distinguishes between 'center of distribution' tasks, which are routine and can be handled efficiently by cheaper models like GLM 5.2, and complex, novel problems that require the generalized intelligence of frontier models like Claude or ChatGPT. The speaker emphasizes that the choice of model must align with the user's role and the complexity of the task to ensure efficiency and avoid unnecessary distraction. Ultimately, the goal is to simplify the workflow by matching the right tool to the job, ensuring that model selection itself does not become a burden.

## Key Points

- Distinguish between routine tasks suitable for cost-effective models like GLM 5.2 and complex, novel problems requiring the broad generalization of frontier models like Claude or ChatGPT [00:02:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lq2fP7wC7d8&t=138).
- Evaluate model choice based on the specific work requirements and the efficiency of the surrounding harness, noting that strong intelligence is useless if the interface makes input/output difficult [00:05:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lq2fP7wC7d8&t=342).
- Adopt a simplified strategy for businesses by identifying the five most critical recurring artifacts and finding the simplest, most direct path to generate them with AI [00:07:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lq2fP7wC7d8&t=456).
- Follow five rules of the road: do not copy others blindly, assess task difficulty, validate output quality, keep model selection simple, and avoid picking too many models [00:12:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lq2fP7wC7d8&t=762). [MM:SS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lq2fP7wC7d8&t=SECONDS)
