# Are Your AI Agents Doing Real Work Or Productivity Theater? Here Are 4 Principles I Use.

## Executive Summary

The video argues that many AI agent setups are 'productivity theater'—activity that looks productive but doesn't lead to meaningful outcomes. The speaker proposes four principles to ensure agents do real work: accounting for the human effort after agent output, running more experiments than commitments, defining stopping conditions for recurring tasks, and giving an agent 'chief of staff' a budget for your attention. The core message is that AI requires more intentionality and focus from humans, not less, and that agents should be managed to support your priorities rather than dictate them.

## Key Points

- Principle 1: Calculate the total cost by counting the human work added after the agent completes its job; a cheaper report doesn't solve the need to be purposeful. [00:01:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIRFVjttNvA&t=117)
- Principle 2: If unsure what to focus on, run more light experiments before committing—parallel agents can explore multiple approaches, but you must prioritize developing your own conviction. [00:05:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIRFVjttNvA&t=333)
- Principle 3: Decide when a task should stop before making it recurring; recurring agents can outlive their purpose and burn tokens without being read. [00:09:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIRFVjttNvA&t=565)
- Principle 4: Give the agent that manages your work a budget for your attention—a chief of staff agent should understand your real priorities and help you focus, not scatter you. [00:12:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIRFVjttNvA&t=739)
