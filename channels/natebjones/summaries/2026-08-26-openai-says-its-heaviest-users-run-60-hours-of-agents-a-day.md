# OpenAI Says Its Heaviest Users Run 60 Hours Of Agents A Day. Somebody Has To Watch That.

## Executive Summary

The video argues that AI agents are not replacing human labor but rather generating significantly more work for humans to manage, verify, and correct, a phenomenon known as the Jevons effect. While small businesses struggle with high management costs and lack of resources to effectively integrate agents, enterprises succeed by investing in deep integration and specialized teams to handle complex workflows. The core takeaway is that future professional success depends on developing 'agent management' skills and focusing on verifiable domains where AI output can be easily validated.

## Key Points

- Agent token usage has surged 14-fold, with heavy users generating over 60 hours of agent activity daily, forcing humans to move 'above the loop' to manage workflows rather than just execute tasks. [00:03:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpEaSa7tgfc&t=218)
- Small businesses often fail to see ROI because they lack the capital and time for deep integration, whereas enterprises succeed by deploying dedicated teams to handle security, data access, and quality control. [00:16:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpEaSa7tgfc&t=971)
- Successful AI adoption requires focusing on 'verifiable domains' like legal or coding, where errors are easy to spot, rather than ambiguous tasks that require expensive human oversight. [00:26:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpEaSa7tgfc&t=1592)
- The role of managers is evolving into an 'agent management' position focused on allocation and ROI, creating a new layer of labor that mirrors the challenges faced by small business owners. [00:25:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpEaSa7tgfc&t=1518)
