# AI Agents Want To Run Your Life. The Internet It Needs Will Close More Doors Than You Think.

## Executive Summary

The video argues that the internet is becoming fragmented into zones of access for AI agents, driven by security concerns like 'Black October' and business decisions, rather than a single open internet. The creator shares his personal use of multiple agents (Grok Bot, Dots, Muse, Duet, Instinct) for different tasks, and highlights emerging protocols and partnerships (Visa, Stripe, Cloudflare, Shopify) that aim to create authorized agent access. He warns that consumers will face unequal access, with some sites blocking agents, and emphasizes the need for technical literacy and careful communication about agent capabilities to non-technical users.

## Key Points

- The internet is becoming fragmented into partnership, novice actor, and forbidden zones for AI agents, driven by business decisions and security concerns like 'Black October'. [00:18:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c3HHFL_lsLM&t=1090)
- Banks and other businesses struggle to distinguish between legitimate agent access and attacks, leading to stricter controls and the need for trusted intermediaries like Cloudflare, Google, and Stripe. [00:06:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c3HHFL_lsLM&t=391)
- Emerging protocols and partnerships (Visa/Bill/Billtrust, Stripe Link, Cloudflare signed agents, Shopify's agent-specific data) are shaping how agents gain authorized access, but no universal standard exists yet. [00:09:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c3HHFL_lsLM&t=577)
- Consumers should choose agents based on specific use cases (e.g., Instinct for travel, Muse for shopping) and be cautious about security risks, such as malicious agent instruction files. [00:23:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c3HHFL_lsLM&t=1419)
- The creator advises tech-savvy users to communicate agent capabilities responsibly to non-technical people, focusing on what agents do well to build trust. [00:26:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c3HHFL_lsLM&t=1613)
