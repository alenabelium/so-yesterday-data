# China's New AI Model Costs Way More to Run Than You Think

## Executive Summary

The video analyzes Moonshot's Kimmy K3, an open-source model that challenges the narrative of Chinese AI efficiency by requiring significant compute resources and offering lower token efficiency than US counterparts. The speaker argues that closed-source labs like OpenAI and Anthropic maintain a substantial lead in serving efficiency and true frontier capabilities, despite the rapid advancement of open-source alternatives. Consequently, the release of Kimmy K3 signals a shift where open-source models become viable cyber threats, necessitating stricter personal and corporate security measures. The analysis concludes with strategic advice on adapting to increased government regulation and the growing importance of creative prompt engineering to derive value from powerful AI systems.

## Key Points

- Kimmy K3 requires 64 accelerator cores and is not cost-efficient, debunking the myth that Chinese models are inherently cheaper to run than US frontier models [00:03:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ZpZhsjoUK4&t=212).
- Open-source models like Kimmy K3 now pose significant cyber threats, requiring individuals and companies to implement multi-layered security defenses against AI-driven fraud [00:08:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ZpZhsjoUK4&t=485).
- Future competitive advantage lies in human imagination and the ability to ask complex questions, as models become commoditized and government regulation increases [00:14:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ZpZhsjoUK4&t=843).
