# Microsoft governs 1 million employee-built tools. Your company probably governs none.

## Executive Summary

The video argues that the rise of AI and low-code tools has shifted the Product Manager's role from rationing scarce engineering resources to governing an abundance of employee-built software. As generation costs collapse, the primary bottleneck becomes human judgment regarding which tools provide value and which should be deleted or ignored. To manage this 'prototype commons,' PMs must adopt a 'production class ladder' that categorizes tools by risk and scope, ensuring that only high-value assets are promoted to official production status. This approach allows organizations to harness creative capacity while preventing sprawl and technical debt.

## Key Points

- AI lowers the cost of software creation, shifting the PM's focus from building prototypes to classifying and strategizing with the resulting abundance of internal tools and agents [00:01:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b6J387xJvHg&t=75).
- The new PM role requires deep technical judgment to evaluate model behavior, data access, and reliability, moving beyond traditional ticket-brokering to manage the 'prototype commons' [00:03:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b6J387xJvHg&t=208).
- Organizations should implement a 'production class ladder' with distinct rungs for personal tools, team betas, supported internal products, and customer-facing features to govern software lifecycle and demotion [00:09:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b6J387xJvHg&t=550).
- Effective governance involves a 'default allow' policy for experimentation paired with strict promotion paths, ensuring the company builds what matters rather than maintaining a graveyard of demos [00:11:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b6J387xJvHg&t=682).
