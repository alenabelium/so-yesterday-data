# Pangram Scanned A Million Posts For AI. 40% of Longform on LinkedIn was FAKE.

## Executive Summary

Chris Best, CEO of Substack, discusses the platform's integration of Pangram's AI detection tool to address the proliferation of low-effort 'slop' content, noting that 40% of long-form LinkedIn posts are AI-generated. He argues that the critical distinction is not the use of AI tools, but the human intent and effort behind the creation, advocating for transparency over censorship. Best emphasizes that true value in the public square lies in high-variance, original ideas and genuine human connection, which cannot be replicated by averaged AI outputs. The conversation concludes with a call for new cultural norms to protect human attention as the ultimate scarce resource in an AI-saturated world.

## Key Points

- Pangram's data reveals that 40% of long-form writing on LinkedIn is fully AI-generated, signaling a major shift in the digital public square where readers must constantly verify authenticity [00:02:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m_ZyTNmCDeY&t=163).
- Substack is introducing Pangram integration to provide transparency on AI usage, distinguishing between responsible tool use that enhances human intent and cynical 'slop' designed to game algorithms [00:08:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m_ZyTNmCDeY&t=521).
- Best argues that the 'alpha' of human creativity lies at the edges of the idea distribution, where high-variance, original thinking occurs, unlike the averaged, central distribution favored by LLMs [00:21:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m_ZyTNmCDeY&t=1304).
- As AI makes content creation cheaper, human attention becomes the most valuable non-inflationary resource, requiring new cultural norms to ensure that human effort and connection remain rewarded [00:43:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m_ZyTNmCDeY&t=2638).
