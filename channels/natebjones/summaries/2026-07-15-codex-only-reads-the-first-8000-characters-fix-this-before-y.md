# Codex Only Reads The First 8,000 Characters. Fix This Before You Prompt.

## Executive Summary

The video argues that AI harnesses—the collection of custom instructions, skills, and files surrounding a model—often become bloated and counterproductive, causing models like Codex to fail by exceeding their context window limits. The speaker introduces a 'cleaner' skill that maps and audits these harnesses to identify redundant or mis-timed instructions that confuse the AI. By applying six principles of harness management, users can shift from blaming model failures to fixing structural issues in their prompts and system configurations. This approach ensures that specialist knowledge is loaded only when needed and that hard constraints are enforced via schemas rather than verbose text.

## Key Points

- The speaker discovered their AI harness had grown to 172 instruction files and 66 skills, creating 'bloat' that obscured which rules were actually necessary for the new models. [00:01:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDJfciNhyHU&t=117)
- Six principles for harness optimization include mapping the system before cleaning, blaming the setup layer rather than the model for failures, and consolidating duplicate rules into single sources of truth. [00:04:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDJfciNhyHU&t=299)
- Specialist knowledge should be loaded dynamically at the right phase of work rather than all at once, and hard constraints like word counts must be enforced via testable schemas instead of prose instructions. [00:06:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDJfciNhyHU&t=416)
- Different models like Fable 5 and ChatGPT 5.6 have distinct failure modes when faced with heavy harnesses, requiring tailored strategies for context loading and tool usage to maintain delivery reliability. [00:10:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDJfciNhyHU&t=604)
