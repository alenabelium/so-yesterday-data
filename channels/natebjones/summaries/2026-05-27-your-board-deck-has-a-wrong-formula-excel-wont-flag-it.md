# Your Board Deck Has a Wrong Formula. Excel Won't Flag It.

## Executive Summary

The video argues that relying on simple prompts to generate Excel and PowerPoint files is dangerous because AI models often produce visually polished but factually incorrect artifacts that Excel does not flag. To ensure reliability, professionals must shift from a prompt-centric mindset to a workflow-centric approach that treats AI-generated documents as outputs of a rigorous pipeline. This pipeline consists of four critical stages: preparing and indexing source data, defining a structural blueprint, creating the artifact in constrained layers, and verifying it with a hostile reviewer. By implementing these steps, users can maintain ownership of the truth and prevent costly errors in high-stakes business decisions.

## Key Points

- The core problem is that AI models optimize for the requested artifact rather than source discipline, leading to silent errors like incorrect formulas in Excel or blended data in PowerPoint that look correct but are fundamentally flawed [00:02:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MFzxIT88zfg&t=148).
- A reliable workflow requires four stages: preparing a controlled source inventory, creating a structural blueprint (narrative spine for decks, tab architecture for spreadsheets), building the artifact in layers, and verifying with a hostile reviewer [00:04:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MFzxIT88zfg&t=261).
- Verification should involve using a separate model to act as a skeptical reviewer that enumerates issues without fixing them, creating an autonomous loop to catch errors before human review [00:12:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MFzxIT88zfg&t=768).
- Deep knowledge work cannot be fully automated into a generic 'push-button' solution because it requires specific domain context and custom assembly of evidence, necessitating human oversight and structured processes [00:18:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MFzxIT88zfg&t=1088).
