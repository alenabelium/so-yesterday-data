# Shopify Made 5,938 People Better at AI. Not With Training. By Watching.

## Executive Summary

Shopify successfully scaled AI adoption across its engineering team by mandating that all interactions with its coding agent, River, occur in public Slack channels rather than private messages. This approach addresses the 'apprenticeship gap' by making the implicit reasoning, context, and review processes of senior engineers visible to the entire organization. The video argues that organizational learning depends on exposing the full workflow—including failures and revisions—rather than just static prompts or final outputs. By enforcing constraints that prevent private agent use, companies can transform individual AI proficiency into collective intelligence and reusable workflows.

## Key Points

- Shopify's internal agent River is used by nearly 6,000 employees, with its success rooted in the design choice that all interactions must happen in public Slack channels, allowing peers to observe how tasks are scoped and solved [00:00:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NRBQmwlILjk&t=55).
- The core problem in AI adoption is the 'apprenticeship gap,' where individual employees get smarter through private usage while the company remains static because the implicit knowledge and judgment processes are never shared [00:02:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NRBQmwlILjk&t=165).
- Effective learning requires sharing four specific components of AI work: the task, the context provided to the model, the interaction pattern including prompts and pushbacks, and the review standards for what was accepted or rejected [00:05:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NRBQmwlILjk&t=351).
- Leaders must model this behavior by running real, high-stakes work in public channels to demonstrate judgment and active supervision, ensuring that junior staff learn the craft of AI usage rather than just copying static prompts [00:10:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NRBQmwlILjk&t=615).
- Organizations should measure success through metrics of learning and reuse, such as the number of adopted reusable workflows and reduced duplication of effort, rather than just raw usage volume [00:12:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NRBQmwlILjk&t=774).
