# They are buying your work, but who gets the money?

## Executive Summary

NateBJones discusses the emerging market where AI labs like Google buy vast archives of corporate work records (e.g., Spirit Airlines' emails and Teams messages) to train agents, arguing that these records capture only the chaotic imitation of work, not the true value of intellectual labor. He highlights the disconnect between what buyers pay for and what actually constitutes meaningful work, the risks of training agents on flawed data from failing companies, and the need for employees to articulate and defend the real value of their contributions. The video urges a rethinking of what work means in the age of AI, emphasizing that agents should augment human judgment rather than replace it.

## Key Points

- Google's $10 million bid for Spirit Airlines' work records reflects a belief that AI can learn intelligent work from Slack messages, emails, and documents, but these records capture only the chaotic traces of work, not its true value. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wep2EQ9Y_Tc&t=0)
- The sale of work archives raises critical questions about what constitutes 'real work' versus imitation, as agents trained on these records may learn to mimic processes without understanding the tacit knowledge and judgment that drive success. [00:04:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wep2EQ9Y_Tc&t=244)
- Employees and unions are beginning to challenge the sale of their work data, and leaders report passive resistance to AI tools, highlighting a growing need for a more productive conversation about the value of human work. [00:12:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wep2EQ9Y_Tc&t=752)
- The value of an agent lies not in doing the work itself but in identifying and automating repetitive, boring tasks, while humans focus on the complex, judgment-heavy aspects that are hard to scale. [00:15:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wep2EQ9Y_Tc&t=915)
- To protect their value, employees must clearly articulate how they increase revenue or reduce costs, and companies should carefully consider whether selling work data truly benefits their long-term performance. [00:18:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wep2EQ9Y_Tc&t=1121)
