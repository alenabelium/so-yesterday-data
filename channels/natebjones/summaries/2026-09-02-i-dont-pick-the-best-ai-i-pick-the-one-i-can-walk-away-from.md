# I Don't Pick The Best AI. I Pick The One I Can Walk Away From.

## Executive Summary

The video analyzes the emerging strategic alliances in the AI industry by examining OpenAI's custom chip development, Nvidia's ecosystem dominance, and Anthropic's multi-vendor strategy. It argues that consumers face significant risks of vendor lock-in and data loss when relying on single providers, particularly following recent corporate acquisitions like SpaceX's purchase of Cursor. To mitigate these risks, the author advocates for a decentralized approach where users maintain control over their own memory and files while distributing their spending across multiple AI models based on specific use cases.

## Key Points

- OpenAI is vertically integrating by developing its own inference chips (Jalapeno) to reduce costs and control the full stack, while Nvidia remains essential for training and general infrastructure, creating a complex dependency between the two camps. [00:03:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9xXnPqVfnM&t=229)
- Anthropic represents a third strategic camp by diversifying its compute suppliers across Amazon, Google, Microsoft, and SpaceX, allowing it to maintain flexibility and avoid the vendor lock-in risks that OpenAI's closed-loop strategy creates for users. [00:08:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9xXnPqVfnM&t=490)
- Users should protect their productivity by keeping memory, code, and instructions in independent systems (like OpenBread) rather than inside specific AI apps, ensuring they can switch providers without losing their accumulated intelligence. [00:11:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9xXnPqVfnM&t=661)
- For heavy users spending over $200 a month, the author insists on outcome-based value from each provider (Anthropic, Codex, Grok), demanding that every subscription pays for itself through time savings and expanded capabilities.
