# Is NVIDIA Building More AI Than Anyone Will Pay For?

## Executive Summary

The video analyzes whether NVIDIA's massive AI infrastructure buildout will be justified by actual demand, given that customers are increasingly switching to cheaper AI models. It highlights data showing slow growth in token spending, which contrasts with the huge capacity commitments made by labs and NVIDIA. The discussion also covers export controls on chips to China, the Microsoft 365 outage, and the importance of preserving engineering culture as AI adoption accelerates. The author concludes that while AI usage will grow, the revenue growth may not match the cost increases, potentially creating a mismatch.

## Key Points

- Customers are increasingly switching to cheaper AI models, which reduces revenue growth for labs despite rising token usage. [00:02:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1aXBg-qQzgA&t=134)
- Google's new Orchestrator feature automatically switches between expensive and cheap models, cutting costs by 24% and improving speed by 19%, exemplifying the trend toward cost-efficient AI use. [00:05:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1aXBg-qQzgA&t=318)
- NVIDIA's Jensen Huang expressed concerns about the avalanche of committed chip costs and the need for solvent customers, highlighting the financial risks of the AI buildout. [00:06:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1aXBg-qQzgA&t=407)
- Export controls on chips to China are tightening, with bipartisan support and new proposals like the Chip Security Act, which could impact NVIDIA's sales. [00:13:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1aXBg-qQzgA&t=783)
- The Microsoft 365 outage and similar incidents underscore the risk of losing engineering expertise as companies push AI adoption, which could undermine reliability and long-term growth. [00:15:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1aXBg-qQzgA&t=940)
