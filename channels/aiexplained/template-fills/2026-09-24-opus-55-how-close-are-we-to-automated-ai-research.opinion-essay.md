## Thesis

The sudden release of Opus 5.5 is a side effect of labs' accelerating internal models, and we are closer than most realize to recursive self-improvement, a race no one wants but no one can stop.

## Argument

The mechanism is the internal model. Anthropic almost certainly has a much more powerful internal model, Fable 5.5, whose answers can be compressed into Opus 5.5. This stronger model generates problems, tasks, and learning environments for the smaller model, and can evaluate its responses to speed up learning. This is not speculation; it's backed by a dozen works. The cost decrease of Opus 5.5 and the prohibition on kernel development in its system card are direct evidence of this internal strength. The labs are essentially using AI to accelerate the creation of the next AI, as shown by the darkening orange chart. Anthropic's own Codebench shows Opus 5.5 can diagnose root causes in 56% of cases, a step toward the 85% needed to replace a researcher. Yet, they admit their internal metrics do not show a sustainable two-fold acceleration, a key threshold in their 2024 Responsible Scaling Policy. But when Meta evaluated them, they found a 30% chance of two-fold acceleration already. Anthropic's commitments have shifted: the strong positive argument only applies when they are leading, and delays only apply when they win the race. This is a race they don't want to be in, as admitted by OpenAI's chief scientist focusing on RSI. The fog is thickening: models are becoming better at predicting the future than super-forecasters, and they are solving problems like the Millennium Prize. We are entering a Tarraco scene, an age of confusion where intelligence outpaces understanding.

## Counterpoints

- Anthropic claims Opus 5.5 remains well below the level needed to replace research scientists and engineers, and that their internal metrics do not show a sustainable two-fold acceleration.
- Some argue that AI successes could be the result of expensive training efforts, but lab insiders see the magical properties directly, while outsiders can always plausibly see them as man-made.
- OpenAI says fully autonomous recursive self-improvement is not happening today and shouldn't be done until it can be done safely, yet their top priority is an automated AI researcher by March 2028.
- A compromise state where extreme capabilities require massive computation could allow for real-time threat detection and unilateral lab commitments to avoid RSI.
- China might catch up, but in a compromise state, they would see labs have closed RSI, buying trust and leading to their own restrained demonstrations.
