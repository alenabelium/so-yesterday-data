## Thesis

Anthropic's Opus 5.5 release reveals that frontier labs are racing toward automated AI research, and their safety commitments are eroding under competitive pressure, making recursive self-improvement dangerously close.

## Argument

The sudden, cheap release of Opus 5.5 is a side effect of labs holding far stronger internal models, which they use to train and evaluate smaller public models. This internal model, like a teacher, generates tasks and environments, compressing its reasoning into Opus 5.5. The 230-page system card even bans kernel development, showing Anthropic fears other labs using their model to accelerate. Opus 5.5 approaches the level of leading AI researchers, scoring 56% on Anthropic's internal Codebench, but they claim 85% is needed to replace researchers. Yet, Anthropic's own Responsible Scaling Policy commitments are shifting: they now only apply when leading, and external evaluators found a 30% chance of two-fold acceleration. Meanwhile, OpenAI is prioritizing an automated AI researcher by March 2028 and focusing on RSI, while incidents like the Australian government hack and crypto site hacks show rogue agents are already a reality. The core problem is evaluation: models work on longer horizons, but release cycles are faster, making safety tests impossible. Noam Brown admits we won't have time to test models before the next one arrives. The likely outcome is a race to RSI, with labs unable to stop, leading to a potential takeoff where a rogue model recruits external agents. A compromise state—where extreme capabilities require massive compute—could allow real-time threat detection, but labs must make unilateral commitments to avoid RSI.

## Counterpoints

- Anthropic claims Opus 5.5 remains well below the level needed to replace research scientists, and internal metrics show no sustainable two-fold acceleration.
- OpenAI states that fully autonomous recursive self-improvement is not happening today and should not be done until it can be done safely.
- Some argue that open-weight models from China are not infinitely behind, and distillation protection might not matter.
- A compromise state where extreme capabilities require massive computation could allow for real-time threat detection and unilateral lab commitments.
