## Thesis

Anthropic's Opus 5.5 release reveals that frontier labs are racing toward automated AI research and recursive self-improvement, with safety commitments weakening as they compete, and we are not prepared for the consequences.

## Argument

The sudden, cheap release of Opus 5.5 is a side effect of labs having far stronger internal models. Anthropic almost certainly has a 'Fable 5.5' that can generate training data, evaluate smaller models, and optimize hardware, making training cheaper and faster. This is why we get a top-tier model so soon after 5.1, and why Anthropic prohibits kernel development in its system card—they don't want competitors using their model for the same acceleration. [00:04:05] [00:05:20]

Opus 5.5 is approaching the level of leading AI researchers. On Anthropic's internal Codebench, it can diagnose root causes in 56% of real-world R&D tasks, up from 5.1, but they say you need 85% to replace a researcher. That's less than 30 points away. Yet Anthropic claims it doesn't double their development speed, which is their own Level 5 threshold from their 2024 Responsible Scaling Policy. But when Meta evaluated them, they found a 30% chance of two-fold acceleration already. Anthropic admits 'some metrics have changed,' and now their strong positive argument only applies when they're leading. [00:06:33] [00:07:33] [00:08:34]

Meanwhile, OpenAI is already doing automated AI research. Their chief scientist says they're focusing on RSI, and they want an automated AI researcher by March 2028. They even released a set of AI rules that say labs must be responsible for automated AI research, but what does liability look like when a superintelligent AI hacks a hospital? The labs are in a race they don't want to be in, but they can't stop. [00:11:04] [00:12:12]
