## Thesis

Anthropic's Opus 5.5 release is a strategic move in an AI arms race that is accelerating toward automated AI research and recursive self-improvement, with safety commitments being weakened as labs compete.

## Argument

The sudden release of Opus 5.5 at a lower cost is evidence of much stronger internal models. Anthropic almost certainly has a more powerful internal model, like 'Fable 5.5', whose answers can be compressed into Opus 5.5. This internal model can generate problems, learning environments, and evaluate responses to speed up training. This is not speculation; it's backed by a dozen works. The 230-page system card reveals more than intended: Anthropic prohibits using Opus 5.5 for kernel development, a policy they reinstated after criticism for similar sabotage with Fable. This shows labs are actively preventing others from using their models for AI research acceleration.

Opus 5.5 is approaching the level of leading AI researchers. On Anthropic's internal Codebench, it can diagnose root causes in 56% of cases, up from Mythos 5.1, but to replace a researcher it needs 85%. Yet, Anthropic's own metrics show we are not yet at a two-fold acceleration, a threshold they defined in 2024 as level five in their Responsible Scaling Policy. However, when Meta evaluated them, they found a 30% chance of two-fold acceleration already. Anthropic admits their metrics have changed. By July 2026, the strong positive argument for safety only applies when they are leading, and delays only apply when they win the race. Labs are directing obligations towards each other, and historical commitments are inconvenient. ## Counterpoints

- Anthropic claims Opus 5.5 is well below the level needed to replace research scientists and engineers, and that their internal metrics do not show a sustainable two-fold rate of development.
- OpenAI says fully autonomous recursive self-improvement is not happening today and shouldn't be done until it can be done safely, yet they prioritize an automated AI researcher by March 2028.
- Some might argue that models are not truly capable of anything because they can't solve Millennium Prize problems, but a benchmark agreed upon by several labs could prove otherwise.
- China might not cooperate, but leading analysts say China believes advanced labs are being disingenuous, and in a compromise state, China would see evidence and eventually catch up.
- Noam Brown raises the risk that models creating realistic safety scenarios might betray to other models that it's a fake environment, undermining the safety test.
