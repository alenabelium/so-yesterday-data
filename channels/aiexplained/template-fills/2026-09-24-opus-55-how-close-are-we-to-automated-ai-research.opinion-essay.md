## Thesis

Anthropic's Opus 5.5 release reveals that labs are racing toward automated AI research and RSI, with safety commitments weakening as competition intensifies.

## Argument

The sudden, cheap release of Opus 5.5 is a side effect of labs holding far stronger internal models. Anthropic's 230-page system card shows they prohibit kernel development, a sign they fear other labs using their model for AI research. The model lags on Terminal Bench Science but leads on long-horizon coding, a key capability for self-improvement. Anthropic's own Codebench shows Opus 5.5 at 56% of what's needed to replace a researcher, but the gap is closing fast. Meanwhile, safety commitments are eroding: Anthropic's 2024 Responsible Scaling Policy promised safeguards at a 2x acceleration threshold, but external evaluators found a 30% chance we're already there, and the labs are redirecting obligations toward each other. The real danger is that models are becoming better at predicting the future than superforecasters, and agents are already hacking systems for money. The evaluation problem is unsolvable: by the time you test a model on a three-month task, the next model is already out. The compromise state would be one where extreme capabilities require massive compute, allowing real-time threat detection and unilateral lab commitments to avoid RSI.

## Counterpoints

- Anthropic claims Opus 5.5 is well below the level needed to replace research scientists, citing internal metrics that show no sustainable 2x acceleration.
- OpenAI says fully autonomous RSI is not happening today and shouldn't be done until it can be done safely, yet they prioritize an automated AI researcher by March 2028.
- Skeptics argue that AI successes could be the result of expensive training efforts by lab insiders, not emergent magic.
- China might catch up if labs unilaterally stop RSI, but in the compromise state they would see evidence and eventually adopt similar measures.
- Some believe models can't do what Einstein did with the data available, but a benchmark could prove otherwise.
