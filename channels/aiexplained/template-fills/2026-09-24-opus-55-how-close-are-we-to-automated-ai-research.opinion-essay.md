## Thesis

The sudden release of Opus 5.5 reveals that frontier labs are racing toward recursive self-improvement, and we have no reliable safety mechanism to stop them.

## Argument

The frequency and low cost of Opus 5.5's release are themselves evidence of much stronger internal models. Anthropic almost certainly has a more powerful 'Fable 5.5' that generates tasks, evaluates responses, and performs core and systems engineering to make training and inference cheaper. This is why they prohibit kernel development in the system card—they don't want other labs using their model to do the same. The gap to replacing researchers is closing: Opus 5.5 scores 56% on Codebench, but Anthropic says 85% is needed to replace their staff. Yet Anthropic's own Responsible Scaling Policy commitments have shifted: the 'strong positive argument' and 'delays' now only apply when they are leading. OpenAI is already focusing on RSI, with a chief scientist saying 'We are focusing OpenAI's research on RSI,' and they've set a goal for an automated AI researcher by March 2028. The real problem is that we have no mechanism to test these models: Noam Brown admits that by the time we test a model on multi-month tasks, the next model is already out. We're entering a period where models can work for three months, but the release cycle is two months—so we can't evaluate them to their full potential. This is a product issue and a safety issue. The labs are in a race they don't want to be in, but they can't stop. The compromise state would be one where extreme capabilities require massive computation, allowing for real-time threat detection and unilateral commitments to avoid RSI.

## Counterpoints

- Anthropic claims Opus 5.5 remains well below the level needed to replace research scientists and engineers, and that their internal metrics do not show a sustainable AI attribute that is twice the rate of development.
- Some argue that models like Opus 5.5 are not yet capable of autonomous AI research, and that we still need to hold the models' hands for them to be useful.
- OpenAI says 'Fully autonomous recursive self-improvement is not happening today' and that we shouldn't be doing it until it can be done safely.
- There is a belief that China will catch up if labs unilaterally commit to not pursuing RSI, but the host argues that China works on evidence and would eventually reach the same compromise state.
