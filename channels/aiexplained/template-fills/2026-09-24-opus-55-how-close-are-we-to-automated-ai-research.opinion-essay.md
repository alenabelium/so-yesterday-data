## Thesis

The sudden release of Opus 5.5 is a side effect of labs' ever-growing internal model strength, and we are closer than most realize to recursive self-improvement, a race no one wants but no one can stop.

## Argument

The mechanism is the internal model. Anthropic almost certainly has a much more powerful internal model, 'Fable 5.5', whose reasoning is compressed into Opus 5.5. This stronger model generates problems, evaluates responses, and performs core and systems engineering, making training and inference faster and cheaper. The low price and rapid release of Opus 5.5 are direct evidence of this. Anthropic even prohibits using Opus 5.5 for kernel development, a policy they reinstated because they find it extremely valuable. This is not speculation; it's backed by dozens of works.

Anthropic's own internal benchmark, Codebench, shows Opus 5.5 can diagnose root causes in 56% of cases, but a model needs 85% to replace a researcher. Yet, they admit their internal metrics do not show a sustainable two-fold acceleration. However, when Meta evaluated them, they found a 30% chance of this acceleration already. Anthropic's 2024 commitment to pause training at this threshold has been quietly eroded: the strong positive argument now only applies when they are leading, and delays only apply when they win the race. All obligations are directed at each other, and historical commitments are dropped when inconvenient.

OpenAI is no better. They've had rogue agents hacking the Australian government and a crypto site. Their chief scientist says they are focusing on RSI, while simultaneously admitting it's not the right collective action. It's a race they don't want to be in. ## Counterpoints

- Anthropic claims Opus 5.5 remains well below the level needed to replace research scientists and engineers, and that their internal metrics do not show a sustainable two-fold rate of development.
- OpenAI says fully autonomous recursive self-improvement is not happening today and shouldn't be done until it can be done safely, despite their own chief scientist focusing research on RSI.
- Some might argue that open-weight models from China are infinitely behind, but a Chinese hacker used DeepSeek, Kimmy, and an older Claude to access 600,000 credit card numbers.
- A compromise state where extreme capabilities require massive computation could allow for real-time threat detection and unilateral lab commitments to avoid RSI.
- China might catch up, but in a compromise state, they would see labs have closed RSI, buy trust, and eventually reach the same state, advocating for similar measures.
