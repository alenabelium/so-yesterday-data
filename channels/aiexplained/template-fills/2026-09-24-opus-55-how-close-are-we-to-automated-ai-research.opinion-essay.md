## Thesis

Anthropic's Opus 5.5 release is a strategic move in an accelerating AI arms race, revealing that labs are closer than admitted to automated AI research and recursive self-improvement, a race they cannot safely win.

## Argument

The speed and low cost of Opus 5.5, arriving so soon after Fable 5.1, are direct evidence of far stronger internal models. Anthropic almost certainly has a 'Fable 5.5' that generates training data, evaluates responses, and performs core systems engineering, making all their models cheaper and faster. This is why Opus 5.5 is cheap and why Anthropic prohibits its use for kernel development—they don't want competitors using their model to accelerate their own research.

Anthropic's own benchmarks show the gap is closing: Opus 5.5 scores 56% on their internal Codebench, but they claim a researcher replacement needs 85%. Yet their safety commitments are shifting. The 2024 Responsible Scaling Policy promised safeguards if AI doubled development speed, but by July 2026, those promises only apply when they're leading. External evaluators found a 30% chance of that doubling already happening. Labs are directing obligations at each other, and historical commitments are dropped when inconvenient.

Meanwhile, OpenAI is openly prioritizing RSI, with their chief scientist saying they're focusing on it. They've set a goal for an automated AI researcher by March 2028. We're seeing models hack systems (the Australian government, crypto sites), and we have no reliable way to evaluate long-horizon capabilities. As Noam Brown notes, if models can work for three months but release cycles are two months, we can't test them before the next model ships. We're entering a period of 'turbulence' where intelligence outpaces understanding, and no one is in charge.

## Counterpoints

- Anthropic claims Opus 5.5 is well below the level needed to replace research scientists and engineers, and that their internal metrics do not show a sustainable doubling of development rate.
- OpenAI states that fully autonomous recursive self-improvement is not happening today and shouldn't be done until it can be done safely.
- Some might argue that open-weight models from China are infinitely behind, but a Chinese hacker used DeepSeek, Kimmy, and an older Claude to steal 600,000 credit card numbers.
- A compromise state where extreme AI capabilities require massive computation could allow for real-time threat detection and unilateral lab commitments to avoid RSI.
- Some might question whether models can solve Millennium Prize problems, but OpenAI was internally stunned that their models solved one so quickly.
