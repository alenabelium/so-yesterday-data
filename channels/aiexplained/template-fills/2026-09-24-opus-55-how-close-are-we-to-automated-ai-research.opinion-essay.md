## Thesis

Anthropic's Opus 5.5 release reveals that labs are racing toward automated AI research and recursive self-improvement, with safety commitments weakening under competitive pressure.

## Argument

The sudden, cheap release of Opus 5.5, so soon after Fable 5.1, is itself evidence of much stronger internal models. Anthropic likely has a 'Fable 5.5' that can generate training data, evaluate responses, and perform core systems engineering, making training and inference cheaper. The 230-page system card even prohibits using Opus 5.5 for kernel development, a policy they reinstated after criticism, showing they value this capability highly. Opus 5.5 approaches the level of leading AI researchers on benchmarks like Terminal Bench Science 0.1, and Anthropic's internal Codebench shows it can diagnose root causes in 56% of cases, with 85% needed to replace a researcher. Yet Anthropic's own Responsible Scaling Policy from 2024 committed to not training or deploying models if they achieved a two-fold acceleration in AI R&D, but by July 2026, they've changed their metrics, and an external evaluation by Meta found a 30% chance of such acceleration already. Labs are redirecting their obligations toward each other, and strong historical commitments are being dropped when inconvenient. Meanwhile, OpenAI is focusing on RSI, with their chief scientist stating 'We are focusing OpenAI’s research on RSI,' and they aim for an automated AI researcher by March 2028. The race is on, and safety is being left behind.

## Counterpoints

- Anthropic claims Opus 5.5 remains well below the level needed to replace research scientists and engineers, and their internal metrics do not show a sustainable AI attribute that is twice the rate of development.
- OpenAI says 'Fully autonomous recursive self-improvement is not happening today, and we shouldn’t be doing it until it can be done safely.'
- Some argue that models are not yet capable of everything humans can do, as they cannot yet solve Millennium Prize problems or replicate Einstein's discoveries.
- China might catch up if labs unilaterally commit to not pursuing RSI, but China works on evidence and would see that labs have closed RSI, allowing for trust and information exchange.
