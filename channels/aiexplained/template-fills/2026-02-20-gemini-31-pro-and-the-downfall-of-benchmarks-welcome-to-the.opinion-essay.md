## Thesis

Traditional AI benchmarks are collapsing because post-training specialization creates domain-specific 'Vibe Era' performance, making universal leaderboards obsolete.

## Argument

The shift from pre-training dominance to post-training optimization means models like Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 4.6 excel in specific domains while lagging in others, rendering direct comparisons misleading. Benchmarks no longer measure general intelligence but rather how well a model has been tuned for a specific task's shortcuts or data distribution. For instance, Gemini 3.1 Pro’s high score on ARC-AGI-2 may rely on spurious arithmetic patterns in color encoding rather than true reasoning, while its failure on GDP Val highlights domain gaps that generalist metrics previously masked. The host argues that we are entering an era where 'vibe'—the subjective feel of performance in a specific context—matters more than aggregate scores. Furthermore, the industry is pivoting toward longer context windows and specialized reinforcement learning to handle this fragmentation, as seen in Anthropic’s bet that specializing in enough niches will eventually generalize across them. This renders old universal benchmarks obsolete, replaced by a chaotic landscape of domain-specific optimizations where a model can be state-of-the-art in coding but mediocre in factual accuracy.

## Counterpoints

- Models like Gemini 3.1 Pro still hit record Elo in live competitive coding, proving that some domains see genuine, measurable exponential improvement.
- Anthropic’s Dario Amodei argues that specializing in many narrow RL environments is a valid path to generalization, reducing the need for continual learning on user data.
- Forecasting benchmarks like Metaculus show models approaching human-level predictive accuracy, suggesting some universal intelligence metrics still hold value.
