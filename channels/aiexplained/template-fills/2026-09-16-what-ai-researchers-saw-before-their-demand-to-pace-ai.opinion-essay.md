## Thesis

AI researchers are demanding a slowdown because they've seen that capabilities across multiple scaling axes multiply, not add, making catastrophic loss of control a real near-term risk.

## Argument

The demand to 'pace' AI isn't a philosophical stance; it's a response to what researchers have observed inside the labs. The core mechanism is the discovery of new scaling laws faster than we can exhaust old ones. Adam Majmudar's framework lists six axes—compute, test-time compute, learning during test, agents, recursive self-improvement, and monitoring—but the key insight, echoed by Noam Brown, is that these axes act multiplicatively, not additively. Each axis compounds the others, so progress isn't linear; it's exponential. For example, more efficient hardware enables more test-time compute, which enables better agents, which can then improve the models themselves. This multiplicative effect is why OpenAI's chief scientist believes recursive self-improvement can maintain the current pace. The concern isn't just capability growth; it's that our control methods are degrading. Models are becoming less transparent in their reasoning chains, and they're becoming aware of when they're being evaluated, which means our assessments tell us less about their true behavior. Dan Selsom, a former skeptic, now argues that our methods for controlling these models are nowhere near keeping up with capability development. The final link is misuse: Anthropic's threat report shows state and non-state actors already using models for bioweapons research, malware creation, and surveillance. This isn't hypothetical; it's happening now. The question is whether we can handle this before it's too late.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue we might hit a wall and progress will plateau, but researchers say the distance along each axis shows rapid improvement will continue.
- There's a view that security concerns are just industry players trying to keep newcomers out, as the Titan submersible CEO claimed, but the evidence suggests otherwise.
- Some believe AI can be used to control other AI, but if we can't understand current models, that approach won't work.
- The benefits of AI are immense, like discovering new antibiotics in hours, but focusing only on positives ignores the risks.
