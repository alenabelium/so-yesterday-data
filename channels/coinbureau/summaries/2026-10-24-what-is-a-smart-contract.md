# What Is a Smart Contract?

## Executive Summary

This video from Coin Bureau explains what smart contracts are, how they work, and why they are foundational to the crypto ecosystem. It traces the concept back to Nick Szabo's 1994 idea, illustrates the mechanics with a simple exchange example, and highlights their role in DeFi, stablecoins, NFTs, and tokenized assets. The video also addresses limitations such as code vulnerabilities and reliance on oracles, concluding that smart contracts are transforming finance by removing intermediaries and enabling trustless transactions.

## Key Points

- Smart contracts are programs on the blockchain that automatically execute transactions when conditions are met, replacing traditional intermediaries like banks and lawyers. [00:01:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l21SOJR5Mbk&t=72)
- The concept was coined by Nick Szabo in 1994, who compared smart contracts to vending machines; Ethereum, launched in 2015, made them practical. [00:02:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l21SOJR5Mbk&t=144)
- Smart contracts are not intelligent or legal contracts by themselves; they are immutable code that runs on the blockchain, and their execution involves five steps: write, deploy, block, launch, and agree. [00:03:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l21SOJR5Mbk&t=229)
- Smart contracts power DeFi, stablecoins, NFTs, DAOs, and tokenized real-world assets, with hundreds of billions of dollars locked in them. [00:08:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l21SOJR5Mbk&t=537)
- Limitations include code vulnerabilities (e.g., The DAO hack) and dependence on oracles for external data, which can be manipulated. [00:12:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l21SOJR5Mbk&t=724)
