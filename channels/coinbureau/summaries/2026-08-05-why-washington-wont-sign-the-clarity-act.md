# Why Washington Won't Sign the Clarity Act

## Executive Summary

The Clarity Act, a major U.S. crypto regulatory bill, is stalled not by Wall Street or Senate gridlock but by a single ethics provision sent to the White House on July 30, which would prohibit federal officials and their spouses from issuing digital assets. The White House has remained silent, likely due to President Trump's family earning an estimated $1.4 billion from crypto in 2025, creating a direct conflict of interest. Despite public support from major financial institutions and pressure from Treasury, the bill's passage odds have dropped sharply, and the industry faces a regulatory risk premium. The video argues that the real obstacle is this conflict of interest, and while clarity will eventually come, the timing remains uncertain.

## Key Points

- The Clarity Act's ethics section, sent to the White House on July 30, would ban federal officials and spouses from issuing digital assets, but the White House has not responded. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blget2AiT-4&t=0)
- President Trump's 2025 crypto income is estimated at $1.4 billion, including $636 million from his memecoin, creating a direct conflict with the bill's ethics provision. [02:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blget2AiT-4&t=178)
- Wall Street and community banks are not blocking the bill; they support it with minor amendments, while the real hold-up is the White House's refusal to agree to the ethics clause. [06:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blget2AiT-4&t=384)
- The delay is costing the industry, with Bitcoin flat and Coinbase and Circle stocks falling, while crypto super PACs are preparing to punish politicians in the November midterms. [09:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blget2AiT-4&t=542)
