# What's Actually Replacing the US Dollar?

## Executive Summary

The video argues that the US dollar is not collapsing but undergoing a gradual transformation, with its four distinct roles—pricing commodities, settling trade, serving as a reserve asset, and acting as global collateral—being eroded or expanded in different ways. Central banks are diversifying into gold following the freezing of Russian reserves, while alternative payment systems like China's CIPS and mBridge are fragmenting the settlement infrastructure. Meanwhile, stablecoins are paradoxically expanding the dollar's reach, especially in emerging markets, even as Bitcoin emerges as a hedge against the credibility of dollar issuers. The dollar's dominance persists due to the stickiness of dollar-denominated debt and the lack of a viable alternative, but its role is being carved up piece by piece.

## Key Points

- The dollar serves four distinct functions—pricing commodities, settling trade, serving as a reserve asset, and acting as global collateral—each with its own exit door, and no single currency is replacing it; instead, its roles are being carved up. [00:00:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sEgOXMfJTLU&t=50)
- Central banks have dramatically increased gold purchases since the 2022 freezing of Russian reserves, with annual buying jumping from 400-500 tons to over 1,000 tons, signaling a shift away from dollar custody. [00:03:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sEgOXMfJTLU&t=203)
- Stablecoins, particularly USD-pegged ones, are expanding the dollar's reach in emerging markets, with monthly settlement volumes surpassing the US ACH network and Tether becoming a major holder of US Treasuries, effectively financing the US deficit.
