# Europe's War On Tether Is About To Get MUCH WORSE

## Executive Summary

The video analyzes Europe's regulatory crackdown on Tether under MiCA, explaining how the EU has effectively pushed licensed exchanges and financial firms to stop offering USDT without banning it outright. It details Tether's refusal to comply with reserve custody rules, the market shift toward bank-issued stablecoins like USDC and EURC, and how similar regulatory templates are being adopted globally. The video argues that the real battle is over who gets to issue digital dollars and control the on-ramps, not consumer protection. It concludes that while the regulated system is tightening, permissionless crypto infrastructure remains beyond government control.

## Key Points

- MiCA's e-money token rules and the July 2026 grandfathering deadline forced exchanges like Coinbase, Crypto.com, Binance, and Kraken to phase out USDT, with Revolut auto-converting remaining balances by August 31. [00:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yh-tTK7Rulg&t=58)
- Tether refused MiCA's reserve custody mandate, arguing that bank deposits are riskier than short-dated U.S. Treasuries, citing Circle's USDC depeg during the Silicon Valley Bank collapse as evidence. [02:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yh-tTK7Rulg&t=146)
- Circle became the main beneficiary of the regulation, securing an EMI license in France and seeing its USDC and EURC dominate the regulated European stablecoin market, while Tether invested in compliant issuers like Quantoz and Stabler as a workaround. [04:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yh-tTK7Rulg&t=298)
- The EU's phase two AML package will require identity verification for transfers over 1,000 euros to self-hosted wallets and ban privacy coins like Monero and Zcash by July 2027, extending the regulatory reach to individual wallets. [10:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yh-tTK7Rulg&t=647)
- The regulatory template is being replicated globally—through the GENIUS Act in the U.S., FCA rules in the UK, and licensing regimes in Hong Kong, Singapore, Japan, and Canada—shifting stablecoin issuance toward traditional financial institutions. [09:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yh-tTK7Rulg&t=586)
