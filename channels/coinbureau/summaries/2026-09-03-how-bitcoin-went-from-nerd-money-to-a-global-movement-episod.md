# How Bitcoin Went From Nerd Money To A GLOBAL Movement (Episode 2)

## Executive Summary

This episode of Coin Bureau's Bitcoin series chronicles Bitcoin's transition from a niche experiment among cryptography enthusiasts to a global financial phenomenon. It highlights key milestones such as the first Bitcoin transaction, the first fiat exchange, the famous pizza purchase, and the rise of Silk Road, which brought both notoriety and mainstream attention. The narrative also covers the emergence of exchanges like Mt. Gox, the first major boom-and-bust cycles, and the initial regulatory responses from the US government. Ultimately, the episode argues that despite its chaotic early years and repeated predictions of its demise, Bitcoin survived and began to attract serious investment and institutional interest by the end of 2013.

## Key Points

- Satoshi Nakamoto's last public post was in December 2010, and by April 2011 he had handed the project to Gavin Andresen and disappeared, leaving over 1 million BTC untouched. [00:03:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18Za5qNnWoc&t=197)
- The first known purchase of a real-world item with Bitcoin occurred on May 22, 2010, when Laszlo Hanyecz paid 10,000 BTC for two Papa John's pizzas, a transaction now celebrated as Bitcoin Pizza Day. [00:07:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18Za5qNnWoc&t=470)
- Silk Road, launched in early 2011, used Bitcoin for illegal transactions and brought Bitcoin into the public spotlight, causing its price to triple to around $27 within a week of a Gawker article, and leading to FBI shutdown in October 2013. [00:13:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18Za5qNnWoc&t=790)
- Mt. Gox, originally a Magic: The Gathering card exchange, became the dominant Bitcoin exchange by mid-2011, handling about 90% of transactions, but suffered a major security breach in June 2011 that exposed the risks of centralized services. [00:16:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18Za5qNnWoc&t=983)
- Bitcoin experienced its first major boom-and-bust cycles, reaching $1,000 for the first time in November 2013, and despite being declared dead 475 times, it continued to attract investment, including a $25 million funding round for Coinbase in December 2013. [00:22:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18Za5qNnWoc&t=1327)
