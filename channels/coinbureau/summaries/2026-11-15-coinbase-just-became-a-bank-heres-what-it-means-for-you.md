# Coinbase Just Became a BANK (Here's What It Means For You)

## Executive Summary

Coinbase received a federal banking charter as a National Trust Bank, allowing it to custody digital assets under federal oversight, a significant shift from its past regulatory battles. The charter does not make Coinbase a traditional bank—it cannot take deposits or make loans—but it positions the company to potentially serve as a trustee for retirement accounts and solidifies its role as the dominant custodian for US spot Bitcoin ETFs. The banking lobby has sued to block this, arguing it's a backdoor for crypto firms, but the OCC approved it anyway. This development marks a major step in integrating cryptocurrency into the traditional financial system.

## Key Points

- Coinbase received a National Trust Bank charter from the OCC, which allows it to custody digital assets as a fiduciary, but it cannot take deposits, make loans, or offer FDIC insurance. [00:00:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uns8CWWtxBI&t=56)
- The charter shifts Coinbase's custody business, which holds Bitcoin for over 80% of US spot Bitcoin ETFs, from state (New York) to federal oversight, a key reason the banking lobby is suing the OCC. [00:06:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uns8CWWtxBI&t=368)
- The federal charter could allow Coinbase to act as a trustee for retirement accounts like IRAs and 401(k)s, opening the door to crypto in pension plans, though no products have been announced yet. [00:07:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uns8CWWtxBI&t=423)
