# Global Markets Are On LIFE SUPPORT

## Executive Summary

The video argues that the world's four largest markets—Japan, South Korea, the US, and China—are being artificially propped up by governments using extraordinary measures such as pension funds, regulatory changes, and direct state buying. These interventions, once considered emergency tools, have become permanent fixtures, creating a dangerous dependency where markets rely on state support. The video warns that this support is fueled by confidence, which can collapse, and that the ultimate cost falls on ordinary people's pensions and savings.

## Key Points

- Japan is using its $2.1 trillion GPIF pension fund to stabilize bond yields and the yen, with the finance minister's verbal cues alone moving markets. [00:03:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A60P7_AMOkM&t=186)
- South Korea has turned its National Pension Service into a currency tool, using currency swaps and forward hedging to defend the won, which has recovered to its highest level in two years. [00:06:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A60P7_AMOkM&t=412)
- The US has relaxed the supplementary leverage ratio, resumed Treasury purchases, and had the Treasury buy back its own long-term debt, effectively acting as the buyer of last resort. [00:10:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A60P7_AMOkM&t=615)
- China's 'national team' uses ETFs and state-linked investors to set both a floor and a ceiling on stock prices, keeping the Shanghai Composite nearly flat over the past year. [00:12:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A60P7_AMOkM&t=755)
- Emergency measures are becoming permanent, and the risk is shifting from conscious market participants to ordinary people's pensions and savings, as seen with Japan's GPIF and Korea's NPS. [00:16:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A60P7_AMOkM&t=974)
