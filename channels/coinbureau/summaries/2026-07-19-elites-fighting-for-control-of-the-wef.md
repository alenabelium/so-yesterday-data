# Elites Fighting For CONTROL of the WEF

## Executive Summary

The World Economic Forum is in a leadership crisis after losing both its founder Klaus Schwab and CEO Börge Brende within ten months, amid scandals involving expenses and ties to Jeffrey Epstein. With no permanent leaders, interim co-chairs from BlackRock and other financial giants are consolidating control, shrinking the board and concentrating power. The video argues that this shift toward a smaller, more corporate-dominated Davos threatens democratic accountability, as decisions made there increasingly affect ordinary people's finances and pensions.

## Key Points

- The WEF lost its founder Klaus Schwab and CEO Börge Brende within 10 months, leaving the institution without permanent leadership. [00:03:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkRQlSdCYOk&t=226)
- BlackRock CEO Larry Fink and André Hoffmann have been appointed interim co-chairs, with BlackRock managing over $14 trillion in assets, making them the most powerful figures in Davos. [00:05:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkRQlSdCYOk&t=319)
- A faction is pushing to shrink the WEF board from 28 members to a smaller, more business-dominated group, reducing oversight and increasing the influence of financial elites. [00:06:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkRQlSdCYOk&t=383)
- Klaus Schwab is fighting to install ECB President Christine Lagarde as his successor, but the interim leadership resists, leading to a factional power struggle. [00:10:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkRQlSdCYOk&t=630)
- The video warns that decisions made in this shrinking room affect ordinary people's pensions and savings, as BlackRock and central bankers shape policies that benefit themselves. [00:12:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkRQlSdCYOk&t=767)
