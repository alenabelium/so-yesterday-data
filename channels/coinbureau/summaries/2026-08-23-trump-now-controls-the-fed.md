# Trump Now CONTROLS The Fed

## Executive Summary

The video argues that the Federal Reserve's credibility is eroding under new chair Kevin Worsh, who was appointed by President Trump amid unprecedented political entanglement. Despite Worsh's hawkish stance, markets doubt his independence, leading to rising long-term Treasury yields and a shift in global capital flows. The analysis highlights Japan's record selling of US debt, coordinated currency interventions, and the potential expansion of the FIMA repo facility as signs of stress. The video concludes that this loss of Fed independence could benefit assets like gold and Bitcoin, which are not subject to political control.

## Key Points

- Kevin Worsh was confirmed as Fed chair by a narrow 54-45 Senate vote, with the swearing-in held at the White House, the first since 1987, raising immediate concerns about independence. [00:01:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LVwo-qduqcQ&t=62)
- Despite Worsh's hawkish stance—holding rates steady for five consecutive meetings and facing dissents for hikes—the 30-year Treasury yield rose above 5.2%, a 19-year high, signaling market distrust. [00:02:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LVwo-qduqcQ&t=138)
- Japan, the largest foreign holder of US debt, sold a record $66.7 billion in May 2026, and the US and Japan conducted a coordinated yen-buying intervention, with the Treasury pressuring the Fed to expand the FIMA repo facility to prevent future bond dumping. [00:07:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LVwo-qduqcQ&t=436)
- The loss of Fed credibility is being priced into markets, and if long-term yields keep rising, assets like gold and Bitcoin—which have no central bank chairman—could benefit as hedges against political control of monetary policy. [00:11:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LVwo-qduqcQ&t=673)
