# Is Michael Saylor EXITING Bitcoin?

## Executive Summary

Michael Saylor's Strategy is selling Bitcoin to meet fixed cash obligations from perpetual preferred securities, not because of a loss of conviction. The company's funding machine, which relied on issuing stock at a premium to net asset value, has broken down as MNAV has collapsed, forcing it to sell Bitcoin to pay dividends and buy back preferred shares. This structural flaw is widespread among digital asset treasury companies, many of which now trade below their Bitcoin holdings. While Strategy is not facing bankruptcy, the entire treasury company model is under stress, and the risk of index exclusion could trigger forced selling.

## Key Points

- Strategy's preferred dividend bill has grown eightfold in a year, reaching $400 million in Q2, far exceeding its $122 million software revenue, forcing Bitcoin sales to cover obligations. [00:03:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EfTQ6AZdNUE&t=200)
- The company's MNAV premium has collapsed from 2x to 0.68, making stock issuance value-destructive and leaving Bitcoin as the only source of funds for preferred dividends and buybacks. [00:05:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EfTQ6AZdNUE&t=304)
- Strategy sold 1,638 BTC in a week, splitting proceeds between preferred dividends and defending the Stretch preferred price, while also issuing equity—a pattern indicating the treasury is being used as a working capital account. [00:07:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EfTQ6AZdNUE&t=456)
- The broader treasury company category is suffering, with a median 43% decline in treasury stocks and several companies liquidating or trading below NAV, highlighting a universal design flaw. [00:08:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EfTQ6AZdNUE&t=521)
- Strategy is not bankrupt, with a $4 billion dollar reserve covering two years of obligations, but the risk of MSCI index exclusion could trigger forced selling from index funds. [00:10:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EfTQ6AZdNUE&t=643)
