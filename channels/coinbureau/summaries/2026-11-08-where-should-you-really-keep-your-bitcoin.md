# Where Should You REALLY Keep Your Bitcoin.

## Executive Summary

This video from Coin Bureau examines the trade-offs between storing cryptocurrency on centralized exchanges versus self-custody, highlighting the risks of each approach. It discusses exchange failures, proof of reserves limitations, and real-world hacks like the Binance and Bybit incidents, as well as common self-custody mistakes such as seed phrase mismanagement and address poisoning. The video concludes by recommending a tiered storage strategy—hot, cold, and custodial—to balance convenience, security, and personal risk tolerance.

## Key Points

- Centralized exchanges offer convenience but expose users to counterparty risk, as seen in the Binance hack where $6.3 billion was stolen, and the company's ability to cover losses depended on its own funds. [00:02:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OhRh81S7B2g&t=148)
- Proof of Reserves provides only a snapshot in time and does not cover off-balance-sheet debts, making it insufficient to fully verify an exchange's solvency. [00:04:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OhRh81S7B2g&t=285)
- Self-custody eliminates company risk but introduces user error risks, such as seed phrase loss, address poisoning, and blind signing, which have led to billions in losses. [00:07:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OhRh81S7B2g&t=439)
- Even cold storage is not foolproof, as demonstrated by the Bybit hack where attackers compromised the signing interface, not the keys themselves. [00:08:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OhRh81S7B2g&t=515)
- A balanced approach involves splitting funds across hot wallets for daily use, cold storage for long-term reserves, and custodial options like ETFs for passive ownership. [00:12:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OhRh81S7B2g&t=727)
