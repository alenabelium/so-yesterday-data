# Revolut Just Proved KYC Is DANGEROUS

## Executive Summary

This video analyzes a sophisticated attack on Revolut where criminals compromised an Italian government email account to send fraudulent lawful requests, leading to the exposure of KYC data for 680 crypto-rich individuals. The incident highlights how KYC compliance systems, designed to prioritize speed and data retention under EU regulations, create dangerous vulnerabilities. The video argues that KYC files are now the most dangerous documents attached to a person's name, as they combine identity, financial, and location data, and that regulatory frameworks like AML and GDPR are contradictory, making data minimization effectively illegal. It concludes that self-custody and minimizing data held by platforms are essential for protection.

## Key Points

- A criminal gained control of an Italian government email account and sent fraudulent lawful requests to Revolut's compliance desk, which complied for six months, leaking passport scans, selfies, addresses, bank statements, and Bitcoin transaction histories for 680 people. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2dhf7QxxrI&t=0)
- The attack targeted individuals identified on-chain first, then used KYC data to attach names, faces, and addresses, leading to physical threats like home invasions and extortion, with victims like Mark Karpelès moving his family to a hotel. [00:04:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2dhf7QxxrI&t=249)
- EU regulations (AML, travel rule, DAC8) mandate storing KYC data for up to a decade and sharing it with authorities, contradicting GDPR's data minimization principle, making breaches like this a design flaw rather than just a company failure. [00:09:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2dhf7QxxrI&t=572)
