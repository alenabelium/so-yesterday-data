# $100M DRAINED From Crypto's Safest Wallet!

## Executive Summary

A critical vulnerability in Coldcard hardware wallets allowed attackers to drain over $100 million in Bitcoin by exploiting a flawed random number generator in the firmware. The bug, introduced in 2021, reduced seed phrase entropy to as low as 40 bits, making keys guessable. Users who added their own randomness, used passphrases, or multi-signature were unaffected. The incident underscores the importance of user-added security measures and the broader risk of weak randomness in crypto.

## Key Points

- Attackers exploited a 5-year-old firmware bug in Coldcard wallets, draining over $100 million in Bitcoin from thousands of wallets in automated attacks. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSonLY4u0u4&t=0)
- The vulnerability stemmed from a single line of code that incorrectly checked for the presence of a hardware RNG setting, causing the firmware to use a predictable software generator. [00:03:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSonLY4u0u4&t=199)
- Users who used dice rolls, a strong BIP39 passphrase, or multi-signature were safe, highlighting the importance of adding extra security layers beyond default settings. [00:07:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSonLY4u0u4&t=476)
- The incident is part of a broader pattern of randomness failures in crypto, including MilkSad, Profanity, and Trust Wallet bugs, emphasizing that weak randomness is a systemic risk. [00:11:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSonLY4u0u4&t=664)
- Immediate steps for affected users include generating a new wallet on updated firmware, transferring funds, and never reusing the old seed phrase. [00:13:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSonLY4u0u4&t=796)
