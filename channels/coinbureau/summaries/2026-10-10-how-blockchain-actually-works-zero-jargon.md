# How Blockchain ACTUALLY Works (Zero Jargon)

## Executive Summary

This video from Coin Bureau explains blockchain technology in simple, jargon-free terms, using the analogy of a village notebook to illustrate a shared, immutable ledger. It covers the problem of double-spending, how blocks and hashes create a tamper-evident chain, and the consensus mechanisms (proof-of-work and proof-of-stake) that allow strangers to agree on the state of the ledger. The video also traces blockchain's evolution from Bitcoin to Ethereum's smart contracts, and discusses practical applications like tokens, stablecoins, NFTs, and supply chain tracking, as well as the importance of private keys in crypto wallets.

## Key Points

- Blockchain is a shared, immutable ledger that removes the need for a central authority, using a village notebook analogy to explain how everyone holds a copy and changes are detected. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xCAot75ztsg&t=0)
- Blocks are pages linked by hashes (digital fingerprints), making it nearly impossible to alter past transactions without breaking the chain. [00:03:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xCAot75ztsg&t=235)
- Consensus is achieved through proof-of-work (mining) or proof-of-stake (staking), allowing nodes to agree on new blocks without a central authority. [00:05:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xCAot75ztsg&t=357)
- Bitcoin solved the double-spending problem by using a public ledger, and Ethereum extended blockchain to smart contracts, enabling tokens, stablecoins, NFTs, and supply chain tracking. [00:08:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xCAot75ztsg&t=508)
- Crypto wallets store private keys, not coins; losing them means losing access forever, as illustrated by the story of James Howells. [00:13:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xCAot75ztsg&t=810)
