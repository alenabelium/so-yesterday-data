# Ethereum’s First Big CRISIS and the ICO Explosion

## Executive Summary

This video explores Ethereum's early history, focusing on its first major crisis—the DAO hack—and the subsequent ICO boom. It details how a reentrancy attack led to the theft of 3.6 million ETH, sparking a community debate that resulted in a hard fork and the creation of Ethereum Classic. The video then highlights how Ethereum's ERC-20 token standard facilitated an explosion of ICOs in 2017, raising billions and driving ETH's price up dramatically, while also attracting speculative and questionable projects. It concludes by noting the network's growing scalability issues and the onset of the ICO bubble burst.

## Key Points

- The DAO, launched in April 2016, raised about $150 million in ETH, representing 14% of all ETH in existence at the time, but was soon exploited via a reentrancy attack that stole 3.6 million ETH. [00:05:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fA_Hoz40Kmo&t=324)
- The community's response to the DAO hack led to a hard fork on July 20, 2016, which returned stolen funds to investors but split the network, creating Ethereum Classic for those who rejected the intervention. [00:13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fA_Hoz40Kmo&t=780)
- The ERC-20 token standard, proposed in November 2015, simplified token creation and trading, fueling the 2017 ICO boom where approximately 77% of ICO projects used Ethereum, raising $7 billion globally. [00:15:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fA_Hoz40Kmo&t=917)
