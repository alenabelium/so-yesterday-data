# Wall Street Just Chose Ethereum OVER Bitcoin

## Executive Summary

In July 2026, Ethereum spot ETFs attracted $365 million while Bitcoin ETFs saw record outflows, signaling a shift in institutional preference. Corporate treasuries, led by Bitmain, have become the primary buyers of ETH, staking most of their holdings to generate yield, unlike Bitcoin's static treasury model. Wall Street infrastructure, including BNY and BlackRock, is being built around Ethereum's staking and tokenization capabilities. However, a proposed EIP-8361 to taper issuance and reduce staking yields has sparked controversy, highlighting a governance battle over whether Ethereum should prioritize decentralization or institutional payouts.

## Key Points

- Ethereum ETFs saw $365M inflows in July vs Bitcoin's $172M, with Bitcoin ETFs experiencing record outflows in June, indicating a shift in institutional preference. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9KAAyZ1pX64&t=0)
- Corporate treasuries, holding 8.2M ETH, are staking most of their holdings to generate yield, unlike Bitcoin treasuries that require financing to grow. [00:02:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9KAAyZ1pX64&t=128)
- BNY and BlackRock are building infrastructure for Ethereum staking and tokenized assets, with BNY integrating staking rewards and BlackRock launching staked ETH products. [00:03:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9KAAyZ1pX64&t=198)
- EIP-8361 proposes a tapered issuance burn that would cut staking yields from ~2.6% to ~1.1-1.2%, sparking backlash from DeFi and staking providers. [00:11:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9KAAyZ1pX64&t=660)
- The debate over EIP-8361 highlights a governance conflict between core developers prioritizing decentralization and institutional capital seeking yield. [00:13:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9KAAyZ1pX64&t=799)
