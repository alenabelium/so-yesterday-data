# Visa Just Picked The WINNING Stablecoin (It’s Not USDT or USDC)

## Executive Summary

Visa's integration of OpenUSD (OUSD), a consortium-backed stablecoin, signals a potential shift in the stablecoin market dominated by USDT and USDC. OUSD's model distributes reserve revenue among its 167+ partners, offering free issuance and redemption, which contrasts with the profit-retaining models of Tether and Circle. This could pressure Circle's margins and alter distribution dynamics, though OUSD faces significant adoption challenges. Visa's multi-coin strategy suggests it is hedging its bets, but the market reaction has already impacted Circle's stock.

## Key Points

- Visa's new stablecoin platform supports OpenUSD, a consortium-backed stablecoin with 167+ partners including major corporations, unlike USDT or USDC. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YT0rTZE3r4A&t=0)
- OpenUSD's key differentiator is its revenue-sharing model, distributing reserve profits among partners and offering free issuance/redemption, contrasting with Tether and Circle's profit retention. [00:04:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YT0rTZE3r4A&t=297)
- Circle's stock fell over 17% upon OUSD's announcement, and Mizuho downgraded Circle, citing potential pressure on its revenue-sharing terms with distribution partners. [00:12:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YT0rTZE3r4A&t=733)
- Despite OUSD's potential, analysts caution that an impressive partner list doesn't guarantee adoption, as seen with Paxos' Global Dollar Network, and USDC's established liquidity remains a barrier. [00:15:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YT0rTZE3r4A&t=914)
- Visa's strategy is multi-coin and multi-chain, aiming to benefit from any stablecoin outcome, with OUSD being just the first supported asset in its broader infrastructure play. [00:09:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YT0rTZE3r4A&t=587)
