# Where Did Binance's Stolen $6 Billion Go?

## Executive Summary

This video from Coin Bureau analyzes the largest cryptocurrency theft in history, where $6.3 billion was stolen from Binance's hot wallets. It details the sophisticated laundering process, including converting stablecoins into non-freezable assets, using automated systems across multiple blockchains, and leveraging decentralized exchanges and bridges. The video also highlights the role of stablecoin issuers in freezing funds, the suspected involvement of North Korean hackers, and the challenges faced by tracking firms like Chainalysis. Ultimately, it questions whether investigators can intercept the remaining $3.3 billion before it is fully laundered.

## Key Points

- The theft exploited Binance's hot wallets and used EIP-7702 to batch withdrawals, bypassing approval processes, with the attacker gaining access through a compromised developer tool and stolen credentials. [00:01:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jeKD85EPlAc&t=61)
- Thieves converted stablecoins into non-freezable assets like ETH via lending protocols and DEXs, incurring significant slippage losses but successfully avoiding blacklists. [00:03:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jeKD85EPlAc&t=222)
- The laundering was automated and pre-planned, using Tornado Cash and Thorchain to split funds across 11 blockchains, with over 40,000 transactions in four days. [00:06:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jeKD85EPlAc&t=404)
- Chainalysis, a key tracking tool, was hacked during the investigation, potentially hampering efforts to trace the remaining $3.3 billion. [00:10:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jeKD85EPlAc&t=636)
