# Bitcoin Survived EVERYTHING They Threw At It (Episode 3)

## Executive Summary

This episode of Coin Bureau's Bitcoin series chronicles Bitcoin's survival through major crises from 2013 to 2020, including the Mt. Gox collapse, government crackdowns, the block size war, and multiple price crashes. It argues that Bitcoin's decentralized nature allowed it to withstand the failure of its largest exchange, regulatory pressure, and internal community disputes, ultimately turning survival into its defining trait. The video concludes by noting that by 2020, Bitcoin had attracted institutional interest, setting the stage for its institutional era.

## Key Points

- The Mt. Gox collapse in 2014, where 850,000 BTC went missing, tested Bitcoin's resilience, but the network kept running and other exchanges continued operating, reinforcing the lesson 'not your keys, not your coins.' [00:05:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2uStBt3ZE6E&t=304)
- Government crackdowns, including New York's BitLicense, India's banking ban, and China's mining ban, made Bitcoin harder to access but failed to kill the network itself, as users and miners simply relocated. [00:11:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2uStBt3ZE6E&t=713)
- The block size war (2015-2017) led to a chain split creating Bitcoin Cash, demonstrating that no single group can force changes on Bitcoin, and that decentralization means disagreements result in separate networks rather than one faction taking control. [00:16:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2uStBt3ZE6E&t=961)
- Despite repeated price crashes, including an 85% drop in 2015 and an 80% crash in 2018, Bitcoin recovered each time, and by 2020, public companies like MicroStrategy began adopting BTC as a treasury reserve asset, marking the start of its institutional era. [00:20:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2uStBt3ZE6E&t=1208)
