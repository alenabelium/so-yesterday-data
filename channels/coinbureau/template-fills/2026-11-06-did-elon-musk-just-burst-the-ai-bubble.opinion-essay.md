## Thesis

Elon Musk's warnings expose a fragile AI neocloud sector where over 200 debt-funded companies face collapse, and the entire AI boom is built on rented, increasingly expensive debt.

## Argument

The mechanism is financial fragility. Neoclouds borrow heavily to buy Nvidia chips, lease them to tech giants, and use those contracts as collateral. But chips depreciate faster than loans mature, and when Nvidia releases new generations, older chips lose value. CoreWeave, the largest, has $35 billion in direct debt, with bonds yielding 15% after falling below 80 cents on the dollar—a distress signal. Its $104 billion backlog is mostly a promise from Microsoft, which provided two-thirds of revenue. Smaller operators, over 200, lack both backlog and cash, making them the 'guppies' Musk targets. The sector's interconnectedness amplifies risk: private lenders like Blackstone and Apollo hold GPU collateral, Nvidia owns a stake in CoreWeave and has committed to repurchase $6.3 billion of unused capacity, and major AI labs rent from these clouds. This creates a circular dependency where lenders finance clouds, clouds buy from Nvidia, labs rent capacity, and lab growth convinces lenders to keep lending. Musk's second argument is operational: power, cooling, and network infrastructure lag behind chip delivery, so interest accrues and depreciation eats profits before revenue starts. His own $40 billion GPU debt for SpaceX puts him in direct competition for chips, power, and lenders, giving him a motive to steer capital away from neoclouds.
