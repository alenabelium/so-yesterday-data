## Thesis

Robinhood's new blockchain is coming for the entire on-chain speculative economy and is set to eat Solana's lunch.

## Argument

The attack isn't about tech. Solana won because it was the venue where the action was: cheap, fast, and permissionless, with meme coins as the product. But attention and liquidity are one resource, and gravity can shift. Robinhood never pretended speculation was respectable either — its identity was forged in the meme stock era, and it has 28.4 million funded accounts with compliance already done. The centerpiece is Robinhood Chain, an Ethereum L2 where meme coins pair directly against tokenized stocks, forcing two trading cultures into the same room. The AMC episode proved the point: a CEO's outrage became rocket fuel for a token, and the mechanics let a meme coin set a different price for a real company's shares over the weekend. Crypto spent a decade arguing it was a separate system; Robinhood says it never was. The real competition is about who can best serve the infinite demand for a market with no closing bell and no gatekeeper. Solana is already answering with Stonks Fun, but Robinhood has the distribution to take this mainstream. The winning venue will be whichever is honest about selling the ability to bet on anything at any hour.

## Counterpoints

- Some of Robinhood Chain's activity is driven by temporary gas subsidies and incentives.
- Much of the volume comes from existing crypto traders using third-party platforms, not new Robinhood customers.
- Solana has the culture, tooling, launch machinery, and permissionlessness to spin up markets anytime.
- Solana is already fighting back with Stonks Fun, letting anyone launch meme coins paired against tokenized stocks.
