## Thesis

Robinhood is coming for the entire on-chain speculative economy and wants to eat Solana's lunch.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Robinhood has 28.4 million funded customer accounts, an app already on people's phones, and compliance already done. Solana's edge was never tech—it was being the place where the action was, capturing both attention and liquidity. But Robinhood is attacking that position by pairing meme coins directly against tokenized stocks, creating a new form of degeneracy that forces two trading cultures into the same room. The AMC episode shows the power: a CEO's outrage became rocket fuel for a token paired against tokenized AMC stock, reaching a nine-figure valuation within hours. The mechanics produce strange results—meme coin purchases absorb tokenized stock supply, so on-chain prices drift from the exchange close, especially over weekends. Robinhood's position is that crypto was never a separate system; it's happy to combine the two in the craziest way possible. Solana's dominance in on-chain equity tokens collapsed from 97% to 30% in weeks as these hybrid pools appeared. But Solana is already fighting back with Stonks Fun, letting anyone launch meme coins paired against tokenized stocks. The real competition is about which platform can best serve the demand for 24/7 permissionless speculation—and the winner will be the one honest about what it's selling.

## Counterpoints

- Some of Robinhood Chain's activity is driven by temporary gas subsidies and incentives that won't last.
- Much of the volume comes from existing crypto traders using third-party platforms, not new Robinhood customers.
- Solana has the culture, tooling, launch machinery, and permissionlessness that lets anyone spin up a market.
- Solana is already answering with Stonks Fun, so Robinhood doesn't own the narrative.
