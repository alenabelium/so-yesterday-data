## Thesis

The Fed's hawkish pause is a lagging indicator of strength that historically precedes abrupt easing, and Bitcoin will initially drop but then rally sharply when the pivot comes.

## Argument

The mechanism is historical precedent. In every tightening cycle since 1984, the median gap between the last hike and first cut is about 7.5 months, and the peak is always marked by hawkish confidence. The 2006-2007 playbook is exact: a long pause, inflation complaints, then a sudden 50bp cut as credit markets implode. When the Fed pivots, it doesn't do it gently—normal easing cycles deliver 235bp in the first year, extreme ones 400bp.

Today's cracks are visible. Nvidia's five-year CDS hit an all-time high of 82bp on July 27, jumping 14bp in a single session, because it's reportedly on the hook for $750B in AI infrastructure commitments against only $3.5B in on-book liabilities. Oracle's default insurance exceeds 200bp, Coreweave's 855bp implies a 50% default probability. Private loan defaults hit 6%, commercial bankruptcies rose 13% YoY, and large corporate bankruptcies hit 372—the highest half-year since 2010. Hyperscalers have issued $244B in bonds by mid-July, more than double all of 2025, and investor coverage fell from 5x to under 2x.

The Fed's own data is lagging. June employment showed 57K new jobs versus 115K expected, with prior months revised down by 74K. The unemployment rate fell only because 720K people left the labor force—the participation rate dropped to 61.5%, the lowest since March 2021. This is "low hiring, low firing," and the only indicator that should force action is artificially improving.

## Counterpoints

- Inflation is still a problem; the Fed can't cut now—Warsh insists on a 2% target, no soft target.
- The Fed doesn't need to cut rates to add liquidity; financial stability tools like the reverse repo and QE can be used.
- Rising long-term bond yields could absorb the liquidity impulse, preventing Bitcoin from rallying even if the Fed eases.
