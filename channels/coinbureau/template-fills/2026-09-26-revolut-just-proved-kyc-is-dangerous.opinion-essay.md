## Thesis

KYC files are now the most dangerous documents attached to a person's name, and the regulatory framework that mandates their creation and retention makes data minimization effectively illegal, leaving self-custody as the only real.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory compliance. Revolut's lawful request desk is engineered to say yes to the state quickly, without tipping off the customer. Speed is the product, and that is the vulnerability. An attacker compromised an Italian government email account and sent fraudulent European investigation orders. Every technical check came back clean because the mail was genuine. Revolut's compliance desk answered for six months, handing over passport scans, onboarding selfies, home addresses, bank statements, and full Bitcoin transaction histories for 680 crypto-rich individuals. The targets were identified onchain first, then the compliance desk gave up the person. The assembled file is a document that says: here is a named human being, a recent photograph of their face, the door they walk through at night, and how much Bitcoin they control. This goes beyond digital privacy; it puts people in physical danger. The standard reading is that Revolut messed up, but you could also view this as Revolut complying. Under EU AML regulation, crypto asset service providers are obliged entities, records must be retained for a minimum of five years, extendable by another five. The transfer of funds regulation pushes identity data along with transactions, reaching self-hosted wallets above €1,000. DAC8 forces balances and transaction histories to be reported to tax authorities, creating another copy of the same file. Meanwhile, GDPR Article 5 requires data minimization.
