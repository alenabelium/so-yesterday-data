## Thesis

KYC files are now the most dangerous documents attached to a person's name, and the Revolut breach proves that regulatory frameworks like AML and GDPR are contradictory, making data minimization effectively illegal.

## Argument

The attack on Revolut wasn't a hack; it was a lawful request desk doing exactly what European regulators designed it to do. The desk's mandate is to say yes to the state quickly without tipping off the customer. Speed is the product, and that's the vulnerability. An attacker compromised an Italian government email account and sent fraudulent European investigation orders. Every technical check came back clean because the mail was genuine. Revolut's compliance desk answered for six months, handing over passport scans, selfies, home addresses, and full Bitcoin transaction histories for 680 crypto-rich individuals. The targets were identified onchain first, then the compliance desk attached a name, face, and street address. This is the second half of the KYC file built without asking, because it's required to. The assembled file is a document that says: here is a named human, a recent photo of their face, the door they walk through at night, and how much Bitcoin they control. This goes beyond digital privacy; it puts people in physical danger. The standard reading is that Revolut messed up, but you could also view this as Revolut complying. Under EU AML regulation, records must be retained for a minimum of five years, extendable to ten. The travel rule pushes identity data along with transactions. DAC8 forces reporting to tax authorities, creating another copy of the file. Meanwhile, GDPR Article 5 requires data minimization. Both are EU law, but they cannot both be obeyed. ## Counterpoints

- Revolut messed up: true to some degree, but the system did what European regulators designed it to do.
- Some documents can be replaced: identities cannot be replaced, and 680 people are permanently exposed.
- The ransom theater is the least interesting part: the real problem is what was stolen—faces, passports, addresses.
- KYC is about customer protection: but the customer supplies the data, carries the risk, and gets extorted.
