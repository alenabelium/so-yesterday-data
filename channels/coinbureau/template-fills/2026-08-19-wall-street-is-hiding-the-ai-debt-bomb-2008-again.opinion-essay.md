## Thesis

Wall Street is financing the AI buildout with opaque, off-balance-sheet debt that will trigger a financial shock when GPU values collapse before the debt matures.

## Argument

The mechanism is a maturity mismatch. GPUs lose roughly 50% of their value in three years, yet the debt written against them runs five, ten, or even twenty-four years. Wall Street is not just investing in AI; it is lending against it, and the structure echoes 2006. Morgan Stanley puts the financing gap at $1.5 trillion, filled by private credit, securitized products, and record corporate bond issuance. The trick is keeping this debt off balance sheets. Meta's Hyperion Campus is financed through a bankruptcy-remote vehicle, Big Net Investors LLC, with $27 billion of debt rated A+ and maturing in 2049. Oracle carries $248 billion in off-balance-sheet lease commitments. The Nikkei investigation found $1.65 trillion in hidden AI obligations, growing eightfold in four years. This is the same tranche-and-stamp game as 2008. The collateral is the problem. Operators depreciate GPUs over four to six years, but market data shows real economic decay closer to 50% in three years. The rental collapse from $8 to $2-3 an hour is the evidence. So you have an asset with a three-year commercial life funded by paper maturing in five to twenty-four years. Ratings track the counterparty, not the asset. CoreWeave's debt was rated A3 when anchored by Meta, but Ba2 when tied to non-investment-grade customers. Nvidia's platform to mobilize $500 billion, with Larry Fink comparing it to mortgage-backed securities, only works if chips hold their value. Nobody has seen GPU residual values through a full cycle.
