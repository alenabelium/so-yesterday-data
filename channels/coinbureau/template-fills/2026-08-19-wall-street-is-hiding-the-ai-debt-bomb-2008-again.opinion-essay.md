## Thesis

Wall Street is financing the AI buildout with opaque, off-balance-sheet debt that will trigger a 2008-style financial shock when GPU values collapse.

## Argument

The mechanism is a structural mismatch: GPUs lose ~50% of their value in three years, yet the debt written against them matures in 5–24 years. Wall Street hides this by pushing borrowing into bankruptcy-remote vehicles — Meta's Hyperion Campus, Oracle's leases, Anthropic's chip-backed SPV — so the obligations never hit parent balance sheets. Ratings track the counterparty, not the hardware: CoreWeave's debt dropped from A3 to Ba2 when the tenant changed from Meta to non-investment-grade customers. The weakest link is public Bitcoin miners, who signed 12–20-year AI leases against 3-year hardware, funded partly by selling their treasury Bitcoin. Stress is already visible: CoreWeave's CDS imply a ~50% default probability while its debt tranches stay investment-grade. The losses won't default at the top; they'll relocate down the chain to pension funds and insurers holding rated ABS, and to listed miners inside retail portfolios. The structure breaks not from a bubble burst but from hardware aging faster than the debt.
