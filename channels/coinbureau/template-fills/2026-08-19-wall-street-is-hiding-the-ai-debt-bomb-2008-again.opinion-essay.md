## Thesis

Wall Street is lending against AI through opaque, off-balance-sheet debt structures that echo the lead-up to the 2008 crisis, and the weakest link in this chain is publicly listed Bitcoin miners.

## Argument

The mechanism is credit. Morgan Stanley puts global data center capex at $2.9T for 2025-2028, with hyperscaler cash flow covering only $1.4T, leaving a $1.5T financing gap. That gap is being filled by private credit, securitized products, and off-balance-sheet vehicles. Meta's Hyperion Campus is financed through a bankruptcy-remote vehicle, Big Net Investors LLC, which issued $27B of debt rated A+ by S&P, with a coupon of 6.58% and a maturity in 2049. Meta leases the site back on rolling four-year terms, keeping the debt off its books. Oracle carries $248B of lease commitments through third-party vehicles, and Anthropic's chip-backed financing is structured through an SPV. The Nikkei investigation found $1.65T of off-balance-sheet AI obligations at the five biggest tech firms, growing eightfold in four years.

The collateral problem is that GPUs depreciate 50% in three years, while the debt matures in 5 to 24 years. CoreWeave's ratings track the counterparty, not the asset: an A3/A- facility anchored by Meta, then a Ba2/BB+ facility tied to non-investment-grade customers. Nvidia's platform to mobilize $500B of third-party capital, with BlackRock's Larry Fink comparing it to the creation of mortgage-backed securities, underscores the securitization trend.

Bitcoin miners, having converted to AI landlords to survive mining at a loss, are the most leveraged link. They've signed $70B of AI contracts, sold 15,000+ coins, and taken on 12-20 year obligations against hardware with a 3-year half-life. Core Scientific posted a $347M net loss in Q1 2026, and CoreWeave's CDS hit 855

## Counterpoints

- Hyperscalers are enormously cash generative at the parent level, which could support the debt.
- The optimists rely on GPU residual values holding through a full technology cycle, which hasn't been tested.
- Some argue this is simply a new era for markets, not a repeat of 2008.
- The debt is rated investment-grade (e.g., A+ for Meta's vehicle, A3 for CoreWeave's facility), suggesting lower risk.
- The securitization is backed by real, cash-generative AI contracts, unlike the subprime mortgages of 2008.
