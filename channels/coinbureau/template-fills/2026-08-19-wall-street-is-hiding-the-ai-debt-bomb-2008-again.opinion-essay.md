## Thesis

Wall Street is financing the AI buildout with opaque, off-balance-sheet debt that mirrors the 2008 crisis, and the weakest link—publicly listed Bitcoin miners—will absorb the losses when GPU values collapse.

## Argument

The mechanism is credit. Morgan Stanley puts global data center capex at $2.9T for 2025-2028, but hyperscaler cash flow covers only $1.4T, leaving a $1.5T financing gap. That gap is being filled by private credit, securitized products, and off-balance-sheet vehicles. Meta's Hyperion Campus is financed through a bankruptcy-remote vehicle, Big Net Investors LLC, with $27B in debt rated A+ by S&P, but Meta keeps the debt off its books via rolling four-year leases. Oracle carries $248B in lease commitments through third-party vehicles. The Nikkei investigation found $1.65T in off-balance-sheet AI obligations, growing eightfold in four years. This is the same trick as 2006: pool weaker credit, slice into tranches, get a rating agency stamp. The collateral is the problem. GPUs depreciate 50% in three years, but the debt matures in 5 to 24 years. CoreWeave's ratings track the counterparty, not the asset: a Meta-anchored facility got A3, while a facility tied to non-investment-grade customers got Ba2. Nvidia's platform to mobilize $500B in third-party capital, with Larry Fink comparing it to mortgage-backed securities, only works if chips hold value. Bitcoin miners, already mining at a loss, have signed 12- to 20-year AI leases against hardware with a three-year half-life. Core Scientific lost $347M in Q1 2026 and was bought by CoreWeave. The stress is showing: CoreWeave's CDS hit 855 bps, implying a 50% default chance, while its debt still carried investment-grade ratings. Michael Burry flagged Nvidia's CDS going parabolic. ## Counterpoints

- Hyperscalers are enormously cash generative at the parent level, which could support the debt.
- Some argue this is a new era for markets, not a bubble, and that AI infrastructure will hold value.
- The optimists rely on GPU residual values holding through a full technology cycle, which has never been tested.
