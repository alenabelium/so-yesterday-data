## Thesis

Wall Street is financing the AI buildout with opaque, off-balance-sheet debt that mirrors 2008, and the weakest link in this chain is publicly listed Bitcoin miners.

## Argument

The mechanism is credit. Morgan Stanley puts global data center capex at $2.9T for 2025-2028, but hyperscaler cash flow covers only $1.4T, leaving a $1.5T financing gap. That gap is being filled by private credit and securitized products, not traditional bank loans. Direct private credit to AI has exploded from nothing to $200B outstanding, and global AI debt issuance is tracking toward $570B in 2026, double the prior year.

The structure is designed to hide risk. Meta's Hyperion Campus is financed through a bankruptcy-remote vehicle, Big Net Investors LLC, with $27B in debt rated A+ and maturing in 2049. Meta leases the site back on rolling four-year terms, keeping the debt off its books. Oracle carries $248B in off-balance-sheet lease commitments, and Anthropic uses an SPV with Broadcom guaranteeing part of the senior slice. The Nikkei investigation found $1.65T in off-balance-sheet AI obligations, growing eightfold in four years.

The collateral doesn't hold its value. GPUs depreciate 50% in three years, but the debt matures in 5 to 24 years. CoreWeave's ratings track the counterparty, not the asset: a Meta-anchored facility got A3, but a facility secured by non-investment-grade customers got Ba2. Nvidia's $500B platform, compared by Larry Fink to mortgage-backed securities, assumes chips hold value through a full tech cycle, which has never been observed.

Bitcoin miners are the weakest link. They converted to AI landlords to survive, with $70B in cumulative AI contracts. Core Scientific signed a 12-year deal with CoreWeave, then sold 1,900 BTC to fund the conversion.

## Counterpoints

- Hyperscalers are enormously cash generative at the parent level, which could support the debt.
- CoreWeave's A3-rated facility shows investment-grade debt can be secured against high-performance computing.
- Optimists rely on GPU values holding through a full technology cycle, which has never been observed.
- The AI buildout won't default; exposure relocates through the chain, so losses hit the weakest link, not the whole system.
