## Thesis

Wall Street is lending against AI through opaque, off-balance-sheet debt structures that echo 2008, and the collateral mismatch will trigger a financial shock when GPU values collapse.

## Argument

The AI buildout is funded by credit, not cash flow. Morgan Stanley puts global data center capex at $2.9T for 2025-2028, with a $1.5T financing gap. That gap is filled by private credit, securitized products, and off-balance-sheet vehicles. Meta's Hyperion Campus uses a bankruptcy-remote vehicle, Big Net Investors LLC, to issue $27B of debt rated A+ by S&P, with a 2049 maturity, while Meta leases back on rolling four-year terms. Oracle carries $248B in lease commitments through third-party vehicles. The Nikkei investigation found $1.65T in off-balance-sheet AI obligations, growing eightfold in four years. The collateral is GPUs that depreciate 50% in three years, but the debt matures in 5 to 24 years. Ratings track the counterparty, not the asset: CoreWeave's A3-rated facility is anchored by Meta, but a Ba2-rated facility is tied to non-investment-grade customers. Nvidia's platform to mobilize $500B, compared by Larry Fink to mortgage-backed securities, shows the scale. Publicly listed Bitcoin miners, like Core Scientific and TeraWulf, are the weakest link: they took 12-20 year leases against 3-year hardware, funded by selling Bitcoin. The stress is already visible: CoreWeave's CDS imply a 50% default probability, and Moody's warned on credit quality. The structure won't burst; it will relocate losses down the chain to these leveraged tranches.

## Counterpoints

- Hyperscalers are enormously cash generative at the parent level, which could support the debt.
- Some argue this is a new era for markets, not a bubble.
- The debt is investment-grade and secured against high-performance computing, as seen with CoreWeave's A3 rating.
