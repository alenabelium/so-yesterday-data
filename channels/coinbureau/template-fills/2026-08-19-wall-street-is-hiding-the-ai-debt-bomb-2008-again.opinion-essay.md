## Thesis

Wall Street is hiding a trillion-dollar AI debt bomb by moving borrowings off balance sheets, and if GPU values collapse before the debt is repriced, the losses will hit the weakest link—publicly listed Bitcoin miners.

## Argument

The mechanism is a financing gap. Morgan Stanley puts global data center capex at $2.9T for 2025–2028, but hyperscaler cash flow covers only $1.4T, leaving $1.5T to borrow. That money is coming from private credit, securitized products, and off-balance-sheet vehicles. Meta's Hyperion Campus is a case in point: a bankruptcy-remote vehicle called Big Net Investors LLC issued $27B of debt, rated A+ by S&P, with a 6.58% coupon and maturity in 2049. Meta leases the site back on rolling four-year terms, keeping the debt off its books. Oracle does the same with $248B of lease commitments through third-party vehicles. The Nikkei investigation found $1.65T of off-balance-sheet AI obligations at the five biggest tech firms, growing eightfold in four years.

The collateral is the problem. GPUs depreciate about 50% in three years, but the debt matures in 5, 10, or 24 years. That mismatch is the core risk. The optimists argue that hyperscalers are cash-generative and that Coreweave's $8.5B facility was rated A3/A- because it's anchored by a Meta contract. But when the tenant changed to non-investment-grade customers, the rating dropped to Ba2/BB+. The ratings track the counterparty, not the asset. Nvidia's platform to mobilize $500B of third-party capital, with Larry Fink comparing it to mortgage-backed securities, only works if chips hold their value—and nobody has seen GPU residual values through a full technology cycle.

Bitcoin miners are the weakest link. They converted to AI landlords to survive, signing 12- to 20-year leases against hardware with a three-year half-life. Core

## Counterpoints

- Hyperscalers are enormously cash generative at the parent level, so the debt is backed by strong cash flows.
- Coreweave's investment-grade rating on its $8.5B facility shows that AI debt can be rated highly when anchored by strong counterparties like Meta.
- The AI buildout is a new era for markets, and the debt structures are simply a modern form of financial engineering, as Larry Fink suggested.
