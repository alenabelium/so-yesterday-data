## Thesis

Wall Street is financing the AI buildout with opaque, off-balance-sheet debt that echoes 2006, and if GPU values collapse before the debt is repriced, the losses will hit the weakest link—publicly listed Bitcoin miners.

## Argument

The mechanism is credit. Morgan Stanley puts global data center capex at $2.9T for 2025-2028, but hyperscaler operating cash flow covers only $1.4T, leaving a $1.5T financing gap. That gap is being filled by private credit and securitized products, not traditional balance-sheet debt. Direct private credit to AI has exploded from nothing to $200B, and global AI-related debt issuance is tracking toward $570B in 2026. The trick is keeping this debt off balance sheets. Meta's Hyperion Campus is financed through a bankruptcy-remote vehicle, Big Net Investors LLC, with $27B of debt rated A+ and maturing in 2049, while Meta leases back on rolling four-year terms. Oracle carries $248B in off-balance-sheet lease commitments. The Nikkei investigation found $1.65T of hidden off-balance-sheet AI obligations, growing eightfold in four years. This is the 2006 playbook: pool weaker credit, slice into tranches, get a rating agency stamp. The collateral is the problem. GPUs depreciate 50% in three years, but the debt matures in 5 to 24 years. CoreWeave's ratings track the counterparty, not the asset—A3 when anchored by Meta, Ba2 when tied to non-investment-grade customers. Nvidia is mobilizing $500B through Apollo, BlackRock, and others, with Larry Fink comparing it to the creation of mortgage-backed securities. The weakest link is Bitcoin miners. They converted to AI landlords to survive—mining at a loss with cash costs of $75k-$88k per Bitcoin. They've signed $70B in AI contracts, selling 15,000 coins to fund conversions. ## Counterpoints

- Hyperscalers are enormously cash generative at the parent level, which could support the debt.
- Some argue this is a new era for markets, not a bubble.
- The optimists rely on GPU residual values holding through a full technology cycle, which has never been seen.
- Nobody is going to default on the AI build-out; the exposure just relocates down the chain.
