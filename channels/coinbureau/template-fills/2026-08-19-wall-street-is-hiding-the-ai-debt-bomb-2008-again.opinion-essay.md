## Thesis

Wall Street is lending against AI through opaque, off-balance-sheet debt structures that echo 2008, and if GPU values collapse before the debt is repriced, the losses will hit the weakest link: publicly listed Bitcoin miners.

## Argument

The mechanism is credit. Morgan Stanley puts global data center capex at $2.9T for 2025-2028, but hyperscaler cash flow covers only $1.4T, leaving a $1.5T financing gap. That gap is being filled by private credit and securitized products, not traditional bank loans. Direct private credit to AI has exploded from nothing to $200B, and global AI debt issuance is tracking toward $570B in 2026.

The trick is keeping the debt off balance sheets. Meta's Hyperion Campus is financed through a bankruptcy-remote vehicle, Big Net Investors LLC, which issued $27B of A+ rated debt that doesn't mature until 2049. Oracle carries $248B in off-balance-sheet lease commitments. Anthropic uses an SPV with Broadcom guaranteeing part of the senior slice. The Nikkei investigation found $1.65T of off-balance-sheet AI obligations, growing eightfold in four years.

The collateral doesn't hold its value. GPUs depreciate about 50% in three years, but the debt matures in 5 to 24 years. That mismatch is the core problem. The optimists argue that hyperscalers are cash-generative and that Coreweave's A3 rating proves the debt is sound. But the rating tracks the counterparty, not the asset: when Coreweave's collateral shifted from Meta to non-investment-grade customers, the rating dropped from A3 to Ba2.

Nvidia is now mobilizing $500B of third-party capital, with Larry Fink comparing it to the creation of mortgage-backed securities. ## Counterpoints

- Hyperscalers are enormously cash generative at the parent level, so the debt is safe.
- Coreweave's A3 rating on its $8.5B loan shows the debt is investment-grade and sound.
- Nobody is going to default on the AI build-out; the structure will not fail.
- This is a new era for markets, not a repeat of 2008.
