## Thesis

Wall Street is repeating 2008 by financing the AI buildout with opaque, off-balance-sheet debt that will break when GPU values collapse.

## Argument

The mechanism is structural: Wall Street is lending against AI through bankruptcy-remote vehicles and securitized products, not just investing. Meta's Hyperion Campus is financed via a remote vehicle with $27B in debt rated A+ and maturing in 2049, while the underlying GPUs lose ~50% of value in three years. This mismatch — 3-year hardware funded by 5-to-24-year paper — is the core fragility. The ratings track the counterparty, not the asset: CoreWeave's debt rating dropped from A3 to Ba2 when the tenant changed from Meta to non-investment-grade customers. The exposure relocates down the chain: miners like Core Scientific and TeraWulf, with weak balance sheets, signed 12-to-20-year AI leases funded partly by selling Bitcoin, becoming the most leveraged link. The stress is already visible: CoreWeave's CDS imply ~50% default probability while its debt still carries investment-grade ratings, and Moody's has warned on credit quality at six major firms. The counterargument — that hyperscalers' cash flow covers the gap — fails because the financing gap is $1.5T, and the debt is structured to avoid balance sheets. The bubble doesn't need to burst; if hardware ages faster than the debt, the weakest link — publicly traded miners — breaks first.
