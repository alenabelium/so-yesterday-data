## Thesis

Wall Street is lending against AI through opaque, off-balance-sheet debt structures that echo 2008, and the weakest link in this chain is publicly listed Bitcoin miners.

## Argument

The mechanism is credit. Morgan Stanley puts global data center capex at $2.9T for 2025-2028, but hyperscaler cash flow covers only $1.4T, leaving a $1.5T financing gap. That gap is being filled by private credit, securitized products, and off-balance-sheet vehicles. Meta's Hyperion Campus, for instance, is financed through a bankruptcy-remote vehicle, Big Net Investors LLC, with $27B in debt rated A+ and maturing in 2049, while Meta leases the site on rolling four-year terms to keep the debt off its books. Oracle carries $248B in lease commitments through third-party vehicles, and Anthropic's chip-backed financing is structured through an SPV. The Nikkei investigation found $1.65T in off-balance-sheet AI obligations, growing eightfold in four years.

The collateral problem is the mismatch: GPUs depreciate ~50% in three years, but the debt matures in 5 to 24 years. Ratings track the counterparty, not the asset—CoreWeave's debt rating dropped from A3 to Ba2 when the tenant changed from Meta to non-investment-grade customers. Nvidia's platform to mobilize $500B in third-party capital, with Larry Fink comparing it to the creation of mortgage-backed securities, only deepens the exposure.

Bitcoin miners are the most leveraged link. They converted to AI landlords to survive mining at a loss, signing 12- to 20-year leases against hardware with a three-year half-life. Core Scientific signed a $10.2B deal with CoreWeave, TeraWulf a $19B lease with Anthropic, and Hut 8 a $7B lease. They sold over 15,000 coins to fund conversions, and Van Eck estimates a $50B funding gap. Core

## Counterpoints

- Hyperscalers are enormously cash generative at the parent level, which could absorb losses.
- Optimists rely on GPU residual values holding through a full technology cycle, which hasn't been tested.
- Some argue this is simply a new era for markets, not a repeat of 2008.
