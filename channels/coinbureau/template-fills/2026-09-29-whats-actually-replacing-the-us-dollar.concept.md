## TL;DR

The US dollar isn't collapsing; it's being disassembled piece by piece. It performs four distinct jobs—pricing commodities, settling trade, serving as a reserve asset, and acting as global collateral—each with its own exit door. Central banks are diversifying into gold, alternative payment systems are fragmenting settlement, and stablecoins are paradoxically expanding dollar use.

## The argument

### Define the Dollar's Four Jobs
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:50']
- **Claim**: The dollar isn't one thing but a bundle of four distinct jobs: pricing commodities, settling trade between strangers, serving as a reserve asset for central banks, and acting as the collateral underpinning the global financial system. Each job has its own exit door, and those doors are extremely different.
- **Role**: definition

### No Successor, Only Carving Up
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:43']
- **Claim**: No single currency is replacing the dollar. China's renminbi is small and constrained by capital controls; the euro lacks a unified treasury. Instead, the dollar's roles are being carved up piece by piece, starting with the G7's freezing of Russian reserves in 2022, which turned a temporary measure into permanent architecture.
- **Role**: evidence

### Central Banks Rotate into Gold
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:23']
- **Claim**: Central banks responded to the reserve freeze by buying gold, the one reserve asset truly independent of any issuer. Annual purchases jumped from 400-500 tons pre-2022 to over 1,000 tons in 2022-2024, with 2025 at 863 tons. This is a direct vote against custody in the dollar system.
- **Role**: evidence

### Settlement Fragments into Blocks
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:26']
- **Claim**: Alternative payment systems like China's CIPS and mBridge are fragmenting settlement infrastructure, but they aren't converging on a single alternative. These corridors cost more, clear slower, and strand capital—countries are paying a premium to build a second set of pipes they hope never to use.
- **Role**: evidence

### Debt and Stablecoins Trap and Expand
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:58']
- **Claim**: The stickiest part of the dollar's dominance is debt: $14.7 trillion in dollar-denominated obligations owed by non-US borrowers forces continued dollar buying. Meanwhile, stablecoins—99% USD-backed—are expanding the dollar's reach, especially in emerging markets, even as Bitcoin hedges against issuer credibility.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Central bank gold buying: over 1,000 tons annually since 2022, with 2025 at 863 tons; World Gold Council estimates over half of last year's buying went unreported. Stablecoin market peaked at $322 billion, with 99% USD-backed; Goldman Sachs estimates 66% held in emerging markets. In Argentina, stablecoins are 94% of peso crypto volume; Nigeria's on-chain transfers exceed $56 billion. Monthly stablecoin settlement hit $7.2 trillion in February 2026, overtaking ACH. Tether holds ~$141 billion in Treasuries, ranking ~17th largest holder. Caveats: The renminbi's share of Swift payments is only 3-4%, and reserve share can bleed for a decade without dominance moving. The BIS walked away from mBridge due to sanctions, and the system is fragmenting into blocks, not converging. The dollar's dominance persists because of debt stickiness and lack of alternatives.
