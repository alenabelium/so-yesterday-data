## TL;DR

The US dollar isn't collapsing—it's being disassembled piece by piece. The dollar performs four distinct jobs: pricing commodities, settling trade, serving as a reserve asset, and acting as global collateral. Each job has a different exit door, and while no single currency is replacing the dollar, its roles are being carved up by gold, alternative payment systems, and even stablecoins.

## The argument

### The Dollar Is Four Jobs, Not One
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:50']
- **Claim**: The dollar isn't a single thing but a bundle of four distinct jobs: pricing commodities, settling trade, serving as a reserve asset, and acting as global collateral. Each job has its own exit door, and these doors are extremely different in how easy they are to walk through.
- **Role**: definition

### No Candidate, Only Carving
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:43']
- **Claim**: No currency is taking the dollar's place—China's renminbi is too small, the euro lacks a single treasury. Instead, the dollar's role is being carved up. The carving began in 2022 when the G7 froze Russian reserves, turning a temporary measure into permanent architecture and pushing central banks toward gold.
- **Role**: evidence

### Debt Is the Stickiest Job
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:58']
- **Claim**: The world doesn't just use dollars—it owes dollars. With $14.7 trillion in dollar-denominated debt owed by non-US borrowers, debt is the stickiest thing in finance. As long as obligations are written in dollars, borrowers must keep buying dollars, regardless of their opinion of Washington.
- **Role**: counter

### Stablecoins Expand the Dollar's Reach
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:12']
- **Claim**: While governments build corridors around the dollar, citizens are buying digital dollars. Stablecoins, 99% USD-pegged, are expanding the dollar's territory into emerging markets, with flows that resist capital controls. This paradoxically cements dollar dominance even as its custody is questioned.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Central banks bought over 1,000 tons of gold in 2022–2024, nearly double the pre-2022 pace, and the World Gold Council estimates over half of last year's buying went unreported. China's CIPS exceeded 100 trillion yuan in 2025, but the renminbi's share of SWIFT payments remains at 3–4%. mBridge lost BIS sponsorship, and Project Agora is the G7-aligned alternative. Stablecoin flows are largely resistant to capital controls, per BIS research, and the IMF warns local-currency stablecoins could accelerate dollarization. However, the dollar still sits on one side of 88% of FX transactions, and reserve share can bleed for a decade without dominance moving much.
