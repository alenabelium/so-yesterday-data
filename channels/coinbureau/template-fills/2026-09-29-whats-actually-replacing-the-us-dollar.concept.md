## TL;DR

The US dollar isn't collapsing; it's being disassembled piece by piece. It performs four distinct jobs—pricing commodities, settling trade, serving as a reserve asset, and acting as global collateral—each with its own exit door. Central banks are rotating into gold, payment systems are fragmenting, and stablecoins are paradoxically expanding the dollar's reach.

## The argument

### Define the Dollar's Four Jobs
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:50']
- **Claim**: The dollar isn't one thing but a bundle of four separate jobs: pricing commodities, settling trade, serving as a reserve asset, and acting as global collateral. Each job has its own exit door, and those doors are extremely different.
- **Role**: definition

### No Candidate, Only Carving
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:43']
- **Claim**: No single currency is replacing the dollar—China's renminbi and the euro have structural limits. Instead, the dollar's role is being carved up, starting with the 2022 G7 freeze of Russian reserves, which turned a temporary measure into permanent architecture.
- **Role**: evidence

### Central Banks Rotate into Gold
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:23']
- **Claim**: Central banks responded to the reserve freeze by buying gold at nearly double the pre-2022 pace, with over 1,000 tons annually in 2022-2024. Gold is the one reserve asset truly independent of any issuer, and even official numbers may understate the shift.
- **Role**: evidence

### Stablecoins Expand Dollar Reach
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:12']
- **Claim**: While governments build corridors around the dollar, citizens are buying dollars via stablecoins. With ~99% of stablecoin supply being USD-pegged, these digital dollars are financing the US deficit and are largely resistant to capital controls, as the BIS found.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Bitcoin Hedges Credibility
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:39']
- **Claim**: Bitcoin doesn't hedge the convenience of the dollar but the credibility of its issuer. Its correlation with gold has risen while its correlation with the Nasdaq has collapsed, making it the retail equivalent of central banks' gold purchases.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The dollar's dominance persists due to the stickiness of $14.7 trillion in dollar-denominated debt and the lack of a viable alternative. Foreign holdings of US Treasuries still grew 2.3% to $9.3 trillion. Stablecoin settlement volume hit $7.2 trillion in February 2026, overtaking ACH. However, the speaker notes that reserve share can bleed for a decade without dominance moving much, and that alternative payment corridors are inefficient—countries are paying a premium for pipes they hope never to use. The BIS walked away from mBridge, and the IMF warns local-currency stablecoins could accelerate dollarization.
