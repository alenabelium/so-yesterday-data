## TL;DR

The US dollar isn't collapsing; it's being disassembled piece by piece. It performs four distinct jobs—pricing commodities, settling trade, serving as a reserve asset, and acting as global collateral—each with its own exit door. Central banks are rotating into gold, payment systems are fragmenting, and stablecoins are paradoxically expanding the dollar's reach.

## The argument

### Define the Dollar's Four Jobs
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:50']
- **Claim**: The dollar is not a single thing but a bundle of four distinct jobs: pricing commodities, settling trade, serving as a reserve asset, and acting as global collateral. Each job has its own exit door, and these doors are extremely different in difficulty.
- **Role**: definition

### No Successor, Only Carving
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:43']
- **Claim**: No single currency is replacing the dollar. China's renminbi and the euro have structural limitations—capital controls and lack of a unified treasury—so the dollar's role is being carved up piece by piece rather than filled by a successor.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Freeze That Changed Everything
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:23']
- **Claim**: The G7's immobilization of Russian reserves in 2022 revealed that reserve assets are not property. This 'temporary measure' became permanent architecture, prompting central banks to buy gold at nearly double the pre-2022 pace as a hedge against custody risk.
- **Role**: evidence

### Debt Is the Stickiest Job
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:58']
- **Claim**: The dollar's role as global collateral is anchored by $14.7 trillion in dollar-denominated debt owed by non-US borrowers. As long as obligations are written in dollars, borrowers must keep buying dollars to service them, making this job the hardest to exit.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Stablecoins Expand, Bitcoin Hedges
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:12']
- **Claim**: Stablecoins, 99% USD-backed, are expanding the dollar's reach into emerging markets, effectively financing the US deficit. Meanwhile, Bitcoin hedges the credibility of dollar issuers, not the convenience of the dollar, addressing the trust question that gold answers for central banks.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Central bank gold buying jumped from 400-500 tons annually (2010-2021) to over 1,000 tons in 2022-2024, with 2025 at 863 tons—still nearly double the old normal. The World Gold Council estimates over half of last year's buying went unreported, and 95% of central banks expect gold reserves to rise. China's CIPS exceeded 100 trillion yuan in 2025, yet the renminbi's share of SWIFT payments remains at 3-4%. mBridge lost BIS sponsorship, which built its own G7-aligned Project Agora instead. Stablecoin supply peaked at $322 billion, with 66% held in emerging markets; Argentina sees stablecoins at 94% of peso crypto volume, and Nigeria's on-chain transfers hit $56 billion. Monthly stablecoin settlement reached $7.2 trillion, overtaking ACH. Tether holds $141 billion in Treasuries, ranking ~17th among holders. The speaker notes that reserve share can bleed for another decade without dominance moving, and that these alternative corridors are inefficient—costing more, clearing slower, and stranding capital.
