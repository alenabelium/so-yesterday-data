## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and a single law with a deadline kicked off that race.

## Argument

The mechanism is a law with a clock. The GENIUS Act, signed July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework, but the real driver is the hard effective date: January 2027. After that, issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. The OCC targets November to finalize rules, and the Clarity Act's Senate path is still playing out with a procedural vote September 15th. This regulatory certainty is why major institutions are moving now.

Last month, Circle named founding validators for Arc, a permissioned network. BlackRock, ICE (owns NYSE), and DTCC (settles every American stock trade) are running nodes. Visa and MasterCard, who called crypto an experiment for a decade, now help operate the network. This isn't participation; it's ownership. And it's not just Arc: 21 banks, including Goldman Sachs, Citi, and Bank of America, announced a joint venture for their own stablecoin targeting H1 2027, right after the compliance window shuts. Stripe, Visa, MasterCard, Coinbase, and 140+ companies back OpenUSD, sharing reserve earnings. You don't build a competitor to something you think is a fad.

The dollar is acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US debt. A shopkeeper in Lagos holding digital dollars is financing the American government without a bank account. Tether's Treasury exposure was $141 billion as of Q1 2026, making it the 17th largest holder of US debt, above South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. ## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch their own stablecoin right after the compliance window shuts proves they think the format has won.
- This looks like finance adopting crypto, but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network, with stablecoins backed by US debt financing the government.
- Crypto natives argued whether TradFi would accept crypto; the answer is neither yes nor no—TradFi took the technology and rebuilt the dollar.
