## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and Washington still owns the currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline in January 2027. Once the OCC finalizes its rulebook, issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That clock forced every major financial institution to pick a side: build or be left out. [00:02:07]

So they built. Circle's Arc blockchain, launching September 16, is a permissioned network where the validators are named companies—BlackRock, ICE, DTCC, Visa, MasterCard—not anonymous nodes. These aren't passive participants; they're infrastructure owners. [00:04:01] And the consortium moves are even more telling: 21 banks, including Goldman Sachs and Citi, announced a joint dollar stablecoin targeting 2027, while Stripe, Visa, MasterCard, Coinbase, and 140+ companies back OpenUSD. You don't spend two years building a competitor to something you think is a fad. [00:05:23]

The real prize is dollar dominance. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by US Treasuries, so a shopkeeper in Lagos holding digital dollars is financing the American government without a bank account. Tether alone holds $141 billion in Treasuries—more than South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. [00:06:18] Washington sees this as the cheapest dollar expansion available. Treasury Secretary Bessent projects a tenfold stablecoin market growth; the Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee and Brookings have modeled the demand. [00:06:57]

The clearest proof is Europe's panic. 37 institutions across 15 countries formed the Quiverous consortium to issue a Euro

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but that's not what's happening here; 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch a stablecoin proves they see the format as the winner. [00:05:13]
- It might look like finance is adopting crypto, but it isn't; it's the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. [00:06:12]
- Some might think this ends with banks becoming crypto companies, but it actually ends with crypto becoming the infrastructure for the dollar. [00:08:45]
