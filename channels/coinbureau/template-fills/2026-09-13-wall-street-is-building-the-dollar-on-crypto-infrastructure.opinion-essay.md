## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and Washington still owns the currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline in January 2027. That clock forced every major financial institution to pick a side: build or be left out. So they built. BlackRock, ICE, DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard are now validators on Circle's Arc, a permissioned network. Then 21 banks, including Goldman Sachs and Citi, formed a consortium to launch their own regulated dollar stablecoin by H1 2027. Stripe, Visa, Coinbase, and 140+ firms back OpenUSD. You don't spend two years building a competitor to something you think is a fad.

The deeper point: this isn't finance adopting crypto; it's the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries. A shopkeeper in Lagos holding digital dollars is financing the US government without a bank account. Tether alone holds $141B in Treasuries, ranking above South Korea and Saudi Arabia as a US creditor. Washington is counting on this: Treasury Secretary Bessent projects a tenfold stablecoin market growth, and the Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee models trillions in new Treasury demand. This is the cheapest dollar expansion available—no trade deals needed.

Europe's panic confirms the play. 37 institutions across 15 countries formed Quivous to issue a Euro stablecoin, and ECB President Lagarde warns of 'digital dollarization.' Their defensive alliance is the clearest evidence that the US move is real. Crypto built the rails, but Washington owns the currency.

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—companies do it all the time. But 21 banks forming a consortium to launch a competitor is not a fad.
- Some might see this as crypto finally winning, but the host argues it's the dollar winning, taking over the network that was supposed to replace it.
- The Clarity Act's path through the Senate is still uncertain, with deadlines shifting, which could delay the stablecoin race.
