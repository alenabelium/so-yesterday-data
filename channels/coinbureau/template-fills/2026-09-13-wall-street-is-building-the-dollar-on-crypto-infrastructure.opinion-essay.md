## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and the GENIUS Act's deadline is forcing the race.

## Argument

The mechanism is a law with a clock. The GENIUS Act, signed July 2025, creates a federal stablecoin framework with a hard effective date in January 2027. After that, issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. The OCC targets November to finalize rules, but they've already missed one deadline—so the race is on.

Wall Street's response is ownership, not adoption. Circle's Arc is a permissioned network where validators are named companies: BlackRock, ICE (owner of NYSE), and DTCC (settles every American stock trade). Visa and MasterCard, who called crypto an experiment for a decade, now help operate it. Then, 21 banks including Goldman Sachs, Citi, and Bank of America announced a joint venture for their own regulated stablecoin targeting H1 2027, just after the compliance window shuts. Stripe, Visa, MasterCard, Coinbase, and 140+ companies back OpenUSD, which shares reserve earnings. You don't spend two years building a competitor to a fad.

This is the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by US debt, so a shopkeeper in Lagos or an Argentine paying a supplier is financing the American government without a bank account. Tether holds $141B in Treasuries—more than South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. Washington is counting on it: Treasury Secretary Bessent sees tenfold growth, and the crypto czar sees trillions in new demand for Treasuries, pushing rates down. The Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee and Brookings have modeled it. This is the cheapest dollar expansion available.

Europe's panic is the proof. 37

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch a stablecoin proves they think the format has won.
- This looks like finance adopting crypto, but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network.
- Crypto natives argued whether TradFi would accept crypto; the answer is neither yes nor no—it took the tech and rebuilt the dollar.
