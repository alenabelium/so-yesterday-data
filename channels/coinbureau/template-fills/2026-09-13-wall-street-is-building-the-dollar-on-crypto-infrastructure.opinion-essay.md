## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and the dollar—not crypto—will be the ultimate winner.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory and infrastructural. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline in January 2027. That deadline forced every major financial player to pick a side, and they chose to build on crypto rails rather than fight them. BlackRock, ICE, DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard are now running validator nodes on Circle's Arc, a permissioned network. These aren't experiments; they're ownership plays. The Intercontinental Exchange, which owns the NYSE, and DTCC, which settles every American stock trade, have never needed anyone's blockchain. Their presence signals that the format has already won.

But the deeper story is about the dollar itself. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries. That means a shopkeeper in Lagos holding digital dollars is financing the US government without a bank account and without Washington lifting a finger. Tether alone holds over $141 billion in Treasuries, making it the 17th largest holder of US debt—more than South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. Washington isn't just tolerating this; it's counting on it. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent projects the stablecoin market could grow tenfold by the end of the decade, and the White House crypto czar sees trillions in new demand for Treasuries, pushing long-term rates down. This is the cheapest dollar expansion available, and it doesn't require a trade deal.

- Running a node is no big deal; companies do it all the time. But 21 banks, including Goldman Sachs and Citi, forming a joint venture to launch their own stablecoin is not a fad—it's a commitment.
