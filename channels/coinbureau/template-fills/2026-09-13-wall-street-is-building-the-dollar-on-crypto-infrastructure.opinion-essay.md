## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and Washington still owns the currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is the dollar's delivery network. International wires crawl through correspondent banks with cutoff times and banking-day delays, taking three to four days for a distance light covers instantly. Nobody designed this system; it accumulated over a century. Finance has known for 30 years, but regulators never allowed a replacement. The GENIUS Act changed that, creating a federal stablecoin framework with a hard effective date of January 2027. Once the OCC finalizes its rulebook, issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That deadline triggered a race to own the rails.

Circle's Arc blockchain is permissioned, with named validators: BlackRock, ICE (owner of the NYSE), DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard. These aren't passive participants; they own the infrastructure. In September 2026, 21 banks including Goldman Sachs, Citi, and Bank of America announced a joint venture for their own regulated dollar stablecoin, targeting launch right after the compliance window shuts. Stripe, Visa, MasterCard, Coinbase, and 140+ companies back OpenUSD, sharing reserve earnings. You don't build a competitor to a fad; they've decided the format won and they'd rather own it than rent it.

This isn't finance adopting crypto; it's the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries, so a shopkeeper in Lagos or an Argentine paying a supplier is financing the US government without a bank account or Washington lifting a finger. Tether's Treasury exposure was $141 billion as of Q1 2026, making it the 17th largest

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal; companies do it all the time. But 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch their own stablecoin proves they think the format has won.
- Some might see this as finance adopting crypto, but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network, with stablecoins backed by US debt financing the government.
- Europe's defensive response—a Euro stablecoin consortium and Lagarde's 'digital dollarization' warning—shows the stakes, but it's not clear whose win this ultimately is.
