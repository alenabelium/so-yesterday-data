## Thesis

Wall Street is moving the dollar onto crypto infrastructure, not adopting crypto as money.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, creates a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline in January 2027. That deadline forced institutions to act: BlackRock, ICE, and DTCC are now founding validators on Circle's Arc, a permissioned network. This isn't adoption; it's the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by US Treasuries, so a shopkeeper in Lagos holding digital dollars is financing the American government. Tether alone holds $141 billion in US debt, ranking above South Korea or Saudi Arabia. Washington is counting on this: Treasury Secretary Bessent projects a tenfold growth in stablecoins, and the Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee models trillions in new demand for US debt. The clearest proof is the defensive reaction: 37 European banks formed a consortium to issue a euro stablecoin, and ECB President Lagarde warns of 'digital dollarization.' Crypto built the system, but Washington still owns the currency.

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch their own stablecoin shows they think the format has won.
- This looks like finance adopting crypto, but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network.
- Crypto natives argued whether TradFi would accept crypto; the answer is neither yes nor no—it took the tech to rebuild the dollar.
