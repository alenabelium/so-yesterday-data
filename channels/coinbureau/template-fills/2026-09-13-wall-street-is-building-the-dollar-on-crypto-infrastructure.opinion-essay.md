## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, turning stablecoins into the new delivery network for the world's reserve currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, created a federal framework for stablecoins with a hard compliance deadline of January 2027. Once the OCC finalizes its rulebook, issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That clock forced every major financial institution to pick a side—and they chose to own the system rather than rent it. [00:02:07]

Circle's Arc blockchain is the proof. BlackRock, ICE (owner of the NYSE), DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard are all running validator nodes on a permissioned network. These aren't passive investments; they're operational commitments. [00:04:01] Then, in September 2026, 21 banks—including Goldman Sachs, Citi, and Bank of America—announced a joint venture to launch their own regulated dollar stablecoin, targeting the first half of 2027. You don't spend two years building a competitor to something you think is a fad. [00:05:13]

The deeper point: this isn't finance adopting crypto. It's the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries, so a shopkeeper in Lagos holding digital dollars is financing the American government without a bank account. Tether's Treasury exposure was $141 billion as of Q1 2026—more than South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. Washington is counting on this: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says the stablecoin market could grow tenfold, and the White House crypto czar sees trillions in new demand for US debt. [00:06:18]
