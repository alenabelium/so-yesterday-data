## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and Washington still owns the currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline in January 2027. Once the OCC finalizes its rulebook (targeted for November), issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That clock forced the incumbents to move.

So they did. Circle's Arc blockchain, launching September 16, is a permissioned network where the validators are named companies: BlackRock, ICE (owner of the NYSE), DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard. These aren't experiments; they're ownership plays. And the consortium activity confirms it: 21 banks including Goldman, Citi, and BofA are building their own regulated dollar stablecoin for 2027, and Stripe, Visa, MasterCard, Coinbase, and 140+ others back OpenUSD, which shares reserve earnings with distribution partners. You don't build a competitor to a fad.

The real story is the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries, so a shopkeeper in Lagos or an Argentine paying a supplier is financing the US government without a bank account or Washington lifting a finger. Tether's Treasury exposure is ~$141 billion, making it the 17th largest holder of US debt—above South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. Washington is counting on this: Treasury Secretary Bessent sees a tenfold stablecoin market by decade's end; crypto czar David Sacks sees trillions in new Treasury demand pushing rates down. The Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee and Brookings have modeled it. This is the cheapest dollar expansion

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch their own stablecoin shows they think the format has already won.
- Some might see this as finance adopting crypto, but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network.
- Europe's defensive alliance and Lagarde's 'digital dollarization' warning are the clearest evidence of what the American move actually is.
