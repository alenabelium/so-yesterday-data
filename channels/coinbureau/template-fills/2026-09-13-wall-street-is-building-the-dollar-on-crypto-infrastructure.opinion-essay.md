## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and Washington still owns the currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline in January 2027. Once the OCC finalizes its rulebook (targeted for November), issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That clock forced institutions to move. Circle's Arc blockchain, launching September 16, lists BlackRock, ICE (NYSE's parent), and DTCC as founding validators—institutions that never needed anyone's blockchain. Visa and MasterCard, once dismissive, now help operate the network. This isn't participation; it's ownership. Arc is permissioned, with named companies running infrastructure. And the race is broader: 21 banks (Goldman, Citi, BofA) announced a joint venture for their own regulated stablecoin targeting H1 2027, just after the compliance window. Stripe, Visa, MasterCard, Coinbase, and 140+ firms back OpenUSD, sharing reserve earnings. You don't spend two years building a competitor to a fad. The format has won; they'd rather own the system than rent it.

The deeper play is dollar dominance. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries. A shopkeeper in Lagos holding digital dollars is financing the US government without a bank account. Tether's Treasury exposure ($141B as of Q1 2026) makes it the 17th largest holder of US debt—above South Korea, Saudi Arabia, Australia. Washington is counting on this: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent sees stablecoin market growing tenfold; crypto czar David Sacks sees trillions in new Treasury demand, pushing rates down. ## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but that's not what's happening here; it's a permissioned network with named validators, and 21 banks are building a competitor, which you don't do for a fad.
- Some might see this as finance adopting crypto—but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network, with stablecoins backed by Treasuries financing the US government.
- Crypto natives argued whether TradFi would accept crypto—the answer is neither yes nor no; TradFi took the tech and rebuilt the dollar, so crypto becomes infrastructure but Washington owns the currency.
