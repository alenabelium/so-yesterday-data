## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and Washington still owns the currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is legal and infrastructural. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline of January 2027. Once the OCC finalizes its rulebook (targeted for November), issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That clock forced every major financial player to pick a side. [00:02:07]

So they chose to own the rails. Circle's Arc blockchain is permissioned, with named validators: BlackRock, ICE (owner of the NYSE), DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard. These aren't passive experiments; they're running the infrastructure. [00:04:01] Then in September 2026, 21 banks including Goldman Sachs, Citi, and Bank of America announced a joint venture to launch their own regulated dollar stablecoin, targeting H1 2027—just after the compliance window shuts. [00:05:13] You don't spend two years building a competitor to something you think is a fad. They've decided the format has won, and they'd rather own the system than rent it. [00:05:57]

The deeper point: this isn't finance adopting crypto; it's the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries. A shopkeeper in Lagos or an Argentine paying a supplier is financing the American government without a bank account, without Washington lifting a finger. Tether's Treasury exposure was $141 billion as of Q1 2026—more than South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. ## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but that's not what's happening here; 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch a competing stablecoin shows they see the format as the winner. [00:05:13]
- Some might think finance is adopting crypto, but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network, with stablecoins backed by US debt financing the government. [00:06:12]
- Europe's defensive response—the Quiverous consortium and Lagarde's 'digital dollarization' warning—is the clearest evidence that this is a dollar power play, not a crypto adoption story. [00:07:46]
