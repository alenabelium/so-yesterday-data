## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto infrastructure, making crypto the delivery network for the world's reserve currency while Washington retains ownership.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory and infrastructural. The GENIUS Act, signed July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline of January 2027. That deadline forced institutions to move: BlackRock, ICE, DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard now run validator nodes on Circle's Arc, a permissioned network. These aren't experiments—they're ownership plays. A consortium of 21 banks, including Goldman and Citi, is launching its own stablecoin for 2027, and over 140 companies back OpenUSD. The dollar is acquiring a new distribution network, not adopting crypto as money.

The economic logic is clear: every compliant stablecoin is backed by US Treasuries. Tether alone holds $141 billion in US debt, ranking above South Korea or Saudi Arabia. Washington sees this as the cheapest dollar expansion—Treasury Secretary Bessent projects a tenfold stablecoin growth, and the White House crypto czar cites trillions in new Treasury demand. Europe's defensive consortium and Lagarde's 'digital dollarization' warning confirm the stakes. Crypto built the rails, but Washington owns the currency.

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch a stablecoin shows they're not treating it as a fad.
- This looks like finance adopting crypto, but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network.
