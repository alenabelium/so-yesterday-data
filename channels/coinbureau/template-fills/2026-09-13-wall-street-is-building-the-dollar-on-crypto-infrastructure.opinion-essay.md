## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and Washington still owns the currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline in January 2027. Once the OCC finalizes its rulebook, issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That clock forced every major financial institution to pick a side—and they chose to build, not rent.

Circle's Arc blockchain, with founding validators including BlackRock, ICE, and DTCC, is a permissioned network run by named companies. These aren't passive participants; they're owning the infrastructure. And the consortium of 21 banks, including Goldman Sachs and Citi, launching their own regulated stablecoin by H1 2027, proves the format has won. You don't spend two years building a competitor to a fad.

The real prize is dollar dominance. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by US Treasuries, so a shopkeeper in Lagos or an Argentine paying a supplier is financing the US government without a bank account. Tether alone holds more US debt than South Korea or Saudi Arabia. Treasury Secretary Bessent and crypto czar David Sacks see a tenfold growth in stablecoin demand, pushing long-term rates down. This is the cheapest dollar expansion available—no trade deals needed.

Europe's defensive alliance, the Quivorus consortium, and ECB President Lagarde's warning of 'digital dollarization' confirm the stakes. Crypto built the system, but Washington owns the currency. The endgame isn't banks becoming crypto companies; it's crypto becoming the infrastructure for the world's reserve currency.

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch their own stablecoin shows they're not just participating, they're owning.
- Some might see this as finance adopting crypto, but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network.
- Europe's defensive alliance and Lagarde's 'digital dollarization' warning are evidence of the American move, not a counterpoint.
