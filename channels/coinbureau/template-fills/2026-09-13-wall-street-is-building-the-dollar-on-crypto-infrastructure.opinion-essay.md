## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and Washington still owns the currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline of January 2027. Once the OCC finalizes its rulebook (targeted for November), issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That clock forced every major financial institution to pick a side—and they chose to own the rails rather than rent them.

Circle's Arc blockchain is a permissioned network, not an open one. Its founding validators include BlackRock, ICE (owner of the NYSE), DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard—institutions that have never needed anyone's blockchain. They're not participating; they're building the system. Then, in September 2026, 21 banks including Goldman Sachs, Citi, and Bank of America announced a joint venture to launch their own regulated stablecoin, targeting H1 2027—right after the compliance window shuts. Separately, Stripe, Visa, MasterCard, Coinbase, and 140+ companies back OpenUSD, which shares reserve earnings with distribution partners. You don't spend two years building a competitor to something you think is a fad.

The real story is the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries. A shopkeeper in Lagos holding digital dollars is financing the US government without a bank account. Tether's Treasury exposure was $141 billion as of Q1 2026—more than South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. ## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch their own stablecoin shows they're not just participating, they're owning.
- Some might see this as crypto winning by becoming the infrastructure—but the host argues it's the dollar winning, taking over the network that was supposed to replace it.
- Europe's defensive consortium and Lagarde's warnings are framed as evidence of the dollar's move, not as a viable counter-strategy.
