## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and Washington still owns the currency.

## Argument

The mechanism is a law with a clock. The GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, creates a federal stablecoin framework with a hard effective date in January 2027. Once the OCC finalizes its rulebook (targeted for November), issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That deadline forced the race: BlackRock, ICE, DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard are now validators on Circle's Arc, a permissioned network where institutions run the infrastructure themselves. Then, in September 2026, 21 banks—Goldman, Citi, BofA—announced a joint venture for their own regulated stablecoin, targeting H1 2027. Stripe, Visa, MasterCard, Coinbase, and 140+ companies back OpenUSD, which shares reserve earnings with distribution partners. You don't spend two years building a competitor to a fad. They've decided the format won, and they'd rather own the system than rent it.

But this isn't finance adopting crypto; it's the dollar acquiring a new distribution network. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries, so a shopkeeper in Lagos or an Argentine escaping the peso is financing the US government without a bank account. Tether's Treasury exposure hit $141 billion in Q1 2026, making it the 17th largest holder of US debt—above South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. Washington is counting on this: Treasury Secretary Bessent says the stablecoin market could grow tenfold by decade's end; crypto czar David Sacks sees trillions in new Treasury demand pushing rates down. The TBAC and Brookings have modeled it. This is the cheapest dollar expansion available, no trade deal

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch a competitor proves they think the format has already won.
- One might think finance is adopting crypto, but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network, with stablecoins financing US debt.
- Europe's defensive Euro stablecoin consortium and Lagarde's 'digital dollarization' warning show the geopolitical stakes.
