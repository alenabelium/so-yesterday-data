## Thesis

Wall Street isn't adopting crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's infrastructure, and this shift will cement dollar dominance by making it the cheapest, fastest global money.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline: after January 2027, issuing an unauthorized dollar stablecoin becomes a crime. That clock forced every major financial institution to pick a side, and they chose to own the rails rather than rent them. [00:02:07][00:02:43]

Circle's Arc blockchain is the proof. BlackRock, ICE (owner of the NYSE), DTCC, Visa, and MasterCard are all founding validators—not as an experiment, but as infrastructure operators. [00:04:01][00:04:26] And it's not just one network: 21 banks including Goldman Sachs, Citi, and Bank of America announced a joint venture for their own regulated stablecoin, targeting launch right after the compliance window shuts. [00:05:23] You don't spend two years building a competitor to something you think is a fad.

The deeper point is who benefits. Every compliant stablecoin is backed by short-dated US Treasuries. A shopkeeper in Lagos or an Argentine paying a supplier is financing the American government without a bank account, without Washington lifting a finger. [00:06:12] Tether alone holds more US debt than South Korea, Saudi Arabia, or Australia. [00:06:39] Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says the stablecoin market could grow tenfold by decade's end, and the White House crypto czar sees trillions in new Treasury demand pushing rates down. [00:07:12] This is the cheapest dollar expansion available—no trade deals needed.

Europe's panic confirms the play. 37 institutions across 15 countries formed a consortium to issue a Euro

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—companies do it all the time. But the scale here—21 banks forming a joint venture—shows they're not just participating, they're owning the system. [00:05:13]
- Some might see this as crypto finally winning, but the host argues it's the dollar winning, taking over the network that was supposed to replace it. [00:09:00]
