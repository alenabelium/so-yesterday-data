## Thesis

Wall Street isn't absorbing crypto; it's moving the dollar onto crypto's rails to preserve dollar dominance.

## Argument

The mechanism is regulatory. The GENIUS Act, signed July 2025, created a federal stablecoin framework with a hard compliance deadline of January 2027. That deadline forced the hands of traditional finance. BlackRock, ICE, Visa, and MasterCard are now validators on Circle's Arc, a permissioned network. They're not adopting crypto as money; they're using its infrastructure to modernize dollar settlement. The economic incentive is clear: stablecoins backed by US Treasuries effectively finance the US government. Tether alone holds $141 billion in Treasuries, ranking it above South Korea or Saudi Arabia as a creditor. Washington sees this as the cheapest dollar expansion available, with Treasury Secretary Bessent projecting a tenfold growth in stablecoin markets. The proof is in the defensive reaction: 37 European banks formed a consortium to issue a euro stablecoin, and ECB President Lagarde warned of 'digital dollarization.' The conclusion: crypto built the system, but Washington still owns the currency.

## Counterpoints

- Running a node is no big deal—but 21 banks forming a joint venture to launch their own stablecoin shows they see it as a strategic necessity, not a fad.
- This looks like finance adopting crypto—but it's actually the dollar acquiring a new distribution network, with stablecoins backed by US debt.
