## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of American tech giants and making their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The collapse began with DeepSeek's R1 release in January 2025, which matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away for free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash have continued the trend, with Chinese open-weight models now just 2-5 months behind American frontier models while costing a fraction of the price. This price pressure has forced American labs to cut prices and even release open-weight models, but the real response has been a shift to physical infrastructure. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta have poured $511 billion into data centers, with spending projected to top $650 billion this year. However, this construction boom faces severe bottlenecks: nearly half of planned US data center capacity for 2026 has been delayed or cancelled due to grid connection waits of 4-7 years, transformer lead times of 3-5 years, and a reliance on Chinese suppliers for critical equipment. Meanwhile, China added 543 gigawatts of power capacity in 2025 and is building data centers next to power sources, avoiding the grid queue entirely. The export controls meant to slow China down backfired, forcing Chinese labs to become more efficient with Mixture of Experts architectures and domestic chips like Huawei's Ascend, which now holds half of China's AI accelerator market. This dynamic mirrors the telecom bubble of the late 1990s, where massive fiber overbuilding led to a 90% price collapse and $2 trillion in market value wiped out, yet the infrastructure remained and was later exploited

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that American labs still lead in frontier model quality, and the premium for the hardest tasks will sustain their business model.
- Others believe that the massive infrastructure investment will eventually pay off as demand for AI grows, similar to how the internet's fiber eventually became profitable.
- There is a view that export controls and government support will protect American AI dominance, despite the current bottlenecks.
- Skeptics question whether decentralized networks can match the scale and reliability of centralized data centers for enterprise AI workloads.
