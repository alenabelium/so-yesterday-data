## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of US tech giants and turning their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet into a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's R1, released in January 2025, matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away for free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi K3, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash (MIT-licensed) have kept the pressure on. Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 charges $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens; DeepSeek's flash model charges 15 cents on input during off-peak hours. Chinese open-weight models are only 2-5 months behind the closed American frontier, while America's open models lag 6-9 months. So companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity route routine workloads to cheaper Chinese models, reserving premium models for the hardest tasks—which are a small fraction of the work. American labs have responded by cutting prices and releasing open-weight models, but the real response has been physical: Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta poured $511 billion into steel, power, and chips over the last 12 months, with AI infrastructure spending projected to top $650 billion this year. Data center construction spending now exceeds $75 billion a year, ahead of office buildings. But this fixed investment faces bottlenecks: power grid queues of 4-7 years, transformer lead times of 3-5 years, and a 30% dependence on Chinese suppliers for grid equipment. ## Counterpoints

- Some argue that American labs still lead in frontier model quality, and the premium on the hardest tasks will sustain their business model.
- Others believe that the massive infrastructure buildout will eventually pay off as demand for AI grows, just as the fiber optic buildout eventually enabled the internet boom.
- A common objection is that China's power advantage and supply chain dominance are overstated, and US grid bottlenecks will be resolved with policy and investment.
