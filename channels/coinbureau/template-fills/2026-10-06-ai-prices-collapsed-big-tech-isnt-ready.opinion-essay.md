## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of US tech giants and turning their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet into a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price competition. DeepSeek's R1 matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash (MIT-licensed) have kept the pressure on. Chinese open-weight models are only 2-5 months behind US frontier models, while US open models lag 6-9 months. Companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity now route routine workloads to cheap Chinese models, reserving premium US models for the hardest tasks. The US response has been to go physical: Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta have poured $511 billion into data centers, power, and chips, with spending projected to top $650 billion this year. But this infrastructure bet faces severe bottlenecks. Nearly half of planned US data center capacity for 2026 has been cancelled or delayed due to grid connection waits of 4-7 years, and large power transformers now take 3-5 years to arrive. Meanwhile, China added 543 GW of power capacity in 2025 and is building models next to the power source. The chip supply is also concentrated: Nvidia controls ~75% of AI accelerator revenue, and TSMC fabricates nearly all advanced chips, with Nvidia's share of TSMC revenue growing from 4% in 2018 to 20% by 2026. Export controls meant to slow China backfired, pushing Chinese labs to innovate with Mixture of Experts architectures and Huawei's Ascend chips, which now hold ~50% of China's AI accelerator market. ## Counterpoints

- US labs are cutting prices and releasing open-weight models, suggesting they can adapt to competition.
- The telecom fiber analogy is imperfect: AI compute is not a commodity like bandwidth; it's a scarce, high-value resource.
- US export controls and infrastructure investment could still create a durable moat in hardware and power.
