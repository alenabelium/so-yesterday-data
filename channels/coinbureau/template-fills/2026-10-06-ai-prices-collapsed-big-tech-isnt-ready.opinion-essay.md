## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of American tech giants and turning their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet into a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's R1 matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash (MIT-licensed) have kept the pressure on. American labs charge per token: Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 is $4/$20 per million input/output tokens, while DeepSeek's flash model charges 15 cents on input during off-peak. Chinese open-weight models are only 2-5 months behind the closed frontier, while American open models lag 6-9 months. So companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity route routine workloads to cheap Chinese models, reserving premium models for the hardest tasks—which are a small slice of work.

With software margins collapsing, the American AI trade went physical. The big four (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta) poured $511 billion into steel, power, and chips over 12 months, with spending projected to top $650 billion this year. Data center construction now exceeds $75 billion annually, outpacing office buildings. But this physical bet faces bottlenecks: nearly half of planned 2026 data center capacity has been delayed or cancelled due to grid connection waits of 4-7 years, and power transformer lead times have stretched to 3-5 years. Meanwhile, China added 543 GW of power capacity in 2025 and builds next to the power source, like DeepSeek's 160,000 H100 chips in Inner Mongolia.

## Counterpoints

- American labs are fighting back: OpenAI released open-weight models and halved GPT-6 prices, Anthropic cut prices, and Google launched Gemini at aggressive rates.
- The scarcity model might survive on the hardest tasks, which command premium pricing even as routine workloads shift to cheap models.
- America's physical infrastructure, though delayed, could still provide a durable edge that software cannot copy overnight.
- The telecom bubble analogy is imperfect: demand for AI compute is growing faster than bandwidth did, and the infrastructure may be more fully utilized.
