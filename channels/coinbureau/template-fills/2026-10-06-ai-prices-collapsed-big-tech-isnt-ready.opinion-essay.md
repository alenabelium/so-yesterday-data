## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of US tech giants and turning their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet into a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's R1, released in January 2025, matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away for free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash have continued to undercut US models on price, with Chinese open-weight models now only 2-5 months behind the US frontier, while US open models lag 6-9 months. The premium for the best model only survives on the hardest tasks, which are a small fraction of the work. Companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity now route routine workloads to cheaper Chinese models. In response, US labs have cut prices and even released open-weight models, but the real shift is physical: Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta have poured $511 billion into data centers, power, and chips, with spending projected to top $650 billion this year. This construction bet faces hard constraints: nearly half of planned US data center capacity for 2026 has been cancelled or delayed due to grid connection waits of 4-7 years, transformer lead times of 3-5 years, and a 30% dependence on Chinese suppliers for grid equipment. Meanwhile, China added 543 gigawatts of new power capacity in 2025 and is building data centers next to power sources. The chip supply is even more concentrated: Nvidia controls ~75% of AI accelerator revenue, and nearly all chips are fabricated by TSMC in Taiwan. ## Counterpoints

- US labs could maintain a lead by building smarter frontier models, but China's open-weight models are only 2-5 months behind, and the premium only survives on the hardest tasks.
- America's infrastructure bet could pay off if demand for AI grows faster than expected, but history shows that even when demand grows, it may not grow fast enough to justify the investment.
- Export controls were meant to slow China down, but they backfired by forcing Chinese labs to become more efficient, creating the very price pressure now threatening the US trade.
