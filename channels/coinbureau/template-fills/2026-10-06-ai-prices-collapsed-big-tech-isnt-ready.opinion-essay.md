## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of US tech giants and turning their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet into a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is a price war. DeepSeek's R1 matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away for free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash (MIT-licensed) have undercut US models on price—DeepSeek charges 15 cents per million input tokens versus Anthropic's $4—while staying only 2-5 months behind in quality. Companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity now route routine workloads to cheap Chinese models, reserving premium US models for the hardest tasks, which are a small fraction of the work.

Facing this, US labs have cut prices and even opened their models, but the real response has been physical: Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta poured $511 billion into infrastructure, with data center construction spending exceeding $75 billion a year. This bet assumes that if software is commoditized, the edge lies in the machinery. Yet that machinery is bottlenecked: nearly half of planned US data center capacity for 2026 has been delayed or cancelled due to grid connection waits of 4-7 years, transformer lead times of 3-5 years, and a reliance on Chinese suppliers for 30% of grid equipment. Meanwhile, China added 543 GW of power in 2025 and builds its data centers next to the power source.

## Counterpoints

- US labs are cutting prices and releasing open-weight models, suggesting they can adapt to the price war.
- The telecom bubble analogy is imperfect: demand for AI compute is growing rapidly, and the infrastructure may be used more than 5% of the time.
- US export controls could still slow China's progress, and the US retains leadership in frontier model quality.
- Decentralized AI networks face their own scalability and trust challenges, and may not be able to compete with centralized giants.
