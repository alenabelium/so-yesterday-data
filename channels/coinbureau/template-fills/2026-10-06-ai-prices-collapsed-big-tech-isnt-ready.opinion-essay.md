## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of US tech giants and turning their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet into a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is a price war. DeepSeek's R1, released in January 2025, matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks at 1.5% of Anthropic's cost, and it was free to download. Within a week, Nvidia lost $589 billion in market value—the largest one-day loss in Wall Street history. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen has overtaken Meta's Llama as the most downloaded open model, and Chinese open-weight models now trail the US frontier by only 2–5 months, while America's own open models lag by 6–9 months. Companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity now route routine workloads to cheap Chinese models, reserving premium models for the hardest tasks—which are a small fraction of the work. The US response has been to go physical: Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta poured $511 billion into data centers, power, and chips over the last 12 months, with spending projected to top $650 billion this year. But this construction bet faces hard constraints. Nearly half of planned US data center capacity for 2026 has been delayed or cancelled due to grid connection waits of 4–7 years, and large power transformers now take 3–5 years to arrive. Meanwhile, China added 543 gigawatts of new power capacity in 2025 and builds its data centers next to power sources, like DeepSeek's 160,000 H100 cluster in Inner Mongolia. The chip supply is another choke point: Nvidia controls 75% of AI accelerator revenue, and nearly all chips are fabricated by TSMC in Taiwan. US export controls aimed at China backfired, pushing Chinese labs to innovate with Mixture of Experts architectures that cut costs and memory

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that US frontier models remain superior, but Chinese open-weight models are only 2–5 months behind, and the gap is closing.
- Others claim that infrastructure spending will eventually pay off, but the telecom bubble shows that overbuilding ahead of demand can wipe out trillions.
- A common objection is that export controls will protect US chip dominance, but they backfired by forcing Chinese labs to become more efficient and boosting domestic chip makers like Huawei.
- Skeptics say decentralized AI networks are unproven, but they offer a way to utilize existing idle GPUs and bypass the grid and supply chain bottlenecks.
- Some believe that the US will simply outspend China, but China's power advantage and cheaper models undermine the economics of that bet.
