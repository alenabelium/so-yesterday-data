## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the US tech industry's scarcity-based business model and turning America's trillion-dollar infrastructure bet into a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's R1, released in January 2025, matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi K3, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash (MIT-licensed) have kept the pressure on, with Chinese open-weight models just 2-5 months behind US frontier models while American open models lag 6-9 months. The premium for access only survives on the hardest tasks, which are a small slice of actual work—DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity already route routine workloads to cheaper Chinese models.

Facing this, US labs have cut prices, but their real response has been to go physical: Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta poured $511 billion into infrastructure, with data center construction spending now exceeding $75 billion a year. This fixed investment is racing a product whose price keeps falling, and the bottlenecks are severe: power grid queues of 4-7 years, transformer lead times of 3-5 years, and even a Texas moratorium on new data center grid approvals. Meanwhile, China added 543 GW of power in 2025 and builds its data centers next to the power source, sidestepping the queue entirely.

The export controls meant to slow China down backfired, pushing Chinese labs into Mixture of Experts architectures that do more with less—DeepSeek's V4 Pro activates only 49 billion of 1.6 trillion parameters per request.
