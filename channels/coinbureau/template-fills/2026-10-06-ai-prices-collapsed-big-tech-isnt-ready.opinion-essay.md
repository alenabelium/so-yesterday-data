## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, and America's trillion-dollar infrastructure bet is racing a product whose price keeps falling, risking a repeat of the telecom bubble.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's R1 matched OpenAI's o1 at a fraction of the cost, and its latest V4.1 Flash charges 15 cents per million input tokens versus Anthropic's $4. Chinese open-weight models are now just 2-5 months behind the US frontier, while US open models lag 6-9 months. Companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity already route routine workloads to cheaper Chinese models, reserving premium models for the hardest tasks—a small slice of the work. This forces US labs to cut prices and even open their own weights, but the real response has been physical: $511 billion in capex from Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta, with data center construction now outpacing office buildings. Yet power is the bottleneck—nearly half of planned 2026 US data center capacity is delayed or cancelled, and grid connection waits run 4 years in key markets. Meanwhile, China added 543 GW of new power in 2025 and builds next to the grid. The chip supply is even more concentrated: Nvidia controls 75% of AI accelerator revenue, and TSMC fabricates nearly all of it in Taiwan. Export controls backfired, pushing China to innovate with Mixture of Experts and Huawei's Ascend chips, which now hold half of China's AI accelerator market. History rhymes: the telecom bubble saw $2 trillion wiped out when bandwidth prices collapsed, but the fiber stayed in the ground, and the next internet was built on it by companies that didn't pay to lay it. ## Counterpoints

- US labs are responding by cutting prices and releasing open-weight models, but this only accelerates the commoditization of AI.
- The infrastructure buildout is a decade-long commitment, and you can't unpour concrete—but that's also a moat that's hard to replicate.
- China's models are still 2-5 months behind the US frontier, so the premium survives on the hardest tasks, but those are a small part of the work.
