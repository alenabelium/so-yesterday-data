## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, and America's trillion-dollar infrastructure bet on scarcity is set to become a financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's R1 matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and gave it away, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia and topping the US app store. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen overtook Meta's Llama as the most downloaded model family, Moonshot's Kimi K3 topped coding leaderboards, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash shipped under an MIT license. American labs charge per token, with Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 per million tokens, while DeepSeek's flash charges 15 cents on input during off-peak. Chinese open-weight models sit just 2–5 months behind the closed frontier, while America's open models lag 6–9 months. So companies like DoorDash route routine workloads to Kimi, Airbnb uses Qwen, and Perplexity uses DeepSeek. The premium only survives on the hardest tasks, which are a small slice of work. OpenAI responded by releasing open-weight models and halving GPT-6's price; Anthropic and Google cut prices too. China attacked price, not frontier intelligence. With software margins gone, the US trade went physical: Amazon spent $173B on capex, the big four will top $650B this year, and data center construction spending exceeds office buildings. But power is the bottleneck: nearly half of planned 2026 capacity is delayed or cancelled, grid connection waits run 4–7 years, and transformer lead times stretch to 3–5 years. China added 543 GW of power in 2025 and builds next to the source. ## Counterpoints

- Some argue America's lead in frontier models and hardware will keep it ahead, but Chinese models are only 2–5 months behind and catching up.
- Others claim export controls will protect US dominance, but they backfired by forcing China to become more efficient and develop its own chips.
- A common defense is that infrastructure spending will pay off eventually, but history shows telecom overbuild led to massive losses for the builders.
- Skeptics say decentralized AI networks are unproven, but they address real bottlenecks like idle GPUs and verification.
- Some believe demand for AI will grow enough to justify the buildout, but the telecom precedent shows demand rarely keeps pace with overbuilding.
