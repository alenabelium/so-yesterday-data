## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of American tech giants and turning their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet into a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is a price war. DeepSeek's R1, released in January 2025, matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away for free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen has overtaken Meta's Llama as the most downloaded model family on Hugging Face, Moonshot's Kimi K3 topped the coding leaderboard, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash was released under an MIT license. The cost gap is stark: Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 charges $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, while DeepSeek's flash model charges 15 cents on the input side during off-peak hours. Chinese open-weight models are only 2 to 5 months behind the closed American frontier, while America's open models lag 6 to 9 months. Companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity now route routine workloads to cheaper Chinese models, reserving premium models for the hardest tasks—which are a small fraction of the work. American labs have responded by cutting prices and releasing open-weight models, but the software edge is eroding. So the American AI trade went physical: Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta poured $511 billion into steel, power, and chips over the last 12 months, with spending projected to top $650 billion this year. But this infrastructure bet faces severe bottlenecks. Nearly half of US data center capacity planned for 2026 has been cancelled or delayed due to grid connection waits of 4 to 7 years, and large power transformers now take 3 to 5 years to arrive. Texas Governor Greg Abbott halted new data center grid approvals pending an audit.

## Counterpoints

- American labs are responding by cutting prices and releasing open-weight models, but this only accelerates the commoditization of AI software.
- The US could maintain an edge through physical infrastructure, but power grid bottlenecks and supply chain dependencies undermine this strategy.
- Export controls were meant to slow China down, but they backfired by pushing Chinese labs to become more efficient, creating the very threat now pressuring the trade.
