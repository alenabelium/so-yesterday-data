## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, and America's trillion-dollar infrastructure bet is becoming a financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's R1 matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi K3, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash (MIT-licensed) have kept the pressure on. American labs charge per token—Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 per million tokens—while DeepSeek's flash model charges 15 cents on input during off-peak. Chinese open-weight models are only 2–5 months behind the American frontier, while America's open models lag 6–9 months. So companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity route routine workloads to cheap Chinese models, reserving premium models for the hardest tasks. The premium only survives on a small slice of work.

Facing this, American labs cut prices and OpenAI even released open-weight models. But the real response was physical: Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta poured $511 billion into infrastructure, with spending projected to top $650 billion this year. The bet is that software can be copied overnight, but a data center is a decade-long commitment. However, the buildout is hitting walls. Nearly half of planned 2026 data center capacity has been delayed or cancelled due to grid connection waits of 4–7 years. Transformer lead times stretch to 3–5 years, and GE Vernova takes gas turbine orders out to 2031. Meanwhile, China added 543 GW of power in 2025 and builds next to the grid, while America waits in line.
