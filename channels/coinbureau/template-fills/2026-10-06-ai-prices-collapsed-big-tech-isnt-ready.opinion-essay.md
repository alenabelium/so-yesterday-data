## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, and America's trillion-dollar infrastructure bet is racing a product whose price keeps falling, likely ending in a financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek delivers comparable output for 1.5% of Anthropic's price, and anyone can download it. That undercuts the entire Wall Street trade, which was priced on intelligence being scarce and owned by a few American companies. The scarcity model is falling apart. Chinese labs went after price, not frontier quality. Alibaba's Qwen overtook Meta's Llama as the most downloaded model family. Moonshot's Kimi K3 topped coding leaderboards. DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash is MIT-licensed. American labs responded by cutting prices and even opening their own models, but that only accelerates the commoditization. So Big Tech moved the edge into physical infrastructure: $511 billion from Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta in 12 months, with $650 billion projected this year. Data center construction now outpaces office buildings. But this fixed investment is racing a falling price. Power is the bottleneck: nearly half of planned 2026 capacity is delayed or cancelled, grid connections take 4-7 years, and transformers take 3-5 years. China, meanwhile, added 543 gigawatts of power in a year and builds next to the grid. Chips are another choke point: Nvidia controls 75% of AI accelerator revenue, and TSMC fabricates nearly all of it in Taiwan. Export controls backfired, pushing China to Mixture of Experts efficiency and Huawei's Ascend chips. History rhymes: the telecom bubble laid fiber that was only 5% used, prices collapsed 90%, and the builders lost while later companies profited. The same pattern is repeating. ## Counterpoints

- Some argue American labs still lead in frontier quality, but Chinese open-weight models are only 2-5 months behind, while American open models are 6-9 months behind.
- Some say export controls will protect the US, but they backfired by forcing China to become more efficient and develop its own chips.
- Some believe demand will grow to justify the infrastructure, but the telecom bubble shows demand grew, just not fast enough.
- Some think the premium will survive on hardest tasks, but those are a small fraction of the work, as DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity show.
