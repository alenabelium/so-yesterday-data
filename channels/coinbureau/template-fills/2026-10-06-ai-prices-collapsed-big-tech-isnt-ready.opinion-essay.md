## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, and America's trillion-dollar infrastructure bet on scarcity is becoming a financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is a price war. DeepSeek's R1 matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen has overtaken Meta's Llama as the most downloaded model family, Moonshot's Kimi K3 topped coding leaderboards, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash is MIT-licensed. Chinese open-weight models are only 2-5 months behind the US frontier, while US open models lag 6-9 months. The premium for frontier models only survives on the hardest tasks, which are a small fraction of actual work. DoorDash routes routine workloads to Kimi, Airbnb uses Qwen, Perplexity uses DeepSeek. American labs have responded by cutting prices and releasing open-weight models, but their real answer was to go physical: $511 billion in capex from Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta, with data center construction spending now exceeding office buildings. Yet this fixed investment is racing a product whose price keeps falling. Power is the bottleneck: nearly half of planned 2026 data center capacity is delayed or cancelled, grid connections take 4-7 years, and transformer lead times have stretched to 3-5 years. Meanwhile, China added 543 GW of power in 2025 and builds next to the grid. The chip supply is equally fragile: Nvidia controls 75% of AI accelerator revenue, TSMC fabricates nearly all chips in Taiwan, and export controls backfired by pushing Chinese labs to innovate with Mixture of Experts, cutting memory footprints and using Huawei Ascend chips. History rhymes: the telecom bubble saw $2 trillion wiped out when fiber prices

## Counterpoints

- Some argue American labs still lead in frontier quality, but the gap is only 2-5 months and shrinking.
- Export controls were meant to slow China, but they backfired by forcing efficiency gains like Mixture of Experts.
- The telecom bubble analogy is imperfect because demand for AI compute is growing faster than bandwidth did.
