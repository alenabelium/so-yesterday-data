## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of US tech giants and turning their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet into a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's R1 matched OpenAI's o1 at 1.5% of Anthropic's cost, and its V4.1 Flash under MIT license undercuts Claude Opus 5.5 by over 90% on input tokens. Chinese open-weight models trail the US frontier by only 2–5 months, while US open models lag 6–9 months. So enterprises like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity route routine workloads to cheap Chinese models, reserving premium US models for a shrinking slice of hard tasks. US labs respond by cutting prices and releasing open-weight models, but that only accelerates commoditization. So Big Tech pivots to physical infrastructure—$650B in AI capex this year, with Microsoft holding $329B in lease obligations for data centers not yet built. But this bet faces hard constraints: nearly half of planned 2026 US data center capacity is delayed or cancelled due to grid connection waits of 4–7 years, and power transformer lead times stretch to 3–5 years. Meanwhile, China added 543 GW of power in 2025 and builds next to the grid. Export controls backfired, pushing China to Mixture-of-Experts efficiency and Huawei's Ascend chips, which now hold ~50% of China's AI accelerator market. The historical parallel is the telecom bubble: $2T in market value wiped out, but the fiber stayed in the ground and the next internet winners didn't pay to lay it. Decentralized networks like Akash, Render, io.net, Bittensor, and Chainlink are positioned to capitalize on cheap open models by monetizing idle GPUs and providing verifiable compute.

## Counterpoints

- US labs still lead on frontier quality, and the hardest tasks remain premium-priced.
- China's open-weight models may be 2–5 months behind, but the gap could widen if US innovation accelerates.
- Infrastructure spending could create a durable moat that software alone cannot replicate.
- Decentralized AI networks face their own scalability and trust hurdles.
