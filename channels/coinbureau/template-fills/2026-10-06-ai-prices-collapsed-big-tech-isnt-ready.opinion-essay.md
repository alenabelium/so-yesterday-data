## Thesis

China's open-weight AI models have collapsed AI prices, undermining the scarcity-based business model of US tech giants and making their trillion-dollar infrastructure bet a potential financial blunder.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's R1 matched OpenAI's o1 on key benchmarks and was given away free, triggering a $589 billion single-day loss for Nvidia. Since then, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash (MIT-licensed) have kept the pressure on, with Chinese open-weight models now just 2-5 months behind US frontier models while US open models lag 6-9 months. The premium for 'best' only survives on the hardest tasks, which are a small fraction of work. Companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, and Perplexity already route routine workloads to cheap Chinese models.

US labs responded by cutting prices, but the real pivot was physical: $511 billion in capex from Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta, with data center construction spending now exceeding $75 billion annually. This bet faces hard constraints. Power is the first bottleneck: nearly half of planned 2026 data center capacity has been delayed or cancelled, grid connection waits run 4-7 years, and transformer lead times have stretched to 3-5 years. China, by contrast, added 543 GW of new power in 2025 and builds next to the source. Chips are the second: Nvidia controls ~75% of AI accelerator revenue, and nearly all fabrication runs through TSMC in Taiwan. Export controls intended to slow China backfired, pushing Chinese labs to Mixture-of-Experts efficiency (DeepSeek's V4 Pro activates only 49B of 1.6T parameters) and Huawei's Ascend chips now hold ~50% of China's AI accelerator market.

## Counterpoints

- US labs argue that frontier models still command a premium for the hardest tasks, and they are cutting prices to compete.
- Some claim the infrastructure buildout is necessary for AGI, and that the telecom analogy is flawed because AI demand is growing faster than bandwidth did.
- China's power advantage may be offset by its own export controls and reliance on TSMC for advanced chips.
- Decentralized networks are unproven and may not achieve the scale or reliability of centralized data centers.
