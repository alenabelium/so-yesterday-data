## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the exhausted AI trade into crypto projects with real cash flows, and this rotation will be big.

## Argument

The rotation is visible in the market's reaction to earnings. Nvidia beat consensus by over 4% on revenue and 6% on EPS, yet its stock gained just 2.8% over the trailing month. Broadcom reported revenue up 86% year-on-year and AI semiconductor revenue up 221%, but its shares fell after the print and are down over 16% over the month. When a trade is healthy, good news moves prices; when it's exhausted, good news is already priced in and only disappointment is left to trade. Positioning data confirms the shift: Bank of America's Global Fund Manager survey showed long semiconductors as the most crowded trade at 82% in July, falling to 53% by August—a third of the consensus trade stepped back in a single month.

Money that spent two years in one trade doesn't go to cash; allocators who were paid for volatility won't settle for 4.7% in treasuries. They look for the next asset with a similar volatility profile and a more compelling story. The list is short: liquid enough to absorb size, volatile enough to matter, and early enough in its narrative. Crypto ticks all three boxes. Over the past month, the NASDAQ 100 is down 0.6%, the S&P 500 down 0.5%, and tech down 0.3%, while Bitcoin is up over 21%, Ethereum up 30%, Coinbase up 20%, and Hype up 53%.

## Counterpoints

- The $23 million stake is pocket change—just 0.4% of Druckenmiller's $5.2 billion equity book—and one filing doesn't make a rotation.
- The AI trade is not collapsing, just 'tired'—good news still moves prices for some names, and the rotation could be a trickle that fails to turn into something big.
