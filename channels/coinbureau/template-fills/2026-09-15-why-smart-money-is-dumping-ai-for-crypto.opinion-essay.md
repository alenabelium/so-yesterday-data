## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the exhausted AI trade into crypto assets with real cash flows, and this rotation is just beginning.

## Argument

The rotation is visible in the market's reaction to earnings. Nvidia beat consensus by over 4% on revenue and 6% on EPS, yet its stock gained only 2.8% over the trailing month; Broadcom's AI revenue surged 221% and guidance was raised, yet its stock fell over 16% in the same period. When good news fails to move prices, the trade is exhausted. Positioning data confirms this: Bank of America's survey showed the percentage calling long semiconductors the most crowded trade fell from 82% in July to 53% in August. Allocators who've been paid for volatility don't move to cash; they seek the next asset with a similar volatility profile and a more compelling story. That asset is crypto, and the flows prove it: Bitcoin up 21%, Ethereum up 30%, Coinbase up 20%, and Hype up 53% over the past month, while the NASDAQ 100 is down 0.6%. The institutional footprint is unmistakable. Druckenmiller's family office liquidated Broadcom, Intel, and Micron, and took a new $23 million stake in Hyperliquid Strategies, alongside a $64 million stake in Bitfarms. BlackRock, State Street, Citadel, Jane Street, Morgan Stanley, and Invesco all disclosed positions in Bitfarms, and institutional holders jumped 122% in three months. ETF inflows are record-breaking: $986 million into spot Bitcoin ETFs in one week, $3.8 billion over three weeks, and $356 million into Hype ETFs since launch. - The $23 million stake is pocket change, just 0.4% of Druckenmiller's equity book, and one filing doesn't make a rotation.
