## Thesis

The AI trade is exhausted, and smart money is rotating into crypto assets with real cash flows, like Hyperliquid, marking the start of a major institutional shift.

## Argument

The mechanism is market exhaustion. When a trade is healthy, good news moves prices; when it's exhausted, good news is already priced in. Nvidia beat on revenue and guidance, yet its stock gained only 2.8% over the month. Broadcom's AI revenue surged 221% and guidance was raised, yet shares fell 16%. That's the signature of a tired trade. Positioning confirms it: BofA's survey showed the 'long semiconductors' crowded trade dropped from 82% to 53% in a single month. Money doesn't go to cash; it rotates to assets with similar volatility and a fresher story. Crypto fits: Bitcoin up 21%, Ethereum up 30%, Hype up 53% over the same month. The evidence is in the filings: Druckenmiller's family office bought a $23M stake in Hyperliquid Strategies, a company that holds 29.3M HYPE tokens and generates staking revenue. He also added $64M in Bitfarms, plus positions in Riot, Hut 8, and Iron. Institutional holders in Bitfarms jumped 122% to 202. ETF flows confirm: $986M into spot Bitcoin ETFs in one week, $3.8B over three weeks, and $356M into HYPE ETFs since launch. The key is Hyperliquid's business model: it generates $419M in gross protocol fees in H1 2026, settles 40-50% of onchain perps volume, and routes 97-99% of fees into buybacks that permanently burn HYPE. With a buyback yield of ~3.9%, investors can now value HYPE like a traditional exchange, not just a leveraged bet.
