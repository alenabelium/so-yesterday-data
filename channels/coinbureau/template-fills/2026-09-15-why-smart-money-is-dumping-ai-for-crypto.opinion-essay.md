## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the exhausted AI trade into crypto projects with real cash flows, like Hyperliquid, marking the start of a major institutional shift.

## Argument

The AI trade isn't collapsing; it's tired. Nvidia's blowout quarter yielded a 2.8% monthly gain, and Broadcom's 86% revenue growth was met with a 16% stock drop. When good news stops moving prices, the trade is exhausted. Positioning data confirms it: the 'long semiconductors' crowded trade fell from 82% to 53% in a month. Allocators don't park in cash; they seek similar volatility with a better story. That's crypto. Bitcoin is up 21%, Ethereum 30%, Hype 53% in the same window. The evidence is in the filings: Druckenmiller's new stake in Hyperliquid Strategies, plus institutional positions in Bitfarms, Riot, Hut 8, and Iron. ETF inflows are record-breaking. But the real signal is the valuation framework. Hyperliquid generates $419M in fees, routes 97-99% of fees into buybacks, and now adds USDC yield. That's a buyback yield of 3.9%, making it valued like a business, not a bet. This cycle, institutions are arriving through cash-flow analysis, not digital gold narratives. Bitcoin isn't the front door.

## Counterpoints

- Druckenmiller's $23M stake is pocket change (0.4% of his book), and one filing doesn't make a rotation.
- The AI trade isn't collapsing, just tired; good news still beats estimates, but prices don't respond.
