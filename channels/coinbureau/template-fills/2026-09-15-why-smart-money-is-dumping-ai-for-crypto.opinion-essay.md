## Thesis

Smart money is rotating from the AI trade into crypto, specifically into projects with real cash flows like Hyperliquid, as evidenced by Stanley Druckenmiller's new stake and significant ETF inflows.

## Argument

The mechanism is the exhaustion of the AI trade. Nvidia and Broadcom both reported blowout quarters, yet their stocks stagnated or fell, signaling that good news is already priced in. Positioning data confirms this: Bank of America's survey showed the percentage calling long semiconductors the most crowded trade dropped from 82% in July to 53% in August. Allocators who've been paid for volatility don't move to cash; they seek the next asset with a similar profile and a more compelling story. Crypto fits: over the past month, Bitcoin is up 21%, Ethereum 30%, Coinbase 20%, and Hype 53%, while the NASDAQ 100 is down 0.6%. The rotation is not just into Bitcoin. Druckenmiller's $23 million stake in Hyperliquid Strategies is small, but the point is his valuation framework: revenue, fee take rates, buyback yield, comparables to Coinbase and CME. Hyperliquid generates $419 million in gross protocol fees in H1 2026, settles 40-50% of onchain perp volumes, and has a buyback yield of 3.9%. This cycle, institutional capital is arriving through cash flow analysis, not the digital gold narrative. The AI trade trained allocators to be comfortable owning things they can't fully price, and that muscle is now being applied to crypto assets that can be valued like exchanges.

## Counterpoints

- Druckenmiller's $23 million is pocket change, just 0.4% of his equity book, and one filing doesn't make a rotation.
- The AI trade is not collapsing, just tired; good news still moves prices in healthy trades.
