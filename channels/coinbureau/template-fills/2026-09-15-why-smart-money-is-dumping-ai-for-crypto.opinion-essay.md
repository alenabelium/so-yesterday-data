## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the exhausted AI trade into crypto projects with real cash flows, like Hyperliquid, marking the start of a major capital shift.

## Argument

The AI trade isn't collapsing, but it's tired. Nvidia's blowout quarter yielded a 2.8% gain; Broadcom's 86% revenue growth and raised guidance saw its stock fall 16%. When good news stops moving prices, the trade is exhausted. Positioning data confirms: BofA's survey shows long semiconductors as the most crowded trade dropped from 82% to 53% in a month. Allocators don't sit in cash; they seek similar volatility with a better story. Crypto fits: Bitcoin up 21%, Ethereum up 30%, Hype up 53% in a month while indices are flat. Druckenmiller's $23M Hyperliquid stake is small, but it's a signal—he's applying his valuation framework: revenue, fee take rates, buyback yield. Hyperliquid generates $419M in fees, settles 40-50% of onchain perps, and burns 4.8% of supply via buybacks. This is the first institutional capital arriving via cash-flow analysis, not digital gold. The AI trade trained allocators to own what they can't price; that muscle is now moving to crypto assets that can be valued like exchanges.

## Counterpoints

- Druckenmiller's $23M is pocket change (0.4% of his book), and one filing doesn't make a rotation.
- The AI trade isn't collapsing—it's just tired, and good news still moves prices in healthy trades.
- Crypto's volatility might not appeal to allocators who just left a crowded trade.
- The rotation could be a trickle that fails to turn into something big.
