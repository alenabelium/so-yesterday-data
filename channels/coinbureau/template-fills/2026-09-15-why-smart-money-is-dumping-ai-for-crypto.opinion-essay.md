## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the exhausted AI trade into crypto assets with real cash flows, like Hyperliquid, and this marks the beginning of a major institutional shift.

## Argument

The AI trade is not collapsing, but it is tired. When Nvidia beats on revenue and guidance yet the stock gains only 2.8% over a month, and Broadcom's blowout quarter is met with a 16% drop, good news is already priced in. The only thing left to trade is disappointment. Positioning data confirms the shift: BofA's Global Fund Manager Survey saw the 'long semiconductors' crowded trade fall from 82% to 53% in a single month. Money that has spent two years in a trade does not go to cash; it seeks the next asset with a similar volatility profile and a more compelling story. That asset class is crypto. Over the past month, while the NASDAQ 100 is down 0.6% and the S&P 500 down 0.5%, Bitcoin is up 21%, Ethereum up 30%, and HYPE up 53%. The rotation is visible in flows: spot Bitcoin ETFs pulled in $986 million in a week, Ethereum ETFs had their best month in a year, and HYPE ETFs have taken in $356 million since launch. The key signal is Druckenmiller's new stake in Hyperliquid Strategies—a company holding 29.3 million HYPE tokens, generating staking revenue and running buybacks. Hyperliquid itself behaves like a business: $419 million in gross protocol fees in H1 2026, 40-50% of onchain perp volume, and 97-99% of fees routed into buybacks that permanently destroy tokens. This creates a buyback yield of ~3.9%, making HYPE valued for cash flows, not just as a leveraged bet. The AI trade trained allocators to be comfortable owning things they can't fully price; that muscle memory is now being applied to crypto assets that can be valued like exchanges. This is how institutions start

## Counterpoints

- Druckenmiller's $23 million stake is pocket change, just 0.4% of his equity book, and one filing does not make a rotation.
- The AI trade is not collapsing, just choppy, so the rotation might be temporary.
- Crypto assets are still too volatile and unproven for institutional capital to fully embrace.
