## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the exhausted AI trade into crypto assets with real cash flows, and this rotation will be big.

## Argument

The mechanism is valuation fatigue. When a trade is exhausted, good news no longer moves prices—Nvidia beat on revenue and EPS, yet the stock gained just 2.8% over the month; Broadcom raised guidance to $230B of AI revenue by 2028, then fell 16%. Positioning data confirms the stall: BofA's survey saw long semiconductors drop from 82% to 53% in a month. Allocators who've been paid for volatility don't park in cash; they rotate into the next asset with a similar volatility profile but a fresher narrative. That asset is crypto, and the flows prove it: Bitcoin up 21%, Ethereum up 30%, Hype up 53% over the same month the NASDAQ was flat. The evidence isn't just price—Druckenmiller's family office bought a $23M stake in Hyperliquid Strategies, a company that holds 29.3M HYPE tokens, generates staking revenue, and runs buybacks. Hyperliquid itself earned $419M in gross fees in H1 2026, settles 40-50% of onchain perp volume, and its buyback yield sits at 3.9%. This is the first time institutions can value a crypto asset like an exchange—revenue, fee take rates, buyback yield—rather than as a digital gold narrative. The AI trade trained allocators to own things they can't fully price, and that muscle doesn't switch off; it gets used elsewhere.

## Counterpoints

- Druckenmiller's $23M position is pocket change, just 0.4% of his equity book, and one filing doesn't make a rotation.
- The AI trade isn't collapsing—it's just tired, and good news still moves prices for some names.
- Crypto remains too volatile and unregulated for serious institutional capital to rotate into at scale.
