## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the AI trade into crypto assets with real cash flows, as evidenced by Stanley Druckenmiller's Hyperliquid stake and record ETF inflows.

## Argument

The rotation is already visible in the price action. While Nvidia and Broadcom report blowout earnings, their stocks stagnate or fall—Nvidia up just 2.8% over a month despite a 4% revenue beat, Broadcom down 16% after a 221% AI revenue surge. That's the signature of an exhausted trade: good news is already priced in, leaving only disappointment to trade. Positioning data confirms the shift: BofA's Global Fund Manager Survey saw long semiconductors drop from 82% to 53% in a single month. Allocators who've been paid for volatility don't park in cash; they seek the next asset with a similar volatility profile but a fresher narrative. Crypto fits: Bitcoin up 21%, Ethereum up 30%, Coinbase up 20%, Hype up 53% over the same month. The evidence goes beyond price. Druckenmiller's filing shows a $23 million stake in Hyperliquid Strategies, a company holding 29.3 million HYPE tokens, generating staking revenue and running buybacks. Hyperliquid itself behaves like a business: $419 million in gross protocol fees in H1 2026, 40-50% of onchain perp volume, and 97-99% of fees routed into buybacks that permanently destroy tokens. The buyback yield sits at 3.9%, and new USDC yield funding adds $135-200 million annually. This is the first time institutional capital is arriving via cash flow analysis, not digital gold narratives. The AI trade trained allocators to own things they can't fully price; that muscle is now being applied to crypto assets that can be valued like exchanges.

## Counterpoints

- Druckenmiller's $23 million stake is pocket change, just 0.4% of his equity book, and one filing doesn't make a rotation.
- The AI trade isn't collapsing; it's just tired, and good news still moves prices in healthy trades.
