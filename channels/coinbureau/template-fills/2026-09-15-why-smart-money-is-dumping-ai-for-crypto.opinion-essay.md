## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the exhausted AI trade into crypto assets with real cash flows, and this rotation will be big.

## Argument

The AI trade is tired, not broken. Nvidia beat on revenue and guidance, yet its stock barely moved; Broadcom crushed earnings and raised guidance, then fell 16%. When good news stops moving prices, the trade is exhausted. Positioning data confirms it: BofA's survey showed the long-semiconductor trade dropping from 82% to 53% crowded in a single month. That money doesn't go to cash—allocators who got paid for volatility won't settle for 4.7% treasuries. They rotate into the next asset with a similar volatility profile and a better story. That asset is crypto. Over the past month, NASDAQ is down 0.6% while Bitcoin is up 21%, Ethereum up 30%, and HYPE up 53%. The evidence is in the filings: Druckenmiller's family office liquidated Broadcom, Intel, and Micron, and took a $23 million stake in Hyperliquid Strategies—a company that holds 29.3 million HYPE tokens and generates staking revenue. He also bought $64 million of Bitfarms, with BlackRock, Citadel, and Morgan Stanley piling in too. ETF flows confirm the rotation: $3.8 billion into spot Bitcoin ETFs over three weeks, Ethereum ETFs adding $1.85 billion in August, and HYPE ETFs pulling in $356 million since launch. The key is that Hyperliquid behaves like a business: $419 million in gross protocol fees in H1 2026, 40-50% of onchain perp volume, and 97-99% of fees routed into buybacks that permanently destroy HYPE. Buybacks total $1.3 billion, with 4.8% of supply burned, and new USDC yield adds $135-200 million annually.
