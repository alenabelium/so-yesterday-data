## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the exhausted AI trade into crypto assets with real cash flows, and this rotation will be big.

## Argument

The AI trade isn't collapsing, but it's tired. Nvidia's blowout quarter yielded a 2.8% monthly gain; Broadcom's 86% revenue growth and raised guidance saw its stock fall 16%. When good news stops moving prices, the trade is exhausted. BofA's survey shows the 'long semiconductors' crowd fell from 82% to 53% in a month. That money doesn't go to cash—it seeks similar volatility with a better story. Crypto fits: Bitcoin +21%, Ethereum +30%, Hype +53% in a month while equities were flat. Druckenmiller's $23M Hyperliquid stake is small, but it's the framework that matters: revenue, fee take rates, buyback yield, comparables to Coinbase and CME. That's how institutions start every position they eventually size up. Hyperliquid's 97-99% fee buyback mechanism, $1.3B cumulative buybacks, and new USDC yield funding (est. $135-200M/year) give it a 3.9% buyback yield—a cash-flow valuation. The AI trade trained allocators to own things they can't price; that muscle now applies to crypto. The first serious institutional capital this cycle arrives via cash-flow analysis, not digital gold.

## Counterpoints

- Druckenmiller's $23M is pocket change (0.4% of his book) and one filing doesn't make a rotation.
- The AI trade is not collapsing, just choppy—good news still beats, but prices don't respond.
- Crypto's volatility and regulatory risk may deter traditional allocators despite cash-flow narratives.
