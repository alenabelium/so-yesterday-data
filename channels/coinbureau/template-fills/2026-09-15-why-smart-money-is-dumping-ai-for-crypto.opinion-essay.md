## Thesis

Smart money is rotating out of the exhausted AI trade into crypto projects with real cash flows, like Hyperliquid, and this marks the beginning of a major institutional shift.

## Argument

The rotation is already visible in price action: while Nvidia and Broadcom beat earnings, their stocks stagnate or fall, signaling an exhausted trade where good news is priced in. Positioning data confirms it—Bank of America's survey shows the 'long semiconductors' trade dropping from 82% to 53% crowded in a month. Allocators don't sit in cash; they seek similar volatility with a better story. That's crypto, and the flows prove it: Bitcoin ETFs pulled $986M in a week, Ethereum ETFs had their best month in a year, and Hyperliquid ETFs took in $356M since launch. Druckenmiller's $23M stake in Hyperliquid Strategies is small, but it's the framework that matters—he's valuing it like an exchange, with revenue, fee take rates, and buyback yield. Hyperliquid generates $419M in fees, burns 4.8% of supply, and now adds USDC yield to buybacks, creating a ~3.9% buyback yield. This is the first institutional capital arriving via cash-flow analysis, not digital gold. The AI trade trained allocators to own things they can't price; that muscle is now moving to crypto assets that can be valued like traditional exchanges.

## Counterpoints

- Druckenmiller's $23M stake is pocket change (0.4% of his book) and one filing doesn't make a rotation.
- The AI trade isn't collapsing—it's just 'tired,' and good news still moves prices in healthy trades.
