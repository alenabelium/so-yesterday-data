## Thesis

US export controls have forced China to win the AI race on price, and the world is shifting to cheaper Chinese infrastructure, making massive American capital expenditures unjustified.

## Argument

The mechanism is price. DeepSeek's V4 Flash costs 14 cents per million input tokens versus $5 for a comparable American model—36 times cheaper for the same unit of work. This efficiency was born from necessity: US export controls cut China off from top-tier Nvidia chips, forcing labs to innovate with limited hardware. The result is a market split into two stacks: the premium American stack selling 'thinking' at a high price, and the Chinese industrial stack selling intelligence for almost nothing. American startups are already voting with their wallets—Lindy moved 100% of traffic from Claude to DeepSeek, cutting inference costs by 90%; Coinbase built an internal gateway to route routine work to Chinese open-source models, halving AI spending; Pinterest retrained Alibaba's Qwen and saw a 90% cost reduction plus 30% accuracy gains. Meanwhile, US export controls have extended to deployed AI models, cutting off the rest of the world from American frontier systems and pushing them toward free, self-hostable Chinese models. China now dominates OpenRouter traffic, processing 21.37 trillion tokens in a week versus America's 5.76 trillion. The US still leads in high-value, complex tasks—Anthropic earns 46% of OpenRouter's dollar revenue on 12% of tokens—but that leadership is measured in months, not years. Hyperscalers plan to spend over $725 billion on capex this year, with Goldman projecting $1 trillion annually by 2027, yet Sequoia flags a $600 billion gap between spending and AI revenue. If the world adopts Chinese infrastructure, who returns that trillion?

## Counterpoints

- China dominates volume, not value: Anthropic gets 46% of OpenRouter's dollar revenue on 12% of tokens, and US models still lead in complex tasks by 3-8 months.
- Data security: Chinese model APIs route through servers in China, and PRC law obliges companies to cooperate with the state—hence Lindy hosting in the US.
- US still controls 74% of high-end AI computing capacity, and China's best chip runs at only 60% of the H100's power.
- Anthropic alleges Alibaba's Qwen carried out a massive distillation attack, but Anthropic itself faces a copyright lawsuit from music labels.
