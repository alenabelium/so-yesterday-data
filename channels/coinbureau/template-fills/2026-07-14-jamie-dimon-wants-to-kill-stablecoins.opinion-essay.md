## Thesis

Jamie Dimon's campaign against income-generating stablecoins is not about systemic risk but about protecting banks' lucrative deposit margins from competition.

## Argument

The mechanism is margin protection. Banks pay near-zero interest on deposits while earning 4% or more by lending or investing, a spread that constitutes the most profitable business in modern finance. A stablecoin that returns 4% directly to users forces banks to compete for savings, threatening this easy money. Dimon's public attacks—calling Coinbase's CEO 'complete crap' and lobbying against the Clarity Act—are a direct response to this competitive threat, not a genuine concern for financial stability.

The systemic risk narrative collapses under scrutiny. The White House Council of Economic Advisers found that a complete ban on stablecoin income would increase bank lending by only $2.1 billion, a 0.02% increase, while costing consumers $800 million in lost benefits. Stablecoin issuers are legally prohibited from lending reserves under the Genius Act, so they cannot engage in fractional-reserve banking, the actual source of bank panics. The 'shadow banking' accusation is therefore a smokescreen.

The hypocrisy is blatant. While Dimon publicly condemns stablecoins, JP Morgan launched its own tokenized dollar, JPMD, on Coinbase's Base network—the very network and company he disparages. His shareholder letters reveal he views stablecoins as an existential threat and urges the bank to compete. His goal is to own the technology while restricting others' access.
