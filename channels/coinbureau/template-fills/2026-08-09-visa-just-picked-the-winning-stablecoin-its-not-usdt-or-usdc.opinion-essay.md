## Thesis

Visa's choice of OpenUSD (OUSD) as the first stablecoin on its new platform signals a structural shift in the stablecoin market, threatening the dominance of USDT and USDC by redistributing reserve revenue to partners.

## Argument

The mechanism is economic. OpenUSD, created by the Open Standard consortium of 167+ companies including Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, and Coinbase, flips the traditional stablecoin revenue model. Unlike Tether and Circle, which retain most reserve interest—Circle earned $2.75B in 2025, Tether over $10B—OUSD distributes nearly all reserve revenue to its partners and offers free issuance and redemption. This turns every partner into a stakeholder with a direct financial incentive to promote OUSD adoption. For example, if OUSD generates $1B in its first year, each of the 167 partners could earn ~$6M, plus savings on fees. This is a powerful incentive that existing stablecoins lack. Visa's support is part of a broader multi-coin strategy, but the choice of OUSD as the first asset on its enterprise platform is telling. The market reacted immediately: Circle's stock fell 17% on the day OUSD was announced and another 5% when Visa confirmed it as the first supported stablecoin. Even without OUSD overtaking USDC, it pressures Circle to share more revenue with distributors, as Mizuho analysts note, raising Circle's distribution costs and cutting its EBITDA forecast. This could erode Circle's margins without OUSD even gaining significant market share. The old thesis that USDT and USDC are untouchable due to network effects is now questionable because OUSD changes the issuer-distributor relationship, giving large partners a real alternative.

## Counterpoints

- Visa's CEO says they don't pick winners, but the company's multi-coin strategy means OUSD is just one of many supported assets, not a bet on a single winner.
- Paxos' Global Dollar Network uses a similar consortium model but its USDP token remains small, suggesting an impressive partner list alone doesn't guarantee adoption.
- Analysts like William Blair call OUSD 'a solution in search of a problem,' and Bernstein maintains an 'outperform' rating on Circle, indicating the market may be overreacting.
- OUSD hasn't launched yet and needs to build liquidity, user trust, and real integrations, which could take years and may not happen at all.
