## Thesis

Wall Street has structurally chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as its operating layer, and the only thing that could break that thesis is a governance fight over staking yield.

## Argument

The shift is visible in the flows. In July, US spot Ethereum ETFs pulled in $365 million while Bitcoin ETFs saw record outflows — $2.4 billion out in May and $4.5 billion in June. But the real change is who is buying. Corporate treasuries have replaced ETFs as the main engine of ETH accumulation: 67 companies now hold over 8.2 million ETH, roughly 6.8% of supply. They aren't running the old Strategy playbook of buy-and-hold. They're staking. Bitmain, the largest, has over 87% of its stack staked, generating roughly $291 million in annualized rewards. That cash flow arrives whether or not they ever raise another dollar. Bitcoin balance sheets need financing to grow; Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves.

Wall Street built the infrastructure for this while everyone watched price charts. BNY wired native staking rewards into its custody platform, working with Galaxy. BlackRock launched ETHB, a staked Ethereum product that passes through 82% of gross staking rewards, and even did a reverse split on ETHA to cut trading costs for institutional desks. Tokenized treasuries now total $15.2 billion, and Ethereum settles 43% of them. The message is clear: ETH is being accumulated not as a bet on crypto going up, but as core infrastructure.

This doesn't mean Bitcoin lost. It keeps its identity as a reserve asset and sovereign hedge. But institutions now want one asset to store value and a different asset to move it. ## Counterpoints

- Some might see the ETH/BTC rotation as traders chasing higher volatility, not a structural shift.
- Bitcoin isn't collapsing — it still has a strong bid, with spot ETFs taking in $170M and $211M on consecutive days in August.
- EIP-8361 authors argue that without issuance taper, staking participation could pass 55% by 2028, concentrating the network in a few custodians and taxing non-stakers through dilution.
- Critics like Avy Stein Kulakov and Mike Siligadze argue the proposal is 'actively hurtful' and breaks DeFi credit markets, while Lido's Isidoros Satis calls the research 'too theoretical.'
