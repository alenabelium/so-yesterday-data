## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the asset class for institutional finance, and the only thing that could break this thesis is a governance battle over staking yields.

## Argument

The shift is visible in the flows. In July, Ethereum ETFs pulled in $365 million while Bitcoin ETFs saw record outflows. But the real story is who is buying ETH and why. Corporate treasuries have replaced ETFs as the main engine of accumulation. Bitmain holds 5.8 million ETH, 87% staked, generating $291 million in annualized staking rewards. That cash flow arrives whether or not Bitmain ever raises another dollar. Bitcoin balance sheets need financing to grow; Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves. Wall Street built the infrastructure for this. BNY wired native staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked Ethereum product, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total $15.2 billion. Institutions now view Bitcoin as a reserve asset and Ethereum as programmable financial infrastructure. The numbers reflect that split: ETH outperformed Bitcoin by 11 points in July and beat the Nasdaq by 2500 basis points. But there's a risk. EIP-8361 proposes to taper issuance and cut staking yields from 2.6% to 1.1%. That would hit the very yield that institutions are buying ETH for. The pushback was immediate and loud. This is a fight about who Ethereum is being run for: core developers optimizing for decentralization, or institutional capital optimizing for payout. If you own ETH, you're betting on a governance process that can reprice your yield depending on a vote. That's the risk that comes with a productive asset.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing; it retains its identity as a digital reserve asset and sovereign hedge, and the Bitcoin bid is still alive with ETF inflows in early August.
- EIP-8361 authors argue that without a cap, staking participation could pass 55% by 2028, concentrating the network in a few custodians and taxing non-stakers through dilution.
- Some argue the proposal doesn't achieve its goals and could break DeFi credit markets that rely on looped borrowing strategies.
- The proposal may slip to a later upgrade since no formal pull request has been opened for the next fork.
- Sharplink's weighted average cost basis of $3.5k shows that yield doesn't save you from price, but it does change what the asset is for.
