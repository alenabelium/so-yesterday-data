## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the infrastructure layer for institutional finance, driven by staking yield and tokenization, but a governance battle over EIP-8361 threatens to reprice that yield and could break the entire.

## Argument

In July 2026, Ethereum spot ETFs pulled in $365 million while Bitcoin ETFs saw record outflows, but the real shift is deeper: corporate treasuries, led by Bitmain, have replaced ETFs as the main engine of ETH accumulation, staking over 87% of their holdings to generate yield. This is a fundamentally different model from Bitcoin's static treasury, which requires constant financing to grow. Wall Street has built the infrastructure around this yield: BNY wired staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked ETH product and adjusted its ETF for institutional desks, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total $15.2 billion. The thesis is that Ethereum is not just a bet on price but on productive, programmable financial infrastructure. However, EIP-8361, proposed on August 4th, would taper issuance and cut staking yields from ~2.6% to ~1.1-1.2%, sparking immediate backlash from DeFi leaders and institutions. This exposes the core tension: Ethereum's governance optimizes for decentralization and bounded supply, while institutional capital optimizes for payout. The risk is that a governance vote could reprice the yield that attracted all this capital, potentially sending $10 billion of treasury capital elsewhere.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing: it retains its identity as a digital reserve asset and sovereign hedge, and spot Bitcoin ETFs still took in $170-211 million on August 3-4.
- Some argue EIP-8361 is necessary to prevent network concentration and dilution of non-stakers, as staking participation could pass 55% by January 2028.
- Critics like Avy Stein Kulakov and Mike Siligadze argue the proposal is 'actively hurtful' and breaks DeFi credit markets, with a 48-hour comment window being 'disappointing on every level.'
