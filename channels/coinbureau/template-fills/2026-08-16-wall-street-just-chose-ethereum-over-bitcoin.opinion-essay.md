## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the asset class for institutional finance, and the only thing that could break this thesis is a governance battle over staking yields.

## Argument

The shift is visible in the flows: July saw $365M into Ethereum ETFs versus $172M into Bitcoin ETFs, after Bitcoin ETFs bled $2.4B in May and $4.5B in June. But the real change is who is buying and why. Corporate treasuries, led by Bitmain, now hold 8.2M ETH and stake most of it—Bitmain stakes 87% of its 5.8M ETH, generating ~$291M in annualized rewards. This is a fundamentally different model from Bitcoin's static treasury, which must raise new capital to grow. Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves through staking yield.

Wall Street built the infrastructure for this. BNY wired native staking rewards into its custody platform on August 4, working with Galaxy. BlackRock launched ETHB, a staked Ethereum product, and adjusted ETHA for institutional desks. Tokenized treasuries, with $15.2B total, settle 43% on Ethereum. Institutions now view Bitcoin as a reserve asset and Ethereum as programmable financial infrastructure—the thing you use. The numbers reflect this: ETH outperformed BTC by 11 points in July and the Nasdaq 100 by 2500 basis points.

But the thesis faces a threat: EIP-8361, proposed on August 4, would taper issuance and cut staking yields from ~2.6% to ~1.1-1.2%. This sparked immediate pushback from DeFi leaders and institutions. The proposal aims to prevent over-concentration of staking, but it directly attacks the yield that brought institutional capital in. The fight is about who Ethereum is run for: core developers optimizing for decentralization, or institutional capital optimizing for payout. If yields drop, the $10B treasury capital might leave. ## Counterpoints

- Some might see the ETH strength as just rotation, traders chasing higher volatility, but the infrastructure build suggests a deeper shift.
- Bitcoin isn't losing—it remains a reserve asset and sovereign hedge, with spot ETFs still taking in $170M and $211M on August 3-4.
- EIP-8361 authors argue it prevents network concentration and taxes non-stakers through dilution, a real concern as staking ratio hits 33.33%.
- Critics like Avy Stein Kulakov and Mike Siligadze argue the proposal is hurtful, breaks DeFi credit loops, and was rushed with a 48-hour comment window.
