## Thesis

Institutional finance has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as its operating layer, and the only thing that could break this thesis is Ethereum's own governance cutting the staking yield that made it attractive.

## Argument

The mechanism is yield. Bitmain, the largest corporate ETH holder, stakes 87% of its 5.8 million ETH and earns ~$291M annually without raising new capital — a self-growing balance sheet that Bitcoin's static treasury model cannot match. Wall Street built for this: BNY wired staking rewards into custody on Aug 4, BlackRock's staked ETHB product passes 82% of rewards to holders, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum hit $15.2B. But EIP-8361, proposed Aug 4, would taper issuance and cut baseline yield from 2.6% to ~1.1%, sparking backlash from Lido, Ether.fi, and Aave. The fight is over who Ethereum serves: core devs optimizing for decentralization vs. institutions optimizing for payout. If the yield dies, the $10B treasury capital and the 43-day validator queue may leave — that's the risk of owning a productive asset.
