## Thesis

Wall Street has structurally chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the infrastructure layer for institutional finance, a shift visible in flows, treasuries, and custody, but one that now hinges on a governance battle over yield.

## Argument

The shift is not a rotation but a change in what the asset is for. Bitcoin's treasury model can't grow without financing; Ethereum's balance sheets grow themselves through staking yield. Bitmain, holding 5.8 million ETH, stakes 87% of it, generating ~$291M annualized rewards without raising capital. This is a different beast from Strategy's static Bitcoin stack. Wall Street built the rails: BNY wired native staking into custody, BlackRock launched a staked ETH product and adjusted ETHA for institutional desks, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now settle 43% of $15.2B. Institutions now fund Bitcoin as a reserve and Ethereum as programmable infrastructure. But the entire thesis rests on yield, and EIP-8361 proposes to taper issuance, cutting baseline yield from ~2.6% to ~1.1-1.2%. The pushback was immediate: DeFi leaders called it harmful, and the 48-hour comment window was criticized. The authors argue it prevents concentration and dilution, but the real fight is over who Ethereum is run for: core developers optimizing for decentralization versus institutional capital optimizing for payout. If yield is cut, $10B of treasury capital could leave.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing; it retains its identity as a reserve asset and sovereign hedge, with ETF inflows still strong.
- Yield doesn't save you from price declines, as Sharplink's cost basis of ~$3.5k shows.
- EIP-8361 authors argue it prevents network concentration and taxes non-stakers through dilution.
- The proposal may slip to a later upgrade since no formal pull request has been opened for the next fork.
