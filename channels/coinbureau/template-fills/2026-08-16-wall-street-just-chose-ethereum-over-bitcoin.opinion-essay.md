## Thesis

Institutional preference has shifted from Bitcoin to Ethereum because Ethereum's staking yield turns corporate treasuries into self-growing balance sheets, making it the infrastructure Wall Street actually operates on.

## Argument

The mechanism is yield. Corporate treasuries like Bitmain stake 87% of their ETH, generating ~$291M annually without new financing, while Strategy's Bitcoin treasury sold assets in July. Wall Street built for this: BNY wired staking rewards into custody on Aug 4, BlackRock's ETHB passes 82% of staking yield, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum hit $15.2B. But EIP-8361, proposed Aug 4, would slash staking yield from ~2.6% to ~1.1%, triggering backlash from Lido, Ether.fi, and Aave. This is a governance fight: core devs want decentralization, institutions want payout. If yield drops, $10B of treasury capital could flee. Bitcoin remains a reserve asset, but Ethereum's productive yield is why institutions chose it.
