## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as its institutional infrastructure asset, and the only thing that could break this thesis is a governance fight over staking yields.

## Argument

The shift isn't rotation; it's a change in what institutions are buying. Bitcoin's treasury model—buy a scarce asset, never sell—can't grow without financing. Ethereum's staking yield makes balance sheets self-compounding. Bitmain holds ~5.8M ETH, 87% staked, generating ~$291M annualized rewards without raising capital. BNY wired staking rewards into custody; BlackRock launched ETHB and adjusted ETHA for institutional desks. Tokenized treasuries on Ethereum hit $15.2B, with 43% settled on-chain. Institutions now split the market: Bitcoin as reserve, Ethereum as programmable infrastructure. But EIP-8361, a proposal to taper issuance and cut staking yields from ~2.6% to ~1.1%, threatens the yield that underpins this institutional build. The fight is over who Ethereum is run for: core developers optimizing for decentralization, or institutional capital optimizing for payout.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing; it retains its reserve asset status, and Bitcoin ETFs still see inflows, so this is a shift, not a replacement.
- EIP-8361 authors argue that unchecked staking concentration and dilution of non-stakers justify the yield cut.
- Some critics say the proposal is theoretically flawed and could break DeFi credit markets, not just reduce yields.
