## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the infrastructure layer for institutional finance, and the only thing that could break this thesis is a governance battle over staking yields.

## Argument

The shift is visible in the flows. In July, Ethereum ETFs pulled in $365 million while Bitcoin ETFs saw record outflows, and corporate treasuries replaced ETFs as the main engine of ETH accumulation. Bitmain, the largest ETH treasury, holds 5.8 million ETH, 87% staked, generating over $250 million in annualized rewards. Unlike Bitcoin, which requires financing to grow, Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves through staking yield. Wall Street has built the infrastructure to capture this: BNY wired staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked ETH product and adjusted its ETF for institutional desks, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total $15.2 billion. The institutional view is split: Bitcoin is the reserve asset, Ethereum is programmable financial infrastructure. But this thesis faces a governance risk. EIP-8361 proposes tapering issuance, cutting staking yields from 2.6% to 1.1-1.2%, which would undermine the very yield that attracted institutions. The pushback was immediate, and the fight is about who Ethereum is run for: core developers optimizing for decentralization versus institutional capital optimizing for payout. If you own ETH, you're betting on a governance process that can reprice your yield.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing; it retains its identity as a reserve asset and sovereign hedge, with spot ETFs still taking in hundreds of millions.
- EIP-8361 has a real argument: staking concentration and dilution of non-stakers justify tapering issuance.
- Yield doesn't save you from price; Sharplink's cost basis is around $3.5k, so price still matters.
