## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as its preferred institutional asset, because Ethereum's staking yield and tokenization infrastructure make it a productive, self-growing balance sheet asset, while Bitcoin remains a static.

## Argument

In July 2026, Ethereum ETFs pulled in $365 million while Bitcoin ETFs saw record outflows, but the real shift is deeper: corporate treasuries, led by Bitmain, now hold 8.2 million ETH and stake most of it, generating yield that grows their balance sheets without new financing. Wall Street infrastructure—BNY's staking custody, BlackRock's ETHB and reverse split, and tokenized treasuries—is built around Ethereum's programmability. This is a fundamental reclassification: Bitcoin is the reserve asset, Ethereum is the operating layer. However, EIP-8361, a proposed taper of issuance, threatens to cut staking yields from ~2.6% to ~1.1%, sparking a governance battle between decentralization and institutional payouts. The outcome will determine if Ethereum remains the institutional choice.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing: it still has a strong bid, with $170M and $211M inflows on Aug 3-4, and retains its identity as a digital reserve asset.
- EIP-8361 authors argue it's necessary to prevent over-staking (33.33% and rising) from concentrating network control in a few custodians and taxing non-stakers via dilution.
- Some critics, like Avy Stein Kulakov, say the proposal is 'actively hurtful' and breaks DeFi credit markets that rely on looped borrowing strategies.
