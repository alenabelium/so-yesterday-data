## Thesis

Institutional capital has structurally shifted from Bitcoin to Ethereum, not as a rotation but because Ethereum's staking yield and tokenization infrastructure make it the asset Wall Street actually operates on, while Bitcoin remains a.

## Argument

The shift is visible in the flows: July saw $365M into Ethereum ETFs against $172M for Bitcoin, after Bitcoin's worst outflow months on record. But the real story is who is buying and why. Corporate treasuries have replaced ETFs as the main engine of ETH accumulation, with 67 companies holding over 8.2M ETH. Bitmain, the largest, stakes 87% of its 5.8M ETH stack, generating ~$291M annualized yield without raising new capital. This is a fundamental difference: Bitcoin treasuries must finance growth, Ethereum treasuries compound on their own.

Wall Street built the infrastructure for this. BNY wired staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked ETH product (ETHB) passing through 82% of yield, and even adjusted ETHA for institutional trading. Tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total $15.2B, with Ethereum settling 43% of them. Institutions now view Bitcoin as a reserve asset and Ethereum as programmable infrastructure—the thing you use.

But this thesis faces a governance risk. EIP-8361 proposes tapering issuance to zero at 60.25M ETH staked, cutting baseline yield from ~2.6% to ~1.1-1.2%. This would directly hit the yield that attracted Bitmain, BNY, and BlackRock. The proposal has sparked fierce pushback from DeFi leaders, but also support from those worried about staking concentration. The core fight is about who Ethereum is run for: decentralization or institutional payouts. If yield is cut, $10B of treasury capital could flee. This is the risk of holding a productive asset—Bitcoin holders never face this.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing: it still has its own inflows and remains the reserve asset, with a separate institutional bid.
- EIP-8361 authors argue it's needed to prevent staking concentration and protect non-stakers from dilution.
- Some argue the proposal is theoretical and rushed, with a 48-hour comment window, and may not achieve its goals.
