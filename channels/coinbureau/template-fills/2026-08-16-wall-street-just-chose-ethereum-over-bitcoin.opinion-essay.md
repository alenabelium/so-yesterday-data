## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the infrastructure layer for institutional finance, a shift driven by staking yield and tokenization, but a governance battle over EIP-8361 now threatens that yield and the entire thesis.

## Argument

The shift is not a rotation but a structural change in what institutions buy. For six years, corporate treasuries followed the Bitcoin model: buy a scarce asset, never sell, and let scarcity do the work. That model can't grow without financing—every coin must be paid for with new equity or debt because the asset produces nothing. Ethereum is different. In the first half of 2026, corporate treasuries replaced ETFs as the main engine of ETH accumulation. 67 companies now hold over 8.2 million ETH, and they're staking most of it. Bitmain, the largest, has over 87% of its stack staked, generating roughly $291 million in annualized rewards. That cash flow arrives whether or not Bitmain ever raises another dollar. Bitcoin balance sheets need financing to grow; Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves. This is why Wall Street built infrastructure around Ethereum. BNY wired native staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked ETH product and adjusted its ETF for institutional desks, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total over $6 billion. Institutions now view Bitcoin as a reserve asset to hold and Ethereum as programmable financial infrastructure to use. The flows confirm it: Ethereum ETFs pulled in $365 million in July while Bitcoin ETFs saw record outflows. But this thesis has a fault line. EIP-8361, proposed on August 4th, would taper issuance and cut staking yields from 2.6% to roughly 1.1%. The proposal has sparked fierce opposition from DeFi leaders and institutions alike. The fight is about who Ethereum is run for: core developers optimizing

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing; it retains its identity as a digital reserve asset and sovereign hedge, with spot ETFs still taking in hundreds of millions on some days.
- Some argue EIP-8361 is necessary to prevent over-concentration of staking among large custodians and to protect non-stakers from dilution.
- The proposal may not pass; no formal pull request has been opened for the next fork, so it could slip to a later upgrade.
