## Thesis

Institutional preference has shifted from Bitcoin to Ethereum because Ethereum's staking yield and infrastructure make it a productive asset, while Bitcoin remains a static reserve.

## Argument

The shift is driven by a fundamental difference in asset utility. Bitcoin treasuries like Strategy can't grow without financing because the asset produces nothing. Ethereum treasuries like Bitmain generate yield through staking, allowing balance sheets to grow themselves. This is why corporate treasuries replaced ETFs as the main buyer of ETH, staking 87% of their holdings. Wall Street built infrastructure around this: BNY wired staking rewards into custody, BlackRock launched staked ETH products, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum reached $15.2 billion. Institutions now view Bitcoin as a store of value and Ethereum as programmable infrastructure, funding both separately. The risk is governance: EIP-8361 proposes cutting staking yields, which could threaten institutional interest.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing; it retains its identity as a digital reserve asset and sovereign hedge.
- EIP-8361 authors argue it's needed to prevent network concentration and dilution of non-stakers.
- Some say the proposal is theoretical and could break DeFi credit markets.
