## Thesis

Institutional preference has shifted from Bitcoin to Ethereum because Ethereum's staking yield turns it into self-growing infrastructure, while Bitcoin remains a static reserve asset.

## Argument

The shift is visible in the flows: July saw Ethereum ETFs pull in $365 million while Bitcoin ETFs suffered record outflows, and corporate treasuries—led by Bitmain—have replaced ETFs as the main buyers of ETH. Bitmain stakes over 87% of its 5.8 million ETH, generating roughly $291 million in annualized staking rewards, which means its balance sheet grows without new financing. Bitcoin treasuries like Strategy must raise capital to grow, but Ethereum treasuries compound on their own. Wall Street is building around this: BNY wired staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked ETH product (ETHB) and adjusted ETHA for institutional desks, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total $15.2 billion. Institutions now view Bitcoin as a reserve asset and Ethereum as programmable financial infrastructure—they fund both separately. The risk is governance: EIP-8361 proposes tapering issuance to near zero, which would cut staking yields from 2.6% to about 1.1%, threatening the very yield that attracted institutions. This is a fight over who Ethereum is run for—core developers optimizing for decentralization versus institutional capital optimizing for payout.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing: it still has its bid, with spot Bitcoin ETFs taking in $170M and $211M on consecutive days in early August, and it retains its identity as a digital reserve asset.
- Some argue the rotation is just traders chasing higher volatility, not a structural shift.
- EIP-8361's authors argue that without tapering, staking participation could pass 55% by January 2028, concentrating the network in a few custodians and taxing non-stakers through dilution.
- Critics like Avy Stein Kulakov claim the proposal is 'actively hurtful' and breaks DeFi credit markets, while others object to the rushed 48-hour comment window.
