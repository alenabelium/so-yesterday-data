## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the infrastructure layer for institutional finance, and the only thing that could break this thesis is a governance battle over staking yields.

## Argument

The shift is visible in the flows. In July, Ethereum ETFs took in $365 million while Bitcoin ETFs saw record outflows, and corporate treasuries replaced ETFs as the main engine of ETH accumulation. Bitmain, the largest treasury, holds nearly 5.8 million ETH, with over 87% staked, generating roughly $291 million in annualized rewards. This is a fundamental difference: Bitcoin balance sheets need financing to grow, but Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves through staking yield. Wall Street has built infrastructure around this: BNY wired staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked Ethereum product and adjusted its ETF for institutional desks, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total $15.2 billion. The market now splits the two assets: Bitcoin as a reserve asset, Ethereum as programmable financial infrastructure. But the thesis faces a governance challenge. EIP-8361 proposes tapering issuance to zero at 60.25 million ETH staked, cutting baseline yield from 2.6% to 1.1-1.2%. This has sparked a fight between core developers optimizing for decentralization and institutional capital optimizing for payout. The outcome will determine whether Ethereum remains the institutional choice.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing: it still has its bid, with ETFs taking in $170M and $211M on consecutive days in August, and it retains its identity as a reserve asset.
- EIP-8361 authors argue that without tapering, staking participation could pass 55% by 2028, concentrating the network in a few custodians and taxing non-stakers through dilution.
- Some critics say the proposal is 'actively hurtful' and breaks DeFi credit markets, while others object to the short comment window and call the research too theoretical.
