## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as its operational layer, and the only thing that can break this thesis is a governance vote on staking yield.

## Argument

The mechanism is yield. Bitcoin's treasury model can't grow without financing because the asset produces nothing, but Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves through staking. Bitmain, the leading ETH treasury, has 87% of its stack staked, generating roughly $291 million annually without raising a dollar. Wall Street built the infrastructure for this: BNY wired staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked ETH product and re-engineered its ETF for institutional desks, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now settle 43% of a $15.2 billion market. The flows confirm the shift: July saw $365 million into ETH ETFs versus record Bitcoin outflows, and corporate treasuries replaced ETFs as the main buyer of ETH. Institutions now split the assets: Bitcoin is the reserve, Ethereum is the infrastructure you use. But the entire thesis rests on staking yield, and EIP-8361 proposes to cut it in half. The pushback was immediate and loud, with DeFi leaders calling it harmful and questioning the process. This is a fight about who Ethereum is run for: core developers optimizing for decentralization versus institutional capital optimizing for payout. If you own ETH, you're betting on a governance process that can reprice your yield. That's the risk of a productive asset, and Bitcoin holders will never face it.

## Counterpoints

- Some might see the ETH/BTC rotation as traders chasing higher volatility, not a structural shift.
- Bitcoin isn't collapsing; it still has its bid as a reserve asset and sovereign hedge, and nobody is being replaced.
- EIP-8361 authors argue that capping staking rewards prevents network concentration and taxes non-stakers through dilution.
- Sharplink's weighted average cost basis of ~$3.5k shows yield doesn't save you from price risk.
