## Thesis

Wall Street has structurally chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the infrastructure layer for institutional finance, a shift driven by staking yield and tokenization, not mere price rotation.

## Argument

The shift is visible in the flows: July saw $365M into Ethereum ETFs versus $172M for Bitcoin, after record Bitcoin outflows in May and June. But the deeper story is who is buying and why. Corporate treasuries, led by Bitmain with 5.8M ETH (87% staked), now hold 8.2M ETH across 67 companies. Unlike Bitcoin's static treasury model, Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves through staking yield—Bitmain generates ~$291M annually without new financing. Wall Street built the rails: BNY wired staking rewards into custody, BlackRock launched ETHB (staking 95% of assets) and adjusted ETHA for institutional desks, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum hit $15.2B. The thesis: institutions now want Bitcoin as a reserve asset and Ethereum as programmable infrastructure—the asset you use, not just hold. This is a fundamental repricing, not a rotation.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing—it remains the digital reserve asset and sovereign hedge, with spot ETFs still pulling in hundreds of millions.
- EIP-8361 could slash staking yields from ~2.6% to ~1.1%, potentially breaking the institutional thesis and sending treasury capital elsewhere.
- Critics argue the proposal is theoretical, rushed, and could hurt DeFi credit markets built on staking yields.
- Ethereum's yield doesn't protect against price declines—Sharplink's cost basis is ~$3.5k, so yield alone isn't a savior.
