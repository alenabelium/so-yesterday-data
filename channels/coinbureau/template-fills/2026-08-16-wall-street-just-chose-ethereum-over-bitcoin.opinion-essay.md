## Thesis

Wall Street has structurally chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the asset class for institutional finance, driven by staking yield and tokenization infrastructure, not just market rotation.

## Argument

The shift is not a rotation but a change in what institutions are buying. For six years, corporate treasuries followed the Bitcoin model: buy a scarce asset, never sell, and let scarcity do the work. That model has a fatal flaw: it can't grow without financing. Every additional coin must be paid for with new equity or debt because the asset produces nothing. Ethereum, by contrast, produces yield. In the first half of 2026, corporate treasuries replaced ETFs as the main engine of ETH accumulation. 67 companies now hold over 8.2 million ETH, and they're staking most of it. Bitmain, the largest, has over 87% of its stack staked, generating roughly $291 million in annualized staking rewards. That cash flow arrives whether or not Bitmain ever raises another dollar. Bitcoin balance sheets need financing to grow; Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves. Wall Street built the infrastructure for this. BNY wired native staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked Ethereum product, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total $15.2 billion. Institutions now want one asset to store value and a different asset to move it. Bitcoin is the reserve asset; Ethereum is the programmable financial infrastructure. The flows confirm it: Ethereum ETFs took in $365 million in July while Bitcoin ETFs bled out. This is not about Bitcoin losing; it's about Ethereum winning a new role.

## Counterpoints

- Some might see this as rotation, traders chasing higher volatility, but the infrastructure build-out shows a structural shift.
- Bitcoin isn't collapsing; it still has a bid as a reserve asset, and nobody is being replaced.
- EIP-8361 could cut staking yields, potentially driving institutional capital away, but the authors argue it's needed to prevent concentration.
- Sharplink's weighted average cost basis is around $3.5k, showing yield doesn't save you from price, but it changes what the asset is for.
