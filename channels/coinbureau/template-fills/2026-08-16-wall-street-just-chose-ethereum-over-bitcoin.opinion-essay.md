## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the infrastructure layer for institutional finance, a shift driven by staking yield and tokenization, but a proposed issuance change now threatens that choice.

## Argument

The shift is not a rotation but a structural change in what institutions buy. Corporate treasuries, led by Bitmain, now hold 8.2 million ETH and stake over 87% of their stacks, turning Ethereum balance sheets into self-growing machines that generate cash flow without new financing. Bitcoin's treasury model, by contrast, requires constant capital raises because the asset produces nothing. Wall Street built the rails for this: BNY wired staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked ETH product and adjusted its ETF for institutional desks, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total $15.2 billion. The flows confirm the split: Ethereum ETFs pulled in $365 million in July while Bitcoin ETFs bled record outflows. Institutions now fund Bitcoin as a reserve asset and Ethereum as programmable infrastructure. But this entire thesis rests on staking yield, and EIP-8361, proposed on August 4th, would cut that yield in half by tapering issuance. The proposal has sparked a governance battle between core developers optimizing for decentralization and institutional capital optimizing for payout. If the yield drops, the $10 billion in treasury capital and the staked ETF products could unwind. The risk is not price but governance: ETH holders are betting on a process that can reprice their yield at any vote.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing; it retains its identity as a reserve asset and sovereign hedge, and its ETFs still saw inflows on some days in August.
- Yield doesn't save you from price: Sharplink's cost basis is around $3.5k, so staking rewards don't offset a price decline.
- EIP-8361 authors argue that without tapering, staking participation could pass 55% by 2028, concentrating the network in a few custodians and taxing non-stakers through dilution.
