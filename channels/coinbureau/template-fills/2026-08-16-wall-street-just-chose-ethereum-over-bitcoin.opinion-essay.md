## Thesis

Wall Street has chosen Ethereum over Bitcoin as the infrastructure layer for institutional finance, and the only thing that could break this thesis is a governance decision to cut staking yields.

## Argument

The shift is visible in the flows: in July, Ethereum ETFs took in $365 million while Bitcoin ETFs saw record outflows, and corporate treasuries replaced ETFs as the main engine of ETH accumulation. The mechanism is yield. Bitmain, the leading Ethereum treasury, has staked over 87% of its 5.8 million ETH, generating roughly $291 million in annualized staking rewards without ever raising another dollar. Bitcoin balance sheets need financing to grow; Ethereum balance sheets grow themselves. Wall Street built the infrastructure for this: BNY wired native staking rewards into its custody platform, BlackRock launched a staked Ethereum ETF that passes through 82% of gross rewards, and tokenized treasuries on Ethereum now total $15.2 billion. Institutions now view Bitcoin as the reserve asset and Ethereum as programmable financial infrastructure — one to hold, one to use. But the whole thesis hinges on a governance battle. EIP-8361, proposed on August 4, would taper issuance and cut staking yields from ~2.6% to ~1.1-1.2%. The pushback was immediate: DeFi leaders called it hurtful, and Lido, Ether.fi, and Aave were urged to refuse it. The authors argue it prevents concentration of the network in a few custodians and taxes non-stakers. This is a fight over who Ethereum is run for: core developers optimizing for decentralization versus institutional capital optimizing for payout. If the yield is cut, $10 billion of treasury capital could look elsewhere.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin isn't losing: it retains its identity as a digital reserve asset and sovereign hedge, and spot Bitcoin ETFs still took in $170M and $211M on August 3-4.
- EIP-8361 authors argue that without a taper, staking participation could pass 55% by January 2028, concentrating the network in a few custodians and taxing non-stakers through dilution.
- Some argue the proposal doesn't achieve its goals and is 'actively hurtful' for Ethereum, breaking looped borrowing strategies in DeFi credit markets.
- The proposal may slip to a later upgrade since no formal pull request has been opened for the next fork.
