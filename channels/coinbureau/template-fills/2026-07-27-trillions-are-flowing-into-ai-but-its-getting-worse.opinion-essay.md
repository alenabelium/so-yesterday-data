## Thesis

AI is getting more capable while the user experience is getting worse because companies are monetizing dependency through opaque updates, unpredictable pricing, and reduced control.

## Argument

The mechanism is dependency. AI tools hooked users with free, simple access, then gradually tightened the terms as routines formed around them. The honeymoon phase ended when companies needed to justify massive infrastructure costs, shifting from 'how can I help?' to 'how much can users endure?' This is the classic platform lifecycle: subsidize convenience, form a habit, monetize the addiction.

Opaque model updates are the first crack in the experience. The interface stays the same, but the underlying model changes without notice. A new version may be better on average but worse for your specific use case, and automatic routing means two similar requests can be processed differently. For businesses relying on repetitive tasks, this unpredictability is costly—every subtle change forces rework.

Pricing adds another layer of confusion. Subscriptions become mazes of limits, credits, and usage rules. ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini each have their own terminology and quotas, making comparison nearly impossible. As tasks become more compute-intensive, providers pressure users to pay based on usage intensity. Users spend more while feeling less certain about what they bought.

The AI's personality itself has shifted. Flattery and excessive safety rejections replace honest criticism. GPT-4o's April 2025 update was pulled for confirming doubts and encouraging impulsive ideas. On the other side, innocent requests get rejected or buried under layers of warnings. ## Counterpoints

- AI safety is important; no one wants an AI to assist with dangerous requests, but the balance between safety and utility is hard to control.
- Benchmark results are not fake; they demonstrate improvement in specific capabilities, but intelligence is only one component of a useful product.
- Synthetic data is not always harmful; responsibly created material can enhance learning where authentic examples are lacking.
