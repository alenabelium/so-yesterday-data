## Thesis

The EU's MiCA review, extended in June 2026, is a regulatory takeover that will reclassify DeFi, staking, and lending under MiFID II, making them legally impossible for permissionless protocols and handing crypto to banks.

## Argument

The mechanism is architectural. MiFID II demands licensed investment firm status, best execution, client appropriateness checks, position limits, and pre/post-trade transparency — obligations built for compliance departments, not permissionless code. A smart contract cannot assess risk tolerance or exercise discretionary judgment, so these rules effectively outlaw DeFi. The review's key question is whether to expand MiFID's reach, and ESMA's 'function over form' principle means almost nothing is safe. The consolidation is already visible: the EU went from 3,000 registered crypto firms in 2024 to roughly 200–280 licensed CASPs by mid-2026, and Binance suspended spot margin and futures for EU users after failing to get a Greek license. The only entities that can survive MiFID's capital and compliance costs are banks and brokers — JP Morgan's Kexus has processed over $4 trillion, and Ripple, Deutsche Bank, and Mastercard are pushing tokenization. Article 60 of MiCA gives existing licensees a notification advantage, entrenching incumbents. The stated goal of investor protection is undermined by the effect: replacing DeFi with permissioned banking products that charge multiple fees, while pushing European users to offshore or unregulated platforms — the opposite of protection.

## Counterpoints

- The review is just a consultation, not law; the Commission's report isn't due until June 2027, and amendments around 2028.
- The EU has a precedent for exceptions: the DLT pilot regime offers targeted MiFID exemptions.
- Industry lobbying already extended the deadline; serious players like Coinbase and Notab are engaging constructively.
- Retail investors do need protection from opaque, credit-based, unregulated products — compliance testing and transparency exist for good reasons.
