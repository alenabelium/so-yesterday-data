## Thesis

Pump.fun's free launch button turned crypto into a casino where the platform and creators always win, while retail traders overwhelmingly lose.

## Argument

The mechanism is structural. Pump.fun gave away coin creation for free and charged a fee on every trade, so its revenue depends on volume, not longevity. With over 24 million coins launched and 79% dead after day one, the platform's business model is built on churn, not survival. Creators exploit this by bundling their own coins and selling to later buyers, earning $579 million versus $32 million for snipers. The platform's revenue of over $1 billion, 87% from coins less than a day old, proves that the death of most coins is not a bug but the product. Even when celebrities and presidents launch coins, the pattern repeats: insiders profit, retail loses. The Trump coin saw 58 wallets earn $1.1 billion while 764,000 lost money. The platform's dominance persists despite a shrinking meme coin market, capturing 92% of launches. Legal challenges, like the RICO lawsuit, have not slowed it down. The question is whether this is a neutral tool or an exploitative design.

## Counterpoints

- Pump.fun is a neutral tool that people simply abused.
- The abuse was the business model from the beginning.
- Economic damage in itself does not equal a crime (judge's reasoning in the Libra case).
- Pump.fun actively moderates illegal content on the site.
