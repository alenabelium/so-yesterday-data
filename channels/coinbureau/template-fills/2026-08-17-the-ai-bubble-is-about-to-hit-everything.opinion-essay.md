## Thesis

The AI boom is now a credit story, not a tech story, and the debt financing behind it will hit pensions and insurers if AI revenue arrives late.

## Argument

The mechanism is credit. Nvidia's $500 billion financing package, with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR, isn't about growth—it's about debt. The market's 2.6% drop on announcement day signals the shift. Hyperscaler capex is exploding, but cash flows are collapsing: Amazon posted negative $8.8 billion free cash flow, Alphabet negative $5.9 billion, Oracle negative $1.9 billion. PIMCO says capex eats 94% of hyperscaler operating cash flow. So where's the money coming from? The bond market. Tech issuance jumped from $16.7 billion in 2024 to $193 billion by mid-2026, and Morgan Stanley and JP Morgan see $1.5 trillion more needed through 2028. Off-balance-sheet obligations are massive—Moody's tracks $1.22 trillion in data center leases, with $820 billion on unbuilt facilities. The structure is circular: Nvidia and others use vendor financing, which the BIS flags as a top-three global stability risk. The risk is pushed into SPVs and private credit funds, but Nvidia may backstop up to $125 billion. GPUs age like laptops, but the debt runs like a toll road. Michael Burry argues depreciation is understated by $176 billion, overstating Oracle's 2028 profits by 27% and Meta's by 21%. This spreads systemically: the Magnificent 7 are 34% of the S&P 500, so a repricing hits pensions directly. Rising bond yields—10-year at 4.72%—crowd out other borrowers. Private credit exposure to AI is over $200 billion, with Coreweave pledging GPUs as collateral. This mirrors the dot-com bust: Lucent and Nortel financed customers, then ate defaults. ## Counterpoints

- These are the most creditworthy companies on the planet; this isn't 1999.
- Nvidia's vendor financing is safer than dot-com era because risk is pushed off balance sheet and investors bring outside money.
- Nvidia circulated a memo arguing 4-6 year depreciation is realistic because chips cascade into inference and enterprise workloads.
- Jensen Huang says compute is now an investable asset class; BlackRock's Larry Fink compares it to the birth of mortgage-backed securities.
