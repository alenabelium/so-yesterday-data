## Thesis

The AI boom is no longer a tech story but a credit story, financed by debt and circular structures that could hit pensions and insurers if revenue arrives late.

## Argument

The AI buildout is increasingly debt-funded, not profit-funded. Nvidia's $500 billion financing deal and the negative free cash flows at Amazon, Alphabet, and Oracle show the shift. The debt is structured through vendor financing and special purpose vehicles, which the BIS flags as a systemic risk. Market concentration, rising bond yields, and private credit exposure amplify the risk. If AI revenue is delayed, the damage will land on pensions and insurers, not just tech companies.

## Counterpoints

- These are the most creditworthy companies on the planet, with diversified revenue and investment grade ratings.
- Nvidia's vendor financing is more protected than dot-com era deals because risk is pushed to outside investors.
- Chips are fungible and broadly adopted, making compute an investable asset class.
- Depreciation schedules are realistic because chips cascade from training to inference and enterprise workloads.
