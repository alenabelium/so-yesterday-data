## Thesis

The AI boom is now a credit story, not a tech story, and if AI revenue arrives late, the damage will hit pensions and insurers, not just tech stocks.

## Argument

The mechanism is debt. Nvidia's $500 billion financing package, with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR, was treated as bad news because it signals the buildout is no longer funded by profits. Amazon, Alphabet, and Oracle all posted negative free cash flow in their most recent quarters, while capex eats 94% of hyperscaler operating cash flow. The gap is filled by bonds: tech issuance jumped from $16.7 billion in 2024 to $193 billion by mid-2026, with Morgan Stanley and JP Morgan projecting $1.5 trillion more needed through 2028. Off-balance-sheet commitments are even larger, with Moody's tracking $1.22 trillion in data center leases, $820 billion of which is for unbuilt facilities. The BIS flags circular AI financing as a top-three risk to global financial stability, alongside sovereign debt fragility. This spreads through three channels: concentration (Mag 7 is 34% of the S&P 500), rates (10-year at 4.72%, 30-year at 5.25%, driven partly by hyperscaler issuance), and private credit (exposure above $200 billion, with Coreweave pledging GPUs as collateral). The dot-com parallel is explicit: Lucent and Nortel financed their own customers, and over 90% of telecom high-yield debt defaulted. The demand thesis wasn't wrong then, just a decade early and financed on too short a timeline. Today, the same mismatch exists: GPUs age like laptops but are financed like toll roads.

## Counterpoints

- These are the most creditworthy companies on the planet, with investment-grade ratings and diversified revenue that doesn't depend on AI monetization to survive.
- The risk is pushed off Nvidia's balance sheet into SPVs and private credit funds, giving Nvidia more protection than dot-com-era vendor financing.
- Nvidia circulated a memo arguing that 4-6 year depreciation is realistic because chips cascade from training to inference and enterprise workloads.
- BlackRock's Larry Fink compares the buildout to the birth of mortgage-backed securities, and Blackstone's John Gray says compute will be underwritten like mortgages.
