## Thesis

The AI boom is now a credit story, not a tech story, and if AI revenue arrives late, the damage will hit pensions and insurers, not just tech stocks.

## Argument

The mechanism is credit. Nvidia's $500 billion financing package, with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR, was treated as bad news because it signaled the buildout is now debt-funded. The cash machines have stopped being cash machines: Amazon posted negative $8.8 billion free cash flow, Alphabet negative $5.9 billion, Oracle negative $1.9 billion, and only Microsoft remains positive. PIMCO says capex eats 94% of hyperscaler operating cash flow. So the bond market fills the gap: tech issuance hit $225 billion in H1, hyperscaler issuance jumped from $16.7 billion in 2024 to $193 billion by mid-2026, and Morgan Stanley and JP Morgan see $1.5 trillion of new tech debt needed through 2028. The structure is circular: vendor financing, flagged by the BIS as a top-three global stability risk. Nvidia pushes risk into SPVs and private credit funds, but Jensen Huang admits up to $125 billion of exposure. The mismatch: GPUs age like laptops, debt runs like a toll road. Michael Burry estimates depreciation understated by $176 billion, overstating Oracle's 2028 profits by 27% and Meta's by 21%. The systemic channels: concentration (Mag 7 at 34% of S&P 500), rates (10-year at 4.72%, 30-year at 5.25%), and private credit (exposure above $200 billion, Coreweave's $12.4 billion loans with GPU collateral). This mirrors the dot-com fiber bust: demand was right, but a decade early and financed with short-dated paper. If revenue is 3 years late, the damage lands on pensions and insurers, not the chip buyers.

## Counterpoints

- These are the most creditworthy companies on the planet; they have investment-grade ratings and diversified revenue that doesn't depend on AI monetization to survive.
- The new structures are cleverer: risk is pushed off Nvidia's balance sheet into SPVs and private credit funds, giving more protection than dot-com vendor financing.
- Nvidia circulated a seven-page memo rejecting Enron comparisons, arguing four-to-six-year depreciation is realistic because chips cascade from training to inference.
- Larry Fink compares the buildout to the birth of mortgage-backed securities, and John Gray says compute will be underwritten like mortgages.
