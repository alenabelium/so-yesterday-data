## Thesis

Zcash's 2,200% surge is not a fluke but the compounding of four long-standing arguments—privacy, quantum resistance, bug resilience, and institutional/meme demand—that finally converged, flipping its narrative from relic to top-10.

## Argument

The first mechanism is the world catching up to privacy. Bitcoin's transparency was fine when the network was tiny and ideological, but now every address is a permanent public record and chain surveillance is a mature industry with government customers. ICE surveillance contracts jumped from $310M to $513M, and TRM Labs won a $95M contract without competitive bidding, prompting Chainalysis to protest. Physical attacks are up too: CertiK verified 52 wrench attacks in H1 2026, an 11.8x increase, with home invasions rising from 14% to 37% of those attacks. Meanwhile, privacy workarounds are being dismantled—Samourai founders got prison sentences, Wasabi's coordinator shut down, and Tornado Cash's Roman Storm faces retrial. Zcash didn't win on marketing; it won because privacy was massively undervalued.

The second thread is quantum resistance, where Zcash is ahead. A community vote passed quantum recoverability with 94.6% support, and the Ironwood upgrade activated in July, giving a protocol-level path to re-secure funds without exposing history. Project Tachyon targets 'harvest now, decrypt later' by removing encrypted payloads entirely, with full post-quantum migration in 12-18 months. Bitcoin, by contrast, has BIP 360 and BIP 361 but neither is activated, and the developer base is split—Adam Back wants a voluntary decade-long path, others want a hard cutoff. Over 34% of circulating Bitcoin has exposed public keys.
