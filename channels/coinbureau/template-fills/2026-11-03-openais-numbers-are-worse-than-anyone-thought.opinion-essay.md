## Thesis

OpenAI's real revenue is $50B, not the $70B the market assumed, and its $856B spending plan creates a fragile debt ecosystem that threatens the entire AI infrastructure sector.

## Argument

The $70 billion figure was never OpenAI's official number. It was an analyst adjustment to make OpenAI's net revenue comparable to Anthropic's gross revenue, which counts the full sale price before cloud partners take their cut. Once Axios reported it, the market treated it as fact, and when the FT revealed the real $50B run rate, the correction hit stocks like CoreWeave, Oracle, and SoftBank hard. But the revenue miss is not the core problem. OpenAI's internal plan calls for $856B in infrastructure spending by 2030, up from $600B, while it expects only $36B in revenue this year and cumulative negative free cash flow of $278B through 2030. The debt burden is spread across partners: Oracle has $156B in debt, CoreWeave $52B, SoftBank took a $40B bridge loan and issued $11.1B in junk bonds. Lenders are betting that OpenAI's growth will cover the bills, but the margin for error is zero. This is a house of cards built on borrowed money and optimistic projections.

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's revenue is still growing fast, adding $30B in nine months, so the sales slump isn't the issue.
- The $70B figure was a realistic estimate, just not OpenAI's official revenue, so the correction may be overblown.
- OpenAI's valuation of $1.25T and fresh capital of $152B could provide a buffer against the debt.
