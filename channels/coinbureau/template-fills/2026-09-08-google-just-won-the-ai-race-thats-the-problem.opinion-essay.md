## Thesis

Google has already won the AI race by leveraging its distribution monopoly, not by out-innovating rivals, and that dominance is the real problem for the industry.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Google didn't win by building a better model; it won by embedding Gemini into Android, Chrome, Gmail, and Search, reaching a billion monthly users without a single ad campaign. Meanwhile, OpenAI and Anthropic are paying Google billions to rent TPUs, making them tenants in the very infrastructure they hoped to disrupt. Google designs the chips, sells them, leases them, guarantees the lease, owns the building, and owns a stake in the tenant—a landlord who also owns the brick factory and co-signs your mortgage. This asymmetry extends to the market structure: Google controls the phone OS, browser, email, office suite, ad market, search index, cloud, and chips. AI overviews are already killing publisher traffic—users click traditional links just 8% of the time when an AI overview appears—and there's no alternative at scale. The next phase is AI agents, and Google is positioning itself to own the communication, consent, and commerce layers with A2A, AP2, and UCP protocols. OpenAI's own agent protocol was quickly retired. Google even had a working chatbot a year before ChatGPT but was too nervous to release it, letting OpenAI spend the venture capital and teach the market, only to scoop it up later. The conclusion is stark: distribution decides the outcome, and Google's distribution is unmatched.

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's billion users are weekly, while Google's are monthly, so by engagement intensity OpenAI is still ahead.
- Google still depends on other companies for hardware and faces manufacturing bottlenecks; it doesn't control everything.
- Anthropic is spreading its bets across Amazon, Nvidia, AMD, and others, not relying solely on Google.
- Some argue that Anthropic and OpenAI have better models and that quality will eventually win, though Gemini is now competitive.
