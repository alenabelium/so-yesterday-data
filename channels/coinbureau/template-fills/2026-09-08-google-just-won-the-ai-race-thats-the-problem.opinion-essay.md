## Thesis

Google has already won the AI race by leveraging its unmatched distribution, not by out-innovating rivals, and this dominance is the real problem.

## Argument

The win wasn't a comeback; it was a foregone conclusion. Google didn't acquire a single Gemini user—it hardwired the assistant into Android's 3 billion devices, Chrome's 69% browser share, and a search box handling 8.5 billion daily queries. AI Overviews alone reach 2.5 billion monthly users. Meanwhile, OpenAI and Anthropic, once cast as executioners, now rent compute from Google, signing contracts worth hundreds of billions for TPUs. Google designs the chips, sells them, leases them, guarantees the lease, owns a slice of the building, and owns a piece of the tenant—a landlord who also owns the brick factory and co-signs your mortgage. This asymmetry extends to the balance sheet: Alphabet funds its own infrastructure while rivals pile on off-balance-sheet commitments nearing $3 trillion, a 'subprime data center crisis' per one analyst. The quality argument is collapsing too—Gemini now matches or beats rivals on reasoning and coding while being cheaper to run, and developers show preference with 22 billion tokens per minute in API traffic. Distribution decides the outcome, and it isn't close. Google also controls the phone OS, browser, email, office suite, ad market, search index, cloud, and chips. AI Overviews are killing publisher clicks—users click traditional links just 8% of the time when they appear—and there's no alternative at scale. Antitrust rulings changed nothing. Now Google is building the agent economy's rails: A2A for communication, AP2 for payments, UCP for commerce, all with major partners. OpenAI's rival protocol was quickly retired. ## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's billion is weekly active users, a harder bar than Google's monthly figure, so by engagement intensity OpenAI is still ahead.
- Google still depends on others for hardware; TPU production faces bottlenecks and Google claims it lacks enough compute.
- Anthropic is spreading bets across Amazon, Nvidia, AMD, and others to avoid over-reliance on Google.
- Some argue rivals have better models and quality will eventually win, though this position is weakening as Gemini improves.
