## Thesis

Google has already won the AI race by leveraging its distribution monopoly, not by out-innovating rivals, and this dominance is the real problem for the industry.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Google didn't win by building the best model; it won by embedding Gemini into Android, Chrome, Gmail, and Search, reaching over a billion monthly users without a single ad campaign. Meanwhile, rivals like OpenAI and Anthropic are financially dependent on Google for compute, signing contracts worth hundreds of billions to rent TPUs from the very company they aim to dethrone. Google designs the chips, sells the chips, leases the chips, guarantees the lease, owns a slice of the building, and owns a piece of the tenant. This vertical integration gives Google a structural advantage that no amount of model quality can overcome. Gemini is now competitive on reasoning and coding, and it's cheaper to run, so the 'quality will win' argument is falling apart. But the deeper issue is market shape: Google controls the phone OS, browser, email, office suite, ad market, search index, cloud, chips, and now the AI model. AI overviews are already reducing publisher clicks, with users clicking traditional links just 8% of the time when an AI overview appears. And the alternative doesn't exist yet—Google still delivers 87% of search-driven referral traffic. The next phase is AI agents, and Google is building the infrastructure: A2A protocol, AP2 for payments, UCP for commerce. It's positioning itself to control the communication, consent, and commerce layers of the agent economy. ## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's billion users are weekly, a harder bar than Google's monthly figure, so by engagement intensity OpenAI is still ahead.
- Google still depends on other companies for hardware, with TPU production held back by manufacturing bottlenecks, and it claims it doesn't have enough compute.
- Anthropic is spreading its bets across Amazon, Nvidia, AMD, and others, not relying too heavily on Google.
- Some argue that Anthropic and OpenAI have better models and that quality will eventually win, though this position is weakening.
