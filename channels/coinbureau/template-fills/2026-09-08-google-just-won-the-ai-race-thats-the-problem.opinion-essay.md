## Thesis

Google has already won the AI race by leveraging its unmatched distribution, not by out-innovating rivals, and this dominance is the real problem for the industry.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. While OpenAI acquired users one at a time, Google hardwired Gemini into Android (3B+ devices), Chrome (69% browser share), Gmail, and Search (8.5B queries/day), hitting 1B monthly users with zero ad spend. Rivals are financially entangled: Anthropic rents TPUs from Google, with a deal expanding to ~$200B over 5 years, and Google owns ~14% of Anthropic plus equity in its data centers. Google designs, sells, leases, guarantees, and owns the building and the tenant—a landlord who also owns the brick factory. This asymmetry compounds: Google funds its own infrastructure from ad profits, while OpenAI and others pile up off-balance-sheet commitments (~$3T across nine firms), which one analyst calls a 'subprime data center crisis.' Quality gaps are closing—Gemini leads on some reasoning tests and is cheaper to run—so distribution becomes decisive. Google also controls the emerging agent stack: A2A protocol (150+ orgs), AP2 payments (Mastercard, PayPal, Coinbase), and UCP commerce with Shopify. Meanwhile, OpenAI's rival protocol was retired. The result: Google's AI overviews cut publisher clicks (8% click-through), and with 87% of search referral traffic, there's no alternative. Antitrust rulings changed nothing. Google didn't win by being first; it let OpenAI teach the market, then scooped it with existing infrastructure.

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's 1B weekly users vs Google's 1B monthly—engagement intensity still favors OpenAI.
- Google depends on others for hardware; TPU production bottlenecks and claims of insufficient compute.
- Anthropic diversifies across Amazon, Nvidia, AMD, not just Google.
- Quality could still win—Gemini was behind for a while, but that gap is closing.
- Google's monopoly ruling had limited remedy; it kept Chrome and Android, so structural change is unlikely.
