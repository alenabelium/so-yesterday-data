## Thesis

Google won the AI race by distribution, not innovation, and that dominance is the problem.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Google didn't win by out-innovating; it won by embedding Gemini into Android, Chrome, Gmail, and Search, amassing over a billion monthly users without a single ad campaign. Meanwhile, rivals like OpenAI and Anthropic are financially dependent on Google, signing contracts worth tens of billions to rent TPUs from the very company they aim to dethrone. Google designs the chips, sells the chips, leases the chips, guarantees the lease, owns a slice of the building, and owns a piece of the tenant. It's a landlord who also owns the brick factory, co-signs your mortgage, and owns shares in your business. You can still succeed, but only in a way that pays him.

This asymmetry extends to the market structure. Google holds the phone OS, browser, email, office suite, ad market, search index, cloud, chips, and now the AI model on top. AI overviews are reducing publisher clicks—Pew found users click traditional links just 8% of the time when AI overviews appear. And the alternative doesn't exist yet: Cloudflare data shows Google delivers 87% of search-driven referral traffic, while ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity combined account for 0.29%. Even after being ruled an illegal monopolist, Google changed nothing.

Now, Google is positioning for the next phase: AI agents. It launched A2A, an agent-to-agent protocol, AP2 for agent payments, and UCP for commerce, building the communication, consent, and commerce layers. ## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's billion is weekly users, a harder bar than Google's monthly figure, so by engagement intensity OpenAI is still ahead.
- Google still depends on others for hardware; TPU production is bottlenecked, and Anthropic is spreading bets across Amazon, Nvidia, AMD, and others.
- Gemini was once significantly behind the best models, but that gap has closed, and on some reasoning tests it now leads.
