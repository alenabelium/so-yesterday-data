## Thesis

Google has already won the AI race through distribution, not innovation, and its rivals are now paying rent to the very company they aimed to dethrone.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. Google didn't win by out-innovating; it won by embedding Gemini into Android, Chrome, Gmail, and Search, amassing over a billion monthly users without a single ad campaign. Meanwhile, OpenAI and Anthropic are financially dependent on Google for compute, signing contracts worth hundreds of billions to rent TPUs—Google's own chips. Google designs, sells, leases, and guarantees the hardware, owns a slice of the data center, and holds equity in Anthropic itself. It's a landlord who also owns the brick factory, co-signs your mortgage, and owns shares in your business. You can succeed, but only in ways that pay him.

This asymmetry extends to the model quality debate. Gemini is no longer significantly behind the best models, and on some reasoning tests it's ahead. Its API traffic hit 22 billion tokens per minute in Q2 2026, showing developer preference. But even if Gemini were merely 'good enough,' distribution decides the outcome. Google's AI overviews are reshaping web traffic—Pew found users click traditional links just 8% of the time when AI overviews appear. Publishers are losing hundreds of millions of clicks monthly, and there's no alternative at Google's scale: Cloudflare data shows Google delivers 87% of search-driven referral traffic, while ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity combined account for 0.29%.

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's billion users are weekly, while Google's are monthly, so by engagement intensity OpenAI is still ahead.
- Google depends on other companies for hardware; TPU production faces bottlenecks, and Google claims it still doesn't have enough compute.
- Anthropic is spreading its bets across Amazon, Nvidia, AMD, and others, not relying solely on Google.
- The 'better model wins' argument: for a while, rivals had superior models, but Gemini has closed the gap.
