## Thesis

Google has already won the AI race not by building the best model, but by owning the distribution layer—Android, Chrome, Search, Gmail—that makes its Gemini assistant unavoidable, and this dominance is now reshaping the entire internet.

## Argument

The win wasn't a comeback; it was a distribution ambush. While OpenAI acquired users one at a time, Google hardwired Gemini into Android's 3 billion devices, Chrome's 69% browser share, and a search box handling 8.5 billion queries daily—hitting a billion monthly users with zero ad spend. The real kicker is the compute trap: Anthropic and OpenAI are renting Google's TPUs, with Anthropic's deal ballooning to $200 billion over five years, financed by Apollo and Blackstone with Google backstopping the leases and taking a 14% equity stake. Google designs the chips, sells them, leases them, guarantees the lease, owns the building, and owns the tenant—a landlord who also owns the brick factory and co-signs your mortgage. You can succeed, but only in ways that pay him.

Quality arguments are collapsing too. Gemini now leads on some reasoning tests, its coding is sharp, and its API traffic hit 22 billion tokens per minute. But the deeper story is market structure: AI overviews cut publisher clicks to 8%, and Google still delivers 87% of search referral traffic while ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity combined manage 0.29%. The antitrust remedy was a slap on the wrist—Google kept Chrome, Android, and its default-search payments. Now Google is building the rails for AI agents: verifiable credentials for consent, a commerce protocol with Shopify, and a payments network with Mastercard and Stripe. OpenAI's rival protocol was quietly retired. Even the origin story is wrong—Google had a working chatbot a year before ChatGPT but was too nervous to ship it. It let OpenAI burn

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's billion is weekly active users, a harder bar than Google's monthly figure, so by engagement intensity OpenAI is still ahead.
- Google still depends on others for hardware; TPU production faces bottlenecks, and Google admits it doesn't have enough compute.
- Anthropic is diversifying across Amazon, Nvidia, AMD, and others, not relying solely on Google.
- Some argue OpenAI and Anthropic have better models and that quality will eventually win—a position that's been falling apart as Gemini closes the gap.
