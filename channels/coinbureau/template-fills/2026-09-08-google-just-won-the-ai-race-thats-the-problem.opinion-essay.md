## Thesis

Google has already won the AI race by leveraging its unmatched distribution across Android, Chrome, Search, and Gmail, making rivals like OpenAI and Anthropic financially dependent on it for compute.

## Argument

The win wasn't a comeback; it was a default. Google didn't acquire a single Gemini user—it hardwired the assistant into Android's 3 billion devices, Chrome's 69% browser share, and a search box handling 8.5 billion queries daily. AI Overviews now reach 2.5 billion monthly users, and AI mode alone passed a billion. Meanwhile, rivals pay rent to the very company they aim to dethrone. Anthropic's cloud deal with Google secures up to 1 million TPUs, with commitments ballooning to $200 billion over five years. The structure is telling: Google designs the chips, sells them, leases them, guarantees the lease, owns a slice of the building, and owns 14% of the tenant. It's a landlord who also owns the brick factory and co-signs your mortgage. With TPUs 40-50% cheaper than Nvidia's, Google pays itself and invoices the field. UBS estimates 27% of Google Cloud's revenue comes from OpenAI and Anthropic combined. Alphabet's $120 billion quarterly revenue funds its own buildout, while rivals pile on off-balance-sheet commitments—$3 trillion across nine firms, triple their combined leases. Ed Zitron calls it a 'subprime data center crisis.'

Quality arguments are fading. Gemini now leads on some reasoning tests, coding improved sharply, and API traffic hit 22 billion tokens per minute. But the deeper story is market structure. Google controls the OS, browser, email, office suite, ad market, search index, cloud, chips, and now the model. ## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's billion users are weekly, a harder bar than Google's monthly figure, so by engagement intensity OpenAI is still ahead.
- Google depends on others for hardware; TPU production faces bottlenecks, and Google claims it still lacks enough compute.
- Anthropic is spreading bets across Amazon, Nvidia, AMD, and others, not relying solely on Google.
- Some argue OpenAI and Anthropic have better models, and quality will eventually win—though Gemini is closing the gap.
- Google's dominance could be checked by antitrust remedies, but so far rulings have forced no real change.
