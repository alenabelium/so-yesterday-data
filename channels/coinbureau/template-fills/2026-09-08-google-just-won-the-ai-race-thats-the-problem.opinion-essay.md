## Thesis

Google has already won the AI race by leveraging its distribution monopoly, not by out-innovating rivals, and this dominance is the real problem.

## Argument

The win wasn't a comeback; it was a foregone conclusion. Google didn't acquire a single Gemini user — it hardwired the assistant into Android (3B+ devices), Chrome (69% browser share), Gmail, Workspace, and a search box handling 8.5B queries daily. AI Overviews alone reach 2.5B monthly users. OpenAI's billion is weekly and harder, but Google doesn't need engagement when it owns the default. The asymmetry extends to compute: Anthropic and OpenAI rent TPUs from Google, paying billions for the privilege of competing. Google designs, sells, leases, guarantees, and owns a slice of the building and the tenant — a landlord who also owns the brick factory and co-signs your mortgage. TPUs are 40-50% cheaper than Nvidia, and Google pays itself, then invoices the field. UBS estimates 27% of Google Cloud revenue comes from its rivals. Meanwhile, Google's ad business funds its own infrastructure, while competitors pile on off-balance-sheet commitments — $3 trillion across nine firms, triple their combined leases. The "quality will win" argument is collapsing: Gemini now leads on some reasoning tests, coding improved, and API traffic hit 22B tokens/minute. But the deeper issue is structural: Google controls the OS, browser, email, office suite, ad market, search index, cloud, chips, and now the model. AI Overviews cut publisher clicks to 8%, and Cloudflare data shows Google delivers 87% of search referral traffic while ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity combined account for 0.29%. The antitrust ruling changed nothing. Now Google is building the agent economy's rails — A2A

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's billion users are weekly vs Google's monthly, so by engagement intensity OpenAI is still ahead.
- Google depends on others for hardware; TPU production has bottlenecks and Google claims it still lacks compute.
- Anthropic is spreading bets across Amazon, Nvidia, AMD, and others, not relying solely on Google.
- The "quality will win" argument held for a while, but Gemini is no longer significantly behind on reasoning and coding.
- Google's win isn't total — it doesn't control everything, and rivals can still succeed, but only by paying Google.
