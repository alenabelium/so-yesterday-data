## Thesis

Oracle's Stargate project, the centerpiece of a $500 billion AI infrastructure bet, is already breaking down due to a gas pipeline delay, exposing the fragility of the debt-financed AI boom.

## Argument

The mechanism is a single pipeline. The Green Chile project, a 17-mile natural gas line meant to feed the off-grid Bloom Energy fuel cells at Project Jupiter, has been denied rights-of-way by New Mexico's state land office twice, forcing a reroute and pushing its in-service date from August 2025 to February 2027. The air permit for the fuel cells is still pending, and environmental groups have sued. This is not a chip shortage or a model failure; it's paperwork and a pipe. Oracle responded in late September by issuing a force majeure notice to Blue Owl's Stack Infrastructure unit, seeking the right to defer lease payments by up to three years if the site misses its 2028 target. The fuel cell microgrid is only about 10% built. This is the overlooked weakness of the AI trade: software scales at the push of a button, but power and permits do not. The financial structure amplifies the risk. Project Jupiter runs on $3 billion of Blue Owl equity and $18 billion in construction loans from Goldman Sachs, Santander, and Jefferies. Oracle's capex tripled to $28.5 billion in a single quarter, leaving free cash flow negative and total debt at $156 billion. OpenAI, the customer, funds itself with investor money and is eyeing a fresh $30 billion round. The whole chain—banks lend to the developer, Oracle borrows to build, OpenAI raises to pay Oracle—relies on everyone's cash arriving on schedule. A delay anywhere hits every link. ## Counterpoints

- Stargate is just hitting a rough patch, not a fundamental flaw in the AI boom.
- The pipeline delay is a one-off regulatory issue, not systemic to AI infrastructure.
- Oracle's force majeure notice is a standard legal protection, not a sign of distress.
