## Thesis

Oracle's Stargate project is breaking because the AI boom's debt-financed infrastructure is vulnerable to mundane bottlenecks like gas pipelines and permits, not just chip shortages.

## Argument

The mechanism is a cascade of debt and dependency. Stargate's New Mexico site, Project Jupiter, is anchored by Oracle and serves OpenAI, but its power source—an off-grid microgrid of Bloom Energy fuel cells—depends on the Green Chile gas pipeline. That pipeline is stuck in regulatory delays: New Mexico denied rights-of-way twice, forcing a reroute that pushed service to February 2027. Oracle responded with a force majeure notice, seeking to defer lease payments by up to three years if the site misses its 2028 target. The fuel cell microgrid is only 10% built. This is the overlooked fragility: software scales instantly, but power and permits don't. The financial structure amplifies the risk. Blue Owl put in $3 billion equity and $18 billion in construction loans; Oracle's capex tripled to $28.5 billion in a quarter, leaving free cash flow negative and debt at $156 billion. OpenAI funds itself with investor money, eyeing a $30 billion round. So banks lend to developers, Oracle borrows to build, OpenAI raises to pay Oracle—every link depends on everyone else's cash arriving on schedule. A stalled site means no revenue, but interest keeps ticking. The cost doesn't disappear; it pushes down the chain to lenders and equity holders. Construction loans trade at 89 cents on the dollar, and banks can't offload them. S&P cut Oracle to triple-B, one notch above junk, and CDS costs hit a record. Founder Larry Ellison has pledged 430 million shares as collateral for personal loans, tying his fortune to the stock, which is down 56% from its high. A 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 50 million

## Counterpoints

- One might argue Stargate is just hitting a rough patch, not a systemic failure, and that delays are normal for large infrastructure projects.
- Another view is that the AI boom's demand is so relentless that any temporary setback will be overcome by market forces and government support.
- Some might say the force majeure notice is a prudent legal move, not a sign of distress, and that Oracle is simply protecting its interests.
