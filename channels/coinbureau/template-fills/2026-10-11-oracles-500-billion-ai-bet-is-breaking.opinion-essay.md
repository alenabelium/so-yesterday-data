## Thesis

Oracle's Stargate project, the centerpiece of a $500 billion AI infrastructure bet, is already breaking down due to a gas pipeline and a mountain of debt, exposing the fragility of the entire AI boom.

## Argument

The mechanism is physical and financial. The Stargate data center in New Mexico, Project Jupiter, is stalled because a natural gas pipeline, the Green Chile project, is stuck in regulatory delays. Without the pipeline, the on-site fuel cells can't run, and without power, the data center is just an expensive shed. Oracle has issued a force majeure notice to defer lease payments, signaling that the project may miss its 2028 target. This is the overlooked part of the AI trade: software scales at the push of a button, but power and permits don't. The financial structure is equally fragile. Project Jupiter runs on borrowed money: Blue Owl put in $3 billion of equity, banks arranged $18 billion in construction loans, and Oracle's capex has tripled to $28.5 billion in a single quarter, pushing total debt to $156 billion. OpenAI, the customer, funds itself with investor money. The whole chain relies on everyone's cash arriving on schedule. A delay anywhere hits every link. The stalled site threatens revenue, revenue services debt, debt weighs on the stock, and the stock backs Larry Ellison's personal loans, which are collateralized by 430 million Oracle shares. Oracle's credit rating was cut to tripleB, and its CDS hit a record. The founder's 10b5-1 selling plan, cancelled two days after it was revealed, adds to the picture. This is one tangled mess, and a single failure can drag the rest down. Stargate was meant to prove the boom works, but it's showing the boom's weak point.

## Counterpoints

- The pipeline delay is just a temporary regulatory hiccup, and the project will eventually get built.
- Oracle's debt is manageable given its scale and cash flow, and the AI demand is real.
- The force majeure notice is just a standard legal precaution, not a sign of real trouble.
