## Thesis

Oracle's $500 billion Stargate AI bet is breaking because the New Mexico flagship project is stalled by a gas pipeline, exposing how power and permitting bottlenecks, not chips, can derail the AI boom.

## Argument

The mechanism is physical and financial at once. A 17-mile gas pipeline, the Green Chile project, is stuck in regulatory delays: New Mexico's state land office denied rights of way twice, forcing a reroute that pushed the in-service date from August 2025 to February 2027. The air permit for Bloom Energy fuel cells is still pending, and environmental groups have sued. Oracle responded with a force majeure notice, seeking the right to defer lease payments by up to three years if the site misses its 2028 target. That notice is the tell: the cost of delay doesn't disappear, it gets pushed down the chain. The site runs on borrowed money — Blue Owl put in $3 billion of equity and a bank group arranged $18 billion in construction loans, which are already trading at 89 cents on the dollar. Oracle's capex tripled to $28.5 billion in a single quarter, leaving free cash flow negative and total debt at $156 billion. OpenAI, the customer, funds itself with investor money and is eyeing a fresh $30 billion round. Every link depends on everyone else's cash arriving on schedule. The stalled site threatens revenue, revenue services debt, debt weighs on the stock, and the stock backs Larry Ellison's personal loans — he's pledged 430 million shares as collateral, and Oracle is down 56% from its high. This is the AI boom's weak point: software scales at the push of a button, but power and permits don't. The most hyped project in America is being held up by paperwork and a pipe.

## Counterpoints

- Some might argue Stargate is just hitting a rough patch, not a structural failure.
- Others might say the pipeline delay is a one-off regulatory hiccup, not a systemic problem.
- A skeptic could claim the AI boom's demand is so relentless that any delay will be absorbed by future growth.
