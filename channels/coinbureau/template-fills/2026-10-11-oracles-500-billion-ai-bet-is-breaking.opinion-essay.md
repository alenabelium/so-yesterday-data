## Thesis

Oracle's $500 billion Stargate AI bet is breaking because the real bottleneck isn't chips or models but power and permits, and the debt-financed structure makes a single pipeline delay cascade into financial crisis.

## Argument

The mechanism is physical and financial at once. A 17-mile gas pipeline, the Green Chile project, is stuck in regulatory hell: New Mexico denied rights of way twice, the federal approval came too late, and the air permit for Bloom Energy fuel cells is still pending. That pushed the in-service date from August 2025 to February 2027, and Oracle responded with a force majeure notice, seeking the right to defer lease payments by up to three years if the site misses its 2028 target. The fuel cell microgrid is only 10% built. This is the overlooked weakness of the AI trade: software scales instantly, but power and permits don't. The financial structure amplifies every delay. Project Jupiter runs on $18 billion in construction loans from banks like Goldman Sachs and Jefferies, with only $3 billion in equity from Blue Owl. Oracle's capex tripled to $28.5 billion in a quarter, free cash flow is negative, total debt is $156 billion, and it's raising $40 billion more. OpenAI, the customer, funds itself with investor money and is eyeing a $30 billion round. Every link depends on the next one's cash arriving on schedule. A stalled site earns nothing, but interest ticks daily. The cost of delay doesn't disappear—it gets pushed down the chain onto lenders and equity holders. Construction loans are already trading at 89 cents on the dollar, S&P cut Oracle to triple-B, and credit default swaps hit a record. ## Counterpoints

- Some might argue Stargate is just hitting a rough patch, not a systemic failure, and that delays are normal for mega-projects.
- Others could say the AI boom is unstoppable and that power and permitting bottlenecks are temporary, solvable with time and political will.
- A skeptic might claim the force majeure notice is just prudent legal protection, not a sign of imminent collapse.
- One could argue that Oracle's debt is manageable given its scale and that the stock drop is an overreaction to short-term news.
