## Thesis

Oracle's Stargate project shows that the AI boom's biggest weakness is not chips or models but the physical and financial infrastructure—power, permits, and debt—that can break the entire bet.

## Argument

The first crack appears in the desert. Project Jupiter, Oracle's flagship Stargate site in New Mexico, is stalled because a natural gas pipeline—the Green Chile project—is stuck in regulatory hell. The state denied rights of way twice, forcing a reroute that pushed the in-service date from August 2025 to February 2027. Meanwhile, the air permit for the Bloom Energy fuel cells is still pending, and environmental groups have sued. Oracle responded with a force majeure notice, seeking the right to defer lease payments by up to three years if the site misses its 2028 target. At the time, the microgrid was only 10% built.

The deeper problem is financial. This isn't just a construction delay; it's a debt cascade. Project Jupiter runs on $3 billion of equity and $18 billion in construction loans from banks like Goldman Sachs. Oracle's capex tripled to $28.5 billion in a single quarter, pushing total debt to $156 billion, with $40 billion more planned. OpenAI, the customer, is itself funded by investor money, eyeing a $30 billion round. So banks lend to developers, Oracle borrows to build, OpenAI raises to pay Oracle—and every link depends on the next one's cash arriving on schedule. A stalled site earns nothing, but interest ticks daily. The force majeure doesn't erase the cost; it just pushes it down the chain to lenders and equity holders.

The strain is already visible. Construction loans trade at 89 cents on the dollar, S&P cut Oracle to triple-B, and credit default swaps hit a record. Larry Ellison has pledged 430 million Oracle shares as collateral for personal loans—more

## Counterpoints

- Stargate is just hitting a rough patch; delays are normal for large infrastructure projects and the 2028 target is still years away.
- The AI boom is driven by relentless demand, and any short-term debt stress will be absorbed by the sheer scale of future revenue.
- Oracle's force majeure is a standard legal protection, not a sign of imminent collapse; it's prudent risk management.
