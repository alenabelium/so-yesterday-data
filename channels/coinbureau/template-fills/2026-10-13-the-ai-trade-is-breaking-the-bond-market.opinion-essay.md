## Thesis

The AI boom has outgrown corporate cash reserves and is now being financed with borrowed money, straining the bond market and threatening the entire financial system.

## Argument

The mechanism is a shift from cash to debt. For years, the biggest AI players funded their data centers with profits, but as chips, energy, and land became more expensive and scarce, they started borrowing. Alphabet's debt grew from $41 billion to $112 billion in 12 months, and Meta's from $49 billion to $112 billion. Amazon spent $173 billion in capex versus $161 billion in operating cash flow, and Oracle spent $75 billion against $46 billion. Alphabet even recorded negative free cash flow for the first time in history in Q2 2026, then immediately issued $25 billion in bonds with maturities up to 40 years.

This borrowing has become more creative and riskier. Meta financed its Hyperion campus through an SPV, issuing $27.3 billion in bonds at 6.58% that don't mature until 2049. CoreWeave closed an $8.5 billion loan secured by Nvidia GPUs, and Moody's gave it an investment-grade rating. Now, the market is weighing who is willing to lend to the models, not just what they can do.

The bond market is already crowded. The US government needs to absorb over $40 trillion in national debt, with interest payments exceeding $1 trillion. Hyperscalers issued about $200 billion in investment bonds in the first half of 2026, almost double the total for all of 2025. This influx of supply has pushed yields up: the 10-year Treasury yield rose from 4.18% to 5.31% in a year, and the 30-year from 4.76% to 5.66%. As a result, most hyperscale bonds are trading at a loss, and Oracle's CDS spread jumped from under 50 basis points to over 225, signaling junk-level risk.

The chain is fragile.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that AI companies have huge cash reserves and can fund development without borrowing, but the video shows they've already spent those reserves and are now turning to debt.
- Others might say that corporate debt is manageable relative to the size of these companies, but the video points out that the bond market is already strained by government debt, and the influx of corporate bonds is pushing yields up.
- A common objection is that AI bonds are safe investments, but the video notes that 78 of 91 hyperscale bonds issued in 2026 are trading at higher yields than at issuance, meaning most buyers are already at a loss.
- Some might argue that SPVs and GPU-backed loans are innovative and reduce risk, but the video highlights that these structures create complex dependencies and off-balance-sheet debt that can backfire.
