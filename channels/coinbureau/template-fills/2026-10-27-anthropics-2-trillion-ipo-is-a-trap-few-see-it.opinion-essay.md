## Thesis

Anthropic's $2 trillion IPO is a trap that enriches insiders and its largest investors, Amazon and Alphabet, while public buyers absorb all the risk.

## Argument

The mechanism is a closed financial loop. Amazon and Alphabet are Anthropic's largest investors, its primary cloud providers, and its main marketplace distributors. Every dollar they invest returns to them as cloud bills, and they take a commission on sales through AWS Bedrock and Google Vertex. This is enshrined in contracts: Anthropic owes AWS $110 billion and Google Cloud $111 billion, totaling over $220 billion to its own shareholders. The IPO raises up to $100 billion from public buyers, but that money flows straight to suppliers under fixed 'take or pay' contracts, with $518 billion in commitments, 80% non-cancellable. Meanwhile, revenue is volatile: 83% is usage-based, two customers provide a quarter of revenue, and they can leave at any time. The corporate structure seals the trap: Founder LLC holds 50.1% voting control, a public benefit trust appoints the board, and a foundation can receive 5% of shares (worth $100 billion) plus more on valuation increases, diluting public shareholders. Public buyers get no control and bear all the risk.

## Counterpoints

- Revenue is skyrocketing, with 2026 pace reportedly exceeding $65 billion, making the valuation closer to 30 times revenue.
- The IPO is framed as a bet on future growth, not current performance.
- Some investors are hesitating, dropping expectations to $1.5 trillion.
