## Thesis

The AI industry's $10 million campaign against Alex Bores reveals a desperate attempt to intimidate lawmakers into abandoning even minimal safety regulations.

## Argument

The host argues that the AI industry is engaging in disproportionate intimidation tactics because it fears any regulatory precedent. The target, Alex Bores, sponsored New York's RAISE Act, which mandated basic safety tests and reporting for dangerous AI systems. Although the law was heavily lobbied and neutered—reducing fines to a negligible $1 million for companies with over $500M revenue—it still established a principle of accountability that industry leaders find unacceptable.

The core mechanism of this backlash is not about policy debate but punishment. Venture capitalists, Greg Brockman (OpenAI president), and Palantir co-founders formed a super PAC to spend millions on attack ads. These ads deliberately avoid discussing AI safety, knowing public sentiment favors regulation. Instead, they exploit Bores's past employment at Palantir, ironically funded by one of the PAC's main backers, to tar him by association.

This strategy highlights the industry's vulnerability: they cannot win the argument on AI risks because the public is skeptical of their own claims about extinction-level dangers while demanding safety. Thus, they resort to character assassination and financial bullying. The host notes that this intimidation has failed so far, with Anthropic funding Bores's side and the race remaining neck-and-neck. The outcome sets a critical precedent for whether AI firms can successfully bully lawmakers into total deregulation through sheer financial force.

## Counterpoints

- The RAISE Act was already gutted by lobbying, making the $10M spend an overreaction to a powerless law.
- Bores worked at Palantir, which has controversial ties to ICE, providing a valid basis for attack ads despite the irony of Palantir co-founder funding them.
