# The AI Industry is Spending $10 Million Against One Guy?

## Executive Summary

The video discusses the AI industry's $10 million campaign against congressional candidate Alex Bores, who sponsored New York's RAISE Act requiring AI safety tests. The law was heavily weakened by lobbying, and now industry figures are funding attack ads that avoid AI issues, instead targeting Bores's past at Palantir. The ads are ironic because a Palantir co-founder funds them, and they appear ineffective as the race is neck-and-neck. The outcome could set a precedent for whether AI companies can intimidate lawmakers.

## Key Points

- Alex Bores, a tech-savvy congressional candidate, is targeted by the AI industry for sponsoring the RAISE Act, which mandates AI safety tests and reporting. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gj9vxVK4GEE&t=0)
- The RAISE Act was a modest regulation, but lobbying gutted it, removing the ability to block dangerous AI releases and reducing fines to $1 million for large companies. [00:02:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gj9vxVK4GEE&t=154)
- A super PAC funded by tech leaders, including a Palantir co-founder, is spending over $10 million on attack ads that avoid AI and instead criticize Bores's past Palantir employment, which he left over ICE concerns. [00:04:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gj9vxVK4GEE&t=273)
- Despite the spending, the race is neck-and-neck, and the outcome may determine if AI companies can intimidate lawmakers. [00:06:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gj9vxVK4GEE&t=373)
