# NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman on Reaching the Moon by 2028, 15 Years to Mars & China’s Moon Race

## Executive Summary

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman outlines an aggressive strategy to return humans to the Moon by 2028 and establish a permanent base, using the lunar south pole as a proving ground for future Mars missions. He emphasizes the critical role of AI in processing vast amounts of scientific data and the integration of humanoid robots to build infrastructure before human arrival. Isaacman also discusses the geopolitical implications of the space race with China, the necessity of nuclear propulsion for deep space travel, and his stance on the transparency of Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) data.

## Key Points

- NASA aims to land American astronauts on the lunar south pole in 2028, followed by a permanent base, with humanoid robots expected to arrive within 4-6 years to handle infrastructure construction [00:28:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nV_lyWrkBs8&t=1706).
- Isaacman advocates for a 'whole of government' AI strategy via the Genesis program to leverage NASA's unique data archives and accelerate spacecraft design, while prioritizing nuclear propulsion to overcome the tyranny of distance [00:09:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nV_lyWrkBs8&t=591).
- Regarding the competition with China, Isaacman views the rivalry as healthy and likely to accelerate progress, noting that China's focused, military-integrated approach gives them a second-mover advantage, though he predicts they will hit their 2030 moon goal [00:40:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nV_lyWrkBs8&t=2424).
- On the topic of UAPs, Isaacman confirms that the White House is pushing for full transparency and disclosure of unexplained phenomena data, but he personally has no knowledge of any secret programs involving biological organisms or crashed spacecraft [01:16:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nV_lyWrkBs8&t=4616).
