# We Gave Away $3,500,000.00 for Someone to Build the Next Star Trek 

## Executive Summary

Peter Diamandis announces the results of the Future Vision X Prize, a global competition aimed at creating optimistic narratives about humanity's future with technology, inspired by Star Trek. He argues that just as AI models are trained on data, human minds are shaped by the media we consume, necessitating a shift from dystopian to hopeful storytelling. The event features judges Rod Roddenberry, Mira Lane, Neil deGrasse Tyson, and Neal Stephenson discussing the intersection of art and science before awarding $3.5 million to creators of positive future visions.

## Key Points

- Diamandis launched the Future Vision X Prize to counter dystopian media tropes by incentivizing optimistic stories where technology and humanity collaborate for a better future, receiving over 5,000 applications.[00:01:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_8pHsq0YoNE&t=106)
- The speaker draws a parallel between training AI models and human cognition, arguing that we must 'educate' both with positive data rather than negative cultural garbage to ensure beneficial outcomes.[00:02:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_8pHsq0YoNE&t=168)
- Judges Rod Roddenberry, Mira Lane, Neil deGrasse Tyson, and Neal Stephenson discuss how art and science influence each other, emphasizing that good art provides the imaginative perspective necessary for scientific exploration.[00:13:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_8pHsq0YoNE&t=805)
- The competition awarded $3.5 million across five finalists, with 'The Gifted' taking first place and 'No One Dies on Mars' second, celebrating creators who envision a future worth living in.[00:22:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_8pHsq0YoNE&t=1368)
