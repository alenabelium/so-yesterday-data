# Recursive's $670M Bet on Self-Improving AI, Sonnet 5.5 Hits 70%, Elon Co-Leads Pentagon Push EP 299

## Executive Summary

In this episode, Peter Diamandis and his panel discuss the rapid acceleration of AI, featuring Richard Socher's Recursive and its $670M raise for recursive self-improvement. They debate timelines for ASI, the impact of AI on science and biology, and the release of new models like Sonnet 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon. The conversation also covers the Pentagon's Project Meridian, the ethical and regulatory challenges of AI, and the potential for AI to transform scientific discovery and healthcare.

## Key Points

- Richard Socher discusses Recursive's mission to achieve recursive self-improvement, noting that weak forms already exist and that AI's ability to code is a major shift, with full RSI expected to take off next year. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Blyb1D927pM&t=0)
- The panel analyzes Sonnet 5.5's jump from 10% to 70% on Terminal Bench 4.0, with Alex Weer Gross questioning its cost-performance value compared to Opus 5.5. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Blyb1D927pM&t=0)
- Richard Socher argues that AI will deliver a century of scientific breakthroughs in the next decade by automating the full scientific stack, from hypothesis to validation, and that biology is the biggest domain for AI impact. [00:05:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Blyb1D927pM&t=339)
