# Google I/O 2026 Releases and Cerebras’ $95B IPO w/ Andrew Feldman 

## Executive Summary

The episode provides a comprehensive recap of Google I/O 2026, highlighting Google's massive infrastructure expansion and strategic pivot to AI-native services, including the launch of the Gemini Omni multimodal model and the agent-first Anti-Gravity 2.0 coding environment. The discussion analyzes the competitive landscape, noting Google's strong consumer adoption metrics while critiquing its products as often lagging behind OpenAI and Anthropic in raw frontier capabilities. The second half features an interview with Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman, who details the company's record-breaking $95B IPO and its unique wafer-scale silicon technology designed to accelerate AI inference.

## Key Points

- Google I/O 2026 showcased a 6x increase in capital expenditure to $190 billion and the launch of Gemini Omni, a multimodal model capable of generating realistic video and simulations, marking a strategic bet on modality scaling that distinguishes it from other US labs.
- The introduction of Anti-Gravity 2.0 signals a shift toward agent-first coding where humans describe intent rather than writing code, though analysts view it as a fast-follow to Cursor, while Gemini Spark offers a deeply integrated but less innovative alternative to OpenClaw.
- Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman discusses the company's $95B IPO and wafer-scale engine technology, explaining how their large SRAM-based chips offer superior inference speed over GPUs, while also addressing the long-term challenges of reshoring semiconductor manufacturing to the US.
- Andrej Karpathy’s move to Anthropic is analyzed as a critical signal that staying within frontier labs is essential for maintaining relevant AI judgment, while Google's new Universal Cart and AI-powered search demonstrate its attempt to monetize user intent and disrupt e-commerce through persistent agents.
