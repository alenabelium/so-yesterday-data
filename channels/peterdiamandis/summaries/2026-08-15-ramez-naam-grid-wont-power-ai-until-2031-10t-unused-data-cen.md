# Ramez Naam: Grid Won't Power AI Until 2031, $10T Unused, Data Centers in Southern Ocean 

## Executive Summary

The primary bottleneck for AI growth is not the cost of energy or power generation, but the severe delays in grid infrastructure and interconnection queues, which can now take up to a decade. To bypass these regulatory and physical constraints, the industry is rapidly adopting behind-the-meter solutions, including modular natural gas turbines, solar-plus-battery systems, and grid flexibility software that timeshifts power demand. Looking further ahead, the conversation explores radical long-term strategies such as space-based data centers powered by solar arrays and ocean-based data centers utilizing wave energy and natural cooling to achieve terawatt-scale compute.

## Key Points

- Grid interconnection queues have expanded from 15 months to nearly 45 months for generation and even longer for data center load, making the 'poles and wires' the critical bottleneck rather than power generation costs [00:08:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LUFJE5QkINg&t=504).
- Hyperscalers are pivoting to behind-the-meter power solutions, such as modular natural gas turbines and solar-plus-battery plants, to avoid grid delays, as power costs remain a small fraction of total data center capital expenditure [00:23:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LUFJE5QkINg&t=1415).
- Regulatory changes in Texas and federal guidance are creating fast-track paths for 'interruptible loads' that use software or batteries to shift power demand, unlocking approximately 100 gigawatts of grid capacity without new transmission lines [00:30:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LUFJE5QkINg&t=1805).
- Fusion energy is transitioning from a theoretical future to a near-term reality, with companies like Commonwealth Fusion and Helion targeting commercial deployment in the early 2030s due to favorable regulatory treatments compared to fission [01:28:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LUFJE5QkINg&t=5311).
- Long-term AI infrastructure strategies include space-based data centers requiring massive Starship launch cadences and ocean-based data centers that leverage cold seawater for cooling and wave energy for power [01:40:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LUFJE5QkINg&t=6053).
