## Key arguments

### Grid Interconnection is the Primary AI Bottleneck
- **Timestamp**: [08:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LUFJE5QkINg&t=504)
- **Summary**: The constraint is not power generation cost but the interconnection queue for poles and wires, which has stretched to 45 months. Hyperscalers are forced to use behind-the-meter solutions like modular natural gas turbines and solar-plus-battery systems to bypass these regulatory delays.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0]

### Grid Flexibility and Timeshifting Unlock Capacity
- **Timestamp**: [32:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LUFJE5QkINg&t=1932)
- **Summary**: New regulations in Texas allow 'interruptible loads' to connect faster if they can shift demand away from peak hours. Using batteries to charge at night and discharge during the day can unlock ~100GW of grid capacity, equivalent to $5 trillion in data center capex, without building new transmission lines.

### Small Modular Reactors and Fusion are Scaling
- **Timestamp**: [01:12:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LUFJE5QkINg&t=4370)
- **Summary**: SMRs aim to use factory manufacturing to reduce costs, with commercial deployment expected in the early 2030s. Fusion is also advancing, with Helion targeting 2028 for power delivery, regulated more lightly than fission because it is inherently fail-safe against meltdowns.

### Space and Ocean Data Centers as Long-Term Hedges
- **Timestamp**: [01:41:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LUFJE5QkINg&t=6113)
- **Summary**: To bypass terrestrial grid limits, compute may move to space (requiring massive Starship cadence) or oceans (using wave energy and natural cooling). These are viable long-term strategies for terawatt-scale AI, though space launch rates face significant regulatory and physical hurdles.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]
