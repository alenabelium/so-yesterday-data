## Key arguments

### Full-body neural net autonomy is the only path forward
- **Timestamp**: [04:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S_fXhVT67Uw&t=294)
- **Summary**: Adcock argues that coding humanoid behavior is a dead end; the entire stack must be neural nets. Helix 2 removes the last 109,000 lines of C++ code, enabling full-body closed-loop control that can generalize to unseen tasks and environments.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0]

### Data is the ultimate barrier to entry
- **Timestamp**: [06:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S_fXhVT67Uw&t=395)
- **Summary**: Adcock emphasizes that accumulated data from real-world operation creates an insurmountable moat. Unlike human knowledge that dies with the individual, robot learning is cumulative—once one robot learns a task, the entire fleet knows it instantly.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### The $50 trillion humanoid market will consolidate to a few players
- **Timestamp**: [17:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S_fXhVT67Uw&t=1026)
- **Summary**: Adcock predicts the humanoid industry will consolidate to far fewer than 10 global players, similar to the early auto industry. Success requires solving general robotics—full end-to-end neural nets in unseen places—not just manufacturing or teleoperation.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [2]

### Robots will build robots this year
- **Timestamp**: [30:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S_fXhVT67Uw&t=1808)
- **Summary**: Adcock announces Figure will put robots on its own production lines in 2026, phasing humans out of manufacturing. This self-improvement loop is essential to scale to millions of robots per year and eventually tens of billions on the planet.

### General-purpose robots will enter homes by 2027-2028
- **Timestamp**: [01:22:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S_fXhVT67Uw&t=4920)
- **Summary**: Adcock gives a best guess of 2027-2028 for the first Figure in a customer's home, emphasizing an iterative approach: first unseen homes with long-horizon work, then scaling from 1 to 10 to 100 to 10,000 homes. He warns against shipping 'slop' prematurely.
