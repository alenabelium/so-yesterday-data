## Thesis

AI coding tools are a dangerous illusion that degrade human skills and organizational knowledge, and true software engineering requires deep human intuition and interaction that LLMs cannot replicate.

## Argument

The core problem is that AI coding tools like Claude Code are fundamentally different from software engineering. Coding is a style-transfer task—interpolating between training data—while software engineering requires deep intuition, mental models, and interactive problem-solving. This is why LLMs fail at novel design: they produce surface-level imitations that break down outside their training distribution. The illusion of control, like a slot machine, leads to over-reliance and skill atrophy. The solution is not to abandon AI but to embed it in rich, interactive environments like notebooks, where humans and AI can collaborate with real-time feedback, preserving and enhancing human understanding.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue LLMs are creative, but they only perform combinatorial creativity within training data, not true extrapolation.
- Dario Amodei and Elon Musk claim AI will automate all coding, but they misunderstand that software engineering is not just writing code.
- Optimistic functionalism suggests we don't need to understand AI-generated code if it works, but this leads to fatal loss of control and unmaintainable systems.
- AI coding can make individuals 50x more productive, but this doesn't transfer to organizations due to knowledge bottlenecks and lack of shared understanding.
- Some say AI tools can be used for learning, but they often lead to autopilot mode and reduced competence, as shown by Anthropic's own research.
