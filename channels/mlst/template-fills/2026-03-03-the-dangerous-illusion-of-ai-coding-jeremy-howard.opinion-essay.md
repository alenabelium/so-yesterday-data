## Thesis

AI coding tools are a dangerous illusion that degrade human skills and organizational knowledge, and true software engineering requires deep intuition that LLMs cannot replicate.

## Argument

The core problem is that LLMs excel at style transfer, not genuine software engineering. They interpolate between training data, producing code that looks correct but lacks the deep understanding needed for novel problems. This is why the 'clean room' C compiler Claude created was actually a copy of LLVM code, not a creative act. The real bottleneck is not code generation but the human ability to design, understand, and integrate components. As Jeremy Howard notes, 'they really bad at engineering software' and this 'will always be true' because they cannot go beyond their training distribution. The solution is not to abandon AI but to place it in rich, interactive environments like notebooks, where humans and AI can collaborate, maintain understanding, and build on each other's strengths. This is the antithesis of the 'vibe coding' approach, which is like a slot machine—an illusion of control that leads to a loss of competence and organizational knowledge.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that LLMs are creative and can solve novel problems, but this is combinatorial creativity within training data, not true extrapolation.
- Anthropic's Dario Amodei claims AI will lead to mass unemployment, but this ignores that software engineering is more than coding and that productivity gains are often illusory.
- The functionalist view suggests that if AI produces working code, we don't need to understand it, but this leads to a fatal loss of control and unmaintainable systems.
- Some believe that AI can automate routine tasks, freeing experts for higher-level work, but this can also lead to skill degradation if not used carefully.
