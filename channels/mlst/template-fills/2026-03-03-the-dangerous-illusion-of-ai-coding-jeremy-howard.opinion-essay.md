## Thesis

AI coding tools like Claude Code create an illusion of control and productivity, but they degrade human skills and organizational knowledge, making them a dangerous gamble for software engineering.

## Argument

The core problem is that AI coding tools are fundamentally different from software engineering. Coding is a style-transfer task—interpolating between training data—while software engineering requires deep intuition, interaction, and understanding. As Jeremy Howard notes, "they really bad at engineering software" and this "will always be true." The evidence is clear: despite claims of 50x productivity, research shows only "tiny growth" in actual software releases. The reason is that AI models lack the ability to go beyond their training distribution; they fail when asked to design something truly novel, producing "a design that surface looks a little similar" but often "a complete failure." This is because they are essentially finding a "nonlinear average point in their training data," not creating new knowledge. The danger is that organizations, seduced by the hype, delegate cognitive tasks to these models, leading to a "gradual decrease of your competence" and a "blurring of knowledge inside organizations." This is akin to a slot machine: you have an illusion of control, but you're just pulling a lever and hoping for cherries. The solution is not to abandon AI but to use it in rich, interactive environments like notebooks, where humans and AI can collaborate, maintain understanding, and build on each other's strengths.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that AI coding tools make them more productive, but this is often an illusion of control, like a slot machine, where occasional wins mask overall losses.
- Proponents claim AI can create 'clean room' implementations, but this misunderstands how LLMs work—they interpolate from training data, not create from scratch.
- There is a belief that AI can handle software engineering, but it fails at tasks requiring novel design or going beyond its training distribution.
- The optimistic view of functionalism suggests that if AI produces working code, the internal process doesn't matter, but this ignores the loss of understanding and control.
- Some argue that AI coding tools can be used effectively with proper AI literacy, but the tools themselves are not designed to foster understanding, making it a tool problem, not a user problem.
