## Thesis

AI coding tools are a dangerous illusion that degrade human skills and organizational knowledge, and true software engineering requires deep understanding that LLMs cannot provide.

## Argument

The core problem is that AI coding tools like Claude Code are fundamentally different from software engineering. They generate code through statistical interpolation of training data, not through understanding. This is why they fail at novel tasks and why the productivity gains are often illusory. The real value of AI is in rich, interactive environments like notebooks, where humans and AI can collaborate with feedback loops, not in linear terminal interfaces that encourage a gambling-like 'vibe coding' approach. The future of software development depends on humans maintaining deep understanding and using AI as a tool to enhance, not replace, their skills.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that AI-generated code is fine as long as it works, but this ignores the loss of understanding and the inability to maintain or debug such code.
- Proponents claim AI makes developers 50x more productive, but this is often based on individual cases and ignores the bottleneck of knowledge transfer in organizations.
- There is a belief that AI can handle entire complex systems, but in practice, it fails on novel problems and produces code that no one understands, creating a 'fatal loss of control'.
