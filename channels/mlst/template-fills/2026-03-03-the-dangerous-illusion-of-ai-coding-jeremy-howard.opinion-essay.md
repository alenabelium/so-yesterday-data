## Thesis

AI coding tools like Claude Code create an illusion of control that degrades human skills and organizational knowledge, and they fundamentally cannot replace the deep understanding required for true software engineering.

## Argument

The core issue is that LLMs excel at style transfer and interpolation within their training data, not at genuine software engineering. When forced to design novel solutions, they fail because they merely find a nonlinear average of existing code, as seen when Claude's C compiler was just a translation of LLVM code. This is why productivity gains are illusory: while individuals like Jeremy Howard report being 50 times more productive, actual research shows tiny growth in shipped software. The real bottleneck is knowledge accumulation and sharing, which AI tools bypass, leading to a gradual erosion of competence. The solution is not to abandon AI but to create rich, interactive environments like notebooks where humans and AI collaborate, maintaining understanding and feedback loops. This is the antithesis of the linear, terminal-based Claude Code experience, which is 'inhumane' and fails to foster the deep intuition that great scientists and engineers develop through direct interaction with their work.

## Counterpoints

- Dario Amodei's essay 'Adolescence of Technology' claims AI will lead to mass unemployment because Anthropic's engineers are so productive, but this extrapolates from elite engineers to average ones without evidence.
- Elon Musk's claim that LLMs will directly output machine code, eliminating programming languages, misunderstands that software engineering is not just coding but a discipline requiring deep intuition and interaction.
- The optimistic view of functionalism suggests that if AI-generated code passes tests, it doesn't matter if no one understands it, but this ignores the fatal loss of control and the risk of hidden failures.
- Some argue that AI coding is a matter of AI literacy, and effective users can create feedback loops, but the tool itself should naturally foster understanding, not require special skills to compensate for its design flaws.
