## Thesis

AI coding tools like Claude Code create an illusion of control and productivity, but they degrade human skills and organizational knowledge, and they are fundamentally poor at software engineering, which requires deep intuition and.

## Argument

The core problem is that AI coding tools are evaluated on their ability to generate code, not on their ability to help humans understand and build software. This is a fundamental misalignment. As Jeremy Howard notes, 'coding is another one of those tasks on style transfer' — it's interpolation within training data, not true engineering. Software engineering, in contrast, is about 'deep intuition, building mental models through interaction with what they are studying.' This is why LLMs fail at novel problems: they 'really bad at engineering software' and 'will always be true' because they can't go beyond their training distribution. The result is a 'fatal loss of control' — you end up with code that 'nobody understands,' like the IPython kernel fix that worked but left Jeremy 'stressed because I didn't completely controlled situation.' This is not just an individual problem; it's an organizational one. When you delegate cognitive tasks to LLMs, you 'blur knowledge inside organizations' and lose the ability to develop it. The solution is not to abandon AI but to create rich, interactive environments like notebooks where humans and AI can collaborate, with feedback loops that foster understanding. This is the antithesis of the 'linear terminal interface' of Claude Code, which is 'really meager' and 'inhumane.'

## Counterpoints

- LLMs can be creative in a combinatorial sense, but they cannot extrapolate beyond their training distribution.
- AI coding can make individuals like Jeremy Howard 50x more productive, but this doesn't translate to organizations due to knowledge bottlenecks.
- Some argue that functionalism means we don't need to understand the code if it works, but this ignores the loss of control and the inability to maintain or extend it.
- The optimistic view that AI will automate routine tasks and free experts to focus on higher-level work is valid, but it requires careful design to avoid deskilling.
