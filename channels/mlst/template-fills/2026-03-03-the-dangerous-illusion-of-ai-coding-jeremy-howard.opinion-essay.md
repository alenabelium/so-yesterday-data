## Thesis

AI coding tools like Claude Code create an illusion of control and productivity, but they degrade human skills and organizational knowledge, and they will never replace the deep intuition required for real software engineering.

## Argument

The core problem is that AI coding tools are fundamentally different from software engineering. Coding is a style-transfer task—interpolating between existing code in training data—but engineering requires deep intuition, mental models, and interactive understanding of a system. As Jeremy Howard puts it, 'they really bad at engineering software' and this 'will always be true.' The evidence is everywhere: Anthropic's own research showed productivity barely grew, and the '50x' claims are hype. The real bottleneck is knowledge accumulation. When you delegate cognitive tasks to an LLM, you blur knowledge inside organizations, creating a 'debt in understanding.' This is like autopilot in a car: you disengage, stop learning, and lose competence. The solution is not to abandon AI but to put it in rich, interactive environments like notebooks, where humans and AI can collaborate in real time, build mental models, and maintain a feedback loop. Tools like Claude Code, with their linear terminal interfaces, are 'inhumane' and fail to foster this. The future of software development depends on finding the right-sized components, understanding them deeply, and using AI as a partner, not a slot machine.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue LLMs are creative and can solve novel problems, but they only perform combinatorial creativity within training data, not true extrapolation.
- Dario Amodei and Elon Musk claim AI will automate all coding, but they misunderstand the difference between coding and software engineering.
- Proponents say AI makes individuals 50x more productive, but this doesn't transfer to organizations due to knowledge bottlenecks.
- Functionalists argue that if AI produces working code, understanding doesn't matter, but this ignores the need for maintainability and the risk of 'code nobody understands.'
- Some believe AI can handle large, complex systems like the IPython kernel, but it required human intervention and left a codebase that no one fully understands.
