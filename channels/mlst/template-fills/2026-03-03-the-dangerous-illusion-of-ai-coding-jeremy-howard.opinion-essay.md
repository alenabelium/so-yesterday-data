## Thesis

AI coding tools create an illusion of control and productivity, but they degrade human skills and organizational knowledge, and true software engineering remains beyond their reach.

## Argument

The core problem is that AI coding tools like Claude Code are fundamentally different from software engineering. Coding is a style-transfer task—interpolating between training data—but engineering requires deep intuition and interaction with the system. LLMs fail when asked to design solutions that don't already exist in their training data, producing surface-similar but fundamentally broken results. This is why the '50x productivity' claims are misleading: they apply only to individuals with decades of experience who can guide the AI, not to organizations where knowledge is a bottleneck. The real danger is that over-reliance on these tools leads to a gradual degradation of skills, as people stop engaging with the code and lose the ability to understand or maintain it. This is like the autopilot problem in aviation: when you stop paying attention, you accumulate a debt of understanding. The solution is not to abandon AI but to create rich, interactive environments—like notebooks—where humans and AI can collaborate in real time, maintaining understanding and control. This is the philosophy behind nbdev and Solve, which put the human and AI inside a Python interpreter, allowing for a feedback loop that fosters learning and creativity. The traditional terminal-based interface of Claude Code is a step backward, isolating the human from the process and leading to the 'slot machine' experience of stochastic wins and losses.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that LLMs are creative and can produce novel solutions, but this is combinatorial creativity within the training distribution, not true extrapolation.
- Proponents claim AI coding tools make everyone more productive, but studies show productivity gains are minimal for most, and only experts with deep domain knowledge benefit.
- The functionalist view suggests that if AI-generated code works, it doesn't matter who wrote it, but this ignores the loss of understanding and the inability to maintain or extend the code.
- Some believe that AI will soon be able to handle all software engineering tasks, but LLMs are fundamentally bad at engineering, which requires intuition and interaction with the system.
