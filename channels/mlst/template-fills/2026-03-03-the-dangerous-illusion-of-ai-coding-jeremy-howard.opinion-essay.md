## Thesis

AI coding tools like Claude Code create an illusion of control that degrades human skills and organizational knowledge, and they will never replace the deep intuition required for true software engineering.

## Argument

The core problem is that AI coding tools are evaluated on their ability to generate code, but software engineering is a different discipline. As Jeremy Howard puts it, 'coding is another one of those tasks on style transfer' — it interpolates between training data. Real engineering requires understanding, interaction, and building mental models, which AI lacks. The evidence is everywhere: the 'ipython kernel' fix that left him with code no one understands, the 'clean room' C compiler that was actually interpolation, and the research showing only 'tiny growth' in actual software quality. The danger is that organizations and individuals outsource their cognitive development to these tools, leading to a 'debt in understanding' and a loss of the very skills needed to innovate. The solution is not to reject AI but to put it in rich, interactive environments like notebooks, where humans and AI can collaborate and maintain understanding.

## Counterpoints

- AI coding tools can make individuals like Jeremy Howard 50 times more productive in certain contexts.
- LLMs can exhibit combinatorial creativity, combining existing knowledge in novel ways.
- Some argue that if AI can perform the necessary actions, the internal understanding doesn't matter (functionalism).
- The 'vibe coding' productivity gains reported by some, like Dario, are extrapolated from elite engineers and don't hold for average developers.
