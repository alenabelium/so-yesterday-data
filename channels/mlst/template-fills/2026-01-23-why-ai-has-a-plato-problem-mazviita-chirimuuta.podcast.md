## Key arguments

### The Platonic Trap in AI Research
- **Timestamp**: [04:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yq318DIwPqw&t=243)
- **Summary**: Chirimuuta argues that many AI researchers hold a 'Platonic' view, believing the universe is written in code and reality is a messy kaleidoscope of underlying mathematical rules. She critiques this as an idealization that ignores the embodied, interactive nature of cognition, treating abstraction as a discovery of eternal truths rather than a tool for finite knowers.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### Haptic Realism vs. Spectator Knowledge
- **Timestamp**: [18:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yq318DIwPqw&t=1128)
- **Summary**: Proposing 'haptic realism,' she contends knowledge arises through active engagement and manipulation, akin to touch, rather than passive observation (the 'spectator theory'). This contrasts with the computational view that treats the brain as a disembodied computer, emphasizing that biological cognition is bound up with living tissue and metabolic constraints.

### The Fallacy of Computation's Causal Power
- **Timestamp**: [37:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yq318DIwPqw&t=2256)
- **Summary**: Chirimuuta challenges the computational theory of mind by asserting that computation is a mathematical formalism with no causal powers; only physical systems do. She argues it is a philosophical leap to assume brains are computers just because they can be modeled as such, ignoring the specific biological mechanisms and energy economies of neural tissue.

### Human Finitude and Digital Immersion
- **Timestamp**: [43:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yq318DIwPqw&t=2604)
- **Summary**: Drawing on Heidegger, she warns against the aspiration to transcend embodiment. She critiques the idea of disembodied knowledge absorbers (like LLMs) as ignoring human finitude. She expresses concern that digital immersion truncates mental life, particularly for children who need face-to-face social interaction for normal development.
