## Thesis

Intelligence is a collective economic phenomenon rooted in social interaction and incentive alignment, not an artificial property of isolated algorithms, making the pursuit of AGI a demoralizing distraction from building trustworthy.

## Argument

The current AI discourse is dominated by science fiction narratives that demonize young researchers with extinction fears while ignoring the economic realities of data extraction. Intelligence emerges from the aggregation of human opinions, cultures, and market mechanisms, not from mimicking brain neurons. Foundation models are merely statistical boxes; true value lies in designing systems where humans and algorithms interact within robust economic frameworks. We must integrate machine learning with game theory and statistics to create data markets that respect privacy and incentivize truthful information sharing. Without this collectivist perspective, AI remains a detached optimization problem that fails to address the uncertainty, asymmetry, and social context essential for real-world utility.

## Counterpoints

- AGI is just a PR term that distorts research priorities and confuses young people about viable career paths.
- Mechanistic interpretability searches for 'circuitry' in neural nets like hunting UFOs, ignoring that input-output behavior is sufficient for engineering.
- Silicon Valley claims behavioral economics is built-in via data, but this ignores the need for explicit incentive structures and equilibrium analysis.
- AlphaFold proves predictive power without understanding, but its confidence intervals are biased because it lacks ground truth on edge cases like quantum fluctuations.
