## Thesis

Intelligence is fundamentally a collective, economic phenomenon rooted in social interaction and market incentives, not an artificial property of isolated algorithms or superintelligence.

## Argument

The current AI narrative is demoralizing science fiction that ignores the real work of building trustworthy systems. We must stop anthropomorphizing models as "understanding" agents and instead view them as statistical components within a larger ecosystem. True intelligence emerges from the aggregation of human opinions, cultures, and economic incentives. Current builders are detached from reality, stealing data without returning value or considering uncertainty. We need to integrate machine learning with economics (game theory) and statistics (uncertainty quantification). For instance, AlphaFold’s predictions lack robustness on edge cases because they ignore statistical context; we need methods like prediction-powered inference to provide honest error bars. Similarly, data markets require mechanism design to align incentives between users, platforms, and buyers, ensuring privacy and value creation. The goal is not superintelligence but improving human decision-making through hybrid systems that respect human agency, uncertainty, and social complexity.

## Counterpoints

- AGI hype demoralizes young researchers by suggesting their work is dangerous or obsolete due to imminent superintelligence.
- Silicon Valley claims behavioral data is sufficient, ignoring the need for explicit economic equilibrium modeling.
- Mechanistic interpretability seeks internal circuits, but Jordan argues input-output predictability and context are more critical.
- Some argue AI will simply replace humans; Jordan insists it must aid human judgment in complex, uncertain environments.
