## Thesis

Intelligence is a collective, economic phenomenon rooted in social interaction and incentive alignment, not an artificial property of isolated algorithms or superintelligence.

## Argument

The current AI discourse is demoralizing young researchers by promoting science fiction narratives of AGI and extinction while ignoring the real-world utility of machine learning. Jordan argues that intelligence is fundamentally a social and economic process where humans aggregate opinions, navigate incentives, and manage uncertainty within a collective ecosystem. He critiques the 'dumb business model' of treating AI as a solitary oracle or replacing human judgment with opaque black boxes. Instead, he advocates for integrating machine learning with economics, statistics, and game theory to build systems that respect human value, quantify uncertainty through mechanisms like prediction-powered inference, and align incentives via data markets. The goal is not to create superintelligence but to enhance human decision-making in complex, adaptive systems where context, privacy, and mutual benefit are paramount.

## Counterpoints

- AGI hype demoralizes young researchers by suggesting their work is dangerous or obsolete.
- Treating AI as a solitary oracle ignores the economic and social context of its deployment.
- Silicon Valley's detachment from reality prioritizes scale over intellectual depth and human value.
