## Thesis

Intelligence is collective and economic, not artificial, and the AGI hype is science fiction that demoralizes young researchers and distracts from building trustworthy, human-centered AI ecosystems.

## Argument

The mechanism is economic. Jordan argues that intelligence arises from collective, social, and market dynamics, not from a single artificial agent. He points to the historical development of machine learning in statistics and operations research, which built systems for supply chains and commerce, and contrasts this with the recent AI buzzword that returned with language models. This buzzword, he claims, has a distortionary effect on research and business models, leading to the creation of the even more hyped term AGI. He emphasizes that the role of humans as producers and consumers in these emerging systems must be respected and amplified. Jordan advocates for a systems-level view that integrates machine learning with economics, statistics, and game theory to build trustworthy AI. He provides concrete examples like the three-layer data market, where platforms offer differential privacy levels, and the statistical contract theory, which connects incentives to evidence gathering. He stresses the need for uncertainty quantification, citing prediction-powered inference to correct biases in foundation models, and the importance of local data and incentives for truthful data sharing. He concludes that the future of AI lies in merging the economic equilibria perspective with the adaptive, data-driven perspective, moving beyond the simplistic 'superintelligence vs. extinction' narrative.

## Counterpoints

- Silicon Valley types claim that multi-agent LLM systems will give you all the economic stuff for free, but Jordan argues this is not a good way to think about engineering, comparing it to chemical engineering without thermodynamics.
- Some argue that building systems you don't understand is bad, but Jordan counters that you can build systems you don't understand as long as you put systems around them for explanation and control, like nearest neighbor systems for loan decisions.
- The belief that AGI is imminent and will be superintelligent or cause extinction is dismissed as science fiction that demoralizes young researchers and ignores the real economic and social opportunities.
- The idea that all behavioral and economic knowledge is already built into the data is considered too naive; Jordan argues that data alone misses the ephemeral, contextual nature of social knowledge and the need for incentive alignment.
- The notion that uncertainty can be handled by just adding more data is refuted by the AlphaFold example, where the model is highly biased on new questions at the edge of knowledge, requiring methods like prediction-powered inference.
