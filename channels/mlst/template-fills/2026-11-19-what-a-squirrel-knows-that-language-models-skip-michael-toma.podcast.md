## Key arguments

### Agency is about control, not intelligence
- **Timestamp**: [03:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=io7qhEjJoik&t=201)
- **Summary**: Tomasello argues that agency emerges when environments are unpredictable, requiring organisms to assess situations and make context-sensitive decisions. He contrasts this with stimulus-response models, emphasizing that even simple control systems like a thermostat capture the basic organization of agency, which is about control and flexible action, not raw intelligence.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0]

### Hierarchy of agency from reptiles to humans
- **Timestamp**: [33:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=io7qhEjJoik&t=1985)
- **Summary**: Tomasello outlines a four-tier hierarchy: basic control systems in reptiles, intentional agency in mammals with executive functions and planning, metacognitive agency in great apes, and uniquely human shared agency. Each tier adds new levels of flexibility and social coordination, culminating in culture and collective intentionality.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### LLMs start at the pinnacle, skipping foundational infrastructure
- **Timestamp**: [17:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=io7qhEjJoik&t=1043)
- **Summary**: Tomasello critiques LLMs for starting with language, the endpoint of human cognitive evolution, rather than building from foundational capacities like shared intentionality, perspective-taking, and executive control. He suggests that true agency requires this infrastructure, and that building AI from the bottom up may be necessary.

### Shared agency and perspective are uniquely human
- **Timestamp**: [37:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=io7qhEjJoik&t=2258)
- **Summary**: Humans form shared agents with joint goals, roles, and commitments, enabling joint attention and perspective-taking. This social infrastructure underlies language and culture, and is what makes human cognition distinct. Tomasello argues that LLMs lack this capacity to create conventions and engage in shared intentionality.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### The dark side of collective agency
- **Timestamp**: [56:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=io7qhEjJoik&t=3394)
- **Summary**: Tomasello discusses how collective agency can be co-opted for nefarious ends, such as political manipulation and group conflict. He notes that lying works because of our cooperative nature, and that our evolutionary history of in-group/out-group distinctions poses challenges in an interdependent modern world.
