# The Brain Is Just Specialized Agents Talking To Each Other — Dr. Jeff Beck

## Executive Summary

Dr. Jeff Beck discusses the nature of agency, arguing that agents are sophisticated objects with internal states and policies, and that identifying true agency requires understanding internal computations like planning and counterfactual reasoning, not just observing behavior. He explores energy-based models, joint embedding prediction architectures (JEPA), and the importance of continual learning and modular, specialized intelligences over a singular AGI. Beck also addresses the future of AI, expressing concerns about human cognitive offloading but maintaining a techno-optimistic view, and suggests safe AI development through empirical reward function estimation and incremental perturbations.

## Key Points

- Agency is defined by sophisticated internal computations like planning and counterfactual reasoning, which are hard to identify from external behavior alone; a pragmatic approach is to use the simplest model that explains behavior, such as Monte Carlo rollouts, to infer agency. [00:05:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ucqfb33GJJ4&t=320)
- Energy-based models differ from standard neural networks by applying cost functions to internal states, requiring dual optimization; they align with Bayesian inference and offer advantages like avoiding intractable normalization. [00:14:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ucqfb33GJJ4&t=848)
- True intelligence requires continual learning and the ability to generate new models on the fly, combining old modules in novel ways—like the brain's evolution—rather than a singular AGI; we should aim for collective specialized intelligences. [00:30:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ucqfb33GJJ4&t=1858)
