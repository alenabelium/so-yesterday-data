# What a Squirrel Knows That Language Models Skip (Michael Tomasello)

## Executive Summary

Michael Tomasello discusses his book 'The Evolution of Agency,' arguing that agency—the ability to make context-sensitive decisions in unpredictable environments—is a more fundamental lens than intelligence for understanding adaptive behavior across species. He outlines a hierarchy of agency from basic control systems in reptiles to executive and metacognitive tiers in mammals and great apes, culminating in uniquely human shared agency and culture. Tomasello critiques large language models for starting at the pinnacle of human achievement (language) rather than building from the foundational cognitive and social infrastructures, and he suggests that building AI from the bottom up, including shared intentionality and perspective-taking, may be necessary for true agency.

## Key Points

- Agency is needed when the environment is unpredictable; it's about making flexible, context-sensitive decisions, not just reacting to stimuli. [00:03:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=io7qhEjJoik&t=201)
- Tomasello outlines a hierarchy of agency: basic control systems (reptiles), executive/intentional agency (mammals), metacognitive/rational agency (great apes), and shared agency (humans). [00:32:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=io7qhEjJoik&t=1926)
- Large language models start at the endpoint of human achievement (language) but skip the foundational cognitive and social infrastructure, such as shared intentionality and perspective-taking, which may be necessary for true agency. [00:43:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=io7qhEjJoik&t=2600)
