# Intelligence is collective, not artificial — Prof. Michael I. Jordan (UC Berkeley / Inria)

## Executive Summary

Professor Michael I. Jordan argues that intelligence is fundamentally collective and economic, not artificial, and criticizes the hype around AGI as science fiction that demoralizes young researchers. He advocates for a systems-level view that integrates machine learning with economics, statistics, and game theory to build trustworthy, human-centered AI ecosystems. Jordan emphasizes the need for uncertainty quantification, incentive alignment, and data markets that respect human value, rather than pursuing superintelligence or extinction narratives.

## Key Points

- Jordan criticizes the AGI hype and alarmist narratives, arguing they demoralize young people and distract from real engineering and economic thinking. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AREWYbVtX64&t=0)
- He advocates for a collectivist economic perspective on AI, where systems are designed as ecosystems with incentives, data markets, and human participation, not just statistical boxes. [00:05:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AREWYbVtX64&t=342)
- Jordan explains the need for uncertainty quantification and prediction-powered inference, using AlphaFold as an example of how foundation models can be made more trustworthy by merging with ground truth data. [00:20:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AREWYbVtX64&t=1227)
