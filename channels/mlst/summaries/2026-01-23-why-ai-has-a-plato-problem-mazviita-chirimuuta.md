# Why AI Has a Plato Problem — Mazviita Chirimuuta

## Executive Summary

Mazviita Chirimuuta discusses her book 'The Brain Abstracted', arguing that AI research often relies on Platonic idealizations that oversimplify the complexity of biological cognition. She critiques the computational theory of mind, suggesting that treating the brain as a computer ignores the embodied, interactive nature of knowledge. Chirimuuta proposes 'haptic realism' as an alternative, emphasizing that knowledge arises through active engagement with the world, not passive observation. She also explores the implications of AI for human finitude, agency, and the ethics of digital immersion.

## Key Points

- Chirimuuta distinguishes between abstraction and idealization, noting that AI researchers often adopt a Platonic view that mathematical representations reveal deeper truths, whereas a more grounded view sees them as pragmatic simplifications due to cognitive limits. [00:04:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yq318DIwPqw&t=243)
- The reflex theory of the early 20th century serves as a cautionary tale: oversimplification can mislead science for decades, and the same risk applies to current computational models of the brain. [00:09:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yq318DIwPqw&t=583)
- Chirimuuta argues that computation itself lacks causal powers; it is a mathematical formalism, and claiming the brain is a computer requires unjustified leaps, as any physical system can be mapped to a computation. [00:37:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yq318DIwPqw&t=2256)
