# How a Voice Agent Learns the Rhythm of Conversation — Shawn Wen

## Executive Summary

Shawn Wen, co-founder of PolyAI, discusses the complexities of building enterprise-grade voice agents, arguing that while speech technology has advanced, real-time conversational AI remains challenging due to the need for natural turn-taking, adaptation, and control. He explains PolyAI's shift from cascaded systems to end-to-end audio-native models that directly perceive audio and output text, enabling better latency, robustness, and enterprise governance. The conversation covers data collection, benchmarking, harness engineering, and the future of voice AI, emphasizing the balance between human-like capability and robotic controllability in enterprise settings.

## Key Points

- Voice AI is harder than expected because it introduces the time dimension and physical-world complexity, requiring adaptation rather than just reasoning. [00:01:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VoAPg8Fj6-c&t=62)
- PolyAI builds custom end-to-end audio-native models to handle turn-taking and interruptions naturally, avoiding the brittleness of cascaded systems. [00:07:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VoAPg8Fj6-c&t=457)
- Latency optimization involves collapsing ASR and LM, pre-caching processing, and training models for latency-budgeted reasoning. [00:27:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VoAPg8Fj6-c&t=1629)
- Enterprises want branded, controllable voice agents, and generic voices perform worse because users associate them with past IVR systems. [00:33:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VoAPg8Fj6-c&t=2018)
- Benchmarking voice agents is difficult due to latency, quality, and task complexity; PolyAI uses real production data and plans to release a public benchmark. [00:36:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VoAPg8Fj6-c&t=2194)
