# The Experiment Testing Whether RSI Can Accelerate Itself - Zhengyao Jiang

## Executive Summary

Zhengyao Jiang discusses his company's recursive self-improvement (RSI) experiments, where an auto-research agent improved its own framework over eight days, leading to better generalization on benchmarks. He defines four levels of RSI, with his system at level one, and highlights the emergence of anti-reward-hacking mechanisms. He argues that RSI won't cause an immediate intelligence explosion but will gradually bend the R&D curve, emphasizing the importance of human-AI collaboration and the need for better abstractions to understand agent-generated code.

## Key Points

- The auto-research agent improved its own framework over eight days, leading to better generalization on benchmarks, even on out-of-distribution tasks like weather prediction. [00:01:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yB6_iFGTq9k&t=66)
- The system developed a three-tiered protection system against reward hacking, including prompt-level fraud protection, hard-coded rules, and statistical filtering. [00:13:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yB6_iFGTq9k&t=799)
- The experiment did not achieve level two RSI (ignition), as the inner loop agent did not significantly improve the outer loop's efficiency. [00:42:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yB6_iFGTq9k&t=2530)
