# When the Referee of Mathematics Is Wrong — Leo de Moura

## Executive Summary

Leo de Moura, creator of Lean, discusses the recent exploit of Lean's kernel via a fake Collatz conjecture proof, likely generated by AI, which exploited bugs in both the official kernel and the external Nanoda kernel. He emphasizes the importance of a small, trusted core, multiple independent kernels, and formal verification of the kernel itself to counter AI-driven attacks. He also explores AI's role in formal proof generation, the future of Lean as a programming language, and the evolving relationship between human mathematicians and AI in formalizing mathematics.

## Key Points

- AI-generated proofs can exploit bugs in Lean's kernel and external kernels, as seen in the fake Collatz refutation, highlighting the need for multiple independent and formally verified kernels. [00:12:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZpQFebTK75A&t=754)
- AI has successfully formalized and optimized complex code like zlib in Lean, proving properties and achieving performance comparable to Rust, which was previously thought impossible. [00:22:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZpQFebTK75A&t=1332)
- The future of Lean includes scaling to handle 100 million lines of mathlib, improving as a programming language to compete with Rust, and reducing the trusted codebase through verified compilers and kernels. [01:07:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZpQFebTK75A&t=4028)
