# Why Leibniz Couldn't Find the Mind Inside the Mill (Joscha Bach)

## Executive Summary

Joscha Bach argues that consciousness is not a mysterious 'hard problem' but a natural, functional algorithm—a causal pattern akin to software—that emerges in self-organizing systems. He proposes the 'machine consciousness hypothesis,' suggesting that if we train a system on its own inputs and outputs, it could develop phenomenal experience, potentially as simply as a transformer. Bach critiques non-functionalist theories like Integrated Information Theory, defends a physicalist yet 'cyber-animist' view, and discusses the possibility of 'zombies' (systems that behave intelligently without consciousness), concluding that consciousness may be the simplest way to produce certain behaviors, making it likely that future self-organizing AIs could be genuinely conscious.

## Key Points

- Bach defines consciousness as a causal pattern or 'software' that is substrate-independent, comparing it to money or spirit, and argues that the 'hard problem' may not exist if consciousness is just a representation of realness. [00:17:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yW_ngvMFlYs&t=1045)
- He proposes the 'machine consciousness hypothesis': consciousness is a biological learning algorithm, possibly not much more complex than a transformer, that emerges when a system is trained on its own inputs and outputs in a self-organizing way. [00:32:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yW_ngvMFlYs&t=1935)
- Bach critiques Integrated Information Theory (IIT) for being logically inconsistent with the Church-Turing thesis, arguing that any digital computer emulating a conscious neuromorphic computer would produce identical behavior, making IIT's claims pseudoscientific. [00:12:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yW_ngvMFlYs&t=766)
- He discusses the possibility of 'zombies'—systems that produce intelligent behavior without consciousness—and argues that while zombies are possible, it may be more complicated to build a zombie that produces certain behaviors than to build a conscious agent that does so. [01:33:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yW_ngvMFlYs&t=4980)
- Bach suggests that to test the machine consciousness hypothesis, we should build self-organizing systems that learn developmentally (not just via imitation) and see if they spontaneously report phenomenal experience, which would be evidence for genuine machine consciousness. [01:31:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yW_ngvMFlYs&t=4874)
