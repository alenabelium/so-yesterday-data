# When AI Discovers the Next Transformer — Robert Lange

## Executive Summary

Robert Lange discusses Sakana AI's Shinka Evolve, an LLM-driven evolutionary algorithm that improves sample efficiency in scientific discovery by co-evolving programs and models. He emphasizes the importance of open-endedness, problem co-evolution, and human oversight, arguing that AI will amplify human creativity rather than replace it. The conversation covers applications like agent design, AL Bench, and loss function optimization, and touches on the future of AI scientists and the potential for AI to discover major architectures.

## Key Points

- Shinka Evolve uses LLMs in an evolutionary loop with model assembly and UCB-based selection to efficiently discover programs, improving on AlphaEvolve's circle packing result with far fewer evaluations. [00:04:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EInEmGaMRLc&t=247)
- A key limitation is that current systems take the problem as fixed, whereas human innovation often involves inventing surrogate problems or reframing; co-evolving problems and solutions is a promising direction. [00:20:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EInEmGaMRLc&t=1226)
- Lange envisions a future where researchers shepherd AI systems that run experiments overnight, democratizing access to scientific discovery, but he remains confident that humans are the source of deep understanding and creativity. [00:36:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EInEmGaMRLc&t=2206)
