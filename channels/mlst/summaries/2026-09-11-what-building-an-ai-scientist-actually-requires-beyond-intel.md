# What Building an AI Scientist Actually Requires Beyond Intelligence — Edward Hughes

## Executive Summary

Edward Hughes discusses the transition from individual AI intelligence to collective intelligence, arguing that true AI scientists require more than raw capability—they need the ability to ask questions, respect and break constraints, and operate within a supportive 'field' of human and machine collaborators. He introduces Inherent's approach, using a small model (Faraday) to supervise frontier coding agents for paper replication, demonstrating that weight-based learning can outperform harness engineering. The conversation explores creativity as satisficing, the importance of evaluation in hindsight, and the vision of a 'recursive company' where agents and humans co-evolve to accelerate scientific discovery.

## Key Points

- Creativity requires recognizing one's own innovation, which is a metacognitive step beyond mere innovation, as illustrated by Move 37. [00:14:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P4bYjTJvD28&t=889)
- Inherent's Faraday agent, a small model trained with GRPO to supervise a frontier coding agent, outperforms frontier models on paper replication tasks, showing the value of weight-based learning over harness engineering. [01:13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P4bYjTJvD28&t=4380)
- The next era is collective intelligence, where many humans and agents interact in a 'recursive company' that self-improves, analogous to reconfiguring factories for electric dynamos. [01:50:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P4bYjTJvD28&t=6635)
