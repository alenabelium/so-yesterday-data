# What Most People Get Wrong About Evolution 

## Executive Summary

The conversation explores artificial life and its implications for understanding intelligence, arguing that evolution is a powerful, non-random algorithm that can be combined with foundation models to search the vast space of possible simulations. The speakers discuss how curriculum learning and path dependency create robust representations, contrasting statistical intelligence with regularity-based intelligence. They highlight the use of LLMs to evolve programs and simulations, demonstrating that evolution plus verifiers can outperform direct generation, and suggest that studying the space of all possible life forms is essential for understanding general principles of intelligence.

## Key Points

- Evolution is anything but random; it is a powerful algorithm for solving discrete combinatorial problems by building on partial solutions. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=615B5nyMFPk&t=0)
- Artificial life studies life as it could be, not just as it is, to understand the space of all possible intelligences and life forms. [00:04:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=615B5nyMFPk&t=259)
- Curriculum learning and path dependency are crucial for building robust, generalizable representations, as seen in natural evolution and human learning. [00:07:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=615B5nyMFPk&t=437)
- Using LLMs as mutation operators in evolutionary loops can beat 96% of human-written Core War warriors, even though LLMs are poor at zero-shot code generation. [00:44:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=615B5nyMFPk&t=2697)
- Foundation models can serve as critics to search the space of simulations, finding open-ended rules that produce emergent complexity, as demonstrated by the ASEL algorithm. [00:31:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=615B5nyMFPk&t=1885)
