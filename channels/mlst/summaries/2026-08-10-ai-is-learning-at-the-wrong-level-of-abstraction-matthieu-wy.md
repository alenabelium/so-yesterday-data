# AI Is Learning at the Wrong Level of Abstraction — Matthieu Wyart

## Executive Summary

Matthieu Wyart, a physicist at Johns Hopkins and EPFL, argues that current AI models learn at the wrong level of abstraction, predicting low-level tokens or pixels rather than higher-level latent concepts. He presents a physics-based framework showing that deep architectures can learn hierarchical abstractions from data, countering Chomsky's 'poverty of the stimulus' argument. Wyart advocates for introspective algorithms that predict in latent space, which are more sample-efficient and can overcome the curse of dimensionality. He also discusses scaling laws and the potential for more efficient generative models.

## Key Points

- Deep architectures have an implicit bias toward building hierarchical abstractions, enabling creativity from limited data, countering Chomsky's poverty of the stimulus argument. [00:30:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=revreN8LZ_M&t=1859)
- Predicting in latent space (introspective learning) is more sample-efficient than token-level prediction, as it reduces noise and accelerates abstraction learning. [00:57:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=revreN8LZ_M&t=3472)
- The curse of dimensionality is overcome by hierarchical data structure; deep networks detect generalized variables, requiring only polynomial data. [00:51:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=revreN8LZ_M&t=3118)
