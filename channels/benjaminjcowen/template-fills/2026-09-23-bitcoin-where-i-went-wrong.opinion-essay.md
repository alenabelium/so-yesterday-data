## Thesis

My assumption that a Fed rate hike would immediately reprice Bitcoin lower was not rooted in historical data, and I was wrong to be so deterministic about it.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a macro chain that I built piece by piece: rising energy prices would lift yields, force the Fed to hike in September, strengthen the dollar, and thereby push Bitcoin down. Every link in that chain played out — energy, yields, the dollar — yet Bitcoin rallied instead. The missing piece was the final assumption: that a rate hike is immediately bearish for Bitcoin. That assumption was never supported by the data. Looking back at the two prior rate-hiking cycles since Bitcoin's inception, the first hike in December 2015 saw Bitcoin continue higher because the bottom was already in, and the first hike in March 2022 saw Bitcoin initially go up. In September of the last two midterm years, Bitcoin also did fine for a month or two after the hike. So the historical record never justified my conclusion that a hike would force an immediate repricing. The lesson is to defer to market structure over deterministic narratives. Bitcoin has now printed a higher high, and the burden of proof has shifted to the bears. If weakness does show up later in Q4, I have to be open-minded that it could resolve as a macro higher low rather than a lower low, especially since the final weakness in prior midterm years has typically come after the midterms themselves. For now, the prudent move is to listen to those who called this move correctly rather than to someone who was wrong.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that a rate hike should still be bearish because it strengthens the dollar, but the data shows Bitcoin has not consistently dropped after hikes.
- Another objection is that this isn't truly the first rate hike of a cycle, but even if you compare to September hikes in midterm years, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two.
- Some might say the stock market correction is inevitable, but 2010 shows a midterm year where the market rallied into year-end without a correction.
- A skeptic could point to the FTX collapse in 2022 as a reason for caution, but that was an idiosyncratic event, not a rate-hike response.
