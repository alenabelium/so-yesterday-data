## Thesis

Rate hikes are not immediately bearish for Bitcoin, and my assumption that they were was the core error behind my wrong September 2026 prediction.

## Argument

The macro chain I built was sound: energy up, yields up, Fed forced to hike, dollar up. But the final link — that a rate hike would immediately reprice Bitcoin lower — was never supported by historical data. Looking back at the two prior rate-hiking cycles since Bitcoin's inception, the first hike in December 2015 saw the bottom already in, and in March 2022 Bitcoin actually went up initially. Even in midterm-year Septembers (2018, 2022), Bitcoin did not drop right after the hike. My error was being deterministic about a narrative instead of deferring to market structure. The market has now printed a higher high, and the burden of proof has shifted to the bears. If weakness comes in Q4, I must be open-minded to a higher low, especially since the final midterm-year weakness historically occurs after the midterms. A stock market correction would likely be needed to push Bitcoin lower, but a 10% S&P drop probably wouldn't suffice. I'm deferring to those who called this move correctly and will respect the higher high unless it's fully retraced.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that this rate hike is not the first in a cycle, but a continuation, making historical comparisons less relevant.
- Some might say that Bitcoin's correlation to traditional markets, via ETFs, has changed its reaction to macro events.
- A skeptic could point out that past performance doesn't guarantee future results, and this time could be different.
- Others might argue that the stock market correction is inevitable, and Bitcoin will eventually follow it lower.
