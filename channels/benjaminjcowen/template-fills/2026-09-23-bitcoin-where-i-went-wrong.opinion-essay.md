## Thesis

Bitcoin's post-rate-hike rally invalidates the assumption that rate hikes are immediately bearish, and the market's higher high must now be respected over deterministic macro narratives.

## Argument

The mechanism is historical precedent. I assumed a rate hike would force Bitcoin to reprice lower, but the data shows that after the first rate hike of a cycle, Bitcoin either rose or had already bottomed. In December 2015, the low was in; in March 2022, Bitcoin went up initially. Even in September of midterm years, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. My macro chain—energy up, yields up, Fed hikes, dollar up—played out exactly as I predicted, yet Bitcoin rallied with the dollar instead of dropping. That divergence forced me to reassess. I now defer to market structure: Bitcoin set a higher high, something it didn't do in 2018 or 2022. The burden of proof has shifted to the bears. If weakness comes in Q4, it likely requires a stock market correction, and even a 10% S&P drop may not be enough to take Bitcoin to a new low. I'm open to a macro higher low, especially after the midterms, but I won't assume it. My mistake was being too deterministic, ignoring the historical data on rate hikes, and not respecting the golden cross and the higher high. I've been wrong before—in 2023, I thought $35K was the ceiling—and I learned then to admit it and move on. Now, I'm telling viewers to listen to those who called this move correctly, not me.

## Counterpoints

- Some might argue that the rate hike is not the first in a cycle, but the technical definition counts it as such, and history shows Bitcoin often rises after the first hike.
- Others might point to the stock market's late-September tops in 2014 and 2018, but 2010 shows that a correction isn't guaranteed in every midterm year.
- A common objection is that Bitcoin's rally is unsustainable and must retrace, but I lack the authority to claim that since I didn't predict the breakout.
- Some might say the dollar's strength should still be bearish, but the divergence this time—Bitcoin rising with the dollar—suggests a structural change, possibly due to ETF-driven correlation with traditional markets.
