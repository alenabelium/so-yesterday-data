## Thesis

I was wrong to assume that a Fed rate hike would be immediately bearish for Bitcoin, because that assumption is not rooted in historical data.

## Argument

My macro chain played out exactly as I predicted: energy went up, yields rose to 5%, the Fed hiked in September, and the dollar strengthened. But Bitcoin rallied instead of repricing lower. The flaw was my final link — assuming a rate hike is immediately bearish. Historical data shows otherwise. In December 2015, the first hike of a cycle, Bitcoin's bottom was already in. In March 2022, Bitcoin initially went up. In September 2018 and September 2022, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. So I should have been more rigorous before concluding a rate hike would cause a reprice.

Now I have to respect the market structure: Bitcoin formed its February low, its summer low, and then rallied 50% — a bigger move than in 2018 or 2022 — and set a higher high. That's a clear signal that something changed. The dollar went up, and Bitcoin went up with it, possibly because ETFs tie Bitcoin more to traditional markets, which often stay bullish into late September in midterm years.

I'm deferring to those who called this move correctly. I don't have the credibility to predict a retrace since I didn't predict the breakout. If weakness comes in Q4, I won't assume it's a lower low — it could be a macro higher low, especially if it corresponds to a stock market correction. The burden of proof has shifted to the bears.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that the rate hike is just a continuation of the previous cycle, not a new first hike, but the technical definition counts it because rates didn't return to zero.
- Some might expect a stock market correction in late September, as in 2018 and 2014, but 2010 shows it doesn't always happen.
- A 10% S&P drop might not be enough to push Bitcoin to a new low; a much larger drop would be needed.
- The dollar's double bottom pattern previously marked Bitcoin's local top, but this time Bitcoin diverged and rallied instead.
