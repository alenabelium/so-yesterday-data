## Thesis

Rate hikes are not immediately bearish for Bitcoin, and my assumption that they were was the core error behind my wrong September 2026 prediction.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a chain of macro assumptions: energy up → yields up → Fed hikes → dollar up → Bitcoin down. I got the first four right, but Bitcoin rallied anyway. The data shows that in past rate-hiking cycles (December 2015, March 2022), Bitcoin did not drop immediately after the first hike — in one case the bottom was already in, in the other it went up. Similarly, in September of 2018 and 2022, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. So my assumption that a hike would cause an immediate repricing was not rooted in historical evidence. Now that Bitcoin has printed a higher high, the burden of proof has shifted to the bears. I defer to those who called the move correctly, and I remain open-minded to a higher low in Q4, contingent on a stock market correction. If the S&P only drops 10%, that likely won't be enough to take Bitcoin to a new low; a much larger drop would be needed. The lesson is to respect market structure over deterministic macro narratives.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that this rate hike is different because it's not the first in a cycle, but the technical definition still counts it as the first since rates didn't return to zero.
- Some might point to the FTX collapse in 2022 as a reason Bitcoin dropped after a hike, but that was a unique event, not a general pattern.
- A skeptic could say that Bitcoin's rally is just a temporary divergence and that the macro chain will eventually catch up, but the historical data shows no immediate bearish reaction to hikes.
- Others might argue that the stock market correction is inevitable in midterm years, but 2010 shows that corrections don't always happen.
