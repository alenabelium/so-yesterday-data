## Thesis

Bitcoin's post-rate-hike rally proves that assuming rate hikes are immediately bearish for Bitcoin was my core error, and I now defer to market structure over deterministic macro narratives.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a macro chain that I got right but that led to the wrong conclusion. I correctly predicted energy prices would rise, yields would follow, the Fed would hike in September, and the dollar would strengthen. But I then assumed Bitcoin would reprice lower. That assumption was not rooted in historical data. Looking at the two prior rate-hiking cycles since Bitcoin's inception, the first hike in December 2015 saw the bottom already in, and in March 2022 Bitcoin actually went up initially. In September of the last two midterm years, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. So the data never supported an immediate bearish reaction.

I also ignored the market structure. Bitcoin formed its February low, then a summer low, and then rallied 50% off that low—bigger than in 2018 or 2022—and set a higher high. That higher high is the key signal. I cannot unwind that in my mind. The burden of proof has shifted to the bears. If weakness comes in Q4, it would likely require a significant stock market correction, not just a 10% drop, and even then I must be open to a higher low rather than assuming a lower low.

My advice: in the short term, defer to those who called this move correctly. I was wrong, and I don't have the credibility to predict the next leg. I'll respect the higher high and stay open-minded about a potential macro higher low after the midterms.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue Bitcoin should have dropped because the dollar went up, but the data shows Bitcoin can rally alongside a stronger dollar.
- Some might say this is just a temporary bounce before a drop, but I have to respect the higher high until it's retraced.
- A skeptic could point to past midterm-year corrections, but 2010 shows the stock market doesn't always top in late September.
