## Thesis

My assumption that a Fed rate hike would immediately be bearish for Bitcoin was not rooted in historical data, and I was wrong to be so deterministic about it.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a macro chain that played out perfectly: energy prices rose, yields rose, the Fed hiked in September, and the dollar strengthened. But Bitcoin rallied anyway, setting a higher high. The missing piece was historical precedent. Looking at the first rate hike of past cycles, December 2015 and March 2022, Bitcoin did not drop immediately — in one case the bottom was already in, in the other it went up. September rate hikes in midterm years (2018, 2022) also didn't trigger immediate drops. So my conclusion that a hike forces a repricing was an assumption, not a data-driven inference. The market structure now shows a higher high, and the burden of proof has shifted to the bears. I defer to those who called this move correctly, and I remain open-minded to a higher low in Q4, contingent on a stock market correction. If the S&P only drops 10%, that likely won't be enough to take Bitcoin to a new low.

## Counterpoints

- The macro chain (energy, yields, dollar) all played out as predicted, so why didn't Bitcoin follow?
- Bitcoin could still drop later, as it did after the 2015 and 2022 rate hikes, but the low may already be in.
- The stock market might not correct in Q4, in which case Bitcoin may not see significant downside.
- My past mistakes (2023, 2022) show I can be wrong, but that doesn't invalidate my future analysis.
