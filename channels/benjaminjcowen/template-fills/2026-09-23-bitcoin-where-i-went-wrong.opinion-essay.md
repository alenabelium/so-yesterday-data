## Thesis

My assumption that a Fed rate hike would immediately be bearish for Bitcoin was not rooted in historical data, and I was wrong to be so deterministic about it.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a macro chain: energy up → yields up → Fed hikes → dollar up → Bitcoin down. Every link played out, yet Bitcoin rallied. The flaw was the final link. Historical data shows that after the first rate hike of a cycle (Dec 2015, Mar 2022) and after September rate hikes in midterm years (2018, 2022), Bitcoin did not drop immediately; in one case the bottom was already in, in another it went up. I over-indexed on a narrative and ignored the market structure. Now Bitcoin has printed a higher high, and the burden of proof has shifted to the bears. I defer to those who called this move correctly, and I remain open-minded to a macro higher low in Q4, contingent on a stock market correction.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin could still drop later, but historically weakness after rate hikes comes a month or two later, not immediately.
- The stock market doesn't always correct in midterm years (2010 is a counterexample), so Bitcoin weakness isn't guaranteed.
- One might argue this isn't the first rate hike of a cycle, but by technical definition it is, and September hikes in midterm years have not been immediately bearish.
