## Thesis

Rate hikes are not immediately bearish for Bitcoin, and my assumption that they were is where I went wrong.

## Argument

The mechanism is historical data. I built a macro chain: energy up → yields up → Fed hikes → dollar up → Bitcoin down. Energy, yields, and the dollar all played out, but Bitcoin rallied instead. The missing link was the assumption that a rate hike is immediately bearish for Bitcoin. Looking back at the two prior rate-hiking cycles since Bitcoin's inception, the first hike in December 2015 saw the bottom already in, and in March 2022 Bitcoin actually went up initially. In September of the last two midterm years, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. So the data never supported my thesis. I should have deferred to market structure: Bitcoin formed its February low, then a summer low, and has now rallied 50% off that low, setting a higher high. That's a structural shift. The burden of proof has shifted to the bears. If weakness comes in Q4, I won't assume a lower low; it could be a macro higher low, especially if it aligns with a stock market correction. But without a significant S&P drop, I don't see Bitcoin making a new low. I'm deferring to those who called this move correctly.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that a rate hike should still be bearish because it strengthens the dollar, which historically pressures Bitcoin.
- Another objection is that this rate hike might not be the first in a cycle, so comparing it to 2015 and 2022 is invalid.
- A third counterpoint is that the stock market could still correct, dragging Bitcoin down, even if it hasn't yet.
