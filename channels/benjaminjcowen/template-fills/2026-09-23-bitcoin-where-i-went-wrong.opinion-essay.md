## Thesis

Rate hikes are not immediately bearish for Bitcoin, and my September 2026 prediction was wrong.

## Argument

My macro chain was right: energy went up, yields went up, the Fed hiked, and the dollar strengthened. But Bitcoin rallied anyway. The flaw was assuming a rate hike would immediately reprice Bitcoin lower. Historical data shows that after the first rate hike of a cycle, Bitcoin either went up or the bottom was already in. In September of prior midterm years, Bitcoin also did fine for a month or two after the hike. The market structure now shows a higher high, and I have to respect that. The burden of proof has shifted to the bears. If weakness comes in Q4, it likely requires a stock market correction, and even then, a 10% drop in the S&P wouldn't be enough to take Bitcoin to a new low. I defer to those who called this move correctly.

## Counterpoints

- Bitcoin could still drop later, but history shows weakness comes a month or two after the hike, not immediately.
- The dollar's strength might still pressure Bitcoin, but this time Bitcoin diverged and followed the dollar up.
- A stock market correction could drag Bitcoin down, but a 10% drop wouldn't be enough to make a new low.
