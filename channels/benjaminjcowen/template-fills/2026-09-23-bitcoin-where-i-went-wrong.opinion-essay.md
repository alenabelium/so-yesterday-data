## Thesis

My assumption that a Fed rate hike would immediately be bearish for Bitcoin was not rooted in historical data, and I was wrong to predict a drop after the September 2026 hike.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a macro chain: I expected energy prices to rise, pushing yields up, forcing the Fed to hike, and strengthening the dollar—all of which I got right. But I then assumed Bitcoin would reprice lower, and it didn't. The data shows that after the first rate hike of a cycle, Bitcoin either continued higher (March 2022) or the bottom was already in (December 2015). In September of midterm years, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. So the rate hike itself was never the bearish catalyst I made it out to be.

I also ignored the market structure: Bitcoin formed its February low, then a summer low, and rallied 50%—more than the 40% typical of prior midterm years—and set a higher high, unlike the lower highs of 2018 and 2022. The dollar went up, and Bitcoin went up with it, a divergence from the past. I now defer to those who called this move correctly and respect the higher high. The burden of proof has shifted to the bears.

If weakness comes in Q4, I won't assume it's a lower low; it could be a macro higher low, especially if it aligns with a stock market correction. Historically, the final weakness in midterm years comes after the midterms, and a 10% S&P drop wouldn't be enough to take Bitcoin to a new low—it would need a much larger drop. I'll be a student of the market and stay open-minded.

## Counterpoints

- Some might argue that a rate hike should be bearish because it strengthens the dollar, but the data shows Bitcoin has not dropped immediately after rate hikes in the past.
- One might say the current rate hike isn't the first in a cycle, but the technical definition counts it as such, and Bitcoin has still rallied.
- A critic could point to the FTX collapse in 2022 as a reason Bitcoin dropped after a rate hike, but that was an exogenous event, not a direct reaction to the hike.
- Some might expect a correction in stocks to drag Bitcoin down, but a 10% S&P drop wouldn't be sufficient; it would need a much larger drop.
