## Thesis

Rate hikes are not immediately bearish for Bitcoin, and my assumption that they were was the core error behind my wrong September 2026 prediction.

## Argument

I built a macro chain: energy up → yields up → Fed hikes → dollar up → Bitcoin down. Every link played out except the last. Bitcoin rallied with the dollar, breaking its May high and printing a higher high. Historical data shows that after the first rate hike of a cycle (Dec 2015, Mar 2022) and after September hikes in midterm years (2018, 2022), Bitcoin did not immediately drop; in one case the bottom was already in. The current move also aligns with the golden cross and a 50% rally off the summer low, exceeding prior midterm years. I must defer to market structure and to those who called this move. If weakness comes in Q4, it likely requires a stock correction, and the low could be a higher low, not a lower low. The burden of proof has shifted to the bears.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that a rate hike should be bearish because it strengthens the dollar, but historical data shows Bitcoin often rises or has already bottomed.
- Some might say that the current rate hike is not the first in a cycle, but the technical definition counts it as such, and September hikes in midterm years have not been immediately bearish.
- A skeptic could point to past midterm-year corrections, but 2010 shows that a correction is not guaranteed, and the Nasdaq is at new highs, consistent with prior Septembers.
