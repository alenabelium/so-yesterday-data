## Thesis

Bitcoin's post-rate-hike rally proves that assuming rate hikes are immediately bearish for Bitcoin is not rooted in historical data, and I was wrong to be so deterministic.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a macro chain: energy up → yields up → Fed hikes → dollar up → Bitcoin down. I got the first four right, but Bitcoin rallied anyway. The data shows that after the first rate hike of a hiking cycle, Bitcoin either went up (March 2022) or the bottom was already in (December 2015). In September of midterm years, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. So the assumption that a hike forces an immediate repricing was never supported. I now defer to market structure: Bitcoin printed a higher high, and the burden of proof has shifted to the bears. If weakness comes in Q4, it likely requires a stock correction, and even then, a 10% S&P drop probably isn't enough to take Bitcoin to a new low. I'm open to a macro higher low, especially after the midterms, and I'll defer to those who called this move correctly.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that a rate hike is a sign of a strong economy, so Bitcoin should be fine long-term, but I wrongly assumed a short-term repricing.
- The dollar's double bottom pattern previously coincided with Bitcoin's local top, but this time Bitcoin went up with the dollar, showing something changed.
- Some might say the stock market always corrects in midterm years, but 2010 shows that's not a rule.
- One could argue that Bitcoin's rally is just a countertrend move, but the higher high suggests otherwise.
