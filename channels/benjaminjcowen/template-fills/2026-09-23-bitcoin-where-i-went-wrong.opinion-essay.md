## Thesis

Rate hikes are not immediately bearish for Bitcoin, and my assumption that they were was the root of my wrong call.

## Argument

The mechanism is historical data. I built a macro chain: energy up → yields up → Fed hikes → dollar up → Bitcoin down. Energy, yields, and the dollar all moved as I predicted, but Bitcoin rallied instead. The flaw was the final link. Looking at past rate-hike cycles, Bitcoin did not drop immediately after the first hike in December 2015 or March 2022; in one case the bottom was already in, and in the other it initially rose. September hikes in 2018 and 2022 also didn't trigger immediate drops. The data simply doesn't support the assumption that a hike is instantly bearish. So I now defer to market structure: Bitcoin has printed a higher high, and the burden of proof has shifted to the bears. If weakness comes in Q4, it would likely need a stock market correction to drive it, and even then a 10% S&P drop might not be enough to take Bitcoin to a new low. I'm open-minded to a higher low, especially after the midterms, as seen in prior cycles.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that the rate hike is a continuation of a prior cycle, not a first hike, but the technical definition still counts it as the first in a new cycle.
- Some might expect a correction because it happened in three of the last four midterm years, but 2010 shows it's not guaranteed.
- A critic could say my macro chain was wrong, but energy, yields, and the dollar all moved as predicted; only the Bitcoin reaction was off.
