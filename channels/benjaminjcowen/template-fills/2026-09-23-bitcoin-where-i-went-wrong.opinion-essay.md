## Thesis

My assumption that Fed rate hikes are immediately bearish for Bitcoin was not rooted in historical data, and I was wrong to predict a drop after the September 2026 hike.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a macro chain that played out perfectly: energy rose, yields rose, the Fed hiked, the dollar strengthened — yet Bitcoin rallied instead of repricing lower. The flaw was the final link: I assumed a rate hike would spook Bitcoin, but the data shows otherwise. In the first rate hike of a cycle (December 2015), the bottom was already in and Bitcoin continued higher; in March 2022, Bitcoin initially rose. In September of midterm years (2018, 2022), Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. So the rate hike was never the bearish trigger I made it out to be.

I compounded this by ignoring market structure. Bitcoin formed its February low, then a summer low, then rallied 50% — a bigger move than in 2018 or 2022 — and set a higher high. That higher high shifts the burden of proof to the bears. I can't be deterministic about a lower low when the market is showing me a higher high. If weakness comes in Q4, it likely needs a stock market correction to materialize, and even a 10% S&P drop may not be enough to take Bitcoin to a new low.

So I defer to those who called this move correctly, and I'll respect the higher high unless it's fully retraced. I'm open-minded to a macro higher low after the midterms, but I won't assume it. My mistake was assuming a rate hike would cause an immediate repricing — that was the weak link in my thesis.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that the rate hike is a continuation of the prior cycle, not a first hike, so the comparison is invalid — but the technical definition counts it as a first hike.
- Some might say Bitcoin's rally is just a temporary divergence and it will eventually drop — but the data shows Bitcoin has not dropped immediately after rate hikes in the past.
- A skeptic could point to 2018 and 2022, where Bitcoin eventually fell after the summer rally — but those were lower highs, whereas 2026 has a higher high.
- One might argue that a stock market correction will drag Bitcoin down — but even a 10% S&P drop may not be enough to take Bitcoin to a new low.
