## Thesis

Rate hikes are not immediately bearish for Bitcoin, and my assumption that they were was the core error behind my wrong September 2026 call.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a macro chain that played out perfectly but led to the wrong conclusion. I correctly predicted energy prices would rise, yields would follow, the Fed would hike in September, and the dollar would strengthen. But I then assumed Bitcoin would reprice lower because of the rate hike. That assumption was not rooted in historical data. Looking back at the two prior rate-hiking cycles since Bitcoin's inception, the first hike in December 2015 saw the bottom already in, and the first hike in March 2022 saw Bitcoin initially rally. In September of the last two midterm years, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. The data simply does not support an immediate bearish reaction.

Instead, the market structure was telling a different story. Bitcoin formed its February low, its summer low, and then rallied 50% off that low—a bigger move than in the last two midterm years. It also set a higher high, unlike the lower highs of 2018 and 2022. The dollar's double bottom pattern, which previously corresponded to a Bitcoin local top, this time saw Bitcoin rally alongside the dollar. This divergence, coupled with a golden cross and the rate hike, created a perfect storm for a breakout.

Given this, I have to defer to the market structure and to those who called the move correctly. I don't have the authority to predict a retrace when I didn't predict the breakout. ## Counterpoints

- One might argue that a rate hike should be bearish because it strengthens the dollar, but historical data shows Bitcoin often rises or has already bottomed after the first hike.
- Some might say the current rate hike isn't the first in a cycle, but by technical definition it is, and even September hikes in midterm years haven't caused immediate drops.
- A skeptic could point to the FTX collapse in 2022 as a reason for caution, but that event caused only a 15% drawdown, not a new low.
- Others might insist on a lower low in Q4, but the higher high and the silver analogy suggest a higher low is possible.
