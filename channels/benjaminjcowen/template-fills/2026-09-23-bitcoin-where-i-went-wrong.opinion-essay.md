## Thesis

Rate hikes are not immediately bearish for Bitcoin, and my assumption that they were was the root of my wrong call.

## Argument

The mechanism is historical data. I built a macro chain—energy up, yields up, Fed hikes, dollar up—and every link played out, yet Bitcoin rallied instead of repricing lower. The flaw was the final assumption: that a rate hike would immediately hurt Bitcoin. Looking back at the two prior rate-hiking cycles since Bitcoin's inception, the first hike in December 2015 saw the bottom already in, and in March 2022 Bitcoin actually went up initially. In September of the last two midterm years, Bitcoin did fine for a month or two after the hike. So the data never supported my bearish conclusion. Now I defer to market structure: Bitcoin set a higher high, and the burden of proof has shifted to the bears. If weakness comes in Q4, it likely requires a stock market correction—and a 10% S&P drop probably wouldn't be enough to take Bitcoin to a new low. I'm open-minded to a macro higher low, especially after the midterms, as in prior cycles. I was wrong, and I'm listening to those who called it right.

## Counterpoints

- Some might argue that this rate hike isn't the first in a cycle, but the technical definition says it is, and history shows rates can rise again without returning to zero.
- A stock market correction could still trigger Bitcoin weakness, but a 10% S&P drop likely wouldn't be enough to force a new low.
- Past midterm years often saw a late-September stock top, but 2010 shows that a correction isn't guaranteed.
- One might expect Bitcoin to follow the gold pattern with a lower high, but silver's higher high without a lower low suggests otherwise.
