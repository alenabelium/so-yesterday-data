## Thesis

My assumption that a Fed rate hike would immediately be bearish for Bitcoin was not rooted in historical data, and I was wrong to be so deterministic about it.

## Argument

The mechanism of my error was a chain of macro reasoning: rising energy prices would push yields up, forcing the Fed to hike in September, which would strengthen the dollar and reprice Bitcoin lower. Every link in that chain played out — energy, yields, the dollar — but Bitcoin rallied instead. The flaw was the final assumption: that a rate hike is immediately bearish for Bitcoin. That assumption isn't supported by the data. In the two prior rate-hiking cycles since Bitcoin's existence, the first hike didn't cause an immediate drop — in December 2015 the bottom was already in, and in March 2022 Bitcoin initially went up. In September of the last two midterm years (2018 and 2022), Bitcoin also did fine for a month or two after the hike. So the historical record contradicts my deterministic view.

Because I got this wrong, I now defer to market structure over my macro narrative. Bitcoin has set a higher high, breaking above the May high, and the burden of proof has shifted to the bears. I don't have the credibility to predict a retrace when I didn't predict the breakout. If weakness comes later in Q4, I won't assume it must be a lower low — it could resolve to a macro higher low, especially if it aligns with a stock market correction. Historically, the final weakness in midterm years has come after the midterms, not before. I'll remain a student of the market and respect the higher high unless it's completely retraced.

## Counterpoints

- Some might argue that a rate hike should still be bearish because it strengthens the dollar, but the data shows Bitcoin has not consistently dropped after rate hikes.
- One might say this rate hike isn't the first in a cycle, but by technical definition it is, and even in September of prior midterm years, Bitcoin didn't immediately drop.
- A skeptic could point to the FTX collapse in 2022 as a reason for post-hike weakness, but that was an idiosyncratic event, not a direct response to the rate hike.
- Others might insist on a lower low in Q4, but I'm now open to a higher low, especially if the stock market doesn't correct significantly.
