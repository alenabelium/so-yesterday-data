# Payrolls Come in Weak, Unemployment Ticks Higher

## Executive Summary

The video analyzes the latest U.S. labor market report, noting that while the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2%, this was driven by increased labor force participation rather than layoffs, which remain low. Nonfarm payrolls came in much weaker than expected at 29,000, signaling hiring difficulties, especially for younger workers. The host discusses the divergence between the S&P 500 at all-time highs and the Russell 2000 dropping, attributing this to rising yields and widening credit spreads. He warns that either the Russell will catch up or the S&P will correct, and highlights the Bank of Japan's likely rate hikes and the potential for the Fed to restart quantitative tightening.

## Key Points

- Unemployment rose to 4.2% due to more people entering the labor force, not layoffs; job losers actually declined. [00:01:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kDJcse482Eo&t=82)
- Nonfarm payrolls came in at just 29,000 versus 90,000 expected, and year-over-year growth is near recessionary levels. [00:06:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kDJcse482Eo&t=401)
- The Russell 2000 has dropped ~10% while the S&P 500 is at all-time highs; either the Russell catches up or the S&P corrects. [00:13:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kDJcse482Eo&t=792)
- Credit spreads are widening and the MOVE index is spiking, but the S&P has not yet reacted; this is a risk to watch. [00:17:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kDJcse482Eo&t=1065)
- The Bank of Japan is likely to raise rates in October and December, while the Fed may hold; the host also discusses the possibility of the Fed restarting quantitative tightening. [00:30:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kDJcse482Eo&t=1821)
