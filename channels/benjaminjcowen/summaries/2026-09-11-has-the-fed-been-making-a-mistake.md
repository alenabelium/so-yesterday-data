# Has the Fed Been Making A Mistake?

## Executive Summary

Benjamin Cowen argues that the Federal Reserve is making a mistake by refusing to raise interest rates despite rising long-term yields, sticky inflation, and a tightening labor market. He contends that the Fed's inaction forces the long end of the yield curve higher, as bond vigilantes revolt, and that only a credible rate hike can restore confidence and lower long-term yields. He highlights the political pressure on the Fed and the conflict between Treasury Secretary Bessent's bond buyback plan and Fed Chair Warsh's hawkish stance. Cowen concludes that a rate hike, while potentially causing short-term market pain, is necessary to prevent a future inflationary spiral.

## Key Points

- The Fed's refusal to raise rates despite rising long-term yields and sticky inflation is causing the long end of the yield curve to climb, as bond vigilantes test the Fed's credibility. [00:04:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3BL-YrZxTZI&t=242)
- The Fed's unwillingness to act, possibly due to political pressure, has led to unprecedented uncertainty, with rate hike odds at only 60% just days before the September meeting, likely causing more violent market reactions. [00:07:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3BL-YrZxTZI&t=476)
- A rate hike is necessary and historically does not signal a weak economy; in 1997, a hike led to a 10% stock drop but was followed by years of gains, and cutting rates into a strong economy risks another inflationary spiral. [00:11:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3BL-YrZxTZI&t=707)
