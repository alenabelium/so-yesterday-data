# Gold: Dubious Speculation

## Executive Summary

Benjamin Cowen analyzes gold's recent decline from $4,100, attributing it to rising long-end yields and a stronger dollar, a pattern observed in prior midterm years like 2018 and 2022. He argues that gold typically bottoms in the summer of midterm years, then rallies and retests lows in Q4, with the outcome (higher or lower low) depending on when the 10-year yield tops. He suggests watching for gold strength as a divergence signal that yields are peaking, and notes that the Fed funds rate being below the neutral rate is a key driver of rising long-end yields, not just energy prices. He remains cautious about timing but expects gold to carve out a low in the coming weeks.

## Key Points

- Gold typically bottoms in the summer of midterm years, then rallies and retests lows in Q4, with outcomes varying between higher and lower lows. [00:01:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZH5ivfBD-KQ&t=68)
- Rising long-end yields and a stronger dollar are the primary short-term bearish drivers for gold, as seen in 2018 and 2022. [00:02:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZH5ivfBD-KQ&t=153)
- Watch for gold to show strength despite rising yields as a signal that the 10-year yield is topping, which historically precedes a gold bottom. [00:03:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZH5ivfBD-KQ&t=224)
- The Fed funds rate being below the neutral rate is a key reason for rising long-end yields, not just energy prices. [00:17:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZH5ivfBD-KQ&t=1063)
- Gold is expected to carve out a low between mid-September and early November, after which yields should decline. [00:22:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZH5ivfBD-KQ&t=1360)
