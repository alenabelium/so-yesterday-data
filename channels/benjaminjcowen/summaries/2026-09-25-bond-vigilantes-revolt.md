# Bond Vigilantes Revolt

## Executive Summary

Benjamin Cowen analyzes the recent surge in long-term Treasury yields, arguing that the bond market is revolting against the Federal Reserve's accommodative policy, which is fueling inflation concerns. He explains that high yields pressure long-duration assets like crypto, leading to underperformance relative to stocks, and that a growth scare or crisis is needed to trigger looser policy and renewed liquidity. Cowen also discusses the inflationary impact of tariffs, Middle East tensions, and the AI buildout, and warns that until the Fed raises rates enough, yields will keep climbing and risk assets will face headwinds.

## Key Points

- The 10-year yield is approaching 5.2% and the 30-year is near 5.12%, signaling that the bond market demands higher rates to combat sticky inflation. [00:01:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F888T3SIBhU&t=60)
- The solution to high rates is high rates: yields must rise until they trigger a growth scare, which then allows them to fall. [00:02:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F888T3SIBhU&t=162)
- Crypto has underperformed the S&P 500 for years because high yields and tight liquidity conditions favor lower-duration assets; a durable altcoin rotation is unlikely until liquidity loosens. [00:08:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F888T3SIBhU&t=487)
- The Fed funds rate is now below the 2-year yield, indicating policy is no longer restrictive, which is why the long end keeps rising. [00:15:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F888T3SIBhU&t=944)
- AI's buildout is inflationary in the short term, adding to the pressures from tariffs and oil prices, but will become disinflationary in the long term. [00:12:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F888T3SIBhU&t=726)
