# Longevity Escape Velocity

## Executive Summary

David Shapiro discusses the concept of 'Longevity Escape Velocity,' where medical and technological advances could extend life expectancy by one year for every year lived, leading to an unlimited lifespan. He clarifies that this involves rejuvenation, not just extended aging, and argues that AI-driven medical breakthroughs will make this feasible within decades. He addresses concerns about access, suggesting that market incentives and government policies will ensure widespread availability. He also estimates that in a world without aging-related deaths, average lifespans could range from 700 to 1,100 years, with societal implications.

## Key Points

- Longevity escape velocity means life expectancy increases by one year per calendar year, leading to unlimited lifespan, but it requires rejuvenation, not just extended aging. [00:00:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-c6MUXkpMs&t=40)
- AI and advanced computing are making medicine and genetics solvable mathematical problems, accelerating the development of rejuvenation therapies and personalized medicine. [00:08:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-c6MUXkpMs&t=521)
- Access to longevity treatments will likely be widespread due to profit incentives for selling to billions and government interest in healthy, productive citizens. [00:11:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-c6MUXkpMs&t=703)
