# Bernie's plan sucks, actually

## Executive Summary

David Shapiro critiques Bernie Sanders' proposal for a sovereign wealth fund that would seize 50% equity in major AI companies, arguing it is unconstitutional, economically dangerous, and prone to political capture. He contrasts this with a more viable model based on the Santiago Principles, advocating for passive index ownership with strict exposure limits rather than active government control. The video outlines a comprehensive blueprint for funding such a fund through ecosystem-wide levies and distributing returns via direct dividends, worker transitions, and infrastructure investment.

## Key Points

- Sanders' proposal is a one-time expropriation of equity that risks capital flight and violates the Takings Clause, making it legally and economically unviable [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-wQ8TM8CD3c&t=0).
- A functional sovereign wealth fund must adhere to the Santiago Principles, utilizing passive index ownership capped at 10% per company to avoid market distortion and political interference [00:17:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-wQ8TM8CD3c&t=1058).
- The recommended blueprint replaces forced seizures with gradual revenue mechanisms like compute levies and windfall taxes, with returns distributed as direct cash dividends and targeted transition funds [00:20:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-wQ8TM8CD3c&t=1243).
- Effective governance requires subsidiarity, allowing states and localities to capture rents from data centers to fund local services while maintaining constitutional protections against federal raiding [00:23:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-wQ8TM8CD3c&t=1435).
