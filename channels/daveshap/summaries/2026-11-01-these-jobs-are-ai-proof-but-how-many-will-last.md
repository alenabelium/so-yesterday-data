# These Jobs are AI-Proof... But How Many Will Last?

## Executive Summary

David Shapiro argues that the traditional three-sector economic model—extractive, transformational, and service—is being disrupted by a fourth, automated sector where value is created with minimal human labor. He critiques past attempts to define fourth and fifth sectors as categorical errors that merely subdivided services by occupation. Shapiro proposes that AI and robotics will dominate GDP within decades, shifting economic power from labor to asset ownership, and introduces new metrics to measure household income from ownership and common wealth.

## Key Points

- The Fisher-Clark model defines sectors by how value is created, not by profession, and services have always been the largest by value, not by job count. [00:04:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LMiadHKsf64&t=257)
- Past proposals for a fourth or fifth sector failed because they merely subdivided the service sector by occupation, repeating a categorical error. [00:05:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LMiadHKsf64&t=331)
- The real fourth sector is automated value creation, which will grow to 75-90% of the economy within decades, making ownership more important than labor. [00:11:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LMiadHKsf64&t=715)
