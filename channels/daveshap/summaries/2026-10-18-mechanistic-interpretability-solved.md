# Mechanistic Interpretability... solved?

## Executive Summary

Nick Cammarata's essay 'Neural Networks Are Already Ordered' argues that neural networks are not chaotic black boxes but ordered structures where neurons have interpretable functions, challenging long-held assumptions in AI interpretability. The essay, published by Good Fire, claims that AI agents can now decode neurons at scale, with about 90% of neurons in large models having a single main task. This has sparked a heated reaction from Eliezer Yudkowsky, who dismisses the findings as primitive, while other researchers urge caution. The video concludes that while interpretability isn't fully solved, this marks a turning point in how we understand AI, potentially weakening doomsday arguments based on the black box metaphor.

## Key Points

- Cammarata's essay claims neural networks are ordered, not chaotic, citing examples like a neuron that recognizes V-shapes (cat muzzles, car fronts) and recursive generalization where neurons branch into related concepts. [00:03:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ubDXXZ8iRAo&t=207)
- His team's audit of Qwen 3 32B found ~90% of neuron poles have one main task, and AI agents can generate examples that activate neurons 76% of the time, suggesting interpretability at scale. [00:04:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ubDXXZ8iRAo&t=287)
- Yudkowsky responded with 18 posts in 5 hours, mocking the work as 'poking a screwdriver into a complicated machine,' while critics like Neel Nanda argue it's not a significant update; the video counters that the black box argument for AI doom is weakened if we can read internal circuits. [00:08:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ubDXXZ8iRAo&t=491)
