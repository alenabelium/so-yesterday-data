# Why people HATE automation

## Executive Summary

This video reexamines the historical Luddite movement, arguing that the Luddites were not anti-technology but rather opposed specific uses of machinery that lowered wages, degraded quality, and ignored craft customs. It draws parallels between the 1811-1812 machine-breaking protests and modern debates about automation, emphasizing that decisions about new technology inherently involve choices about who benefits and who loses. The video concludes that the Luddites' core questions—who owns new tools, who decides their use, and who gets the productivity bonus—remain relevant today.

## Key Points

- The Luddites were skilled workers who knew their machines well and opposed specific uses of technology that lowered wages and quality, not technology itself. [00:06:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9krtvPgAdnY&t=388)
- The government responded with force, making machine-breaking a capital crime and executing or deporting the protesters. [00:04:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9krtvPgAdnY&t=283)
- Modern automation debates echo the Luddite questions: who owns the new tools, who decides how to use them, and who gets the productivity bonus. [00:06:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9krtvPgAdnY&t=388)
