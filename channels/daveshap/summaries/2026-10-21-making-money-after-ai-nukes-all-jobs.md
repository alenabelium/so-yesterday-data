# Making Money After AI Nukes All Jobs

## Executive Summary

The video explores the economic implications of AI-driven automation, arguing that as machines produce more wealth, the critical question becomes who owns the capital. It presents four models for distributing ownership—public wealth funds, production cooperatives, employee ownership, and startup capital—each with strengths and weaknesses. The speaker concludes that combining these approaches could make property ownership a birthright, ensuring that everyone benefits from automation rather than just the wealthy.

## Key Points

- The richest 10% of US households own nearly 90% of corporate shares, while the bottom 50% own only about 0.5%, highlighting the concentration of ownership that automation profits flow to. [00:00:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U4Qx9HRCk2c&t=2)
- Alaska's Permanent Fund and Norway's sovereign wealth fund demonstrate how public value can be converted into private income for all citizens, with Norway's fund owning about 1.5% of global shares. [00:01:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U4Qx9HRCk2c&t=64)
- The Mondragon Corporation in Spain and ESOPs in the US show how worker ownership can align incentives, but they face challenges like capital intensity and risk concentration. [00:02:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U4Qx9HRCk2c&t=143)
- Connecticut's child bond program, which invests up to $3,200 per child at birth, illustrates how startup capital can create wealth for those who inherit nothing, though the amounts remain small. [00:04:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U4Qx9HRCk2c&t=284)
