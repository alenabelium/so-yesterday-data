# The Last 5 Years of Labor

## Executive Summary

David Shapiro revisits his earlier prediction of an 'automation abyss,' clarifying that the displacement of labor by AI is not a single catastrophic event but a series of 'automation cliffs' occurring across individual value streams. He provides concrete examples from companies like Klarna, Salesforce, and Goldman Sachs to illustrate how AI is already replacing human work in specific tasks, while acknowledging that failures and quality issues can slow adoption. The video argues that these incremental cliffs are already causing significant job losses, particularly among young workers, and concludes that the post-labor economy is arriving piecemeal, making ownership of capital increasingly important.

## Key Points

- The automation abyss is real but manifests as thousands of smaller 'automation cliffs' across individual value streams, not one economy-wide event. [00:02:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W6b7MHwUBHk&t=146)
- Real-world examples show AI already replacing human labor: Klarna's AI handled 2/3 of support chats, Salesforce cut support staff from 9,000 to 5,000, and Goldman Sachs now uses AI to prepare 95% of IPO prospectuses. [00:04:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W6b7MHwUBHk&t=258)
- Despite setbacks like Klarna's quality issues and Commonwealth Bank's botched call center cuts, the trend is clear: 376,000 information sector jobs disappeared since ChatGPT's launch, and AI is now the leading cause of job cuts in the US. [00:05:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W6b7MHwUBHk&t=359)
- Young workers are hit hardest, with employment among 22-25 year olds in AI-vulnerable jobs falling 11% since 2022, while those in less vulnerable jobs saw a 10% increase. [00:08:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W6b7MHwUBHk&t=539)
