# This is my next big work

## Executive Summary

David Shapiro introduces his upcoming book, 'Credible Threats,' which addresses the existential risk of humans becoming economically redundant due to automation and AI. He argues that traditional labor leverage is eroding, necessitating a new realist theory of rights and civic equilibrium to protect human dignity. The core thesis is that rights are only effective when backed by credible threats, such as nonviolent general strikes, to extract concessions from elites. Shapiro emphasizes the urgency of building this new political power structure before labor's bargaining power disappears completely.

## Key Points

- Human labor has historically provided leverage through its unique characteristics like scarcity and the ability to refuse work, but automation is severing this elite dependence on human contribution [00:03:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VJn37REPTPM&t=185).
- Shapiro defines 'generative mutualism' and 'double bilateral dependence' to explain how social contracts rely on mutual hostage situations, which are collapsing as labor becomes obsolete [00:05:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VJn37REPTPM&t=340).
- To secure future rights, citizens must engineer new 'veto power' through nonviolent resistance and general strikes, as historical data shows nonviolent campaigns are significantly more effective than violent ones [00:15:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VJn37REPTPM&t=943).
- The author is expanding his research on labor leverage into a 190,000-word book and relies entirely on audience support via Patreon and Kickstarter to maintain intellectual independence [00:19:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VJn37REPTPM&t=1145).
