# Why UBI isn't enough

## Executive Summary

This video argues that Universal Basic Income (UBI) alone is insufficient to address the economic vulnerability of households that rely almost entirely on labor income. By tracing the income of a fictional family, it shows that over 96% of their income comes from salaries, leaving them extremely exposed to automation-driven job loss. The video contrasts this with a retired couple whose income is diversified across Social Security and capital, highlighting the resilience of capital-based income. It concludes that policy should focus on expanding capital ownership channels—such as sovereign wealth funds or employee stock ownership—rather than just income transfers.

## Key Points

- The Rivers family's income is 96.7% from salaries, 2.7% from transfers, and 0.6% from capital, making them highly vulnerable to job loss. [00:02:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8XsbzhuM3ns&t=169)
- If Sam loses his job to automation, the family's income drops by 31%, and their savings would be depleted in about five weeks, illustrating the fragility of single-channel income. [00:04:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8XsbzhuM3ns&t=250)
- To replace Sam's salary with capital income, the family would need $585,000 to $780,000 in assets, a sum far beyond typical savings, underscoring why capital ownership is concentrated. [00:05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8XsbzhuM3ns&t=350)
- The video advocates for policies that expand the capital channel—like sovereign wealth funds or employee stock ownership—because it grows through compound interest and is independent of labor demand. [00:07:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8XsbzhuM3ns&t=437)
