# Does AI Actually Understand? YES!

## Executive Summary

David Shapiro argues that AI systems do understand, countering a viral claim by Gregory Kurtzer that LLMs are merely statistical token predictors. He uses Bloom's Taxonomy and Ashby's Law to demonstrate that AI achieves all levels of understanding—from remembering to creating—based on observable performance. He distinguishes understanding from consciousness, noting that subjective feelings of understanding are unreliable in both humans and machines. He concludes that AI understands unevenly and differently, but genuinely, and that denying this is a category error.

## Key Points

- Bloom's Taxonomy defines understanding by observable actions, and AI demonstrates all six levels from remembering to creating, as shown by examples like AI winning math Olympiads and writing compilers. [00:01:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lhvPU5mAeWc&t=113)
- Ashby's Law and the Conant-Ashby theorem require regulators to model their systems, and AI's success in coding, robotics, and other tasks proves it has built such models. [00:05:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lhvPU5mAeWc&t=320)
- The feeling of understanding is unreliable in humans (illusion of explanatory depth) and AI has its own 'I know that' signals that can misfire, so subjective consciousness is not a valid criterion for understanding. [00:08:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lhvPU5mAeWc&t=524)
