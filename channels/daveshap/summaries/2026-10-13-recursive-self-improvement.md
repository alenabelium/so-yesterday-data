# Recursive Self Improvement

## Executive Summary

David Shapiro explains recursive self-improvement (RSI) as the increasing percentage of AI progress generated by AI itself, debunking sci-fi notions of a runaway superintelligence. He illustrates this with a pie chart showing AI's contribution to code, data, and algorithms growing from 5% to potentially 50-80% by 2027. He highlights formal verification as a key capability that will unlock breakthroughs in materials science and other fields. He concludes by discussing emergentism, comparing AI's current capabilities to moving from an alphabet to sentences, with far more potential ahead.

## Key Points

- Recursive self-improvement is not Skynet; it's simply the percentage of next-generation AI created by AI itself, with no implications about speed or control. [00:07:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=baKLwo2tO4E&t=473)
- AI's contribution to AI progress is growing rapidly, from 5% last year to an estimated 50-80% by 2027, driven by programming, data, and math. [00:06:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=baKLwo2tO4E&t=395)
- Formal verification—proving code, materials, or systems work as intended—is a rare, expensive skill that AI is nearing, enabling breakthroughs across sciences. [00:11:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=baKLwo2tO4E&t=680)
- AI capabilities are entering an emergent phase, like combining letters into words and sentences, with the potential to reach novel-level complexity. [00:15:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=baKLwo2tO4E&t=933)
