# Nobody gets this right

## Executive Summary

David Shapiro critiques the popular notion that 'the world is not made of words' by arguing that modern AI models are increasingly multimodal and capable of approximating physical world models through continuous sensor data and prediction. He contends that the distinction between language models and world models is diminishing as AI converges on unified 'omni models' that handle both abstract reasoning and sensory-motor feedback. Shapiro further argues that concerns about AI safety in critical physical domains like healthcare and industrial control are category errors, as these systems rely on rigorous testing and specialized algorithms rather than generative unpredictability. Ultimately, he suggests that the current hype around 'world models' ignores the long-standing principles of cognitive architectures and the fundamental role of prediction in understanding reality.

## Key Points

- Shapiro argues that the claim 'the world is not made of words' is outdated because modern AI models process multimodal data (video, audio, sensor feeds) and predict continuous physical phenomena just as effectively as text tokens [00:04:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjIU6tv7ipw&t=258).
- He dismisses safety concerns regarding AI in critical physical systems like healthcare and industrial control as category errors, noting that these sectors rely on deterministic code and extensive testing rather than generative unpredictability [00:07:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjIU6tv7ipw&t=457).
