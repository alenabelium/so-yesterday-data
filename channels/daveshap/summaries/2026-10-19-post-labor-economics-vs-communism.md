# Post-Labor Economics vs Communism

## Executive Summary

David Shapiro argues that a post-labor economy, where automation reduces the need for human work, is not communism but rather an extension of capitalism focused on widespread property ownership. He contrasts the historical features of Soviet-style communism—state ownership, central planning, and state dependency—with the post-labor vision of sovereign wealth funds and individual capital ownership. Shapiro emphasizes that the goal is to reduce dependence on government transfers by increasing income from property, as measured by the capital allocation ratio. He uses examples like Norway's sovereign wealth fund and Alaska's Permanent Fund to illustrate how collective ownership can operate within market capitalism. Ultimately, he concludes that the post-labor economy is 'capitalism with more capitalists.'

## Key Points

- The post-labor economy is not communism; it supports capital, property, and ownership, aiming to make people less dependent on the state, not more. [00:00:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8l1v9eEdqsQ&t=2)
- Communism historically involved state ownership, central planning, and treating everyone as a financial ward of the state, whereas the post-labor economy seeks to avoid benefit dependency and promote asset ownership. [00:01:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8l1v9eEdqsQ&t=73)
- Sovereign wealth funds like Norway's and Alaska's Permanent Fund demonstrate that collective ownership can coexist with market capitalism, as they invest in companies without controlling them and pay dividends to residents. [00:06:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8l1v9eEdqsQ&t=367)
