# Making Money After AI Nukes All Jobs

Today in the United States, the answer is mostly : those who already own everything else. When automation increases profits, these revenues go to shareholders, and this is a fairly narrow circle of people. So why not just raise wages? Because only those who work get paid, and the whole idea of automation is that employers will need fewer workers. In 1956, in the Basque town of Mondragon in northern Spain, five young people founded a small company to produce kerosene stoves. The company belonged to the people who worked in it. Today, the Mondragon Corporation is a federation of cooperatives spanning manufacturing, retail, finance, and education, employing over 70,000 people . In its cooperatives, members elect management, and the ratio between the highest and lowest salaries is set by a vote of members.

**[00:03:39]** This ratio is usually around five to one. In contrast, in many large corporations, the ratio between the CEO's salary and the salary of an ordinary employee reaches several hundred to one. The strength of this model lies in democratic control, as ownership gives a real voice. However, its weakness is that cooperatives are difficult to establish in industries that require huge capital , and members eventually find themselves in a situation where both their income and savings are tied to a single enterprise. Employee ownership. In the United States, the most common form of employee ownership is an employee stock ownership plan , or ESOP. This idea belongs to the lawyer and economist Louis Kelso, who believed that working people should receive income from both capital and their labor. In 1956, he developed the first ESOP so that employees of a newspaper company in Palo Alto, California, could buy out the business from retiring owners.

**[00:04:44]** Congress enshrined ESOPs in federal law in 1974 . Today, there are about 6,600 of them, covering over 15 million participants and having over $2 trillion in assets. Britain has its own tradition. The John Lewis department store chain has been owned in trust by its employees since 1950, and these employees are called partners . The power of employee ownership lies in alignment, as the people who do the work share the value they create. Its weakness, again, is the concentration of risks . If a company fails, employees can lose both their jobs and their savings at the same time. Starting capital. The newest idea is also the simplest. Give each child a share at birth.
