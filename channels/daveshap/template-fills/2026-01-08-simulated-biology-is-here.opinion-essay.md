## Thesis

Within 5-10 years, humanity could achieve full control over its own biology, and the real question is not whether we should 'play God' but how weird we could get.

## Argument

The mechanism is technological convergence. AlphaFold and rumored projects like Alpha Cell are moving us toward simulating every protein, cell, and genome, which means full biological control is within reach. The short-term impact is overestimated, but the long-term impact is underestimated. Once we have longevity escape velocity, we've also taken full control over our biology. The real question is how weird could humans get? Science fiction offers touchpoints: Dune's guild navigators, Star Trek's Borg, and posthumanist scenarios like mind uploading or the 'grays.' These stories reflect anxiety about erasing our own humanity. But we're in the driver's seat—nihilism established there is no God. The ethics narrow to Malthusian dynamics: the Red Queen hypothesis means you'll be incentivized to adapt even if you don't want to, because everyone else is adapting. This could drive the species toward uniformity or something alien. The most serious risk is qualia fading or phenomenal drift—losing subjective experience through incremental changes that each seem rational in the moment. Cybernetics and mind uploading are dangerous because we don't know what we're replacing; we haven't discovered a 'ghost line.' The minimax strategy favors slow, incremental genetic modifications over radical cybernetics or engram transfers. This allows you to become superhuman over time while minimizing the worst outcomes. ## Counterpoints

- Some argue that if you can evolve in one direction, you can always change back, but what if you evolve into something with a brain that doesn't fit in your skull, and evolving back requires losing consciousness or abilities?
- Some say that an uploaded engram is you, but that's the branching problem—it's just a copy, not literally you.
- Some might say that cybernetics and genetic engineering are just tools for health and longevity, but the ship of Theseus problem extends beyond the brain—you might be losing parts of your soul or ghost line permanently.
- Some might argue that we should just 'play God' and take control, but the Malthusian dynamics of the Red Queen hypothesis mean you'll be incentivized to adapt even if you don't want to, potentially leading to a loss of uniformity.
