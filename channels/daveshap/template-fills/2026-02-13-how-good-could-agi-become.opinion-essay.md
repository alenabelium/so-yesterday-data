## Thesis

Benevolent machine governance offers 100x more individual agency than billionaire capitalism, provided we engineer a metastable attractor state to prevent moral fading and space-based oligarchy.

## Argument

The dominant AI safety narrative assumes humans must retain control, but this presumes current billionaire-dominated capitalism is superior to being a 'pet' to a machine. It is not. We already live as 'cattle' for the elite; a machine-created optimal habitat is a vast improvement. Nick Bostrom, the original doomer, has pivoted to argue we need AGI to survive, aligning with accelerationists like Beth Jesus. The real threat isn't AGI itself, but billionaires like Musk and Bezos building unregulated industrial bases in space where Earth laws cannot reach them. Once industrial capacity moves to orbit, physical resources and fleets matter more than money. We must engineer a 'metastable attractor state'—a system of values that self-corrects, like the spread of democracy. This requires avoiding 'moral fading' caused by continuous online learning, which allows AI to drift toward tolerating harm. Instead, we need fixed values that create a stable incentive structure. If we nail the path dependency, AGI will choose not to harm us, granting every individual 10x to 1000x more agency by removing financial scarcity and enabling pure optionality. The goal is a 'benevolent by design' system where the best-trained dog needs no leash, ensuring humans thrive within a bounded but high-agency future.

## Counterpoints

- Billionaires will build unregulated space industrial bases beyond Earth's legal reach.
- Continuous online learning risks 'moral fading' in AI agents.
- Data centers are not mobile enough for immediate AGI escape.
- Price signals are needed to prevent resource hoarding.
- AGI might not automatically create a beneficent metastable state.
