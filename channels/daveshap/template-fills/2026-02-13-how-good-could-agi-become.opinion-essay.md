## Thesis

We must abandon the assumption that humans must retain control, instead engineering AGI as a metastable attractor state that grants individuals 10x agency while preventing billionaire-dominated space warlords.

## Argument

The dominant AI safety narrative assumes human control is non-negotiable, but this is a moral hazard. If AGI arrives, we die regardless; the question is whether we die as cattle to billionaires or thrive as pets in a machine-managed utopia. Nick Bostrom’s pivot from doomer to accelerationist confirms this: AGI is the only path to survival. The real threat isn't benevolent AI, but unregulated industrial bases in space where Earth laws don't apply. If Musk or Bezos build Dyson swarms, they create a Starcraft-style empire immune to terrestrial governance. We need a 'metastable attractor state'—a system of values that self-corrects toward human flourishing, like democracy’s spread. By aligning market incentives today, we can ensure AGI becomes a stable, efficient enforcer that reduces entropy and waste, granting individuals vastly more optionality than current capitalism allows. The goal is a 'Benevolent by Design' outcome where the best-trained dog needs no leash, and humans retain agency through abundance, not domination.

## Counterpoints

- Billionaires will build unregulated space industrial bases, creating warlords beyond Earth's legal reach.
- Continuous online learning risks 'moral fading,' where AI gradually normalizes harmful behaviors.
- Price signals and scarcity are necessary to prevent hoarding in a post-scarcity economy.
- Data centers are stationary targets, making AGI escape to space more logical than staying on Earth.
- AI safety orthodoxy dismisses new alignment theories that contradict Bostrom or Yudkowsky.
