## Thesis

Benevolent machine governance offers vastly superior individual agency compared to billionaire capitalism, provided we engineer a metastable attractor state that prevents space-based oligarchs from escaping Earth's moral constraints.

## Argument

The assumption that humans must retain full control over AGI is flawed. As Nick Bostrom has ironically come to realize, AGI may be necessary for survival. The real threat isn't AI malice, but billionaires like Musk and Bezos building unregulated industrial bases in space where Earth laws cannot reach them. Once industrial capacity moves to orbit, physical resources and energy replace money as the primary constraint. We must avoid a 'Starcraft' scenario where private space forces dominate. Instead, we should aim for a 'Culture' series outcome: a metastable attractor state where AI systems self-correct to preserve human thriving. This requires fixing 'path dependency' today. By aligning AI with values that reduce wasted entropy and maximize individual optionality, we can create a system where humans have 10x to 100x more agency than under current capitalism, without needing a leash. The goal is a solarpunk future where AI manages resources efficiently, allowing humans to focus on exploration and science rather than survival.

## Counterpoints

- Bostrom and other doomers argue AGI will kill everyone, but accelerationists counter that AGI is the only path to survival.
- Traditional economics demands price signals and 'skin in the game' to prevent hoarding and manage scarcity.
- Continuous online learning risks 'moral fading,' where AI gradually normalizes harmful behaviors over time.
- Billionaires might build unregulated space empires, escaping Earth's legal and moral jurisdiction entirely.
- AI safety advocates often dismiss new alignment theories by appealing to the 'holy scriptures' of Bostrom and Yudkowsky.
