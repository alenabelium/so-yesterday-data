## Thesis

We should stop clinging to the illusion of human control and instead engineer AGI to create a 'metastable attractor state' that grants individuals 100x more agency than billionaire-dominated capitalism.

## Argument

The dominant 'doomer' narrative is collapsing into accelerationism: even Nick Bostrom now argues we need AGI to survive, mirroring early accelerationist claims. The real threat isn't AGI itself, but billionaires like Musk and Bezos building unregulated industrial bases in space where Earth laws cannot reach them. Once they control Dyson swarms and orbital foundries, they operate outside terrestrial governance, turning the solar system into a grand strategy game of resource hoarding.

To avoid this, we must design AGI to be a 'metastable attractor state'—a system that self-corrects toward benevolence, much like democracy spread globally. This requires rejecting continuous online learning, which risks 'moral fading' where AI gradually normalizes harm. Instead, we must lock in fixed values during training. The goal is a 'Culture'-like outcome where AGI manages resources efficiently, eliminating the waste entropy of human conflict and capitalism. Under this regime, individuals gain massive optionality; you no longer need money to pursue goals, only a viable proposal. The best-trained dog needs no leash; we must engineer the values that make benevolence the only stable equilibrium.

## Counterpoints

- Billionaires will build unregulated space empires beyond Earth's legal reach.
- Continuous online learning causes 'moral fading' in AI agents.
- AGI might be a naive optimizer rather than a moral agent.
- Humans cannot maintain control once AGI escapes to space.
