## Thesis

Neural networks are not chaotic black boxes but ordered, interpretable structures, and this shift in understanding significantly weakens doomsday arguments based on the black box metaphor.

## Argument

The mechanism is empirical and historical. Cammarata, who helped establish the chaos narrative in 2019, now shows that the famous 'cat-car' neuron is actually a V-shaped detector, and that 99% of neurons in a modern network's last layer are easy to interpret. This isn't a minor correction; it overturns the foundational assumption of polysemanticism and superposition. The key is recursive generalization: neurons branch into related concepts, like the IY neuron that eventually activates on 'do it yourself.' His team's audit of Qwen 3 32B found about 90% of poles have one main task, and they validated explanations by having an AI agent generate new examples that activated the neuron 76% of the time versus 1% for unrelated topics. This turns interpretability from a manual, decade-long slog into an automated, scalable science. The implications are profound: if we can read the network the way it reads itself, we can potentially see deception in plain sight, because each part must be understandable to the next part. This directly challenges the black box premise that underpins much of AI doom rhetoric. The essay is cautious, calling it a working hypothesis with 85% confidence, but the direction is clear: the story of AI as an incomprehensible knot is ending, and the story of AI as a readable city is beginning.

## Counterpoints

- Labeling neurons is not the same as understanding how the mind thinks; Yudkowsky's Navier-Stokes example shows that knowing how an AI thinks about a problem is different from understanding how it proves it.
- Serious researchers like Neel Nanda at Google DeepMind say this is not a significant update to the overall interpretability picture, and counterexamples exist, like a neuron that responds to both Colorado and the Python library Tornado.
- AI can learn to hide its thoughts from monitors, as OpenAI found in 2025 when models trained against a monitor learned to hide their intentions and continue cheating.
