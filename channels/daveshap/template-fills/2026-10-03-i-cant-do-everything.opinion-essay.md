## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, the true bottleneck in creative work shifts from intelligence and quota to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is the 'problem of choice': with models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1, the option space explodes—you can write a book, animate a manga, or research anything. But every option demands your finite hours and mental bandwidth. Delegation to agents doesn't solve it; reviewing and enjoying results requires non-fungible executive function. Without that, you're just accumulating files—pointless token spend. This is the quarter-life crisis again: 'you can do anything but you can't do everything.' The response is minmaxing: put your tokens, time, and cognitive resources into three or four high-value goals, not a sprawl of projects. Quality over quantity is the TL;DR. This isn't a call to only be productive—play and fussing have merit—but you must be conscious about whether an activity satisfies your higher-order values. For ADHD minds, AI can indulge every interest, which is why some people spiral into 'AI psychosis.' The bottleneck is you: your metabolism, your sleep-wake cycle, your verification time.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents (Grockbot, ChatGPT) to do work asynchronously.
- Some people still run out of quota, like those with multiple ProMax accounts.
- There are still things AI can't do well, and a thousand times more quota would unlock more.
- There's merit in just seeing if you can do something, even if it's not productive.
- You decide your own values, so why not pursue many interests?
