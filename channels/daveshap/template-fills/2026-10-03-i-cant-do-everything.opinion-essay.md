## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, the true bottleneck shifts from intelligence and quota to human executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmax' of choices to avoid the problem of choice.

## Argument

The mechanism is the 'problem of choice'—more capable models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1 make everything seem possible, but human time and mental bandwidth are finite. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve this because reviewing and enjoying results requires non-fungible executive function; otherwise, it's just 'pointless token spend.' This creates FOMO, but with models like Opus 5.5, quota is no longer the constraint for most people. The real bottleneck is deciding what to actually pursue. This mirrors a 'quarter life crisis'—the realization that you can do anything but not everything. The solution is 'minmaxing': consciously aligning AI use with your highest-order values, focusing on quality over quantity. While there's merit in playful experimentation, you must be conscious about whether an activity satisfies your values or just feeds a billion interests, which for ADHD minds can lead to 'AI psychosis.'

## Counterpoints

- Some people still run out of quota (e.g., multiple accounts), but most mortals don't anymore.
- There are still things AI can't do well, and some tasks would require a thousand times more quota.
- You might argue you decide your own values, but you still have to be conscious about how you spend your time.
