## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time and executive function, not intelligence or quota, become the true bottleneck, forcing deliberate minmaxing of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: as models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1 get more capable, the option space explodes. You can write a book, animate a manga, or research anything—but each option demands your finite hours and mental bandwidth. Delegation to agents doesn't solve it because verification and enjoyment require non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without engaging them is just token spend. This is a quarter-life crisis redux: 'you can do anything, but you can't do everything.' The response is minmaxing—concentrating time, energy, and tokens on a few high-value goals rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity becomes the TLDDR. But this isn't a call for pure productivity; you must consciously align choices with your values, even when 'fussing around' is the choice. For those with ADHD or a billion interests, AI can become a psychosis-inducing indulgence, so the discipline of conscious value alignment is the only guardrail.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple agents (Grockbot, ChatGPT) to parallelize work.
- There's merit in just seeing if you can do something, even if it's not productive.
- Some people still run out of quota, so the bottleneck isn't universal yet.
