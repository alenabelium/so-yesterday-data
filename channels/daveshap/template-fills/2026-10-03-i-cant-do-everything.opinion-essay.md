## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not AI—are the true bottlenecks, forcing deliberate minmaxing of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological and practical. As models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1 become more capable, the range of feasible projects explodes—books, animations, research—but each requires your finite hours and mental bandwidth to verify, use, and enjoy. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve this; it just accumulates files unless you apply executive function, which is not fungible. The bottleneck shifts from quota and intelligence to your metabolism and sleep-wake cycle. This mirrors the 'quarter life crisis'—the realization that you can do anything but not everything. The solution is minmaxing: deliberately allocating your time, energy, and tokens to a few high-value goals that align with your values, rather than spreading thin across a sprawl of projects. Quality over quantity is the TL;DR. Even playful exploration is fine, but it must be conscious—ask if this satisfies your higher-order values or just feeds a billion interests, which is why some people experience 'AI psychosis' when AI indulges every whim.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents (e.g., Grockbot, ChatGPT) to handle tasks asynchronously.
- There is merit in just seeing if you can do something, challenging yourself, or fussing around without productivity goals.
- You decide your own values, so who are you to say what's worth spending time on?
- Some people still run out of quota (e.g., multiple Pro accounts), so the bottleneck isn't universal.
