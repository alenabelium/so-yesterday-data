## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, the true bottleneck in creative and knowledge work shifts from computational limits to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use.

## Argument

The problem of choice is the mechanism. With models like Opus 5.5 and Astra, the host finds he can pursue any project—books, manga, animations—but each requires his limited hours and mental bandwidth. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve this: reviewing and enjoying results demands non-fungible executive function. Without that, outputs are just 'pointless token spend.' This is a new inflection point where quota is no longer the constraint for most users; the bottleneck is the human. The host draws a parallel to the 'quarter life crisis'—the realization that potential is vast but time is finite. The lesson is to minmax: deliberately concentrate AI use on a few high-value goals rather than spreading effort thin. This is a forced re-evaluation of AI usage, moving from 'can do anything' to 'can't do everything.' The host argues this is a necessary adaptation to avoid being overwhelmed by endless possibilities and to ensure quality over quantity.

## Counterpoints

- Some users still run out of quota, like those with multiple premium accounts.
- There remain tasks AI cannot do well, and some would require a thousand times more quota.
- One might think delegation to AI agents solves the bottleneck, but verification and enjoyment still require human time.
