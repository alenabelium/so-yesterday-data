## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, human time, attention, and executive function—not AI capability—are the true bottlenecks, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological and practical. With models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, the option space explodes—you can write a book, animate a manga, or research anything. But the bottleneck shifts from quota to your own metabolism and clock time. Delegation doesn't solve it: verifying and enjoying results requires non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without engaging them is just token spend. This is the 'problem of choice'—analysis paralysis from too many options. It mirrors the quarter-life crisis: you can do anything, but not everything. The solution is minmaxing—optimizing your AI use around three or four specific goals that give the most bang for your buck, rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity. This forces a conscious re-evaluation of values: is this how you want to spend your day? For ADHD minds, AI can indulge every interest, which is why some experience 'AI psychosis.' The discipline is to choose deliberately, not to do everything.

## Counterpoints

- Some people still run out of quota (e.g., multiple accounts), but most mortals don't anymore.
- There's merit in just fiddling around or challenging yourself, not only productive work.
- You decide your own values, but you must be conscious about whether an activity satisfies higher-order values.
