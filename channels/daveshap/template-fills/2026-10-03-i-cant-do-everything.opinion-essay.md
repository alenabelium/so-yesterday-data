## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, the true bottleneck shifts from the technology to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The recent releases of Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1 have made it clear that AI can now do almost anything—write books, animate, research—but the limiting factor is no longer the model or even the quota. It's the human's own mental bandwidth, metabolic limits, and clock time. Delegating to multiple AI agents doesn't solve this because the verification and enjoyment of results still require non-fungible executive function. This creates a new 'problem of choice' and a kind of FOMO, but the real lesson, drawn from the quarter-life crisis, is that you can do anything but not everything. The solution is to minmax: consciously allocate your time, energy, and tokens to a few projects that align with your values, rather than spreading thin across a sprawl of possibilities. Quality over quantity becomes the guiding principle, and even playful exploration should be a conscious choice, not a default.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate tasks to multiple AI agents, but the bottleneck remains your own executive function for verification and enjoyment.
- There is merit in just trying things for fun or challenge, but it must be a conscious value-aligned choice, not an automatic indulgence.
- Some people still run out of quota, but for most, the bottleneck is no longer the account or intelligence—it's the human.
