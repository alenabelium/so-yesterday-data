## Thesis

As AI models grow more capable, human time, attention, and executive function—not model intelligence—become the true bottlenecks, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological and practical. With models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1, the option space explodes—you can write a book, animate a manga, or research anything—but the bottleneck shifts to your own metabolic and cognitive limits. Delegation to agents doesn't solve it: reviewing and enjoying results requires non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without engaging them is just token spend. This mirrors the quarter-life crisis: realizing you can do anything but not everything. The response is to minmax—allocate your finite time, energy, and tokens to three or four projects that align with your values and yield the most satisfaction. Quality over quantity becomes the rule, especially as AI turns slop into a fire hose. But this isn't a call for pure productivity; conscious value alignment matters. For those with ADHD or many interests, AI can induce a kind of psychosis by indulging every whim. The discipline is to choose deliberately.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to AI agents to handle multiple projects simultaneously.
- There is merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself, not only productive work.
- You decide your own values, so why restrict yourself to a few goals?
