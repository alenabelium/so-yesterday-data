## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, the true bottleneck shifts from intelligence and quota to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use around a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The release of Opus 5.5 and other models has created an inflection point where quota is no longer the limiting factor for most people. The real constraint is the human: you can delegate generation, but verifying, enjoying, and using the output requires non-fungible executive function. This mirrors the 'quarter life crisis'—the realization that you can do anything but not everything. The solution is to minmax: consciously allocate your time, energy, and tokens to the few projects that align with your values and give the most return, rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity is the TL;DR. This doesn't preclude playful exploration, but it demands conscious value alignment to avoid being overwhelmed by endless possibilities.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents to work asynchronously, but verification and enjoyment still require your executive function.
- There is merit in just seeing if you can do something, even if it's not productive—but you must be conscious about whether it satisfies your higher-order values.
- Some people still run out of quota (e.g., multiple accounts), but for most, the bottleneck is no longer the account.
