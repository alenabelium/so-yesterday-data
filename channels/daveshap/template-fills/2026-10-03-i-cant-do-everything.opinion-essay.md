## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function become the true bottlenecks, forcing deliberate minmaxing over endless possibilities.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: as models like Opus 5.5 and Astra become more capable, the option space explodes, triggering analysis paralysis. You can now imagine turning a novel into a manga, animating it, or writing a sequel to Cyberpunk 2077—but each path demands your scarce hours and mental bandwidth. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve it; verification and enjoyment still require non-fungible executive function. Without that, outputs are just 'pointless token spend.' This is the quarter life crisis redux: 'you can do anything, but you can't do everything.' The resolution is minmaxing—deliberately concentrating AI use on a few high-value goals that align with your values, rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity, not slop fire hose. Even play has merit, but it must be conscious: ask if this satisfies your higher-order values or just feeds a billion interests. For ADHD minds, AI can indulge every whim, risking 'AI psychosis.' The bottleneck is you—your metabolism, your sleep-wake cycle, your clock time.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents (e.g., Grockbot, ChatGPT) to parallelize work.
- There's merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself, not everything must be productive.
- You decide your own values, so why impose a minmaxing framework?
- Some people still run out of quota, so the bottleneck isn't universal.
