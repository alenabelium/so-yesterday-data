## Thesis

As AI models grow more capable, the true bottleneck shifts from intelligence to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is the psychological 'problem of choice': with models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1, the option space explodes—books, manga, animations, research—but the day still has only so many hours. Delegation to agents doesn't dissolve the bottleneck because verification and enjoyment demand non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without engaging them is just token spend. This is a replay of the quarter-life crisis: 'you can do anything, but you can't do everything.' The resolution is minmaxing—concentrating time, energy, and quota on three or four projects that align with your values, not spreading thin. Quality over quantity, and conscious value alignment, are the antidotes to the slop fire hose and AI-induced FOMO.

## Counterpoints

- Some people still run out of quota, but most mortals don't anymore.
- There are still things AI can't do well, and a thousand times more quota would help.
- There's merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself, not only productive work.
- You decide your own values, but you must be conscious about how you spend time.
