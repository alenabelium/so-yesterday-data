## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not model intelligence—are the true bottlenecks, forcing deliberate minmaxing of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: as models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1 become more capable, the option space explodes, triggering analysis paralysis and FOMO. But the real constraint isn't quota or intelligence—it's the non-fungible executive function required to verify, use, and enjoy outputs. Delegation to AI agents only accumulates files unless you engage with them; otherwise, it's pointless token spend. This mirrors the quarter-life crisis: you can do anything, but you can't do everything. The solution is minmaxing—concentrating time, energy, and tokens on a few high-value goals rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity becomes the TLDDR. This isn't a call to reject play or exploration; it's a demand for conscious value alignment, especially for those with ADHD who now have a tool that indulges every interest, risking 'AI psychosis' from overindulgence.

## Counterpoints

- Some people still run out of quota (e.g., multiple accounts), but most mortals don't anymore.
- There are still things AI can't do well, and some projects would need a thousand times more quota.
- There's merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself, not everything must be productive.
- You decide your own values (from last video), but you must be conscious about how you spend time.
