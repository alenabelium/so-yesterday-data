## Thesis

As AI models grow more capable, the true bottleneck shifts from model intelligence to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate minmaxing of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological and practical. With models like Opus 5.5, the option space explodes—you can write a book, animate a manga, or research anything. But each option demands your finite hours and mental bandwidth. Delegation to agents doesn't solve it: verifying and enjoying results requires non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without engaging them is just token spend. This creates FOMO, but quota is no longer the constraint for most; you are. The quarter-life crisis parallels this: realizing you can do anything but not everything. The solution is minmaxing—deliberately allocating your time, energy, and tokens to three or four projects that align with your values and yield the most satisfaction. Quality over quantity. This isn't a call to abandon play or exploration; it's a call to conscious value alignment. For those with ADHD or a billion interests, AI can become a psychosis-inducing indulgence, but the discipline of choosing what you'll actually read, publish, or enjoy keeps the fire hose manageable.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents to parallelize work, but verification and enjoyment still require your executive function.
- There's merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself; not everything must be productive, but you must be conscious of your values.
- Some people still run out of quota, but for most, the bottleneck is no longer the account—it's your time and attention.
