## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not AI—are the true bottlenecks, forcing a deliberate minmaxing of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: as models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1 get more capable, the range of possible projects explodes, triggering analysis paralysis and FOMO. But the bottleneck isn't quota or intelligence—it's your own metabolic clock and the non-fungible executive function needed to verify and enjoy results. Delegating to AI just accumulates files unless you engage with them. This mirrors the 'quarter life crisis' millennials faced: realizing you can do anything but not everything. The solution is minmaxing—deliberately focusing your time, energy, and tokens on a few high-value goals rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity, and conscious value alignment, prevent the 'slop fire hose' and the AI psychosis that comes from indulging every interest.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents, but verification and enjoyment still require your executive function.
- Some people still run out of quota, but for most, the bottleneck is no longer the account.
- AI can't do everything yet, and even with unlimited tokens, some tasks remain infeasible.
