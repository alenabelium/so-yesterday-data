## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not model intelligence—become the true bottleneck, forcing deliberate minmaxing of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological and practical. As models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1 get more capable, the range of possible projects explodes—books, animations, research, games—but the human capacity to verify, enjoy, and use outputs remains fixed. You can delegate generation to AI agents, but the executive function to review and integrate results is not fungible. Without that, output is just 'pointless token spend.' This is the 'problem of choice'—analysis paralysis from too many options. The bottleneck shifts from quota or intelligence to your own metabolism and sleep-wake cycle. This mirrors the 'quarter life crisis' millennials faced: realizing you can do anything but not everything. The solution is minmaxing—deliberately concentrating time, energy, and tokens on a few high-value goals rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity. But this isn't a call for pure productivity; there's merit in playful exploration. The key is conscious value alignment: ask if this activity satisfies your higher-order values or just feeds a dopamine loop. For those with ADHD or many interests, AI can become a fire hose of slop, even inducing 'AI psychosis' by indulging every whim. The discipline is to choose.

## Counterpoints

- Some people still run out of quota (e.g., multiple Pro accounts), but most don't—the bottleneck is no longer the account.
- There are still things AI can't do well, and some projects would need a thousand times more quota.
- You might argue for well-roundedness over minmaxing, but the host counters with the need for conscious value alignment.
- Playful exploration has merit, but it must be weighed against higher-order values.
