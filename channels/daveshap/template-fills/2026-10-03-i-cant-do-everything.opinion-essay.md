## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, human time, attention, and executive function become the true bottlenecks, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: the 'problem of choice'—more options lead to analysis paralysis. With models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, I can now do almost anything, but I only have so many hours and so much mental bandwidth. Delegation helps, but verifying and enjoying results requires executive function that is not fungible. Without that, it's just 'pointless token spend.'

This is a new quarter life crisis. In my 20s, I realized I could do anything but not everything; now AI recreates that feeling at scale. The lesson is the same: you have to minmax. In RPGs, you optimize a character around specific traits; for AI, you put your time, energy, and tokens into three or four things that give the most bang for your buck.

Quality over quantity is the TL;DR. Slop is nothing new—AI just makes it a fire hose. So ask: Am I actually going to read this book? Publish it? Enjoy this game? The bottleneck is not quota or intelligence; it's you—your metabolism, your sleep-wake cycle, your clock time.

## Counterpoints

- There is merit in just seeing if you can do something, just challenging yourself, just fussing around.
- You decide your own values, but you have to be conscious about those values—is this how you want to spend this time?
- For ADHD folks with a billion interests, AI can indulge every one, which is why some people get AI psychosis.
