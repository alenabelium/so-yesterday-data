## Thesis

AI's exploding capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not model intelligence or quota—are the true bottlenecks, forcing deliberate minmaxing.

## Argument

The mechanism is the 'problem of choice': as models like Opus 5.5 and Astra get more capable, the option space explodes, but the host's lived experience over the last few weeks shows that 'you can do anything but you can't do everything.' The bottleneck isn't the account or even intelligence—it's metabolic clock time and non-fungible executive function needed to verify and enjoy results. Delegation to agents just accumulates files unless you apply that scarce attention. This mirrors the 'quarter life crisis' lesson: potential is infinite, means are finite. The host's prescription is minmaxing—deliberately concentrating AI use on three or four high-value projects that align with your conscious values, not spreading thin. Quality over quantity is the TL;DR; otherwise AI becomes a 'slop fire hose.' Even play has merit, but only if it's a conscious value choice, not an ADHD-driven indulgence that leads to 'AI psychosis.'

## Counterpoints

- There is merit in just seeing if you can do something, just challenging yourself, just fussing around—not everything must be productive.
- Some people still run out of quota (e.g., jokers with multiple ProMax accounts), so the bottleneck isn't universal yet.
- The host admits he's often 3–6–12 months ahead of others; your lived experience may differ, so take this with a grain of salt.
