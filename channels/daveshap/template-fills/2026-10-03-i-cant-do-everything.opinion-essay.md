## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, the true bottleneck in creative work shifts from model intelligence to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The recent release of Opus 5.5 and other advanced models has created an inflection point where quota is no longer the limiting factor for most people. This amplifies the 'problem of choice'—with endless possibilities, analysis paralysis sets in. You can delegate generation to AI, but the verification and enjoyment of results require non-fungible executive function. This mirrors the 'quarter life crisis' of realizing you can do anything but not everything. The solution is to minmax: consciously allocate your time, energy, and tokens to a few projects that align with your values and give the highest return. Quality over quantity is the TL;DR, but this doesn't preclude playful exploration—just be conscious of whether it serves your higher-order values.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate tasks to AI agents, but the executive function to review and enjoy results is not fungible.
- Some people still run out of quota, but for most, the bottleneck has shifted to human time and attention.
- There is merit in just seeing if you can do something, but you must be conscious about whether it aligns with your values.
