## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not model intelligence—become the true bottleneck, forcing a deliberate minmaxing of AI use toward high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: as models like Opus 5.5 and Astra become more capable, the sheer range of possible projects triggers analysis paralysis. The host describes realizing 'I could do anything'—write a book, animate a novel, or create a manga—but quickly hits the hard limits of hours in a day and mental bandwidth. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve this because verifying and enjoying the output requires non-fungible executive function; otherwise, it's just 'pointless token spend.'

This echoes the 'quarter life crisis' of the 20s, where the realization that 'you can do anything, but you can't do everything' forces a pivot toward focus. The host argues that AI is creating a second such crisis, but with a twist: the bottleneck isn't education or money, but your own metabolism and sleep-wake cycle. The solution is 'minmaxing'—deliberately choosing a few high-value projects that align with your conscious values, rather than spreading effort thin. Quality over quantity is the TL;DR.

Crucially, this isn't a call for pure productivity. The host acknowledges the merit of 'just fussing around' and challenging yourself, but insists on conscious value alignment: ask if an activity satisfies your higher-order values or just feeds a dopamine loop. For those with ADHD or a 'billion interests,' AI can become a dangerous enabler, indulging every whim and potentially contributing to 'AI psychosis.' The takeaway is to treat AI as a tool for depth, not breadth.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to AI agents (e.g., Grockbot, ChatGPT) to handle tasks asynchronously, but the verification and enjoyment time still requires your executive function.
- Some people still run out of quota (e.g., 'two Chat GPT Promax accounts and five Clot accounts'), but for most, the bottleneck is no longer the account.
- There is merit in just seeing if you can do something, challenging yourself, or fussing around—not everything must be productive.
- The host's own previous video argued that you decide your own values, which might seem to conflict with the call to focus—but the resolution is to be conscious about those values.
