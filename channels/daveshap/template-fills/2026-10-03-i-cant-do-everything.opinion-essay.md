## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not model intelligence—become the true bottleneck, forcing deliberate minmaxing of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: as models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1 get more capable, the option space explodes, triggering analysis paralysis. You can delegate generation to agents, but verification and enjoyment still demand non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without engaging them is pointless token spend. This mirrors the quarter-life crisis—realizing you can do anything but not everything. The solution is minmaxing: consciously allocate time, energy, and tokens to a few high-value goals rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity, and align with your own values, not FOMO. Even playful exploration is fine if it's a conscious choice.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents to parallelize work.
- There's merit in just seeing if you can do something, even if unproductive.
- Some people still run out of quota, so the bottleneck isn't universal.
