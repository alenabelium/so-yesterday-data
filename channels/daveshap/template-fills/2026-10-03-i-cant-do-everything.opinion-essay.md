## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, the true bottleneck in creative and knowledge work shifts from the AI's abilities to the human's time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value.

## Argument

The mechanism is the psychological 'problem of choice'—more options lead to analysis paralysis. With models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, the host can now pursue any project, but the limiting factor is no longer quota or intelligence; it's the human's metabolic and cognitive capacity. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve this because the executive function to review, enjoy, and use the output is not fungible. This creates a 'quarter life crisis' for AI users: you can do anything, but you can't do everything. The solution is minmaxing—deliberately allocating time, energy, and tokens to a few high-value projects rather than spreading thin. This requires conscious value alignment: asking whether a project satisfies higher-order values or is just 'slop.' The host acknowledges that playful exploration has merit, but the key is intentionality, especially for those with ADHD who may be prone to 'AI psychosis' from having every interest indulged.

## Counterpoints

- Some people still run out of quota, like those with multiple accounts, but most mortals don't anymore.
- There are still things AI can't do well, and some projects would need a thousand times more quota.
- The host's own previous video argued you decide your own values, so why impose a productivity filter?
- There is merit in just seeing if you can do something, even if it's not productive.
