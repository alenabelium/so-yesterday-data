## Thesis

As AI models grow more capable, the real bottleneck shifts from model intelligence to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate minmaxing of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: the 'problem of choice'—more options lead to analysis paralysis. With models like Opus 5.5 and Astra, the host realizes he could do anything, but only has so many hours and mental bandwidth. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve it because verification and enjoyment require non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without using them is 'pointless token spend.' This is a new 'quarter life crisis'—the realization that you can do anything but not everything. The solution is minmaxing: deliberately focusing AI use on a few high-value goals that align with your values, rather than spreading effort thin. Quality over quantity is the TLDDR. Even playful exploration is fine, but you must be conscious about whether it satisfies your higher-order values. For ADHD folks, AI can indulge every interest, which is why some experience 'AI psychosis.'

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents, but verification and enjoyment still require your executive function.
- There's merit in just seeing if you can do something, even if it's not productive.
- You decide your own values, but you must be conscious about how you spend your time.
