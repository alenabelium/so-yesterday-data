## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not model intelligence—become the true bottleneck, forcing deliberate minmaxing.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: as models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1 get more capable, the option space explodes, triggering analysis paralysis and FOMO. But the real constraint isn't the model—it's your own metabolic and cognitive limits. You can delegate generation to agents, but verification, enjoyment, and use all require non-fungible executive function. Without that, output is just 'pointless token spend.'

This mirrors the quarter-life crisis: realizing you can do anything but not everything. The lesson is minmaxing—optimizing your AI use around a few high-value goals rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity becomes the TL;DR. You must ask: will I actually read, publish, or enjoy this? That's conscious value alignment, not just productivity.

Even play has merit—fussing around is fine—but you must be conscious about whether it serves your higher-order values. For ADHD minds with a billion interests, AI can indulge every whim, which is why some people spiral into 'AI psychosis.' The fix is deliberate focus.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents, but verification and enjoyment still require your executive function.
- Some people still run out of quota, but most mortal humans no longer face that bottleneck.
- There's merit in just seeing if you can do something, even if it's not productive.
- You decide your own values, but you must be conscious about how you spend your time.
