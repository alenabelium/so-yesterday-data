## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, human time, attention, and executive function—not AI capability—are the true bottlenecks, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use around a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is the psychological 'problem of choice': with models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, the option space explodes, but human bandwidth doesn't. You can delegate generation, but verification and enjoyment require non-fungible executive function. Without it, output is just 'pointless token spend.' This mirrors the quarter-life crisis—realizing you can do anything but not everything—and the lesson is to minmax: allocate time, energy, and tokens to the three or four things that give the most bang for the buck. Quality over quantity is the TL;DR. This isn't a call to only be productive; it's a call to be conscious about values, asking if this use satisfies higher-order values or just feeds a billion ADHD interests. AI can now indulge every interest, which is why some people get 'AI psychosis.' The bottleneck is you: your intellect, your metabolism, your sleep-wake cycle.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents, but verification and enjoyment still require executive function.
- There's merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself, not everything must be productive.
- You decide your own values, but you must be conscious about whether your time use aligns with them.
