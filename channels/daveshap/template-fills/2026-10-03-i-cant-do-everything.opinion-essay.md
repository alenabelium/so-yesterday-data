## Thesis

As AI models grow more capable, human time, attention, and executive function—not AI capability—become the true bottleneck, forcing a deliberate minmaxing of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is the psychological 'problem of choice'—more options lead to analysis paralysis. With recent models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, I can now do almost anything, but I only have so many hours and mental bandwidth. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve this because verifying and enjoying results requires non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without engaging them is just pointless token spend. This creates FOMO, but the real bottleneck shifts from quota to my own metabolism and sleep-wake cycle. This mirrors the 'quarter life crisis'—realizing you can do anything but not everything. The lesson is to minmax: optimize AI use around specific traits and goals, putting resources into three or four high-impact projects rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity, and conscious value alignment, prevent being overwhelmed by endless possibilities.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to AI agents, but verification and enjoyment still require your executive function.
- Some people still run out of quota, but for most, the bottleneck is no longer the account.
- AI can't do everything yet, and some tasks would need a thousand times more quota.
- Humans have always produced slop; AI just makes it a fire hose.
