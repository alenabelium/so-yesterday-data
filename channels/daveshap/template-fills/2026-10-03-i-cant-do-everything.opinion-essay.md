## Thesis

As AI models grow more capable, the true bottleneck shifts from model intelligence to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmax' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is the psychological 'problem of choice': with models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, the option space explodes—write a book, animate a manga, build a game—but each option demands scarce human hours and mental bandwidth. Delegation to agents doesn't solve it; verification and enjoyment still require non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without engaging them is 'pointless token spend.' This is a new inflection point: quota is no longer the constraint for most, so the bottleneck is you—your intellect, metabolism, and sleep-wake cycle. The lesson echoes the 'quarter life crisis' of your 20s: you can do anything, but not everything. The response is to minmax—consciously align AI use with your higher-order values, prioritizing quality over quantity. Even playful exploration is fine, but it must be a deliberate choice, not a FOMO-driven scatter. For those with ADHD, AI's ability to indulge every interest can spiral into 'AI psychosis.' The discipline is to ask: will I actually read, publish, or enjoy this?

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple agents (Grockbot, ChatGPT) to parallelize work.
- There is merit in just seeing if you can do something, even if it's not productive.
- You decide your own values, so why impose a minmax framework?
- Some people still run out of quota, so the bottleneck isn't universal.
