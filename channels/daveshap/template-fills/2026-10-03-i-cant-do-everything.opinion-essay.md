## Thesis

As AI models grow more capable, the true bottleneck shifts from intelligence to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use around a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is the 'problem of choice'—with models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, the option space explodes, but human bandwidth doesn't. You can delegate generation, but verification and enjoyment demand non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without using them is just token spend. This creates FOMO, but quota is no longer the constraint for most; you are. It's a quarter-life crisis redux: 'you can do anything, but you can't do everything.' The solution is minmaxing—concentrating time, energy, and tokens on three or four high-impact projects rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity, and conscious value alignment, prevent slop and overwhelm. AI's ability to indulge every interest is why some experience 'AI psychosis.'

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents, but verification and enjoyment still require your executive function.
- Some people still run out of quota, but for most, the bottleneck is no longer the account.
- There are still things AI can't do well, and some tasks would need a thousand times more quota.
