## Thesis

AI's exploding capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not model intelligence—are the true bottlenecks, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is the 'problem of choice': as models like Opus 5.5 and Astra get more capable, the option space explodes, but human verification and enjoyment are non-fungible. You can delegate generation, but reviewing results still costs executive function. This is a new 'quarter life crisis'—'you can do anything, but you can't do everything.' The resolution is minmaxing: consciously choose a few projects that align with your values, prioritizing quality over quantity. Even 'fussing around' is fine, but it must be a conscious value choice, not FOMO-driven token spend.

## Counterpoints

- Some users still hit quota limits, so the bottleneck isn't universal yet.
- There's merit in just seeing if you can do something—play and challenge are valid.
- You can delegate to multiple AI agents asynchronously, spreading the load.
