## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time and executive function, not model intelligence, become the true bottleneck, forcing deliberate minmaxing.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological. As models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1 get more capable, the sheer number of possible projects—books, animations, research—overwhelms the mind, leading to analysis paralysis. The bottleneck isn't the AI's intelligence or even quota; it's your own metabolic limits, sleep-wake cycle, and the non-fungible executive function required to verify and enjoy outputs. Delegation to multiple bots only accumulates files; without your attention, it's pointless token spend. This mirrors the 'quarter life crisis' of your 20s, where you realize you can do anything but not everything. The solution is minmaxing: deliberately allocate your time, energy, and tokens to a few high-value goals that align with your values, rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity—ask if you'll actually read, publish, or enjoy the output. This forces a conscious re-evaluation of how you use AI, especially for those with ADHD and a billion interests, where AI can indulge every whim, potentially leading to 'AI psychosis.'

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents to handle different tasks.
- There is merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself, not everything must be productive.
- Some people still run out of quota, so the bottleneck might be account limits for some.
