## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, the true bottleneck shifts from intelligence to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: the 'problem of choice'—more options lead to analysis paralysis. With models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, I can now do anything: write a book, turn a novel into a manga, animate, research. But I only have so many hours and mental bandwidth. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve it—verification and enjoyment require non-fungible executive function. Without that, outputs are just 'pointless token spend.'

This is a new inflection point: quota is no longer the bottleneck for most mortals. The bottleneck is metabolic and temporal—your sleep-wake cycle, your clock time. This forces a re-evaluation of AI use, echoing the 'quarter life crisis' millennials faced: realizing you can do anything but not everything. The lesson is to minmax—optimize your character (and your AI usage) around specific traits, putting all resources into three or four things that give the most bang for the buck.

Quality over quantity is the TL;DR. Instead of producing slop (AI just turns it into a fire hose), focus on what you actually care about and will actually use. This requires conscious value alignment—asking if this satisfies higher-order values. For those with ADHD and a billion interests, AI can indulge every one, which is why some experience 'AI psychosis.' The solution is deliberate focus, not endless sprawl.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to AI agents, but verification and enjoyment still require executive function that isn't fungible.
- There's FOMO about not using all your quota, but with models like Opus 5.5, most people aren't running out anymore.
- There's merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself, not everything must be productive.
- You decide your own values (from my last video), but you still have to be conscious about how you spend your time.
