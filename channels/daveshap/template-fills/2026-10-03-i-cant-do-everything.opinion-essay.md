## Thesis

As AI models grow more capable, the true bottleneck in creative and knowledge work shifts from model intelligence to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological and practical. With models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, the option space explodes—you can write a book, animate a comic, or research anything. But the bottleneck isn't the model or even the quota; it's your own metabolic clock and executive function. Delegating to multiple agents just accumulates files unless you verify and enjoy the results, which is non-fungible. This creates a 'problem of choice' and FOMO, but the real lesson is the quarter-life crisis: you can do anything, but you can't do everything. So you must minmax—put your time, energy, and tokens into three or four high-value goals rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity is the TL;DR. This isn't a call to abandon play or exploration; it's a call to be conscious about your values and whether each project feeds your higher-order goals. For those with ADHD or a billion interests, AI can become a slop fire hose, indulging every whim—hence 'AI psychosis.' The solution is deliberate focus.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents to parallelize work, but verification and enjoyment still require your own executive function.
- There's merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself; not everything must be productive, but you should be conscious of your values.
- Some people still run out of quota, but for most, the bottleneck is no longer the account—it's human time and attention.
