## Thesis

AI's growing capability creates a 'problem of choice' where human time, attention, and executive function—not model intelligence—become the true bottleneck, forcing deliberate minmaxing of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological and practical. As models like Opus 5.5 and Soul 6.1 become more capable, the option space explodes—you can write a book, animate a manga, or research anything. But the bottleneck shifts: you can delegate generation, yet verification, enjoyment, and integration all require non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without using them is just 'pointless token spend.' This mirrors the quarter-life crisis: realizing you can do anything but not everything. The solution is minmaxing—deliberately allocating time, tokens, and cognitive resources to a few high-value goals rather than spreading thin. Quality over quantity becomes the TL;DR. This isn't a call to abandon play or exploration; it's a call to conscious value alignment, asking whether a given activity satisfies higher-order values or just feeds the slop fire hose.

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate tasks to different AI agents, but reviewing results still requires your executive function.
- There's merit in just fiddling around and challenging yourself, not everything must be productive.
- You decide your own values, but you must be conscious about how you spend your time relative to those values.
