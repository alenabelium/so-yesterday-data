## Thesis

As AI models become more capable, the true bottleneck shifts from technology to human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use.

## Argument

The mechanism is psychological: the 'problem of choice'—analysis paralysis from too many options—intensifies as models like Opus 5.5 and Astra make nearly any project feasible. You can delegate generation to AI, but verification and enjoyment demand non-fungible executive function. Accumulating files without engaging them is 'pointless token spend.' This mirrors the 'quarter life crisis'—realizing you can do anything but not everything. The solution is 'minmaxing': consciously aligning AI use with a few high-value goals, prioritizing quality over quantity. This isn't a call for pure productivity; it's about conscious value alignment, especially for those with ADHD, where AI can indulge every interest, risking 'AI psychosis.'

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents, but executive function to review results is not fungible.
- Some people still run out of quota, but for most, the bottleneck is no longer the account.
- There is merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself, not only productive tasks.
- You decide your own values, but you must be conscious about whether your time aligns with higher-order values.
