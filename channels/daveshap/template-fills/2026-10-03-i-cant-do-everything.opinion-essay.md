## Thesis

With AI models becoming capable enough to make nearly any project possible, the true bottleneck is no longer intelligence or quota but human time, attention, and executive function, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use around a few.

## Argument

The mechanism is the psychological 'problem of choice'—more options lead to analysis paralysis. With recent models like Opus 5.5, Astra, and Soul 6.1, the host finds he could do anything: write a book, turn a novel into a manga, animate, and more. But hours and mental bandwidth are finite. Delegation to AI agents doesn't solve the bottleneck because reviewing and enjoying results requires non-fungible executive function. Without that, outputs are just 'pointless token spend.' The host draws a parallel to the 'quarter life crisis,' where the lesson is 'you can do anything, but you can't do everything.' AI and automation recreate that crisis at scale. The solution is 'minmaxing'—consciously choosing a few projects that align with your values and that you'll actually use or enjoy. Quality over quantity is the TL;DR. This doesn't mean only doing productive things; there's merit in fussing around. But you must be conscious about whether an activity satisfies your higher-order values. For ADHD minds with a billion interests, AI can indulge every whim, which may explain 'AI psychosis.'

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to AI agents, but executive function is not fungible—you still must review and enjoy results.
- Some people still run out of quota, but most mortal humans no longer face that limit.
- There are still things AI can't do well, and some projects would need a thousand times more quota.
- The host's own previous video argued you decide your own values, so why restrict yourself?—but you must be conscious about those values.
