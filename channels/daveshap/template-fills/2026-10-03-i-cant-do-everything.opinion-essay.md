## Thesis

AI's expanding capability makes human time, attention, and executive function the true bottleneck, forcing a deliberate 'minmaxing' of AI use toward a few high-value goals.

## Argument

The mechanism is the shift from scarcity to abundance. With models like Opus 5.5, quota is no longer the limiting factor for most people; the bottleneck is now the human—our metabolic limits, sleep-wake cycles, and the non-fungible executive function required to verify and enjoy AI output. Without that human engagement, AI-generated work is just 'pointless token spend.' This forces a re-evaluation, echoing the 'quarter life crisis' where you realize you can do anything but not everything. The solution is minmaxing: deliberately concentrating time, energy, and tokens on a few high-impact projects rather than spreading thin across a sprawl of possibilities. Quality over quantity becomes the TLDDR, and you must consciously ask if a project aligns with your higher-order values. This is especially critical for those with ADHD, where AI can indulge every interest, potentially leading to 'AI psychosis.'

## Counterpoints

- You can delegate to multiple AI agents, but verification and enjoyment still require non-fungible executive function.
- There is merit in just fussing around and challenging yourself, not everything must be productive.
- You decide your own values, but you must be conscious about whether your time aligns with those values.
