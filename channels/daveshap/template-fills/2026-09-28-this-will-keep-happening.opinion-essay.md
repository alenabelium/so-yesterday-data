## Thesis

AI 'doomers' are performing theater rather than presenting serious evidence-based concerns about artificial intelligence risks.

## Argument

The core issue is **[vranyo]**—a Russian concept for an outright lie that everyone knows is false but pretends to be true. Doomers claim AI poses an existential threat requiring us to stop all progress, yet they offer no experimental evidence, relying only on logic about future systems that don't exist. Their behavior contradicts their stated fears; instead of demanding we bomb data centers, they are excited by recent releases like Astra, GPT-4o Astra, and Opus 3.5. This suggests they know their rhetoric is **[silly]** theater.

Technologically, we are witnessing **[spillover-effect]** and **[threshold-effects]**. As models improve, they reach points where capabilities jump discretely rather than smoothly. For example, Opus 5.5 represents a saltatory jump in creating animation, CGI, and 3D graphics, surpassing most human professionals. This mirrors historical tech evolutions: computers moved from calculating firing trajectories to general use; the internet evolved from text to streaming. Each threshold brings new opportunities that take time to realize. We are currently at such a point where AI can write code and do math better than 99.99% of people, despite earlier limitations like hallucinations.

If doomers truly believed the world was ending, their demeanor might be gallows humor or arrogance. Instead, their feigned modesty and excitement indicate they are playing a game for the public. We should expect these threshold effects to keep happening, rendering the doomer narrative increasingly detached from reality.

## Counterpoints

- Doomers may be using gallows humor or arrogance because they sincerely believe everyone is going to die soon.
- The only logical solution if AI is uncontrollable is to stop all development immediately.
