## Thesis

AI 'doomers' are performing theater rather than presenting serious evidence, as their own excitement over recent model releases contradicts their existential warnings.

## Argument

The core argument is that the rhetoric of AI doomers is disconnected from reality. They claim to know future AI will be uncontrollable but offer no experimental evidence, relying instead on logic about non-existent systems. This is 'vranyo'—a known lie everyone pretends to accept. The industry's genuine excitement over releases like Astra, GPT-4o Astra, and Opus 3.5/5.5 proves that even these critics are engaged in the technology for fun, not fear.

Technologically, we are witnessing 'spillover effects' and 'saltatory jumps.' Like early computers moving from calculation to general use, or the internet evolving from text to streaming, AI capabilities jump discretely across thresholds. Opus 5.5’s ability to create animation and CGI surpasses most professionals instantly. While the underlying evolution is smooth, the impact is a threshold effect: like a cup filling up until it suddenly spills. We are at such a point where models now handle code and math better than 99.99% of people, creating new opportunities that previous versions lacked. The doomers' inaction during these jumps confirms they don't believe their own warnings.

## Counterpoints

- Doomers might be using gallows humor or arrogance to cope with a sincere belief that the world is ending soon.
- The 'theater' of doom might be a necessary social performance to force attention on risks everyone knows are exaggerated.
