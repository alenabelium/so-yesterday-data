## Thesis

AI 'doomers' are performing theater rather than presenting serious evidence-based concerns about artificial intelligence risks.

## Argument

The core argument rests on the disconnect between the rhetoric of AI doomers and their actual behavior. Shapiro identifies a concept in Russian called "vranyo"—an outright lie that everyone knows is a lie but pretends to be true—to describe how doomers predict existential threats without experimental evidence, relying instead on logic about future models they don't understand. Despite claiming the only solution is to stop development, these same individuals exhibit excitement and amusement when new models like Astra, GPT-4o Astra, and Opus 3.5 are released, rather than escalating their rhetoric to demand immediate shutdowns of data centers.

Shapiro argues that this affect is inappropriate for people who genuinely believe the world is ending, suggesting they do not take themselves seriously. Instead of existential fear, the industry is witnessing practical technological spillover effects. He explains that as technology reaches certain thresholds, it begins doing unexpected things, such as AI now surpassing the skills of most regular professionals in animation and CGI. These are saltatory jumps—discrete transitions where capabilities leap forward even if underlying evolution seems smooth. Just as early computers moved from calculating trajectories to general use, current AI models are creating new opportunities at each threshold, proving that the focus should be on these practical spillovers rather than fabricated doom.

## Counterpoints

- Doomers might be using gallows humor or satire because they sincerely believe everyone is going to die soon.
- The argument assumes doomers are lying; perhaps their logic about uncontrollability is valid even if their behavior is inconsistent.
