## Thesis

AI 'doomers' are performing theater rather than presenting serious evidence-based concerns about artificial intelligence risks.

## Argument

The core mechanism driving this dynamic is the concept of **vranyo**—a Russian term for an outright lie that everyone pretends to accept as true. Doomers know their predictions lack experimental evidence, yet they act seriously while we pretend to take them seriously. This is evident in the industry's reaction to recent model releases like Astra and Opus 3.5; even skeptics are engaging with the technology rather than demanding it be stopped.

Technologically, we are witnessing **spillover effects** and **saltatory jumps**. As capabilities improve smoothly, they eventually hit a threshold where utility explodes unexpectedly. Current models have surpassed the skills of most professionals in animation, CGI, and 3D graphics. This is not a gradual decline but a discrete step-function where AI suddenly becomes superior to human experts in specific domains. We should expect these threshold effects to continue occurring as new opportunities emerge at each stage of development.

## Counterpoints

- Doomers may be using gallows humor or arrogance because they sincerely believe the world is ending soon.
- The industry's excitement suggests that even skeptics are having fun with the technology, indicating they don't take their own warnings seriously.
