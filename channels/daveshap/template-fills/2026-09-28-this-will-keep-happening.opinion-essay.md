## Thesis

AI doomers are engaging in performative theater rather than presenting serious evidence-based concerns about artificial intelligence risks.

## Argument

The core mechanism driving this dynamic is the disconnect between the rhetoric of existential threat and the actual behavior of its proponents. Doomers claim that AI poses an uncontrollable danger, yet they offer no experimental evidence for their predictions, relying instead on logic about future systems that do not exist. Their proposed solution—stopping all development—is based on careful thought rather than data. Meanwhile, the industry reacts to new model releases like Astra and Opus 3.5 with genuine excitement, even among those who claim to fear AI. This suggests that doomers know their warnings are 'vranyo' (an outright lie) but pretend they are true to maintain a narrative.

Technologically, we are witnessing repeated 'spillover effects' and 'saltatory jumps.' As models improve, they reach threshold effects where capabilities suddenly surpass human professionals in specific domains like animation and code. These jumps occur discretely despite smooth underlying evolution. Each threshold brings new opportunities that the world must learn to utilize. Doomers ignore these practical realities, focusing instead on a fictionalized future catastrophe.

## Counterpoints

- Doomers may be using gallows humor or arrogance as a coping mechanism for genuine fear of imminent death.
- The industry's excitement might mask underlying risks that doomers are correctly identifying but poorly communicating.
