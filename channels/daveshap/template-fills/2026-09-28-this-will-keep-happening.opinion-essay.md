## Thesis

AI 'doomers' are performing theater rather than presenting serious evidence-based concerns about artificial intelligence risks.

## Argument

The core issue is that doomers engage in **[vranyo]**, a Russian concept for an outright lie where everyone knows it's false but pretends otherwise. They predict existential threats without experimental evidence, relying only on logic about future models they don't understand. Yet, when major releases like Astra and Opus 3.5 occur, even these critics show excitement rather than the urgency of stopping data centers. This suggests their fear is performative.

Technologically, we are witnessing **[spillover-effect]**s and **[threshold-effects]**. Just as early computers shifted from calculation to general use, AI capabilities jump discretely despite smooth underlying evolution. Opus 5.5 represents a saltatory jump where AI surpasses most human professionals in animation and CGI. These threshold effects create new opportunities that the world must learn to utilize. We should expect these jumps continuously, as seen in the progression from GPT-2 to GPT-6, where each stage overcomes previous limitations like hallucinations.

## Counterpoints

- Doomers may be using gallows humor or arrogance to cope with a sincere belief that the world is ending soon.
- The industry's excitement might mask genuine risks that are currently being ignored due to commercial incentives.
