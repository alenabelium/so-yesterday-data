## Thesis

AI 'doomers' are performing theater rather than presenting serious evidence-based concerns about artificial intelligence risks.

## Argument

The rhetoric surrounding AI existential threats is not a serious debate but a form of **[vranyo]**—a collective lie where everyone pretends the danger is real while knowing it isn't. This conclusion stems from the disconnect between the doomsayers' catastrophic predictions and their actual behavior; they do not demand immediate, drastic action like bombing data centers, but instead engage with new models for entertainment.

The industry's reaction to recent releases like **[astra]**, **[gpt-4o-astra]**, and **[opus-3-5]** (later referred to as **[opus-5-5]**) reveals the true nature of the technology: it is driven by **[spillover-effect]**s and **[threshold-effects]**. As technology improves, it reaches points where capabilities jump discretely, surpassing human professionals in areas like animation and CGI. These **[saltatory-jump]**s create new opportunities that emerge unexpectedly, much like water spilling over the edge of a filling cup.

While older models had known limitations, current versions can write code and perform math better than 99.99% of people. The excitement among accelerationists and the casual engagement of 'doomers' alike prove that the threat narrative is hollow. We should expect these threshold effects to continue, rendering the doomsayer stance increasingly absurd.

## Counterpoints

- Doomers may be using gallows humor or arrogance as a coping mechanism for genuine fear of imminent death.
- The logic used by doomers relies on predicting future AI behavior that doesn't exist yet and cannot be controlled.
