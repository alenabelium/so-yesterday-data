## Thesis

Recursive self-improvement is not a runaway superintelligence but the rising share of AI progress made by AI itself, and its real payoff will be formal verification unlocking breakthroughs across all sciences.

## Argument

The mechanism is the pie chart. Last year AI contributed maybe 5% of the code, data, and algorithms for the next generation; this year it's closer to 30%, and by 2027 it could be 50-80%. That's the definition of RSI: the percentage of the next generation of AI created by AI itself. It says nothing about speed, control, or alignment—so the doomsayer fantasy of a quick takeoff is wrong. Hardware can't improve that fast; a superintelligence can't live in the cloud or on a phone. It runs on real hardware somewhere, so the Skynet or Ultron escape-to-the-internet story is nonsense.

The real consequence is formal verification. That's the ability to prove beyond doubt that code, memory, materials, or cryptography works as intended. Today it's reserved for the NSA and NASA because it's extremely time-consuming and one of the rarest skills on the planet. But current closed models like Fable 5.5 and GPT Bell are probably already close to or capable of it. Once AI can do formal verification, it doesn't just make the next AI better—each level of that ability generalizes to other fields. Materials science, genetics, regenerative medicine, nuclear fusion, batteries: all are mathematically rich and will get a new boost. That's why people say what OpenAI did for math in 2026, we'll do for the rest of the sciences in 2027.
