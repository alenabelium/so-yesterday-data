## Thesis

Recursive self-improvement is not a runaway superintelligence but the rising share of AI progress generated by AI itself, and its real consequence is a cascade of breakthroughs across all sciences.

## Argument

The mechanism is the pie chart. Last year AI contributed 5% of the code, data, and algorithms for the next generation; this year it's closer to 30%, and by 2027 it will be 50-80%. That's what RSI actually means—not Skynet, not a quick takeoff. The hardware constraints alone make a runaway impossible: superintelligence can't live in the cloud or escape to the internet; it runs on real hardware somewhere. So the doomsayer fantasy is wrong.

The real consequence is formal verification. This is the ability to prove beyond doubt that code, materials, or cryptography work as intended—currently reserved for the NSA and NASA because it's so time-consuming and rare. But current closed models are already close to or capable of it. Once AI masters formal verification, it doesn't just make the next AI better; it generalizes to materials science, genetics, regenerative medicine, chips, fusion, batteries. OpenAI's math breakthroughs in 2026 will be applied to the rest of the sciences in 2027.

We're already seeing the side effects. Claude Code edited an entire episode and made all the animations for my post-work economy videos. These are threshold effects—emergentism. We've gone from an alphabet of primitives to sentences of AI capability, but we're not even at novels yet. The space of possibilities is about to explode.
