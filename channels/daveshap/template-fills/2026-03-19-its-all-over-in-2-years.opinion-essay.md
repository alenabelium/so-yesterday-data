## Thesis

Human intellectual labor will be rendered obsolete within two years by super-exponential AI growth, forcing a painful societal transition toward post-labor economics.

## Argument

The host argues that AI is undergoing a super-exponential buildout rivaling the Apollo and Manhattan projects, leading to cognitive hyperabundance where human brains can no longer contribute meaningfully. This is not just a tech shift but a convergence of the Fourth Turning, long-term debt cycles, and the fourth industrial revolution, upending all societal norms like schooling and retirement. While creative domains like novel-writing remain mathematically infinite and thus safe, rigorous intellectual work in economics, physics, and chemistry is already being surpassed by AI. The host frames his current work on post-labor economics as potentially his final significant contribution before this transition completes. The emotional core is 'vesperence' shifting to existential dread, as society burns out on compulsory labor and awaits the automation that obviates the need for human input. The future involves a major contraction of social networks and a weird, incoherent narrative as we rebuild from scratch, driven by the US-China AI race.

## Counterpoints

- Creative output like novels is mathematically infinite, so human creativity remains viable.
- Some argue humans still choose problems or provide taste, but the host calls these 'copes'.
- Historical phase changes are disorienting but inevitable, like rural-to-urban shifts.
