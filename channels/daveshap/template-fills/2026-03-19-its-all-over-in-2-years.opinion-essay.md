## Thesis

Human intellectual labor is ending within two years as AI's super-exponential growth renders cognitive contribution obsolete, leaving only unautomatable social and creative dimensions.

## Argument

The mechanism is the convergence of three historical forces: the Fourth Turning, long-term debt cycles, and the Fourth Industrial Revolution. This creates a unique disorientation where norms like schooling, employment, and retirement are upending simultaneously. The material driver is the industrial-scale buildout of data centers and GPU hardware, which accelerates model performance toward cognitive hyperabundance. As AI surpasses human capability in speed, cost, and safety, the value of human problem-solving drops to zero. The host argues that while we can cope with platitudes about 'choosing problems' or 'human taste,' the economic reality is that machines will do the heavy lifting. This leads to 'vesperance'—a wistful nostalgia for the ending era—and 'extinction rage' against compulsory labor. However, the host identifies a boundary: creativity and social dynamics cannot be automated because the space of possible novels exceeds the atoms in the universe. Thus, the host's work on post-labor economics may be their final rigorous contribution before the singularity reshapes society from scratch.

## Counterpoints

- AI will always leave room for human choice in problem selection and taste.
- Creative output like novels is mathematically infinite and cannot be fully automated.
- Social commentary and celebrity gossip will persist as long as humans exist.
- Historical disruptions eventually lead to reinvention, not just destruction.
