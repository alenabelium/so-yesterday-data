## Thesis

Human intellectual labor is ending within two years as AI achieves cognitive hyperabundance, rendering rigorous knowledge work obsolete and leaving only unautomatable creative and social dimensions.

## Argument

The mechanism is economic and physical: AI investment rivals the Apollo and Manhattan projects, driven by exponential GPU and model improvements. This leads to cognitive hyperabundance where human brains cannot compete. The host identifies a shift from 'vesperance' (nostalgia) to existential dread as norms like 'school, job, house' collapse under converging debt cycles and industrial revolutions. While machines will solve all rigorous problems—math, physics, chemistry, and coding—the value of human intellectual contribution drops to zero. However, dimensions of humanity that cannot be automated remain: the number of possible novels exceeds atoms in the universe, ensuring creative and social commentary persist. The host frames his current work on post-labor economics as potentially his final rigorous contribution before the singularity. The future involves a major contraction of social networks (Dunbar's number) and increasing weirdness as the US-China AI race reshapes global life, leaving individuals powerless over the currents but urging them to steer what they can.

## Counterpoints

- Copes like 'you still choose problems' or 'human taste' are dismissed as insufficient against machines that are better, faster, and cheaper.
- Creative output is not obsolete because the combinatorial space of novels and art is astronomically larger than the universe's atoms, making it unautomatable.
- Social norms like education and retirement are ending, but the host remains sanguine about technology pushing humanity through phase changes.
