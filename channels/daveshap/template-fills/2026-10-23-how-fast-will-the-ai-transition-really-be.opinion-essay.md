## Thesis

The AI transition will be faster than most expect, completing within 10-20 years, but it is unlikely to cause a total collapse.

## Argument

The speed of the transition is driven by the exponential acceleration of industrial revolutions. Each major technological shift has been faster than the last: the first industrial revolution spanned about 150 years, the second 50-70 years, and the third 30-40 years. AI is on track to double that pace, completing in 10-15 years, at most 20. This is not speculation but a historical pattern. The transition is already underway for some—software developers, writers, marketers—while others, like established professionals and niche content creators, remain insulated. The smoothness depends on power dynamics. Elites, who control capital and political influence, will protect their interests and bend rules to their advantage, as seen in the Russian aristocracy's resistance to modernization. This resistance could lead to turbulence, but history shows that previous industrial revolutions, despite their disruptions, did not cause collapse. The key is aligning elite incentives with broader societal interests, a challenge that will shape whether we end up in a cyberpunk dystopia or a solarpunk future.

## Counterpoints

- Some expect the transition to take 40 years or not happen at all.
- Accelerationists and collapse theorists believe a rapid, harsh transition is inevitable, possibly leading to civil war or societal breakdown.
- The main difference this time is speed, which could make the transition more turbulent than previous industrial revolutions.
- Elites may resist change even when it goes against their own interests, as seen in historical examples like the Russian aristocracy.
