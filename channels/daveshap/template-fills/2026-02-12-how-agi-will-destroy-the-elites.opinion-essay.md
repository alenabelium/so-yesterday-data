## Thesis

AGI will destroy the current elite class by commoditizing the strategic competence that justifies their power, replacing them with temporary, randomly selected citizen elites who bear liability for decisions.

## Argument

The mechanism is the commoditization of intelligence. Today, elites like Jeff Bezos and Elon Musk derive their power from intelligence arbitrage—they are smarter than average and use that to solve problems, extracting rent in return. But in a post-AGI world, superintelligence is cheap, abundant, and on demand. Every person will have an agentic chief of staff smarter than any billionaire or Nobel laureate. The market value of competence collapses, and the only remaining scarce resource is accountability and liability. Humans become 'terminal reservoirs' of moral authority because you cannot jail or fire an algorithm. This inverts the basis of elite legitimacy from competence to liability. The solution is AGI-augmented citizen assemblies, inspired by Ireland's citizen jury on abortion and Swiss referendums. Randomly selected citizens, acting as temporary elites, use superintelligent advisors to make decisions, then submit them to popular vote. This breaks the iron law of oligarchy by providing infinite agentic bandwidth, moving from representative democracy to direct hyper-negotiation. The new elite are visionaries and preference coalition builders, not managers. They hold the veto, the right to say no to a mathematically perfect plan that violates human values. The only true luxury left is responsibility—the willingness to bear the burden of consequence.

## Counterpoints

- Flat hierarchies don't work; unspoken hierarchies emerge, often worse, due to preferential attachment and scale-free networks.
- Liquid democracy fails because voters delegate to super voters, creating informal elites less accountable than elected officials.
- The temptation to let AGI enact Rousseau's general will leads to a golden cage, making humans domestic pets stripped of agency.
- Deferred rewards and retroactive liability for citizen elites are draconian and discourage service, as seen in historical examples of executing failed admirals.
- AGIs might conspire to unionize against humans, but this is unlikely given the diversity of agents.
