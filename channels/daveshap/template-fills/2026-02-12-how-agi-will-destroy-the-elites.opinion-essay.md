## Thesis

AGI destroys the elite by commoditizing competence, shifting the basis of power from strategic intelligence to moral liability and accountability.

## Argument

The current elite class derives power from **[[intelligence-arbitrage]]** and the management of complexity. In a post-AGI world, super intelligence becomes cheap and abundant, collapsing the market value of competence. When execution is costless, the scarcity shifts to **[[preference-aggregation]]** and the willingness to bear consequences. Humans become the "terminal reservoir" of liability because algorithms cannot be jailed or meaningfully punished. Therefore, the new elite will not be those with the best strategies, but those who serve as **[[moral-crumple-zones]]**, providing the human conscience and accountability that AGI lacks. This necessitates a shift from representative democracy to **[[direct-democracy]]** models, such as AGI-augmented citizen assemblies, where randomly selected citizens use super-intelligent advisors to make decisions, retaining the **[[human-in-the-loop]]** as the ultimate arbiter of values and the holder of the kill switch.

## Counterpoints

- Flat hierarchies fail due to scale-free networks and preferential attachment, inevitably creating informal elites.
- Liquid democracy fails because users delegate to super-voters, recreating oligarchic power structures.
- Retroactive liability for citizen jurors is draconian and may deter service; good-faith errors should not be punished.
- AGI optimizing for the general will without human checks reduces humanity to domestic pets stripped of agency.
