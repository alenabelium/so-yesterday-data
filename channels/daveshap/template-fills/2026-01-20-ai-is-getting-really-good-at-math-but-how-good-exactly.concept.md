## TL;DR

AI has shifted from pure scaling to inference-time search, achieving superhuman performance on math benchmarks via hybrid neurosymbolic systems. This paradigm commoditizes advanced reasoning, enabling formal verification of code and high-fidelity simulation of physics, effectively replacing experimental trial-and-error with provable certainty.

## The argument

### Paradigm Shift to Inference-Time Search
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:26', '00:05:16']
- **Claim**: The primary driver of AI math capability is no longer training data scaling, but inference-time compute and Monte Carlo tree search, allowing models to explore high-dimensional spaces dynamically.
- **Role**: definition

### Hybrid Neurosymbolic Architecture
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:07', '00:08:30']
- **Claim**: The breakthrough mechanism combines neural networks for creative intuition with symbolic engines (like Lean) for rigorous proof, creating a self-correcting loop that generates functionally unlimited high-quality synthetic data.
- **Role**: evidence

### From Approximation to Formal Verification
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:19', '00:17:56']
- **Claim**: Mathematical rigor applied to code and physics simulations replaces traditional testing, moving industries from 'ship and fix' to crash-proof standards where correctness is provable rather than observed.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The New Bottleneck: Specification and Reality
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:29:13', '00:32:25']
- **Claim**: While computation becomes commoditized, the remaining constraints are human intuition for problem specification and physical reality, which serves as the final validation layer for any simulated outcome.
- **Role**: counter

## Evidence and caveats

Frontier Math benchmarks jumped from <2% in 2024 to ~40% in early 2026. OpenAI and DeepMind both achieved IMO gold. Terence Tao uses 10 parallel AI agents as 'super scopes' for exploration. The SEL4 microkernel verification required 20 person-years for 8,700 lines of code, illustrating the cost of traditional rigor. Caveats include the 'last mile' problem where the final 10-15% of benchmarks are hardest, and the fact that AI currently automates grunt work rather than inventing fundamentally new math (solved zero Millennium Prize problems). Physical reality remains the ultimate validator.
