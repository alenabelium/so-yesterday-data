## Thesis

Violence against AI developers is futile because acceleration is the default policy driven by U.S.-China competition and free market dynamics, making adaptation the only rational response to the coming wave of AI-driven labor disruption.

## Argument

The arrest of an individual for throwing a Molotov cocktail at Sam Altman’s house marks a dangerous escalation from online debate to stochastic terrorism. While I distinguish this from legitimate resistance—such as artists losing livelihoods or workers using union actions to passively resist AI rollouts—we must confront the anger fueling these acts. The fear is real: copywriters are seeing work dry up, and the full wave of AI-based layoffs has not yet hit. However, violence will achieve nothing. Acceleration is the default policy, driven by crystal-clear incentive structures: geostrategic competition between the U.S. and China, and domestic free-market pressures. These attractor forces make resistance impossible. Just as electricity took decades to harness for light, motors, and radios, generative AI is a general-purpose technology undergoing continuous evolution. We cannot stop it; we can only adapt. Optimism requires realism: we must acknowledge the fear and the messiness of this transition, but focus energy on adapting to a post-labor economy where dignity is not tied to labor, rather than resisting the inevitable.

## Counterpoints

- Violence might slow AI development or force regulatory pauses.
- Deceleration is a rational choice to allow society time to prepare for irreversible changes.
- Legitimate anger from artists and writers losing work justifies extreme measures.
