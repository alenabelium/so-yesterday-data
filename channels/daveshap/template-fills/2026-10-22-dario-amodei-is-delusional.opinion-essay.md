## Thesis

Anthropic's public benefit fund is a PR stunt that keeps control in the company's hands, not a genuine transfer of wealth to the public.

## Argument

The mechanism is control. Anthropic announced a 5% share donation to a public benefit fund, but the fund is an LLC wholly owned by Anthropic itself. This means Anthropic decides what counts as public good, who gets the money, and when. It's not charity; it's a reserve fund with a better name. The company even admits the fund could fund research that aligns with its own worldview. This is not the same as giving the public a real stake. The deeper issue is the 'AI psychosis' of CEOs like Dario Amodei and Sam Altman, who extrapolate parabolic revenue growth to absurd levels—Anthropic's revenue went from $1B to $9B to $65B, and they imagine $200 trillion in five years, which is bigger than the global economy. This delusion explains why they'd create a fund they control: they think they'll own the world and want to be benefactors on their own terms. The fix is simple: transfer shares to a government-owned sovereign wealth fund with transparent governance, tax incentives, and adherence to the Santiago principles. That's real public ownership, not a private foundation with a public name.

## Counterpoints

- Some praise the fund as the largest philanthropic commitment in history, but it's a private LLC, not philanthropy.
- A law professor supports the move but notes philanthropy doesn't replace public voice.
- Anthropic's own strategy paper suggests sovereign wealth funds and basic income, yet they kept control for themselves.
