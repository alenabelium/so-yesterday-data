## Thesis

The rumor that AI will replace all human work within 300 days is false; while digital knowledge work faces immediate disruption, enterprise inertia, legacy infrastructure, and corporate risk aversion prevent widespread adoption on that.

## Argument

The 'last 300 days' rumor, amplified by Kevin Roose, reflects frontier lab saturation of benchmarks like GDP val, not economic reality. While tools like Claude and Gemini have reached a tipping point for grunt work in marketing and sales, and Slack/Canva integrate copilot features, this is distinct from enterprise adoption. Small, agile companies with leadership prioritizing AI learning adopt quickly, but large enterprises are paralyzed by legacy code, physical logistics, and compliance. As seen in my time at Cisco and Auto Parts, brick-and-mortar businesses view software as a friction point, not the core product. Refactoring legacy systems takes 18 months minimum, and CTOs/CISOs block deployment due to fear of 'resume generating events' like prod database deletions. The real impact is a 'junior crisis' with 9-13% drops in new grad hiring, not total labor elimination. Silicon Valley overestimates short-term disruption and underestimates long-term transformation, ignoring that physical infrastructure and corporate risk aversion create massive barriers to rapid AI integration.

## Counterpoints

- Frontier labs are spending millions on tokens for agents, signaling imminent saturation of work capabilities.
- Software costs are approaching zero, giving corporations incentive to rewrite legacy code.
- Agentic AI can likely take over my specific digital knowledge work within 300 days.
