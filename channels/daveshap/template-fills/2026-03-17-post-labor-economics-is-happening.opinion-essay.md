## Thesis

Human labor is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary household income source and must be replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted from primary (extraction) to secondary (manufacturing) to tertiary (services) sectors, with the tertiary sector peaking around 2015. As automation erodes the need for human labor, the definition of post-labor economics becomes clear: human labor input is decoupled from economic output. This decoupling means wages no longer drive household income. To prevent a deflationary death spiral, two other income buckets must fill the void: transfers (UBI, social security, welfare) and capital (stocks, bonds, rental properties, businesses). Most discourse focuses only on transfers, ignoring the critical need to boost capital-based income. The goal is to achieve 'mimetic saturation' of this idea among voters, politicians, and think tanks. A high-production book serves as the gold standard vehicle to unify these disparate groups. The immediate mechanism for success is a viral Kickstarter campaign to hit the front page, ensuring the message reaches the 'conservative father-in-law' demographic and broader political circles before the July launch.

## Counterpoints

- Education is useless because you never use what you learned in school professionally.
- Soft skills are the most valuable asset for remaining relevant in the job market.
- Focusing only on supply-side economics ignores the demand side of the equation.
- People pay for education and expect it to guarantee job security, not hear it is irrelevant.
