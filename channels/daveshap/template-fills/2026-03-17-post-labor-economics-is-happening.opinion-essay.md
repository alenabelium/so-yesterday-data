## Thesis

Human labor is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary household income source and must be replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted through three sectors: primary (extraction), secondary (manufacturing), and tertiary (services). Data suggests the tertiary sector peaked around 2015, mirroring the decline of farming and manufacturing. As AI automates labor, the definition of post-labor economics emerges: human labor input is decoupled from economic output. Consequently, wages vanish as the primary income source. Households must rely on two remaining buckets: government transfers (UBI, social security) and capital (stocks, rental properties). The critical insight is that we cannot ignore capital; we must actively boost capital-based income to maintain consumer demand and avoid a deflationary death spiral. This book aims to prove this new economic model to voters and politicians, starting with a Kickstarter campaign to achieve mimetic saturation and consensus among the right stakeholders.

## Counterpoints

- Students worry about job security and ask what to study, but soft skills are insufficient if the entire job market is disrupted.
- Critics might argue that education is useless, but the deeper issue is that AI threatens every power structure and job market.
- Some may ask why publish a book instead of free blog posts, but a book is the gold standard for getting politicians and voters on the same page.
- The fear that post-labor economics is just an idea without real-world traction, requiring viral momentum to become reality.
