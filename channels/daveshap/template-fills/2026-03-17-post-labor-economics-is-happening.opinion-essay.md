## Thesis

Human labor input is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary source of household income and must be replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted from primary (extraction) to secondary (manufacturing) to tertiary (services) sectors, with the tertiary sector peaking around 2015. As automation renders human labor increasingly redundant, the traditional wage-based model collapses. Post-labor economics defines this decoupling: economic output no longer requires proportional human input. To prevent a deflationary death spiral, household income must pivot from wages to two new buckets: government transfers (UBI, social security) and capital income (stocks, businesses, rentals). The critical mechanism for survival is "capital boosting capital"—ensuring citizens own the means of production to sustain aggregate demand. This is not just a theoretical exercise but a political necessity. The goal is to achieve "mimetic saturation" among voters, politicians, and think tanks. A polished book serves as the gold standard vehicle to align these disparate groups, starting with convincing the skeptical conservative voter to shift the entire political consensus.

## Counterpoints

- Critics may argue that education remains valuable for learning how to learn, but this ignores the economic reality that degrees no longer guarantee wage-based employment.
- Some might suggest focusing on AI alignment or safety, but the immediate threat is economic disruption to jobs and power structures, not existential risk.
- Publishing content for free via blog series is insufficient; a physical book is required to establish a unified standard for political and academic consensus.
