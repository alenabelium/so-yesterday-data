## Thesis

Human labor input is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary source of household income and must be replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted from primary extraction to secondary manufacturing, and now to a tertiary service sector that peaked around 2015. As automation renders human labor redundant, the traditional wage model collapses. Post-labor economics defines this decoupling: economic output no longer requires proportional human input. To prevent a deflationary death spiral, household income must pivot from wages to two other buckets: government transfers (UBI, social security) and capital assets (stocks, businesses, rentals). The critical mechanism is "capital boosting capital"—ensuring citizens own the means of production to sustain aggregate demand. This isn't just theory; it's a political imperative. The book aims to achieve mimetic saturation among voters, politicians, and think tanks. The goal is to convince the "conservative father-in-law" archetype that this transition is inevitable and necessary, thereby building the consensus required for labor-zero policies to take hold before the economic reality forces them upon us.
