## Thesis

Human labor input is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary source of household income and must be replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted from primary extraction to secondary manufacturing, and now the tertiary service sector is peaking around 2015, mirroring the decline of farming and industry. As automation erodes the need for human labor, the definition of post-labor economics becomes clear: wages disappear as the dominant income source. Households must then rely on two other buckets: government transfers (UBI, social security) and capital (stocks, rental properties, businesses). The critical insight is that we cannot rely on transfers alone; we must actively boost capital-based income to maintain aggregate demand and avoid a deflationary death spiral. This book is not just for the informed but is a deliberate vehicle to reach voters, politicians, and think tanks who need to understand this structural shift. The goal is to achieve mimetic saturation in the right conversations, proving that labor zero is not just an idea but an economic inevitability that requires immediate political and social consensus.
