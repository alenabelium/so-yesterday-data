## Thesis

Human labor input is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary source of household income and must be replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted through three sectors: primary (extraction), secondary (manufacturing), and tertiary (services). Data indicates the tertiary sector peaked around 2015, following the decline of manufacturing. As automation renders human labor increasingly redundant, the definition of post-labor economics is that economic output is no longer tied to human labor input. Consequently, wages will not be the main income source. The solution lies in balancing two domains: government transfers (UBI, social security) and capital-based income (stocks, businesses, rentals). The critical mechanism is "capital boosting capital" to ensure aggregate demand remains robust and avoids a deflationary death spiral. This is not just a theoretical exercise but a political necessity. The goal is to achieve "mimetic saturation" among voters, politicians, and think tanks. A high-quality book serves as the gold standard to align these disparate groups, specifically targeting the "conservative father-in-law" demographic to build consensus. The current Kickstarter campaign is the make-or-break moment to fund production and, more importantly, to generate the viral signal needed to force this new economic framework into the mainstream political discourse.

## Counterpoints

- Education is useless because the point is to learn how to learn, not specific skills like Pascal or C++.
- Soft skills and communication are the most valuable assets for STEM workers in an AI world.
- Publishing content for free as a blog series is insufficient to align politicians and voters on complex economic theory.
- Historical analogies show that entire sectors like farming and manufacturing can vanish as primary employment sources despite seeming permanent.
