## Thesis

Human labor is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary household income source, replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted from primary (extraction) to secondary (manufacturing) to tertiary (services) sectors, with the tertiary sector peaking around 2015. As automation renders human labor input irrelevant to economic output, the traditional wage model collapses. Household income must therefore pivot to two remaining buckets: government transfers (UBI, social security) and capital-based income (stocks, businesses, rentals). The critical insight is that transfers alone are insufficient; we must actively boost capital ownership to maintain aggregate demand and avoid a deflationary death spiral. This book serves as the unified vehicle to convince voters, politicians, and think tanks of this necessary economic transition.

## Counterpoints

- Critics may argue that soft skills or the ability to learn are sufficient future-proofing, ignoring the structural decoupling of labor from output.
- Some may believe education remains valuable for its own sake, but the economic reality is that wages are disappearing as a primary income source.
- Objections might focus on the difficulty of acquiring capital, but the argument insists capital boosting is essential alongside transfers to sustain consumer demand.
