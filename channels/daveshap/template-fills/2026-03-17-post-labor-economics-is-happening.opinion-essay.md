## Thesis

Human labor input is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary source of household income and must be replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted from primary (extraction) to secondary (manufacturing) to tertiary (services) sectors, with the tertiary sector peaking around 2015. As automation erodes the need for human labor, the traditional wage bucket disappears. Post-labor economics requires replacing wages with two other income sources: government transfers (UBI, social security) and capital-based income (stocks, businesses, rentals). The critical insight is that we must actively boost capital ownership to prevent a deflationary death spiral, as transfers alone are insufficient. This book aims to establish a consensus among voters, politicians, and think tanks by proving that this economic shift is inevitable and providing a framework for survival through capital accumulation and policy change.
