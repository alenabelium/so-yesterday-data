## Thesis

Human labor input is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary source of household income and must be replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted from primary (extraction) to secondary (manufacturing) to tertiary (services) sectors, with the tertiary sector peaking around 2015. As automation renders human labor less necessary for economic output, the traditional wage model collapses. Post-labor economics defines this decoupling: wages vanish as the dominant income source, necessitating a new structure built on two pillars. First, government transfers like UBI and social security provide a baseline. Second, and critically, capital-based income from stocks, bonds, and rental properties must be boosted to maintain aggregate consumer demand and prevent a deflationary death spiral. The goal is not just survival via transfers, but economic stability via capital ownership. This requires a massive shift in public consensus, which is why the book serves as a unified vehicle to convince voters, politicians, and think tanks that this transition is inevitable and manageable.

## Counterpoints

- Education is useless because schools teach outdated skills like Pascal instead of modern tools.
- Soft skills are the only safe bet for jobs, but they cannot sustain the entire economy.
- Focusing only on supply-side economics ignores the critical demand-side problem of consumer spending.
- People fear AI alignment and X-risk more than immediate job displacement.
- Publishing as a free blog series is sufficient, but a book is needed for political and academic consensus.
