## Thesis

Human labor is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary household income source, replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted from primary (extraction) to secondary (manufacturing) to tertiary (services) sectors, with the tertiary sector peaking around 2015. As automation erodes the need for human labor, the traditional wage-based model collapses. Post-labor economics defines this decoupling: economic output no longer requires proportional human input. To sustain aggregate demand and avoid a deflationary death spiral, household income must pivot from wages to two other buckets: government transfers (UBI, social security) and capital-based income (stocks, businesses, rentals). The critical insight is boosting capital ownership alongside transfers. This book aims to establish a consensus among voters, politicians, and think tanks by presenting a unified, high-quality vehicle for this new economic framework, rather than scattered blog posts.

## Counterpoints

- Education is useless because skills learned in school are rarely used professionally.
- Soft skills are the only safe bet for future employment.
- Publishing as free blog content is sufficient for spreading ideas.
- Universal Basic Income alone solves the post-labor income gap.
- The tertiary service sector will not decline like previous sectors.
