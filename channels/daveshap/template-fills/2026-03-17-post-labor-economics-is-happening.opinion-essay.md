## Thesis

Human labor is decoupling from economic output, meaning wages will cease to be the primary household income source and must be replaced by government transfers and capital-based income.

## Argument

The economy has historically shifted through three sectors: primary (extraction), secondary (manufacturing), and tertiary (services). Each sector peaked and declined as automation took over. The tertiary sector peaked around 2015, signaling that service jobs are next to be automated. As wages disappear, household income must rely on two buckets: government transfers (UBI, social security) and capital (stocks, businesses, rentals). The critical insight is that we must actively boost capital ownership to maintain consumer demand and avoid a deflationary death spiral. This book is a tool to build consensus among voters and politicians, starting with convincing a 'conservative father-in-law' to understand the inevitability of post-labor economics.

## Counterpoints

- Students worry about job security, not just AI alignment, demanding practical advice on what to study.
- Critics argue focusing only on supply-side economics ignores the demand side of the equation.
- Some suggest publishing content for free via blog series instead of a paid book to reach more people.
- Historical analogies show that industries like farming and manufacturing were once thought permanent but declined.
- The fear that without widespread adoption, post-labor economics remains just an idea without real-world impact.
