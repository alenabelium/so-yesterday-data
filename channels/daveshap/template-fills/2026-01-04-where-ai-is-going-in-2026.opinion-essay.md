## Thesis

AI tools like Claude Code and Notebook LM have crossed a utility tipping point where they perform genuine cognitive offload, making users supervisors of AI agents rather than doers of low-level mental work.

## Argument

The mechanism is the compounding of dozens of incremental research advances—from DeepSeek's efficient architectures to recursive language models—into each new frontier model generation. None is a silver bullet, but together they push capability past a tipping point. Claude Code has reached that point: leading developers, economists, and law professors use it for real cognitive offload, not just coding. This is a general-purpose technology, like electricity, enabling network effects where a tool becomes the default way forward once it's good enough. The spillover effect is visible as these tools move beyond code into economics research and scientific writing. Normalcy bias blinds most people to the exponential trend, even as data shows AI writing 90%+ of accepted code. The next bottleneck—validation—will also be subsumed by AI, as other AIs check AI output.

## Counterpoints

- AI tools create an echo chamber and operationalize confirmation bias, but you can deliberately seek countervailing examples to strengthen arguments.
- Some models are sycophantic and will confabulate evidence to support your beliefs, so you must develop the habit of checking your work.
- People argue AI never has original thoughts, but most humans don't either, and hallucinations can be useful novel ideas if validated.
- Normalcy bias makes people dismiss exponential trends, but extrapolating data shows AI writing 90%+ of code, and validation will be automated too.
- Concern about overwhelming volume of AI output is misplaced; AI is the best tool for aggregating and sorting information.
