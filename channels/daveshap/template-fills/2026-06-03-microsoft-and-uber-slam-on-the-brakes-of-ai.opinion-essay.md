## Thesis

Recent corporate cost-cutting by Microsoft and Uber does not signal an AI bubble pop or industry slowdown, as the broader Fortune 500 and VC commitment ensures AI development continues regardless of short-term financial fluctuations.

## Argument

The narrative that AI is too expensive is a misinterpretation of isolated corporate events. Microsoft cancelling internal Claude use and Uber's budget overruns are specific operational adjustments, not industry-wide retreats. Microsoft remains all-in on building its own AI tools. The definition of a bubble as an overvalued asset does not equate to the cessation of the underlying technology's development. Even if AI companies like Anthropic see their valuations crash from mega-cap to medium-cap, the physical buildout continues. The evidence lies in the scale of infrastructure investment: while $150 billion in data centers may be delayed or cancelled, this is a fraction of the $750 billion aimed for this year. A quarter of delay is not a halt. The driving force is the unanimous commitment of Fortune 500 CEOs and venture capitalists who view AI as the most transformative technology of the decade. Until these key stakeholders declare AI unexciting or move on, the momentum is irreversible. The current friction is administrative and financial optimization, not a loss of faith in the technology's potential.

## Counterpoints

- Critics argue AI infrastructure costs are unsustainable, citing Uber's $500 million token spend and Microsoft's cancellation of internal Claude use as proof the bubble is popping.
- Some believe AI valuations are artificially inflated, comparing current hype to SpaceX's IPO pricing which attaches AI premiums to non-AI core businesses.
- The concern that if major companies like Anthropic crash in value, the entire AI ecosystem will lose funding and slow down significantly.
