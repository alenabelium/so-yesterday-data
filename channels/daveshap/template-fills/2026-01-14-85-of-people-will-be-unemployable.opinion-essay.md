## Thesis

When AI and robots surpass humans on all functional tasks, labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand is constrained by the finite physics of attention, not production capacity.

## Argument

The model assumes three stipulations: stable aggregate demand (solved via UBI or wealth dividends), full supply-side automation (machines are better, faster, cheaper, and safer), and a human premium for authenticity, liability, and care. Under these conditions, the irreducible core of employment consists of a statutory floor (1.5-2.3% LFPR) for legally required human roles like judges and CEOs, and an authenticity premium (6-10% LFPR) for artisans, entertainers, and care workers. Even in an optimistic demand-side expansion scenario where increased leisure time drives demand for relational density and hobby-to-market activities, the labor force participation rate caps at 23-40%. This collapses back to 10-15% due to the physics of attention: the hard cap of 24 hours per day. The economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity, governed by extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the majority of attention. With AI erasing skill barriers, supply floods the market, leading to rent dissipation where earnings collapse toward the reservation wage. The result is a lottery, not a labor market, where status competition shifts from career titles to skill and taste, leaving 85% of people unemployable in the traditional sense.

## Counterpoints

- Machines might not be better at all tasks, preserving human relevance in specific niches.
- Regulatory frameworks could mandate human presence in more sectors than currently required.
- New, unforeseen job categories may emerge that we cannot currently model.
- Universal Basic Income or sovereign wealth funds could decouple survival from labor participation.
- Human preference for human-made goods might expand beyond the estimated 10% authenticity premium.
