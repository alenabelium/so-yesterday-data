## Thesis

Labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because finite human attention, not production capacity, is the ultimate economic constraint.

## Argument

The model assumes machines surpass humans in all functional tasks, leaving only a statutory floor of 1.5-2.3% for legally required roles and an authenticity premium of 6-10% for human-centric services. Even with optimistic demand-side expansion from increased leisure time, the hard cap of 24 hours per day prevents infinite consumption. The economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity, governed by extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the majority of attention. This creates a lottery-like market rather than a traditional labor market, where skill barriers are erased by AI, leading to rent dissipation and wage collapse. The final estimate remains 10-15% participation, with the rest of the population relying on UBI and capital distribution rather than wage labor.

## Counterpoints

- Machines might not be better at all tasks, but the model assumes maximal super intelligence to establish the upper bound of automation.
- Demand might not be infinite due to lack of purchasing power, but the model assumes stable aggregate demand via UBI or wealth dividends.
- New jobs might emerge, but the physics of time limits the total hours available for human-to-human interaction.
- Regulatory frameworks might change to allow AI in statutory roles, but the model uses current laws as a conservative baseline.
- Human preference for inefficiency might sustain more jobs, but attention economics favors the top performers, not the middle.
