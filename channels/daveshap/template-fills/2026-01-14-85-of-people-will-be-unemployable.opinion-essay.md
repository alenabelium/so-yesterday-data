## Thesis

When AI and robots surpass humans on all functional tasks, labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand for goods is infinite but demand for human labor is capped by the physics of attention.

## Argument

The argument rests on three stipulations: stable aggregate demand (solved via UBI or wealth dividends), full supply-side automation (machines are better, faster, cheaper, and safer), and the persistence of a "human premium" where humanness is a product feature. This residual employment breaks into a statutory floor (1.5-2.3% LFPR for legally required human roles like judges or CEOs) and an authenticity premium (6-10% for artisans, entertainers, and care workers). Even in an optimistic demand-side expansion scenario where increased free time drives demand for relational density and local experiences, the total LFPR caps at 23-40%. However, this optimism fails against the physics of attention: the 24-hour day is a hard cap that no technology can expand. Jevons paradox applies to content consumption, but not to time creation. The economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity, governed by extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the lion's share of attention. With AI erasing skill barriers, the market floods, leading to rent dissipation where earnings collapse toward the reservation wage. The result is not a broad labor market but a lottery of concentrated stardom for a few and precarious striving for the rest, making wage labor largely obsolete outside of niche, non-scalable human connection roles.

## Counterpoints

- Machines might not be better at all tasks, specifically high-stakes cognitive work like frontier research or surgery.
- Regulatory frameworks may evolve to artificially preserve human roles, such as replacing elected officials with AI.
- Increased efficiency might create entirely new categories of work that we cannot currently imagine.
- Universal Basic Income or sovereign wealth funds will decouple survival from labor, changing the definition of 'employment'.
- Human demand for other humans is infinite, driven by parasocial relationships and the need for authentic connection.
