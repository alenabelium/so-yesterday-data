## Thesis

When AI and robots surpass humans on all functional tasks, labor force participation will collapse to 10-15%, as finite human attention—not infinite demand—becomes the hard cap on employment.

## Argument

The model rests on three stipulations: machines are superior on all functional dimensions, aggregate demand is stabilized via mechanisms like UBI, and human preference persists only where "humanness" is a product feature. This leaves a statutory floor of 1.5-2.3% LFPR for legally required roles and an authenticity premium of 6-10% for artisans, entertainers, and care workers. Even in an optimistic demand-side expansion scenario where increased leisure drives demand for relational density and hobby-to-market roles, the LFPR only reaches 23-40%. This collapses back down due to the physics of attention: the 24-hour day is a hard cap that no technology can expand. The economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity, governed by extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the majority of attention. With AI erasing skill barriers, the market floods, leading to [[rent dissipation]] where earnings collapse toward the reservation wage. The result is a lottery, not a labor market, where survival depends on luck and parasocial connection rather than work ethic or skill.

## Counterpoints

- Machines cannot replicate the value of human authenticity, liability, and care in specific roles.
- Increased leisure time will expand demand for human-centric services like therapy and community building.
- Statutory and regulatory frameworks will legally mandate human involvement in critical sectors.
- Hobby-to-market transitions will allow individuals to monetize passions in a post-scarcity economy.
