## Thesis

Labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand for goods is infinite but demand for human labor is capped by the physics of attention, making the future economy a lottery, not a labor market.

## Argument

The model assumes machines are superior on all functional dimensions (better, faster, cheaper, safer) and that aggregate demand is stabilized via mechanisms like UBI. Under these conditions, employment survives only in three buckets: the statutory floor (1.5-2.3% for legally required human accountability), the authenticity premium (6-10% for artisans, entertainers, and care workers where human presence is the product), and a demand-side expansion that optimistically reaches 40% but collapses due to the hard cap of 24 hours per day. The economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity. In this attention economy, extreme power laws dictate that the top 1% of creators capture the lion's share of attention, while the bottom 50% capture less than 5%. AI erases skill barriers, flooding markets and causing rent dissipation where earnings collapse toward the reservation wage. The result is not a broad distribution of work but concentrated stardom for a few and precarious striving for the rest. The final estimate is a 75% drop in employment, leaving only 10-15% of the workforce employed in a fragmented, part-time, or lottery-like structure.

## Counterpoints

- Demand-side expansion could reach 23-40% LFPR if infinite free time creates proportional demand for human services.
- Statutory and regulatory requirements will preserve a floor of 1.5-2.3% for roles requiring human legal accountability.
- The authenticity premium preserves 6-10% for artisans, entertainers, and care workers where human connection is valued.
- AI may solve aging and longevity, adding more workers to the pool rather than reducing the need for labor.
- One-to-one relational roles (therapists, coaches) offer a viable, albeit non-scalable, path to employment.
