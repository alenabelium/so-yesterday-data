## Thesis

When AI and robots surpass humans on all functional tasks, labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand is constrained by finite attention, not production capacity.

## Argument

The core mechanism is the collision between infinite production capacity and finite consumption capacity. As AI and robotics become better, faster, cheaper, and safer than humans at every task, the economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity. The irreducible human employment floor consists of a statutory core (1.5-2.3% LFPR) for legally required human accountability and an authenticity premium (6-10% LFPR) for roles where human presence is the product, such as care, entertainment, and artisanal craft. Even in an optimistic demand-side expansion scenario where increased leisure time drives demand for relational density and local experiences, the hard physics of the 24-hour day caps total allocable attention. This creates an extreme power law where the top 1% of creators capture the lion's share of attention, turning the remaining viable labor market into a lottery rather than a predictable employment sector. Consequently, the economy must pivot from wage-based labor to capital-based distribution like UBI, as traditional labor markets cannot sustain the majority of the population.

## Counterpoints

- Demand-side expansion: Increased leisure time could theoretically boost demand for human-centric services like therapy and local events.
- Statutory floor: Legal requirements for human accountability (e.g., judges, CEOs) will preserve a small baseline of employment.
- Authenticity premium: Humans prefer other humans for status, entertainment, and care, creating a durable niche for human labor.
- One-to-one care: Local, non-scalable relational roles may remain viable for those who can secure clients.
- Voluntary labor: People may find meaning in unpaid community building or exocortex contributions despite lack of market validation.
