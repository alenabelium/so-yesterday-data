## Thesis

When AI and robots surpass humans in all functional tasks, labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand for other humans is finite, constrained by the physics of attention.

## Argument

The central mechanism is the collision between infinite production capacity and finite consumption capacity. While AI and robotics can theoretically provide unlimited goods and services, human attention is capped by the 24-hour day. This creates an attention economy governed by extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the majority of value, turning employment into a lottery rather than a labor market.

Residual employment is limited to three buckets: a statutory floor of 1.5-2.3% for legally required human accountability; an authenticity premium of 6-10% for artisans and entertainers where human imperfection is the product; and a care sector that expands with free time but remains non-scalable. Even in optimistic scenarios where demand expands due to leisure, the hard cap of 14-16 allocable hours per person prevents mass employment. The economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity, where skill barriers are erased by AI tools, leading to rent dissipation and wage collapse toward the reservation wage. The only viable paths are concentrated stardom or local, non-scalable relational roles.

## Counterpoints

- Demand-side expansion: If everyone has free time, demand for human services like therapy and hospitality will rise, potentially boosting participation to 23-40%.
- Statutory persistence: Laws requiring human accountability for liability and governance will preserve a small floor of jobs like judges and CEOs.
- Authenticity premium: Humans inherently prefer other humans for entertainment and care, creating a durable niche for artisans and performers.
- Longevity solutions: AI might solve aging, adding more workers to the pool, which further exacerbates the scarcity of roles rather than solving it.
