## Thesis

Labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand for goods is infinite but demand for human labor is capped by the physics of attention.

## Argument

The central mechanism is the collision between infinite production capacity and finite consumption capacity. We are moving from a labor-constrained economy to an attention-constrained one. The hard cap is 24 hours per day; no amount of AI efficiency can manufacture more time. Even if aggregate demand expands due to increased leisure, the attention economy follows extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the lion's share of attention, making it a lottery, not a labor market. The residual employment consists of a statutory floor (1.5-2.3% for legally required human roles) and an authenticity premium (6-10% for artisans, care, and entertainment). While optimistic demand-side expansion might reach 23-40%, it collapses back down when constrained by the physics of attention. The final estimate is a 75% drop in employment, leaving only a small elite and distributed care roles viable.

## Counterpoints

- Machines will create new, unforeseen jobs that require human touch.
- Universal Basic Income will solve the distribution crisis.
- Demand for human interaction is infinite and will scale with leisure time.
- Regulatory frameworks will mandate human oversight in all sectors.
- AI will lower skill barriers, allowing more people to compete globally.
