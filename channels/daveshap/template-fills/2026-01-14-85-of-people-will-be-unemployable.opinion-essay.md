## Thesis

When AI and robots surpass humans on all functional tasks, labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand for goods is infinite but demand for human labor is capped by the physics of attention.

## Argument

The central mechanism is the collision between infinite production capacity and finite consumption capacity. As AI and robotics become better, faster, cheaper, and safer than humans at all tasks, the economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity. The irreducible core of human employment consists of a statutory floor (1.5-2.3% for legally required human roles like judges or CEOs) and an authenticity premium (6-10% for artisans, entertainers, and care workers where 'humanness' is the product). Even in an optimistic demand-side expansion scenario where increased free time drives demand for relational density and local experiences, the hard cap of 24 hours per day prevents infinite growth. The attention economy follows extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the lion's share of attention, making viable creator jobs a lottery rather than a labor market. Consequently, the residual labor force participation rate collapses to 10-15%, with the remaining 85% relying on UBI or capital distribution rather than wage labor.

## Counterpoints

- Machines might not be better at all tasks; humans will always prefer human interaction for care and authenticity.
- New jobs will emerge that we cannot currently imagine, expanding the labor market.
- AI will solve aging and longevity, adding more workers to the pool rather than reducing demand.
- Universal Basic Income or sovereign wealth funds will decouple survival from traditional employment.
- Local attention economies and one-to-one care roles will provide stable, non-scalable income for many.
