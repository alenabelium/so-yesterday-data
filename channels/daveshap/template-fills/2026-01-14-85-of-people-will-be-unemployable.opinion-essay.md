## Thesis

Labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand for goods is infinite but demand for human labor is capped by the physics of attention.

## Argument

The model assumes machines are superior on all functional dimensions and that aggregate demand is stabilized via UBI or similar mechanisms. Under these conditions, the irreducible core of human employment consists of a statutory floor (1.5-2.3% LFPR) for roles requiring legal accountability, and an authenticity premium (6-10% LFPR) for roles where human presence is the product, such as entertainment and care. Even in an optimistic demand-side expansion scenario where increased free time drives consumption of human-centric services, the labor force participation rate caps at 23-40%. This ceiling collapses back to 10-15% due to the hard constraint of the 24-hour day. Human demand for goods is infinite, but human demand for other humans is not. The attention economy follows extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the majority of attention, making success a lottery rather than a labor market. As AI lowers skill barriers, supply floods the market, leading to rent dissipation where earnings collapse. The economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity, meaning the future of work is defined by the topology of human connection, not skill arbitrage.

## Counterpoints

- Machines will create entirely new categories of jobs that we cannot yet imagine.
- AI will solve aging and longevity, adding more workers to the pool.
- Regulatory frameworks will evolve to ban AI in many current statutory roles.
- Universal Basic Income will decouple survival from labor participation entirely.
