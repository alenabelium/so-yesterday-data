## Thesis

When AI and robots surpass humans on all functional tasks, labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand for other humans is capped by the finite physics of attention, not production capacity.

## Argument

The future economy is defined by the collision of infinite production capacity with finite consumption capacity. While AI and robotics will eliminate the scarcity of cognitive and dexterous labor, human attention remains the hard cap. With only 24 hours in a day, the demand for human-centric services—authenticity, care, and entertainment—cannot scale to absorb the workforce. The current 62% labor force participation rate in the US will plummet to a residual 10-15%. This residual is composed of a statutory floor (1.5-2.3% for legally required human roles like judges or CEOs) and an authenticity premium (6-10% for artisans, entertainers, and caregivers). Even in an optimistic demand-side expansion scenario where increased leisure time drives new service demand, the total caps at 23-40% before hitting the physics of attention. The economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity, creating extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the lion's share of attention. This is not a labor market but a lottery, where success depends on parasocial resonance and luck rather than skill or work ethic. Consequently, wage labor becomes obsolete for the majority, necessitating a pivot to UBI and capital-based income distribution.

## Counterpoints

- Demand-side expansion: Increased leisure time could create new service jobs, but this is capped by the 24-hour day and does not scale to full employment.
- Statutory floor: Legal requirements for human accountability (e.g., judges, CEOs) preserve a small core of jobs, but this is only ~2% of the workforce.
- Skill arbitrage: Lowering skill barriers floods markets, leading to rent dissipation where earnings collapse to the reservation wage.
- AI substitution in care: While care is a premium, AI may eventually be preferred for therapy due to availability and lack of shame, further reducing human demand.
- Longevity: AI solving aging could add workers to the pool, worsening the competition for the finite number of remaining human-centric roles.
