## Thesis

Labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand for goods is infinite but demand for human labor is capped by the physics of attention.

## Argument

The central mechanism is the collision between infinite production capacity and finite consumption capacity. As AI and robotics become superior on all functional dimensions—better, faster, cheaper, and safer—supply-side automation eliminates most wage labor. The residual economy rests on three pillars: a statutory floor (1.5-2.3% LFPR) for legally required human accountability; an authenticity premium (6-10% LFPR) for status, entertainment, and care; and a demand-side expansion scenario that optimistically reaches 40% but collapses under Jevons paradox. Efficiency increases consumption but cannot manufacture time. The hard cap of 24 hours per day creates an attention economy governed by extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the lion's share of attention. Consequently, the economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity, turning employment into a lottery rather than a labor market. The final estimate collapses back to 10-15% LFPR because human demand for other humans is not infinite, even if demand for goods is. The only viable path forward is decoupling survival from wage labor via UBI and capital distribution, as the attention bucket is finite and cannot be expanded.

## Counterpoints

- Demand-side expansion could reach 40% LFPR if everyone has infinite free time and money.
- Statutory roles might vanish in a sci-fi future where AI replaces elected officials and judges.
- AI therapists may replace human caregivers due to lower cost and lack of shame.
- Zero-barrier markets might allow hobbyists to monetize skills via AI tools.
- Longevity solutions could add more workers to the pool, worsening competition.
