## Thesis

Labor force participation will collapse to 10-15% because human demand for goods is infinite but demand for human labor is capped by the physics of attention.

## Argument

The model assumes three stipulations: machines surpass humans on all functional dimensions, aggregate demand is stabilized via UBI or wealth dividends, and employment persists only where 'humanness' is a product feature. The irreducible core consists of a statutory floor (1.5-2.3% LFPR) for legally accountable roles and an authenticity premium (6-10% LFPR) for artisans, entertainers, and care workers. Even in an optimistic demand-side expansion scenario where increased leisure time drives consumption of human-centric experiences, the labor force participation rate caps at 23-40%. This ceiling collapses back to 10-15% due to the 'physics of attention': the hard constraint of 24 hours per day prevents infinite consumption of human services. The economy shifts from labor scarcity to attention scarcity, governed by extreme power laws where the top 1% of creators capture the majority of attention. Consequently, the residual labor market becomes a lottery rather than a viable economic engine, necessitating a pivot to capital-based income distribution.

## Counterpoints

- Machines may not be better than humans at all tasks, particularly in complex physical environments or frontier research.
- Regulatory frameworks could evolve to mandate human involvement in areas currently dominated by automation.
- Longevity solutions might extend working lives, though this increases competition for finite roles.
- New, unforeseen job categories may emerge that rely on human preference for inefficiency or novelty.
