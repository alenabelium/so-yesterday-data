## Thesis

Advanced mathematics is being commoditized via neurosymbolic AI, shifting science from hypothesis-testing to simulation-verification and making formal proof the new standard for all software.

## Argument

The shift from scaling to inference-time compute and Monte Carlo tree search has driven Frontier Math benchmarks from <2% to ~40% in two years. This enables a neurosymbolic workflow: neural networks provide creative intuition while symbolic engines (Lean, Isabelle) formally verify proofs. This creates a self-play loop with functionally unlimited synthetic data because math is decidable.

The implications are transformative. First, formal verification will replace testing as the standard for all code, eliminating bugs and security exploits. Second, science will move from approximation to solution, allowing high-fidelity simulations to replace experimental testing for everything from protein folding to aerospace engineering. Third, this democratizes Terence Tao-level capability to anyone with a smartphone.

However, bottlenecks remain. The primary constraint is no longer mathematical capability but specification: humans must still provide the intuition and define the problem space. Additionally, physical reality remains the final validation layer; simulations must eventually be grounded in the lab. The cost of computation also persists, though it will decrease. Ultimately, we are entering an age of verification where rigor is commoditized, making technology more robust and invisible.

## Counterpoints

- AI cannot yet solve Millennium Prize problems like the Riemann hypothesis or P vs NP.
- The last 10-15% of benchmarks is often harder than the first 50%.
- Physical reality remains the final validation layer for simulations.
- Humans must still provide the initial intuition and problem specification.
- Computation costs remain high despite efficiency gains.
