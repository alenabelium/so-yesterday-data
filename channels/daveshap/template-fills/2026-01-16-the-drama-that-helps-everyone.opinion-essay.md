## Thesis

The revolving door of talent between AI labs is a net positive for humanity, as it cross-pollinates expertise and prevents monopolies, despite the dramatic public fallout.

## Argument

The recent departures from Mira Murati's Thinking Machines, with alleged defections to OpenAI, exemplify a systemic feature of the AI industry: the 'boomerang' effect. While Sam Altman’s OpenAI thrives on broadcasting this drama for attention, the underlying mechanism is beneficial. There is no intellectual property moat in AI; research is largely open-source, and California’s ban on non-compete agreements ensures talent flows freely. This circulation mimics the historical journeyman system, where practitioners learn from different masters, inculcating diverse cultural values and technical approaches. This cross-pollination raises the tide for everyone, preventing any single entity like Nvidia from dominating the accelerator market. Competition drives down prices and increases quality, ultimately benefiting humanity more than any single company’s secrecy or stability could.

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI’s drama is driven by Sam Altman’s attention-seeking behavior and desire for status.
- Talent poaching can look 'skevy' when companies claw back vested shares from departing founders.
- Fake accounts on social media may distort the narrative of who is actually defecting.
