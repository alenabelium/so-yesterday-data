## Thesis

The revolving door of talent between AI labs is a net positive for humanity, as it cross-pollinates expertise and prevents any single entity from monopolizing progress.

## Argument

The recent drama at Thinking Machines, where the CTO allegedly defected to OpenAI, is not a unique crisis but a feature of the modern tech ecosystem. Sam Altman thrives on this attention, treating drama as a PR win, but the underlying mechanism is beneficial. There is no intellectual property moat in AI; research is largely open-source, and California’s ban on non-compete agreements ensures talent mobility. This circulation mirrors the historical journeyman system, where practitioners learn from different masters to gain a well-rounded education. By allowing experts to carry cultural values and technical intuitions across companies like OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and XAI, we raise the tide for everyone. This competition drives down prices and increases quality, preventing a monopoly on AI progress similar to Nvidia’s dominance in hardware. The gossip is entertaining, but the structural flow of talent is what ultimately advances humanity.

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI broadcasts drama for attention, making the situation seem more chaotic than it is.
- Companies may try to claw back vested shares to punish defectors, which looks 'skevy'.
- Some trade secrets exist, such as specific coding optimizations or math capabilities, but they are not moats.
