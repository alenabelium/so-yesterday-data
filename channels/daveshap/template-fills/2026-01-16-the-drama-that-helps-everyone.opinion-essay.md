## Thesis

The revolving door of talent between AI labs is a net positive for humanity, as it cross-pollinates expertise and prevents monopolies, despite the public drama.

## Argument

The recent departures from Meera Murati's Thinking Machines, with alleged defections to OpenAI, exemplify a beneficial 'boomerang' effect. While OpenAI thrives on this drama for attention, the underlying mechanism is economic and historical. There is no intellectual property moat in AI; research is largely open-source, and California bans non-compete agreements, making talent circulation inevitable. This mirrors the historical journeyman system, where experts move between 'masters' to gain diverse perspectives. This socialization raises the tide for everyone by cross-pollinating cultural values and research approaches. Without this circulation, we risk monopolies like Nvidia's dominance in accelerators. Competition among multiple frontier labs (OpenAI, Google DeepMind, XAI, Meta) drives down prices and increases quality, ultimately benefiting humanity more than any single entity holding all the cards.

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI's drama is manufactured for attention and PR wins, not just organic talent movement.
- Fake accounts on social media may exaggerate the scale of defections.
- Companies may use clawback provisions for vested shares to discourage defection, though this looks 'skevy'.
- Some competitors like XAI or Anthropic are quieter, suggesting less public drama but potentially tighter control.
- Nvidia's near-monopoly in AI accelerators shows that without competition, one entity can dominate critical infrastructure.
