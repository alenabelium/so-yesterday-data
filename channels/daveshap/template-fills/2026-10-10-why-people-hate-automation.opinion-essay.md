## Thesis

The Luddites were not anti-technology but opposed specific uses of machinery that lowered wages and degraded quality, and their core questions about who owns and benefits from new tools remain unresolved in today's automation debates.

## Argument

The popular image of the Luddites as technology-hating reactionaries is a distortion. The real Luddites were skilled workers who understood their machines intimately—many had worked on them for decades. Their protests were not against technology per se, but against specific applications that undermined their livelihoods and craft standards. In 1811, Nottinghamshire hosiery masters broke wide looms that produced cheap, shoddy 'scraps'—goods that undercut their wages and quality. They targeted only machines owned by employers who paid below set rates, leaving others untouched. This was a disciplined negotiation, not mindless destruction. The Yorkshire shearers' slogan—'Enoch made them, and Enoch must break them'—captured the irony that the same forge produced both the machines and the hammers used to smash them. Their demands were explicit: full-time work at the old price, established by custom and law. This is the language of people negotiating over the distribution of profits, not rejecting innovation. The government's response—troops, spies, and the death penalty—showed whose interests mattered. The Luddites lost, and within a generation, Lancashire weavers went from among the highest-paid workers in England to among the poorest. Today, the machines are different—they write, think, draw, and drive—but the underlying questions are unchanged: Who owns the new tools? Who decides how they are used? And when productivity increases, who gets the bonus? The Luddites were the first to raise these questions, and we should remember them for that, not ridicule them for their defeat.

## Counterpoints

- The common objection that Luddites were simply afraid of technology and wanted to stop progress is addressed by noting their deep familiarity with machines and their targeted, disciplined protests.
- The claim that automation inevitably benefits everyone is countered by the historical outcome: Lancashire weavers, once highly paid, became among the poorest within a generation after the machines were introduced.
