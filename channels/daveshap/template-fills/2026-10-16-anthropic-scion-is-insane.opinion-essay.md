## Thesis

Anthropic's Scion proves that the next 10x productivity gain comes from rebuilding human-scale tools for machines, not from AI agents alone.

## Argument

The mechanism is infrastructure. Anthropic's code merges exploded from 2,500 to over 30,000 per day in four months, but Git—designed for offline humans—couldn't handle the load. Their CI alone checked out code 25 million times daily, requiring hundreds of mirrors and moving 600 petabytes. Instead of patching Git, they built Scion, a server-centric system where agents fetch only what they need. The result: 200x less infrastructure, checkouts in seconds, and a team of seven migrated the entire monorepo in five months—with Claude's help. This is the productivity J-curve in action: companies that just give engineers agents but keep human-scale review and merge queues move the bottleneck from typing to reading. Anthropic's engineers now check intent, not every line, and merge concurrently, letting Claude fix conflicts faster than humans spot them. This isn't just about code—it's the template for post-labor economics. Human labor stops being the binding constraint when you automate coordination before firing coordinators. The next gains come from asking what every process—approvals, meetings, contracts, peer review—would look like if built for machines first. Don't pave the cow paths; rebuild the roads.
