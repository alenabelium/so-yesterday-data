## Thesis

AI accelerates creative burnout by removing natural rate-limiters, and eliminates the surprise necessary for satisfaction because it delivers exactly what you want.

## Argument

The core mechanism is the removal of structural friction. In traditional creative or professional workflows, gating functions—like instrument practice, band scheduling, or corporate approval processes—act as natural rate-limiters. These forcing functions pace output, allowing the 'creative well' to refill through lived experience. AI removes these barriers, making the human the sole bottleneck. When you can generate infinite content instantly, you drain your inspiration reservoir in weeks rather than years, leading to rapid saturation.

Furthermore, satisfaction in creative consumption relies on novelty and surprise. When AI generates exactly what you desire, it eliminates the unpredictability that makes art engaging. Even when prompted for twists, models regress to the mean. The thrill of consuming fiction or music comes from encountering high-entropy, external inputs you didn't predict. AI creates a 'walled garden' of bespoke content that becomes background noise. As novelty wears off, users disengage, not because the tool fails, but because it succeeds too well at mirroring their own limited perspective. True creativity requires external friction and surprise, which AI, by design, suppresses.

## Counterpoints

- Commercial artists apply skills to produce products rather than relying on personal inspiration, so they may not experience this specific type of burnout.
- The author acknowledges his high need for novelty and ADHD may make him more susceptible to saturation than the average user.
- New form factors like agents may restart the engagement cycle, similar to how the iPhone revitalized the cell phone market.
