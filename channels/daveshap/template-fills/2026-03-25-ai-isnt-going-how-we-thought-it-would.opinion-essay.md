## Thesis

AI accelerates creative burnout and novelty saturation by removing the natural rate-limiters that previously paced human output, making the individual the sole bottleneck.

## Argument

The core mechanism is the removal of structural friction. In traditional creative or professional workflows, gating functions—like instrument practice, band scheduling, approval processes, or budget constraints—act as natural rate-limiters. These forcing functions prevent the human from exhausting their creative well too quickly. AI removes these barriers, allowing users to "barf out" massive amounts of content in weeks rather than years. This speed drains the creative reservoir, leading to a state where the individual becomes the limiting factor. 

Furthermore, when AI generates exactly what the user wants, it eliminates the surprise and novelty essential for sustained engagement. The thrill of consumption comes from the unexpected; when you steer the output, you regress to the mean of your own patterns. This leads to rapid saturation. Even with high-entropy inputs, the bespoke nature of AI content creates a walled garden where the user is isolated from external surprise. The result is a cycle of intense initial engagement followed by deep burnout or disengagement, as the novelty wears off and the infinite possibility becomes background noise.

## Counterpoints

- Commercial artists apply skills differently than personal expression, so they may not experience the same burnout.
- The author acknowledges his high need for novelty and ADHD may make him an outlier in how quickly he gets bored.
- New form factors like agents may restart the engagement cycle by offering new types of interaction.
- Some users still find value in AI for specific tasks, even if general chatbot novelty has worn off.
