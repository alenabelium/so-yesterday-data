## TL;DR

The social contract of trading time for money is collapsing as machines outperform humans on speed, cost, and safety. Wealth concentration necessitates a shift from labor-based income to asset ownership and political leverage. Without redefining power independent of employment, society risks becoming dependent on state transfers or authoritarian bargains.

## The argument

### Define the Breaking Deal
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:19']
- **Claim**: Post-labor economics means every task faces a test of being better, faster, cheaper, and safer. When machines win on all four, humans must justify their premium, which gets harder annually.
- **Role**: definition

### Distinguish Demand Types
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:03']
- **Claim**: Derived demand seeks the result (builder is means), while essential demand requires the person (massage, children). Most labor falls into derived demand, leaving only narrow pockets for human-only work.
- **Role**: definition

### Highlight Wealth Decoupling
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:07']
- **Claim**: Since 1979, productivity grew ~94% while typical pay rose only ~33%. Household income relies on what you do, receive, or own; transfers create dependence, leaving ownership as the key to wealth.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesize Power Shift
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:07']
- **Claim**: When states no longer need people's labor (like petrostates), they share wealth for quiet. We must build leverage independent of jobs because power requires the ability to impose costs.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Automation takes jobs one task at a time, not all at once. AI handles thinking; robots handle physical work. The demand for human labor is real but narrow (massage, children, accountability). Transfers can bridge gaps but create client status. Rights are rarely given; they are taken by making refusal costlier than compliance.
