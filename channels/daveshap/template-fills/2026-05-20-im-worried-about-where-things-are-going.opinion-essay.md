## Thesis

Society is inevitably converging on technofudalism, where humans become economically irrelevant redundant biomass due to geostrategic competition, massive capital investment, and rational economic automation.

## Argument

The host argues that the transition to a post-labor economy is overdetermined by three converging forces. First, US-China geostrategic competition ensures AI and robotics are treated as first-class political priorities, accelerating automation beyond human capabilities. Second, capitalism drives the largest private mega-project in history, with trillions invested in AI infrastructure by 2030, eclipsing past endeavors like the Apollo program. Third, rational economic incentives create an attractor state where businesses and governments universally choose cheaper, faster automated solutions over human labor. The host rejects the notion of a 'useless class' with civic standing, arguing that historical precedents show marginalized groups are treated as disposable. He introduces the term 'redundant biomass' to describe the fate of those without leverage. While acknowledging the potential for technological abundance, he warns that automation inherently renders humans structurally irrelevant. The only remaining leverage is the collective veto of human labor, which is being lost inch by inch as automation expands.

## Counterpoints

- The idea that we are entering a 'useless class' with some residual civic standing is too generous; history shows marginalized groups are often treated as disposable resources.
- Western stability and Enlightenment ideals of natural rights are legal fictions; rights are secured through coercive force, not moral entitlements.
- The belief that the market will solve distribution issues is a delusion; the current trajectory is driven by capital priorities and geopolitical necessity, not benevolence.
