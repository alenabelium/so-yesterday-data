## Thesis

Society is inevitably collapsing into technofudalism, where the majority of humans become economically irrelevant redundant biomass due to geostrategic competition, massive capital investment, and rational economic automation.

## Argument

The trajectory toward **[[technofudalism]]** is overdetermined by three converging forces. First, US-China geostrategic competition ensures AI and robotics are treated as first-class political priorities, accelerating automation beyond human capability. Second, capitalism drives the largest private mega-project in history, with trillions spent on AI infrastructure by 2030, eclipsing the Apollo and Manhattan projects. Third, rational economic incentives create an attractor state where businesses and governments inevitably choose cheaper, faster automated production over human labor. 

Historically, rights are not natural but secured through coercive force; without collective leverage, humans risk becoming **[[redundant-biomass]]** rather than a protected class. The speaker rejects the 'useless class' narrative as too generous, noting that marginalized groups historically suffered lethal exploitation when deemed disposable. While automation promises abundance in cancer research and space exploration, it simultaneously renders humans structurally irrelevant. The only remaining leverage is the collective veto of human labor, which is being lost inch by inch to automation. The path forward requires recognizing that rights are legal fictions sustained by power, not goodwill.

## Counterpoints

- The 'useless class' or 'permanent underclass' labels are too generous because they imply civic standing that may not exist.
- Western stability is an illusion; human nature reasserts itself when push comes to shove, as seen in historical atrocities.
- Enlightenment-era natural rights are legal fictions written after bloodshed, not inherent guarantees.
- Tech optimism about abundance is valid, but it ignores the political irrelevance of the populace.
- The British Empire's indifference to Bengali starvation proves elites will not save the redundant.
