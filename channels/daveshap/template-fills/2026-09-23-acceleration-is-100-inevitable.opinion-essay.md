## Thesis

Acceleration of AI is 100% inevitable because geopolitical competition and government mandates prioritize superintelligence development over safety regulation.

## Argument

The narrative that deceleration is possible is false; the trajectory is locked by structural forces. First, major labs like OpenAI and Anthropic have spent years pushing a "doom narrative," yet the US government has explicitly refused to regulate them, instead threatening antitrust lawsuits if they collude to slow down. Second, the creation of an AI Force—a government department tasked with creating superintelligence—directly contradicts safety goals. This aligns with predictions from the AI2027 paper that state-level projects would accelerate by 2027. Third, international agreements, such as those signed by 27 nations, focus only on keeping AI under human control, not on slowing development. Finally, political dynamics are shifting: Trump’s renaming of the mission to "super intelligence" signals a lean-in approach, while Democrats position themselves as the anti-AI party ahead of midterms. However, this political stance misprices reality; while voters express hatred for AI, their revealed preferences show widespread adoption by knowledge workers. The upcoming election is effectively a referendum on this inevitable acceleration.

## Counterpoints

- Labs like OpenAI and Anthropic have pushed a 'doom narrative' to slow progress.
- International agreements exist to ensure AI remains under human control.
- Democrats are positioning themselves as the anti-AI party for political gain.
