## Thesis

The gap between AI's technical potential and enterprise adoption is widening because Fortune 500s are paralyzed by security, liability, and risk aversion, leaving laggards as dead men walking.

## Argument

The paradigm has shifted from autocomplete to chatbots and now to autonomous agents like OpenClaw, yet the deployment gap is widening. While early adopters leverage these tools for massive efficiency gains, Fortune 500s are at least 18 months away from deploying anything resembling autonomous agents. This lag is driven by cybersecurity fears, insurance liability gaps, and a lack of executive sponsorship. Without CEO-level edicts, organizations remain risk-averse, treating AI as shadow IT rather than a first-class asset. Those dragging their feet are dead men walking, destined to follow the path of Borders, while rapid adopters pull dramatically ahead.

## Counterpoints

- CFOs struggle to track ROI and fear employees will just get lazier if work is done faster.
- Insurance companies currently refuse to underwrite AI, creating an intolerable financial risk.
- Legal and security teams view autonomous agents as functionally malware due to prompt injection risks.
- Some roles, like trucking or face-to-face sales, have limited immediate use cases for chatbots.
