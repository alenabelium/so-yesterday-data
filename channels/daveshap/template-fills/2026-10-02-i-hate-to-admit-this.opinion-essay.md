## Thesis

AI and the post-industrial economy will force people to abandon 'slave morality'—defining identity through service—and adopt self-determined values once financial security is decoupled from work.

## Argument

The mechanism is the shift in material conditions. Today, survival requires selling your time: you need money for housing, food, and quality goods, so you must play by society's rules. This creates a real threat of compulsion, and out of that compulsion grows a moral structure built on duty—what Nietzsche called 'slave morality.' Your identity becomes the services you provide, and you internalize the judgment that you owe your time to the company or society. I felt this myself, writing at 1:30 on a Wednesday, and I caught my inner Panopticon telling me I should be productive, that I was doing something forbidden. I saw elderly men playing golf and thought they must feel like they're missing out—then realized that was the slave morality talking, saying they owe something to society.

But the post-industrial economy breaks this link. With AI, automation, and robotics, we can reach a level of financial security where you don't owe anyone anything—no threat of being fired, no compulsion. Imagine passive income from UBI, universal basic capital, citizen dividends, or sovereign wealth funds, earning $60,000–70,000 a year without lifting a finger. At that point, the moral structure built on duty becomes obsolete. The task ahead is to rid everyone of this internalized slave morality and realize: I don't owe anyone anything. You must free yourself from the structure of duties and service and instead determine your own values—master morality. This is the ontological shift AI forces: not just a change in jobs, but a change in how we define ourselves.

## Counterpoints

- The term 'slave morality' is offensive and has not survived well over time.
- Society's Panopticon says you owe your time to the company or society and must work off your maintenance.
- Some critics say: 'Dave can't even handle his dog; what makes you think we can control AI?'
- Nietzsche is a philosopher I generally criticize, and he didn't even originate this idea—he popularized it.
