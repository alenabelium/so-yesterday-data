## Thesis

AI and the post-industrial economy will force people to abandon 'slave morality'—defining identity through service—and adopt self-determined values.

## Argument

The mechanism is economic. Right now, material insecurity compels you to play by society's rules: you must work, earn, and serve to survive. That's the root of 'slave morality'—building your entire moral structure on duty. But once AI, automation, and universal basic income decouple financial security from work, the threat of compulsion disappears. You no longer owe anyone anything. The internalized Panopticon—that voice saying you must be productive at 1:30 PM on a Wednesday—loses its power. The shift isn't just about money; it's ontological. Your mental model of the world, your identity, must change. You have to shed the duty-based framework and embrace 'master morality': deciding for yourself what makes sense, setting your own values. This is uncomfortable, even offensive, but it's the necessary evolution. The elderly men playing golf mid-week aren't slackers; they're the vanguard of a post-work ontology. We'll all need to learn that freedom, not service, is the default.

## Counterpoints

- The term 'slave morality' is offensive and has not survived well over time.
- Society's Panopticon insists you owe your time and must work off your maintenance.
- Some critics say: 'Dave can't even handle his dog; what makes you think we can control AI?'
