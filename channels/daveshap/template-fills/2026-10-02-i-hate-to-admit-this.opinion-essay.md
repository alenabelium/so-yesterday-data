## Thesis

AI and the post-industrial economy will force people to abandon 'slave morality'—defining identity through service—and adopt self-determined values.

## Argument

The mechanism is economic: material security decouples survival from labor. Currently, financial necessity compels duty-based identity, but with universal basic income or capital, that compulsion vanishes. The host illustrates this through his own 'Panopticon'—the internalized societal judgment that he should be productive at 1:30 PM on a Wednesday. He contrasts this with elderly golfers, who, in a post-scarcity world, owe nothing and thus embody 'master morality.' The shift requires shedding internalized obligations, not just changing policies. This is a philosophical, not technical, challenge.

## Counterpoints

- The term 'slave morality' is offensive and has not survived well over time.
- Critics question the feasibility of controlling AI, citing the host's inability to control his dog.
- The host admits he is generally critical of Nietzsche but finds this one idea compelling.
