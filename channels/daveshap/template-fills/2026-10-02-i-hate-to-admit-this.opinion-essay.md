## Thesis

AI and post-industrial economics will force a shift from slave morality to self-determined values, and we must shed internalized duty.

## Argument

The mechanism is the decoupling of financial security from work. As AI, automation, and robotics eliminate the need for universal labor, the threat of compulsion—the fear of losing income and thus survival—dissolves. This threat is what enforces slave morality, where identity is built on service and duty to others. With universal basic income or similar mechanisms providing material security, the moral imperative to work loses its coercive power. People will need to consciously reject the internalized Panopticon that says they owe their time and effort to society. The shift is not automatic; it requires a deliberate re-evaluation of values, moving from externally imposed duty to self-determined purpose. This is the core challenge of the post-industrial transition.

## Counterpoints

- The term 'slave morality' is offensive and has not survived well over time.
- Some critics argue that if Dave can't handle his dog, he can't control AI.
- People may still feel they owe society even with financial security.
