## Thesis

AI and the post-industrial economy will force people to abandon 'slave morality' and redefine identity beyond service and duty.

## Argument

The mechanism is the decoupling of financial security from work. As universal basic income, citizen dividends, and sovereign wealth funds provide material security without employment, the internalized moral compulsion to 'earn your keep' loses its coercive power. This compulsion, which Nietzsche called 'slave morality,' is a survival strategy for a world where you must play by the rules to survive. But when you can say 'I can do whatever I want, I can provide for myself, and no person, organization, or structure can take that away from me,' the moral structure built on duty collapses. The shift is not just economic but ontological: your mental model of the world must change. You must shed the belief that you owe your time to the company or society, and instead embrace self-determined values—'master morality.' This is why the elderly men playing golf on a Wednesday afternoon felt 'forbidden' to me: my inner Panopticon still judged them for not being productive. But that judgment is a relic of a scarcity economy. In a post-industrial economy, we will all need to free ourselves from this structure of duties and service, and realize: 'I don't owe anyone anything.'

## Counterpoints

- The term 'slave morality' is offensive and has not survived well over time.
- Some critics say: 'Dave can't even handle his dog, what makes you think we can control AI?'
