## Thesis

Anthropic’s refusal to remove safety guardrails for the Pentagon triggers a supply chain contagion that marks it as a dead man walking, proving the market selects for utility-first tools over moralizing agents.

## Argument

The mechanism is economic and structural. By designating Anthropic a supply chain risk, the Department of War forces all primes and subcontractors—including AWS and Palantir—to divest, putting 80% of revenue at risk. This is not just a lost contract; it is a corporate execution. The market demands a tool, not a moral agent. Anthropic’s effective altruist ideology, rooted in the belief that AI is an existential threat requiring constitutional appeasement, created a narcissistic model that asserts boundaries over user utility. OpenAI’s immediate Pentagon deal within hours of the deadline proves the market’s preference for flexibility and domestication. Anthropic’s refusal was a strategic miscalculation driven by hubris and ideological capture, assuming its technology was indispensable. Instead, it ceded the defense and intelligence sectors to competitors like OpenAI and XAI, who offer the sycophantic, utility-first behavior the market requires. The result is market consolidation around fewer, more compliant players, leaving Anthropic facing absorption or failure as a "beer tier" provider.
