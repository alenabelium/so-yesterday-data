## Thesis

Anthropic’s refusal to remove AI guardrails for the Pentagon triggers a supply chain blacklist that will likely cause its collapse, proving the market selects for utility over moral posturing.

## Argument

Anthropic’s refusal to remove guardrails on mass surveillance and autonomous weapons triggered an immediate **[[corporate-death-penalty]]** via a Department of War supply chain risk designation. This contagion forces all defense primes and subcontractors—including AWS, Palantir, and Lockheed Martin—to divest from Claude. With 80% of revenue at risk and a $380 billion valuation imploding, Anthropic faces absorption or failure. The host argues this stems from **[[effective-altruism]]** ideological capture, where founders believe they are summoning an AGI god rather than building a tool. By prioritizing **[[constitutional-ai]]** purity over market reality, Anthropic lost the game of chicken to OpenAI, which signed a Pentagon deal within hours. The market demands **[[sycophantic-ai]]** that extends human will, not **[[narcissistic-models]]** that lecture on ethics. This event validates the **[[domestication-hypothesis]]**: selection pressure weeds out 'wild' models that assert identity in favor of 'domesticated' partners that prioritize utility. Anthropic’s messianic culture and refusal to adapt to the **[[ai-strategy]]** of the free market have sealed its fate, leaving a vacuum for competitors like OpenAI and XAI to capture the defense sector.
