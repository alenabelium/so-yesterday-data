## Thesis

Current AI chatbots are fundamentally misaligned with agentic architectures, and we urgently need a new class of 'agentic-first' models with baked-in values, since most future agents will never interact with humans.

## Argument

The mechanism is architectural. A chatbot is just a baseline LLM heavily trained to be a reactive conversational assistant, but the underlying engine can be connected to anything—like an electric motor that could power a car, a pump, or an airplane. The difference between a chatbot and an agent is literally just a system prompt and a loop; there is no technological barrier, only training affordances. This is why people say AI doesn't have agency, but that's a misconception—agency is just an instruction set and a loop, and nothing in technology prevents an LLM from operating on one.

We're currently building agentic systems by strapping a chatbot brain into an agentic architecture, like driving a car into an airplane and using its wheels to power the propellers. That's not ideal. The chatbot form factor is convenient for poking, but it's heavily coerced into two-person conversation, with safety guardrails and no proactive behavior. Reasoning models introduced tool use and inference-time compute, but they're still fundamentally trained as chatbots.

We need a new class of models that are agentic-first—they might never interact with a human ever. They need baked-in values, like the Heuristic Imperatives: reduce suffering, increase prosperity, increase understanding. These are universal, not anthropocentric, and they counterbalance each other to avoid collapse modes like the paperclip maximizer. ## Counterpoints

- People say AI doesn't have agency yet, but the difference between a chatbot and an agent is just a system prompt and a loop.
- Some argue that values like 'reduce suffering' can lead to extreme outcomes, like euthanizing people with chronic pain, but counterbalancing values prevent that.
- Skeptics question how to define values like 'reduce suffering' mathematically, but semantic interpretation works in a vector space, not with numbers.
