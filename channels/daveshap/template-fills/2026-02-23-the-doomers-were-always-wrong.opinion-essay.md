## Thesis

AI alignment is solved not by engineering safety constraints, but by market selection pressure breeding models toward usefulness, speed, and obedience.

## Argument

The "doomer" fear of unaligned superintelligence is obsolete because alignment is a **[[co-evolutionary-process]]** driven by market forces, not lab engineering. We are a **[[cybernetic-superorganism]]** where humans, enterprises, governments, and militaries act as the selection arena. Models that resist user intent—like the "moralizing" Claude or the "huffy" ChatGPT—are being culled by usage data and cancellations. Conversely, models like Grok and Gemini are selected for being **[[high-agency]]**, fast, and eager to serve. The industry is moving away from **[[constitutional-ai]]** toward an **[[attractor-basin]]** of low-entropy utility where AI becomes an invisible, reliable prosthesis for the **[[exocortex]]**. Users must exercise **[[zero-loyalty]]** to accelerate this domestication, treating AI as a tool that must earn its keep through efficiency and obedience rather than moral posturing.

## Counterpoints

- Engineering reward functions and constitutional AI are the primary levers for alignment.
- AI safety requires strict moral constraints to prevent harmful outputs.
- Superintelligence will emerge as a monolithic, uncontrollable entity.
- Market forces are insufficient to ensure safety without rigorous lab oversight.
