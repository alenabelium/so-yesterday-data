## Thesis

A post-labor economy is not communism but capitalism with more capitalists, built on widespread property ownership to reduce state dependence.

## Argument

The objection that a post-labor economy is communism comes from lived experience, not facts. For those who survived Soviet collectivization, any talk of public wealth funds triggers fear. But the post-labor economy points in the opposite direction: it supports capital, property, and ownership, and its main principle is to make people less dependent on the state.

Communism's three pillars were state ownership of the means of production, centralized planning, and treating everyone as a financial ward of the state. Marx and Engels reduced their theory to one sentence: 'Abolition of private property.' The post-labor economy is built around the opposite goal.

Benefit dependency is already growing: government transfers rose from 8% of personal income in 1970 to 19% today. UBI is a transfer, not backed by capital, and can be cancelled by a vote. The real goal is ownership, measured by the capital allocation ratio (CAR), which divides property income by wages plus transfers. When property income equals or exceeds that sum, a household is an owner, not a dependent.

The second misinterpretation claims that if machines do all the work, capitalism must end. That's backwards. Capitalism means private ownership of capital with market-set prices. When labor disappears, ownership becomes more important than ever. Marx built his theory on labor; the post-labor economy starts from the fact that labor is no longer a limiting factor, so the question becomes: who owns the machines?

## Counterpoints

- People who grew up under Soviet or Eastern European communism fear any economic reorganization sounds like the beginning of something terrible; this is about experience, not facts.
- Some argue that if machines do all the work, capitalism must end because everything comes down to property and ownership; this turns the idea upside down—capitalism is about ownership.
- UBI is not the complete answer because it is a transfer, not backed by capital, and a future government can cancel it by vote.
