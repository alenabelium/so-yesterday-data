## Thesis

The singularity feels more real now because people are hitting a threshold effect where they realize AI is smarter than them and improving exponentially, triggering a phase change in how they relate to the technology.

## Argument

The mechanism is threshold recognition. Once people see AI outperform them on coding and math, they realize it's better and will stay better, causing a psychological phase change. This is delayed by normalcy bias: our chimpanzee brains evolved for local, geometric worlds and lack machinery for global, exponential thinking. We don't have object permanence for hyperobjects like the singularity. So most people confidently declare the developer will always be useful, then get surprised when the hockey stick goes vertical. The Overton window has shifted dramatically, especially after Claude Code 4.5 and OpenAI's Codex running overnight, leading to the 'did we just get AGI for coding?' sentiment. The next frontier is training models to identify cognitive lacunae—gaps in their own knowledge—which will drive scientific novelty. Once models can sense and articulate these gaps, they'll accelerate science itself, making 2026 the year AI starts to accelerate science.

## Counterpoints

- Some say AI will never get better or that it's just fetching from a database, but half of ChatGPT users don't understand it's generating responses in real time.
- Doomers insist on X-risk, but serious AI safety conversation has moved on; even Hinton and Tegmark have distanced themselves from hardline doomers.
- AGI is a useless term with many definitions; some require physical skills, but underactuated robots show physical navigation requires little intelligence.
