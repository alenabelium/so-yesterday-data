## Thesis

OpenAI and Anthropic are panicking because they face no government regulation, rising open-source competition from China, and unsustainable economics that will eventually force them to be acquired or go bankrupt.

## Argument

The panic is visible in the aftermath of OpenAI's Dev Day, which was polished but lacked fundamental innovation. Immediately after, all heads of large companies signed a self-regulation pledge with Trump, signaling that the US government will not regulate AI. This means Dario and the security team lost. Instead, the market, the Ministry of Justice, and the courts will sort it out. OpenAI and Anthropic see the writing on the wall: they won't get the regulatory moat they wanted, and China keeps releasing open-source models. Meanwhile, competition is exploding—Grok, Meta's Muse, Google's Gemini 4 Pro (if leaks are true), and others. Even OpenAI's own Thibaut is undermining credibility with tweets about ChatGPT Pro's reduced value. The economics are the real problem. They operate at a loss, subsidizing tokens, which is unsustainable. The industry needs $6 trillion a year by 2030 to justify data centers, but only ~3% of Americans pay for AI subscriptions. Reddit laughs at Anthropic's $46 billion loss. The margin isn't there. The real winners will be hardware companies—Nvidia, TSMC, ASML, Intel, Qualcomm, AMD—because they sell the picks and shovels. Selling tokens is just renting GPUs, a middleman business that gets eliminated. When local models run on your own hardware, you won't need tokens. Hardware is the master key. A company losing tens of billions annually has no long-term future. Tesla and SpaceX lost money for 15 years, but can we look that far ahead?
