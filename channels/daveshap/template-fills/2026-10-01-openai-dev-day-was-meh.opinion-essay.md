## Thesis

OpenAI and Anthropic are panicking because they face no government regulation, rising open-source competition from China, and unsustainable economics that will likely end in acquisition or bankruptcy.

## Argument

The panic is visible in the aftermath of OpenAI's Dev Day, which was polished but lacked fundamental innovation. Immediately after, the heads of large companies signed a pledge of self-regulation with Trump, signaling that the US government will not regulate AI. This means Dario and the security team lost the regulatory battle. Instead, the market, the Ministry of Justice, and the courts will sort things out. Meanwhile, China continues to release open-source models, and competitors like Grok, Meta, and Google's Gemini are closing in. The economics are also dire: OpenAI and Anthropic operate at a loss, subsidizing tokens, and the industry may need $6 trillion a year by 2030 to justify data center construction. With only about 3% of Americans paying for AI subscriptions, the numbers don't add up. The real winners will be hardware companies like Nvidia, TSMC, and AMD, because they control the bottleneck. Selling tokens is just renting GPUs, making AI companies middlemen, and middlemen are eventually eliminated. In a few years, you'll run models locally on your own hardware, making cloud token sales obsolete. A company losing tens of billions annually has no long-term future, unless acquired by a larger firm.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that companies like Tesla and SpaceX lost money for 10-15 years and eventually succeeded, so AI companies might too.
- Gary Marcus and others have repeatedly bet on OpenAI going bankrupt, yet it still exists, suggesting the hypothesis might be wrong.
