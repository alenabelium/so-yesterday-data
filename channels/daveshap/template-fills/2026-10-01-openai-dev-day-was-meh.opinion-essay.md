## Thesis

OpenAI and Anthropic are panicking because the US government won't regulate AI, open-source competition is rising, and their token-selling business model is economically unsustainable.

## Argument

The panic is visible in the aftermath of OpenAI's Dev Day. The day after, all the heads of large companies signed a pledge of self-regulation with Trump, signaling that the government won't regulate. Dario and the security team lost. The US said, 'Let the market figure it out.' Meanwhile, China keeps releasing open-source models, and competitors like Grok, Meta, and possibly Gemini 4 Pro are closing in. OpenAI and Anthropic enjoyed maybe two years of dominance, but that's ending. They're also losing money on every token, subsidizing our usage. The numbers are grim: AI companies need to make $6 trillion a year by 2030 to justify data center costs, but only about 3% of Americans have a paid AI subscription. Reddit laughs at Anthropic's IPO reports—revenue skyrockets, but they operate at a $46 billion loss. The real winners will be hardware companies like Nvidia, TSMC, ASML, Intel, Qualcomm, and AMD. They're the lifeline because demand for intelligence is insatiable. Selling tokens is just renting GPUs—you're a middleman, and middlemen get eliminated. In a few years, you'll buy a graphics card and run models locally with unlimited tokens. Hardware is the master key. A company losing tens of billions annually has no long-term future. Tesla and SpaceX lost money for years, but they eventually succeeded. Still, I predict Anthropic and OpenAI will be acquired by larger companies, or if Nvidia releases an open-source model that runs better on their hardware, they might go bankrupt. I'm not betting on it—Gary Marcus and others have been wrong before—but these are my thoughts.

## Counterpoints

- Some say AI companies could justify $6 trillion a year by 2030, but that's $1,000 per person per year, and most will come from businesses, governments, and militaries, not regular subscriptions.
- Tesla and SpaceX lost money for 10-15 years but eventually became successful, so maybe AI companies can too.
- Gary Marcus and others have repeatedly bet on OpenAI going bankrupt, and they're still here, so my hypothesis might be wrong.
