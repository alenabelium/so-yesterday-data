## Thesis

OpenAI and Anthropic are panicking because they won't get the regulatory moat they wanted, and open-source competition plus unsustainable economics will eventually make them obsolete.

## Argument

The panic is visible in the aftermath of OpenAI's Dev Day: instead of the government stepping in to regulate, the US has explicitly chosen self-regulation, leaving the labs to fend for themselves. Meanwhile, China keeps releasing open-source models, and new competitors like Grok and Meta's Muse are entering the arena, with Google's Gemini 4 Pro possibly on the horizon. The economics are the real problem: both OpenAI and Anthropic operate at a loss, subsidizing tokens, and the industry would need to generate $6 trillion a year by 2030 to justify data center spending—a figure that seems impossible given that only about 3% of Americans pay for AI subscriptions. The margin is unsustainable, and the only long-term winners will be hardware companies like Nvidia, TSMC, and AMD, because the real demand is for chips and local compute, not for token-selling middlemen. If Nvidia releases an open-source model that runs better on their own hardware, people will just buy GPUs and run models locally, bypassing cloud services entirely. A company losing tens of billions annually has no long-term future, though acquisition by a larger firm is possible. I'm not betting on bankruptcy—Gary Marcus has been wrong before—but the writing is on the wall.

## Counterpoints

- Some might say I'm crazy or wrong, but I see the panic clearly.
- Tesla and SpaceX lost money for 10-15 years and eventually succeeded, so maybe these companies can too.
- Gary Marcus and others have repeatedly bet on OpenAI going bankrupt, and they're still here, so my hypothesis might be wrong.
