## Thesis

OpenAI and Anthropic are panicking because they face unregulated markets, open-source competition from China, and unsustainable economics, making their long-term survival doubtful.

## Argument

The panic is visible in the aftermath of OpenAI's Dev Day, which was polished but lacked fundamental innovation. The real trigger was the pledge of self-regulation signed by tech leaders with the Trump administration, signaling that the US government will not regulate AI. This means Dario and the safety team lost, and the market will decide. Meanwhile, China keeps releasing open-source models, and competitors like Grok, Meta, and Google's Gemini are closing in. The economics are also shaky: OpenAI and Anthropic operate at massive losses, subsidizing tokens, and the $6 trillion revenue target by 2030 seems unrealistic given that only about 3% of Americans pay for AI. The margin is unsustainable, and the real winners may be hardware companies like Nvidia, TSMC, and AMD, because selling tokens is just renting GPUs—a middleman business that gets eliminated. If models become a commodity and run locally on your own hardware, cloud token sales could collapse. A company losing tens of billions annually has no long-term future, though exceptions like Tesla exist. Acquisition by a larger company is a possible outcome, but if Nvidia releases an open-source model that runs better on their chips, OpenAI and Anthropic could go bankrupt. I'm not betting on it, given past predictions of OpenAI's demise, but these are my thoughts.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that OpenAI and Anthropic are not panicking, just adapting to market realities.
- The $6 trillion revenue target by 2030 is seen as achievable by some, given business and government demand.
- Companies like Tesla and SpaceX lost money for years before becoming profitable, so losses alone don't doom AI firms.
- Gary Marcus and others have repeatedly predicted OpenAI's bankruptcy, yet it still exists, suggesting my hypothesis may be wrong.
