## Thesis

Anthropic's Mythos model creates a short-term structural advantage for attackers, but defenders hold the long-term home-field edge as AI hardens enterprise security, leaving humans as the persistent weakest link.

## Argument

Anthropic's upcoming Mythos model, rumored to be a 10 trillion parameter system, represents a step change in capability with benchmarks jumping 15-20% over previous iterations. The [[glass-wing]] project gives early access to ~40 major tech firms to patch vulnerabilities, highlighting the immediate [[cybersecurity-risk]]. Mythos has already found thousands of zero-day exploits, including a 27-year-old FreeBSD vulnerability and a 16-year-old FFmpeg exploit. While this provides attackers with a short-term advantage due to faster adaptation, defenders have the home-field advantage long-term. As a former IT infrastructure engineer, the host argues that AI tools are integrated into security vendor products, automated penetration testing, and email inspection. The [[defense-in-depth]] model relies on vendors like Cisco and Microsoft using these same tools to harden platforms. Humans remain the weakest link, with breaches often stemming from [[social-engineering]] or [[phishing]] rather than technical exploits. AI automates security behaviors, raising the standard for consistency and reducing the [[pebcak]] errors that humans introduce. Ultimately, AI is just a new automation tool for enterprise security, not a reason for doom.

## Counterpoints

- Attackers adapt more quickly in the short term, creating a structural advantage for them before defenders integrate AI tools.
- Mythos found critical zero-day exploits in legacy systems like FreeBSD and FFmpeg, proving the immediate severity of the risk.
- Humans are consistently the weakest link in security, with breaches often caused by phishing or human error rather than technical failures.
