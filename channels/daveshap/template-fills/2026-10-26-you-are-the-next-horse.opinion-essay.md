## Thesis

Most human workers will be replaced by AI and machines, just as horses were, because machines are better, faster, cheaper, and safer, and the only escape is owning capital.

## Argument

The mechanism is historical precedent. Horses were the best source of kinetic energy for centuries, but machines replaced them job by job because they were better, faster, cheaper, and safer. The same rule applies to human labor. Most jobs are menial: eight of the ten largest occupations pay below the median wage, and 59% of jobs require only a high school diploma or less. Economists argue humans are not horses because we can move to new, complex tasks, but those tasks are increasingly intellectual, and AI is learning to do them. McKinsey estimates that already-demonstrated technology could automate 57% of American working hours. The phrase 'AI won't take your job, someone using AI will' is flawed because it assumes machines always need operators, but self-driving cars are already replacing drivers. The only real difference is that humans can own capital. The post-labor economy is one where human labor is no longer a limiting factor, and owning a stake in machines is the key to thriving.

## Counterpoints

- Economists argue humans are not horses because we have a comparative advantage in new and complex tasks.
- The phrase 'AI won't take your job, someone using AI will' suggests humans will always operate machines.
- Some economists say people will always move to new and more difficult tasks, as they have for 200 years.
