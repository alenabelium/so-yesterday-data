## Thesis

AI and robotics will end capitalism as we know it by severing the link between wages and household income, forcing a new economic order based on capital participation and generative mutualism.

## Argument

The mechanism is economic. A consumption-based economy doesn't need labor; it needs household income to drive demand. Right now, wages are the primary distribution mechanism, but automation is decoupling labor from output, and this will only accelerate. The Reddit hypothetical—AI owns everything, no one works, no one buys—is exactly the crash scenario. To avoid it, we must shift household income from wages to capital. Transfers like UBI are necessary but insufficient; they create an arbitrary floor and lose price signals. The real solution is broadening capital participation through sovereign wealth funds, like Norway's, and employee ownership structures. This is not just about economics; it's about the social contract. The old deal—people provide labor and revenue, the state provides protection and prosperity—breaks down when machines replace labor. The elites' role becomes pure coordination, a Schelling point for attention and preference. But in an internet era with AI, we can coordinate without them. The new narrative is generative mutualism: cooperation and endosymbiosis are superior strategies, as shown by the evolution of life itself. Rational self-interest still applies—billionaires need paying customers, and there are more of us than them. So we'll create policies that let households accumulate wealth from property, not wages, and that's how capitalism as we know it ends.

## Counterpoints

- UBI is a common first intuition, but it's a transfer that becomes a political dogfight and loses price signals.
- Some might ask how broadening capital participation works if everyone has rental properties—who rents?
- The fear that elites won't need us once AI and robots replace labor, breaking the social contract.
- Skepticism about Mr. Beast's ethics, though the point is his attention-preference coordination, not his morality.
