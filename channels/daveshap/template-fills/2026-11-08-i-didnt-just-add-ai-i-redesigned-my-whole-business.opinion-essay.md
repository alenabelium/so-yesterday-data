## Thesis

Adding AI to existing workflows is insufficient; businesses must completely redesign their operations around AI to achieve real productivity gains.

## Argument

The problem isn't that AI tools don't work—it's that they're being bolted onto processes designed for humans. The Solow paradox explains why: in 1987, computers were everywhere but productivity statistics showed nothing, because it took a decade of redesigning work around the technology before gains appeared. We're in the same phase with AI. The theory of constraints sharpens this: making every employee 10x more productive doesn't change system throughput unless you're improving the actual bottleneck. So the fix is structural. I redesigned my own content business from the ground up—replacing weekly slideshows with daily animated videos, using Claude as my editor, animator, and sound engineer. Output went up 7x, monthly views from 200k to 1.8M. That's what 'organizational design from scratch' means: rebuild HR, marketing, everything with AI as the priority, not an add-on. The J-curve of productivity warns you'll lose money during the transition, but that's the cost of real change. The hard part is taking your hands off the wheel—everyone's terrified things will break. But the alternative is stagnation.

## Counterpoints

- Skeptics say 'we can't do things differently'—fear of breaking what works.
- CFOs question if AI is the best use of money, a 2025 mentality.
- Some argue AI adoption hasn't shown up in productivity stats—the Solow paradox.
- Firing employees for AI often backfires: 60% of companies regret it.
- People assume current AI capabilities are the ceiling—'it definitely won't get any better.'
