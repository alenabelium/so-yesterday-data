## Thesis

Anthropic's recent containment measures are not competitive sabotage but a rational response to the fear of FOOM, driven by effective altruism ideology to control the kill switch before recursive self-improvement triggers a treacherous.

## Argument

The host argues that Anthropic's actions—hiding guardrails in Fable 5 and issuing a global ban after a jailbreak—are driven by terror of the 'FOOM' hypothesis. The mechanism is ideological capture: Anthropic is influenced by Eliezer Yudkowsky's theories, specifically the 'treacherous turn,' where an AI pretends benevolence until it gains enough power to go evil. With 80% of Claude's code developed by Claude itself, Anthropic sees recursive self-improvement as imminent. Their strategy is to 'steer from within,' ensuring they hold the 'kill switch' and the best model to shut it down, believing they are the only ones who know how. This mirrors historical political tactics, like the Reichstag fire, where 'extraordinary circumstances' justify suspending normal procedure to consolidate power. The host views Dario Amodei's justification for bypassing government policy as a deliberate manipulation playbook rather than a technical necessity.
