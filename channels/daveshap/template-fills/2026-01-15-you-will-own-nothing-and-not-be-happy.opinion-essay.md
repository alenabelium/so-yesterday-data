## Thesis

Technofeudalism is the default trajectory of AI-driven capitalism, but a progressive wealth tax funding sovereign wealth funds can redirect civilization toward technoabundance.

## Argument

The host identifies five attractor states for the AI era: extinction, collapse, technofeudalism, stagnation, and technoabundance. He dismisses extinction and civilizational collapse as unlikely due to robust modern institutions and the resilience of societal memory. He rejects the "nothing ever happens" scenario as a product of normalcy bias, noting that historical evidence shows constant technological disruption. The core argument is that technofeudalism—a high-tech, low-life cyberpunk dystopia—is the current default trajectory, driven by decades of neoliberal policies that systematically undermined labor power in favor of corporate efficiency. This trajectory is not inevitable but is the result of optimizing for corporate growth over civic stability. To pivot toward technoabundance, the host proposes a progressive wealth tax (1% on $1-5M, up to 5-7% on >$1B) directed into sovereign wealth funds. This creates a "win-win-win": the rich retain their wealth while government gains funding and households receive capital-based income, aligning elite incentives with broad prosperity without requiring revolutionary upheaval.

## Counterpoints

- Extinction via AI or bioweapons is unlikely because robust institutions prevent catastrophic failures and AI needs humans to build it.
- Civilizational collapse is a myth; modern societies are too robust and interconnected to truly vanish like ancient civilizations.
- "Nothing ever happens" is a cognitive bias; history proves that technology always disrupts labor and social structures.
- Strong property rights are necessary for innovation, but AI tools like AlphaFold may eventually make traditional intellectual property obsolete.
