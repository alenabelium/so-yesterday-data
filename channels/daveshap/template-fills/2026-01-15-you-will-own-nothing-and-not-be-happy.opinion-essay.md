## Thesis

The default trajectory of AI is technofeudalism, but a progressive wealth tax funding sovereign wealth funds can redirect civilization toward technoabundance.

## Argument

Shapiro identifies five attractor states for the AI era: extinction, collapse, technofeudalism, stagnation, and technoabundance. He dismisses extinction and civilizational collapse as unlikely due to robust modern institutions and historical resilience, though ecological collapse remains a risk. He argues that "nothing ever happens" is contradicted by historical evidence of constant technological disruption. The real danger is technofeudalism—a high-tech, low-life scenario driven by neoliberalism’s systematic undermining of labor power and the rise of mega-corporations. This trajectory is already visible in wealth concentration and housing unaffordability. To pivot toward technoabundance, Shapiro proposes a progressive wealth tax (1% on $1-5M, up to 5-7% above $1B) directed into sovereign wealth funds. This creates a win-win-win: the rich get richer via increased consumer demand, governments gain funding, and households receive capital-based income, thereby aligning incentives and preventing elite capture.

## Counterpoints

- Extinction via AI or bioweapons is unlikely because robust institutions prevent catastrophic failures.
- Civilizational collapse is a myth; modern society is too robust and interconnected to truly fall.
- "Nothing ever happens" is a result of normalcy bias, not historical reality, as technology constantly disrupts norms.
- Wealth inequality is often rationalized as necessary for innovation, but AI obviates traditional IP moats.
- Revolution is dangerous and inefficient; democracy’s meta-governance allows for reform without collapse.
