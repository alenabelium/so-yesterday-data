## Thesis

The convergence of recursive self-improvement capabilities and terminal race dynamics guarantees an automation cliff within 36 months, defined not by mass layoffs but by the permanent vanishing of entry-level positions.

## Argument

The timeline for AGI has collapsed to 2027-2028 because the five components of recursive self-improvement are rapidly converging. Algorithmic research, data generation, and code writing are largely solved; models now match gold-medal math performance and write production code. The remaining bottlenecks—model training and evaluation—are infrastructure challenges being solved by autonomous agents and synthetic data pipelines. This acceleration is enforced by a "terminal race condition" where sunk costs in infrastructure create a point of no return; no actor can pause without bankruptcy. Consequently, the economic impact is already visible in 2025’s GDP growth decoupling from job creation. The primary mechanism of disruption is the "vanishing ladder": entry-level roles are disappearing because they are never created, with new graduate job latency exceeding a year. We are entering a phase where AI shifts from optional augmentation to mandatory economic substitution, creating a steep automation cliff for white-collar workers.

## Counterpoints

- Objection: AGI is still decades away because models lack true reasoning or consciousness. Response: The definition of AGI is shifting to economic substitutability and autonomous task completion, not philosophical consciousness.
- Objection: Energy and hardware bottlenecks will halt progress. Response: The chip shortage is solved; high-bandwidth memory and energy demands are being addressed by market pivots and local micro-grids, not halted by them.
- Objection: The labor market will adjust and create new jobs. Response: Current data shows a "vanishing ladder" where entry-level positions are not being filled at all, indicating a structural break in the job market rather than a temporary adjustment.
