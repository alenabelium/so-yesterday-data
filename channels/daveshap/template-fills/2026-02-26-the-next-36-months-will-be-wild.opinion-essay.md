## Thesis

The convergence of recursive self-improvement capabilities and a terminal race condition will trigger an automation cliff in 2027-2028, defined by the vanishing of entry-level jobs rather than mass layoffs.

## Argument

The timeline for AGI has collapsed from decades to the 2027-2028 window, driven by a super-exponential increase in machine autonomy. Metrics like METER evals show autonomous task success rates doubling every 90 days, approaching week-long continuous operation. Recursive self-improvement requires five components: algorithmic research, data generation, code writing, model training, and model evaluation. The first three are largely solved; coding agents now outperform most developers, and synthetic data generation is viable. The remaining bottlenecks—training and evaluation—are infrastructure and alchemy challenges that AI agents will soon automate.

This acceleration is locked in by a 'terminal race condition' and a 'hail mary' sunk cost fallacy. With hundreds of billions invested in data centers and chips, no company or nation can pause without facing bankruptcy or geopolitical defeat. While energy demand creates an 'alligator jaw' crisis, the market is pivoting to micro-grids and nuclear solutions. The real societal impact is not immediate mass firing, but the 'vanishing ladder.' GDP growth is already decoupling from job creation, with new graduate job latency exceeding a year. The automation cliff is the disappearance of positions that were never created, signaling a secular regime of jobless growth.

## Counterpoints

- AGI is a linguistic construct; people will only recognize it when they see consciousness or human-like conversation, not just capability.
- High bandwidth memory and energy bottlenecks will halt progress before 2028.
- AI hype is overblown; LLMs are a dead end with limited real-world impact.
- We must slow down to address safety, alignment, and deployment risks.
