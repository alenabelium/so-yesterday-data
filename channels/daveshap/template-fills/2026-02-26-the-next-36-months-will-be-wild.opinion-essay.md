## Thesis

The next 36 months will witness the collapse of the labor market not through mass layoffs, but through the vanishing of entry-level positions, as AI agents cross the threshold of economic substitutability.

## Argument

The convergence of Sam Altman, Jensen Huang, and Dario Amodei around a 2027-2028 AGI window is driven by the rapid solution of five recursive self-improvement components. Algorithmic research, data generation, and code writing are largely solved; Claude and OpenClaw now outperform most developers. The remaining bottlenecks—model training and evaluation—are infrastructure and alchemy problems, not fundamental scientific barriers. We are in a "terminal race condition" where sunk costs in data centers create a point of no return; pausing is fatal. While energy demand creates an "alligator jaw" with production, local microgrids and nuclear restarts will bridge the gap. The real shock is structural: GDP growth is decoupling from job creation. We are entering a regime of "jobless growth" where the automation cliff is defined by ghost jobs—positions that are never filled because AI agents handle the work. Entry-level hiring is imploding, with new graduate job latency exceeding a year. This is not a temporary dip but a secular shift where AI becomes economically substitutable for human labor, rendering the traditional career ladder obsolete.

## Counterpoints

- AGI is a linguistic construct; we will only know it when we see consciousness or soul-like behavior.
- Energy demand will outstrip production, creating an insurmountable bottleneck for AI scaling.
- AI is hype with limited real-world impact; LLMs are a dead end and won't lead to superintelligence.
- We must slow down to address safety, alignment, and reliability bugs before deploying agents.
