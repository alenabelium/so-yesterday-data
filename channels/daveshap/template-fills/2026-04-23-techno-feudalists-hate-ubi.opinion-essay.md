## Thesis

The tech right's opposition to UBI is built on flawed premises, and UBI actually accelerates entropy generation, energy ascent, and human flourishing.

## Argument

The first flaw is historical: the tech right's own heroes—Gates, Jobs, Musk, Oppenheimer—came from financial security. Darwin only survived chronic illness because gentry income let him stay in bed for weeks, giving him decades to develop evolution. If the goal is maximum entropy generation, then universal financial security prevents the 'lost Einstein' phenomenon. UBI is not universal high income; $1,000–$2,000 a month barely covers rent, so incentives to strive and build remain. And a billion-dollar exit dwarfs any UBI check, so the incentive gradient for founders is untouched. On the Kardashev scale, UBI is a demand-side accelerant: more household spending forces more production, more energy use, and more innovation—critical in a demand-constrained economy. Mark Andre's 'work is good' is Protestant work ethic dressed as aristocracy: 'peasants must toil.' Meaningful striving is real, but wage slavery is a toxic prison that merely 'sort of' meets that need. The structural worry about 100% state dependence is fair, but current transfer dependence is 18%, not 100%, and nobody proposes UBI as the only income source. Gil's 'universal basic tokens' is just UBI with extra steps—a dollar is a token.

## Counterpoints

- UBI flattens incentive gradients, removing motivation to aim big.
- UBI leads to total state dependence, which is structurally bad.
- Technology always creates new jobs, so UBI is unnecessary.
- Work is inherently good and humans need it for meaning.
- UBI would reduce energy and entropy generation, hindering Kardashev ascent.
