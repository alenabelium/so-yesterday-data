## Thesis

America is too late to build sovereign wealth funds before the automation cliff; we must bridge the gap with massive transfer spending while accelerating private capital ownership.

## Argument

The core insight is that universal high income requires stacking non-wage income streams because wages are stalling. Labor share of national income has declined from 65% to 56-58% since the 1980s, and productivity has decoupled from compensation. As automation eliminates wage labor, the economy faces a deflationary death spiral if consumers have no income. Household income comes from three buckets: wages, capital, and transfers. To reach a median income of $300,000 by 2060, we must shift from wage dependence to capital and transfers. However, sovereign wealth funds take 25-30 years to mature, while the automation cliff arrives in the 2030s. This creates a dangerous gap that only transfer spending can bridge. We need to broaden private ownership through ESOPs, baby bonds, and auto-enrolled retirement accounts to make capital accumulation automatic. Without this, the rising tide will not lift all boats; instead, capital owners will thrive while wage earners are left behind. The fiscal system is already compensating for labor market failure with $4 trillion in transfers, but we must pivot to capital-based income to ensure long-term stability and alignment of interests between households and the economy.

## Counterpoints

- Automation creates new jobs; this is a temporary disruption.
- We should ban AI to preserve wage labor.
- Sovereign wealth funds can be built quickly enough.
- VAT or wealth taxes are the primary solution.
- Private markets will naturally solve the distribution problem.
