## Thesis

America is decades behind the curve on building sovereign wealth funds, creating a dangerous gap where automation destroys wages before capital income can mature, forcing a reliance on transfers as a stopgap.

## Argument

The path to **[[universal-high-income]]** requires stacking non-wage income streams to replace the stalling engine of wage labor. As **[[automation]]** renders human labor obsolete, the economy faces a **[[deflationary-death-spiral]]** because capital owners need consumers, not just employees. Today, household income is 82% wages, 13% transfers, and 5% capital. To hit $300k median income by 2060, we must flip this ratio, making capital the dominant source.

However, the math reveals a critical timing failure. Sovereign wealth funds take 25-30 years to mature. The US only recently ordered one, meaning its dividends won't arrive until the 2050s. But the **[[automation-cliff]]** hits in the 2030s. We are late. The gap between collapsing wages and maturing capital must be bridged by **[[universal-basic-income]]** and other transfer mechanisms. We must also accelerate private capital on-ramps like **[[esops]]**, **[[baby-bonds]]**, and **[[employee-ownership-trusts]]** to make wealth accumulation automatic. Without this portfolio approach, the fiscal system collapses as payroll taxes vanish.

## Counterpoints

- VAT or robot taxes are the primary solution.
- Automation will create new jobs we can't yet imagine.
- We should ban AI to preserve wage labor.
- Sovereign wealth funds are sufficient on their own.
