## Thesis

Household income must shift from wages to ownership-based streams like dividends and sovereign wealth funds, or the economy faces a deflationary death spiral as AI eliminates jobs.

## Argument

The mechanism is distribution. For centuries, wage labor was the primary way families got their share of the economy, but that path is narrowing. Since 1979, US productivity rose 94% while average salaries rose only 33%, and by 2026 workers received less than 53 cents of every dollar generated—the lowest share since 1947. The problem isn't the workplace; it's household income. The solution is to broaden ownership. We already have the building blocks: Alaska's oil fund has paid annual dividends since 1982, Norway's sovereign wealth fund is worth $1.9 trillion, and over 6,600 ESOPs in America plus 1,700 employee-owned trusts in Britain show that employee ownership works. The goal is a multi-level portfolio where each household gets income from many sources—UBI, state dividends, employee profit-sharing, cooperative bank dividends, baby bonds, and part-time work. None dominates, so losing one is painful but not catastrophic. Without consumer demand, mass production collapses into a deflationary death spiral. Even billionaires face a choice: a smaller share of a growing economy or a larger share of a shrinking one. The transition requires restructuring taxes, since two-thirds of federal revenue comes from income and payroll taxes on wages. New taxes must target profits from AI and automation. And the resource worry is misplaced—if AI can take all jobs, it can produce all food and housing; the only problem is distribution.

## Counterpoints

- Some worry there won't be enough resources for everyone, but if AI can take all jobs, it can produce all food and housing—the problem is only distribution.
- People ask how to get started if they're not rich yet; the answer is that governments and companies can help build ownership through sovereign wealth funds and ESOPs.
- Skeptics doubt the transition is possible, but the elements already exist—from Alaska checks to employee-owned companies—so the task is just to bring them together.
