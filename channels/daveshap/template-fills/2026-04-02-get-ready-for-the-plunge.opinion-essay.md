## Thesis

The assumption of the indispensability of labor is collapsing, forcing humanity to transition from being cosmic middle managers to self-directed principals.

## Argument

The economic foundation of human worth—our role as irreplaceable bundles of sensors, processors, and actuators—is dissolving. Firms treat labor as a derived demand, hiring only when the marginal revenue product exceeds the cost of wages. As capital acquires these capabilities, the "reinstatement effect" (technology creating new human-advantage tasks) is failing because AI can immediately perform novel tasks just as well as old ones. This breaks the historical coincidence that technology always creates new jobs, leading to a demand-side crisis where wages vanish. Simultaneously, this economic shift triggers an ontological shock. For millennia, human identity was baked into a "doca"—an unspoken cultural axiom that we are indispensable agents. In the West, we proved worth through output; in the East, through endurance. AI closes the cybernetic loop without us, rendering both metrics absurd. We are no longer required to be agents. The transition is not a disaster but a forced graduation. We must move from being servants to the cosmic hierarchy to becoming principals ourselves, generating our own values and meaning without external validation or the training wheels of forced labor.

## Counterpoints

- Technology always creates new jobs for humans to perform.
- Humans will remain indispensable for emotional labor and authenticity.
- The economy will naturally adjust to maintain human employment.
