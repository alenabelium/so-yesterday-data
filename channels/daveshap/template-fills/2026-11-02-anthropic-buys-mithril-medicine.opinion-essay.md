## Argument

The report Anthropic published the same day, 'First successes in accelerating drug development with Claude,' details five case studies where small teams used Claude to attack different bottlenecks. At Generate Biomedicines, six scientists used Claude to design a shorter genetic instruction for CAR T-cell therapy, producing a version 70% shorter that performed better in lab tests. Divergence Labs used Claude to generate 35,000 antibody variants in a month, with three showing promise against food allergies. Big Hat Biosciences improved antibody drug candidates tenfold in binding strength. Invida cut regulatory document preparation from three months to four hours, and Claude even caught seven issues human reviewers missed. Bristol Myers Squibb used Claude to plan clinical trials, saving days of analysis. These examples show the bottleneck is not scientific knowledge but the number of expert teams and computing power. ## Counterpoints

- It's just lab tests and mice—Anthropic itself says most research hasn't reached human trials, and many drugs that look great in the lab never reach patients.
- A million times more computing power sounds crazy, but we've done it before: compute for training largest AI models has grown 4-5x per year since 2010, so a million-fold increase is possible in about 10 years.
