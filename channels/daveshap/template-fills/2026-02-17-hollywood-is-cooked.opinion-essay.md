## Thesis

Hollywood is cooked because AI tools like Seedance 2.0 will collapse traditional production costs, forcing a shift to infinite, bespoke, AI-generated content distributed directly to audiences.

## Argument

The collapse begins with **[[seedance-2]]** lowering production barriers to near-zero, prompting immediate **[[copyright-takedowns]]** from studios like Disney and Paramount. This legal pressure forces creators to generate **[[infinite-original-ip]]**, which is legally protected under current copyright frameworks. As **[[indie-distribution]]** via YouTube matures, traditional gatekeepers lose control. Studios will eventually fold or merge, retaining only **[[prestige-gatekeeping]]** for human-centric experiences like opera or symphonies. The real shift is **[[bespoke-entertainment]]**: AI allows individuals to generate custom novels, music, and films tailored to their specific tastes, bypassing the need for mass-market appeal. While major IP like Star Wars remains valuable, the volume of entertainment will be dominated by personalized, AI-generated content that is faster, cheaper, and more relevant than traditional studio output.

## Counterpoints

- Studios are fighting back with takedowns and lawsuits to protect copyrighted IP.
- James Cameron argues AI should only help with VFX, not writing or acting.
- Organic discovery on YouTube may be seen as pre-vetting taste rather than true meritocracy.
- Traditional studios still control physical infrastructure and massive marketing budgets.
- Human brains are wired to index on other humans, preserving prestige for live/human actors.
