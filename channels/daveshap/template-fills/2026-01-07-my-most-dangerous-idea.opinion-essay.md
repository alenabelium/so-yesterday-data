## Thesis

We should deliberately eradicate the need for human labor, because labor's leverage is the only thing keeping citizens from becoming a permanent underclass, and we must replace it with new technological levers of power.

## Argument

The mechanism is leverage. Labor has been the bedrock of civic power because it is inalienable, refusable, mandatory, and perishable. These four properties create a double bilateral dependence: elites need our service and revenue, and we can withhold them. That gives us the power of coercive extraction of concessions and credible threats. But machines are already eroding the four things humans offer the economy—strength, dexterity, cognition, and empathy—and by 2045, Kurzweil's singularity date, machines will subsume everything humans can do. When labor disappears, so does our leverage, and we become a permanent underclass with no utility.

We cannot replace labor one-for-one, but we can build new levers using technology. Radical transparency, like Ukraine's Prozorro, reduces corruption and puts power in people's hands. Self-sovereign identity and open payment rails, like India's UPI and Brazil's Pix, give people control over transactions. Participatory budgeting, even at 1-2% of budgets, shows direct democracy can work. And decentralized coordination, like the Target boycott, demonstrates coercive extraction of concessions without unions. The goal is to create credible threats and veto power through control over money and information, not through labor.

## Counterpoints

- Machines might not replace all labor; there's still demand for authenticity and human uniqueness.
- Labor is currently the best mechanism for distributing wealth; eradicating it could break that system.
- Unions have been self-defeating, as seen in UK coal strikes, turning public opinion against them.
- Blockchain and similar technologies are unproven and might not deliver the promised transparency.
- Direct democracy and participatory budgeting are often limited to small percentages of budgets, not full control.
