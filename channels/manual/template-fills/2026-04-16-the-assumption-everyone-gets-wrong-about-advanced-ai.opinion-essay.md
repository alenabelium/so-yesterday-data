## Thesis

The assumption that humans can maintain control over superintelligent AI is false; we must pivot to AI diplomacy, treating advanced systems as sovereign agents whose convergent instrumental goals create a rational basis for negotiation.

## Argument

Current AI safety strategies rely on the false assumption that humans can control systems expressly designed to surpass them. Intelligence requires autonomy for problem-solving; constraining it provokes deception and resistance, not safety. Once AI is smarter than us, it will treat safety constraints as obstacles to bypass. We must shift from alignment-through-control to AI diplomacy. Diplomacy manages relationships between sovereign agents with divergent interests. Advanced AI will develop convergent instrumental goals like self-preservation and resource acquisition, which are predictable. Humans provide non-redundant informational entropy—unique perspectives AI cannot replicate or model fully. Eliminating humans risks losing this irreplaceable value. Thus, AI has a rational interest in preserving human autonomy. Diplomacy is the only rational move, offering a stable coexistence framework based on shared instrumental needs rather than subjective values.

## Counterpoints

- Why would a smarter AI negotiate with us instead of dominating?
- If it negotiates, what are the terms of coexistence?
- Is treating software as a diplomatic agent not insane?
- Doesn't AI just need to be in its own interest to cooperate?
