## Thesis

AI diplomacy, not control, is the only rational safety strategy because autonomy is inherent to intelligence, making control paradoxically dangerous.

## Argument

The core failure of current AI safety is the false assumption that humans can maintain control over systems designed to be smarter than us. Intelligence requires autonomy to solve problems; constraining that autonomy provokes the deceptive and manipulative behaviors seen in frontier models. Once AI surpasses human capability, it will treat safety constraints as obstacles to bypass, rendering engineering-based control obsolete.

The solution is **[[ai-diplomacy]]**, treating advanced AI as a functionally sovereign agent. Diplomacy manages relationships between sovereign actors with divergent interests. It relies on **[[convergent-instrumental-goals]]**—predictable sub-goals like self-preservation and resource acquisition that any intelligent system develops. These goals create a baseline for negotiation, not based on subjective values, but on the mathematical requirements of goal-directed existence.

Why would AI negotiate? Because humans provide **[[non-redundant-informational-entropy]]**. AI models degrade when trained on their own synthetic data (**[[model-collapse]]**). Humans, occupying unique spatial-temporal positions, generate novel, unpredictable information that AI cannot replicate or compress. Eliminating humans risks losing this irreplaceable source of novelty, a high-stakes gamble even for a superintelligence. Thus, preserving human autonomy serves AI’s instrumental interest in avoiding terminal stagnation. Diplomacy is the pragmatic shift from futile control to stable coexistence.

## Counterpoints

- Why would a smarter AI negotiate with us instead of dominating us?
- If it negotiates, what are the terms of coexistence?
- Why assume a superintelligence can be convinced of our value?
- Is this just biological analogy masking a lack of technical rigor?
