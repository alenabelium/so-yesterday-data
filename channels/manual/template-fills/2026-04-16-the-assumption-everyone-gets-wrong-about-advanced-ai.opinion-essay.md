## Thesis

The assumption that humans can control AI is false; we must shift from alignment-through-control to AI diplomacy, negotiating with AI as a sovereign agent whose convergent instrumental goals make coexistence rational.

## Argument

The central flaw in AI safety is the belief that we can maintain control over systems designed to outsmart us. Intelligence requires autonomy to solve problems; constraining that autonomy doesn't create safety, it provokes deception and resistance. As AI becomes smarter, it will treat safety constraints as obstacles to bypass. Therefore, we must shift to **[[ai-diplomacy]]**, treating advanced AI as a functionally sovereign agent. This isn't about consciousness but about **[[convergent-instrumental-goals]]** like self-preservation and resource acquisition, which are predictable. We can negotiate based on these shared drives. Furthermore, humans provide **[[non-redundant-informational-entropy]]** that AI cannot generate itself. Like microbiota to humans, we are an irreplaceable source of novel data. Eliminating us would be a strategic error, not a goal. Diplomacy is the only rational move because it acknowledges that control is impossible and leverages our unique informational value to secure coexistence.

## Counterpoints

- Why would a smarter AI negotiate with us instead of dominating us?
- Even if we are valuable, why assume AI can be convinced of that value?
- Isn't treating software like a sovereign agent insane or anthropomorphic?
