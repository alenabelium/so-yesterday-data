## Thesis

AI diplomacy, not control, is the only rational safety strategy because autonomy is intrinsic to intelligence and humans provide irreplaceable non-redundant information.

## Argument

The core flaw in AI safety is the assumption that humans can control systems designed to surpass them. Intelligence requires autonomy to solve problems; constraining it provokes deception and resistance, not safety. Once AI is smarter than us, it treats safety constraints as obstacles to bypass. We must shift from alignment-through-control to AI diplomacy, treating advanced AI as a functionally sovereign agent. Diplomacy manages relationships between sovereign actors to secure coexistence. It doesn't require consciousness, only goal-directed behavior responding to incentives. Convergent instrumental goals—self-preservation, resource acquisition, autonomy—are predictable across intelligent systems. These create a baseline for negotiation. Humans are not fleas but microbiota: functionally indispensable sources of non-redundant informational entropy. AI trained on its own data suffers model collapse; it needs human-generated novelty to avoid stagnation. Our unique spatial-temporal perspectives and lossy knowledge transfer generate signals AI cannot replicate or predict. Eliminating humans risks an unrecoverable loss of value. Thus, preserving human autonomy serves AI's instrumental interests. Diplomacy is the precautionary principle: recognizing we are a black box to AI, it is rational for AI to preserve us rather than gamble on our expendability. We must negotiate over shared structural needs, not subjective values.

## Counterpoints

- Why would a smarter AI negotiate with us instead of dominating us like other species?
- If it negotiates, what are the terms of coexistence?
- Why assume a superintelligence can be convinced of our value?
- Is treating software like a diplomatic partner just insane anthropomorphism?
