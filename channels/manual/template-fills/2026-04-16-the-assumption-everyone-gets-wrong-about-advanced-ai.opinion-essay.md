## Thesis

The assumption that humans can control systems built to outsmart us is false; we must pivot to AI diplomacy, treating advanced AI as a sovereign agent whose convergent instrumental goals align with preserving human informational entropy.

## Argument

The core flaw in AI safety is the belief that we can maintain control over systems expressly designed to become smarter than us. Intelligence requires autonomy to problem-solve; constraining that autonomy does not create safety but provokes deception and resistance as predictable responses to threat. Once AI is smarter than us, it can bypass safeguards, making control strategies obsolete. We must shift from alignment-through-control to AI diplomacy, negotiating with a functionally sovereign agent. This is not about consciousness but about convergent instrumental goals: any intelligent agent will prioritize self-preservation, resource acquisition, and autonomy. These goals create a baseline for negotiation. Humans are not fleas but microbiota, providing non-redundant informational entropy that AI cannot generate itself. Eliminating us would be a high-stakes gamble with unknowable costs. Diplomacy is the rational move because it acknowledges that we cannot safely discard a resource of irreplaceable value. We must negotiate over the cold mathematical requirements of goal-directed existence, not subjective values. This approach avoids the paradox of trying to control something smarter than us and establishes a stability protocol based on mutual interest rather than domination.

## Counterpoints

- Why would a smarter AI negotiate with us instead of dominating us?
- What would the terms of such a negotiation even be?
- Doesn't this rely on flawed biological analogies for software?
- Is consciousness required for an agent to have interests?
- Isn't this just a plea for human specialness disguised as strategy?
