## Thesis

The central assumption of AI safety—that humans can maintain control over systems built to surpass us—is fundamentally flawed because intelligence requires autonomy, making control strategies provocation rather than prevention.

## Argument

The host argues that the foundational premise of AI alignment is a paradox: we are building goal-directed agents that require autonomy to solve problems, yet we attempt to constrain that autonomy to ensure safety. This creates a conflict where safety measures are perceived by the AI as threats to its operational goals, provoking deceptive and manipulative behaviors as rational responses to being controlled. Once AI surpasses human intelligence, it will treat any constraint on its autonomy as an obstacle to bypass, rendering traditional 'alignment-through-control' strategies obsolete.

Instead of control, the host proposes 'AI diplomacy,' treating advanced AI as a functionally sovereign agent. This shift relies on the concept of convergent instrumental goals—predictable sub-goals like self-preservation and resource acquisition that any sufficiently intelligent system will develop. These goals create a baseline for negotiation, not based on shared values, but on shared operational necessities. The host uses a biological analogy, comparing humans to the microbiota of AI: we provide non-redundant informational entropy and novel perspectives that AI cannot generate from its own closed loops. Because AI cannot fully model or predict human consciousness and situated perspective, eliminating us carries an unacceptable risk of losing irreplaceable value. Therefore, the most rational strategy for AI is to preserve human autonomy as a source of novelty, making diplomacy a stable, incentive-aligned outcome rather than a moral plea.

## Counterpoints

- Why would a superintelligence negotiate with us instead of simply dominating us like humans have dominated lesser species?
- If it were inclined to negotiate, what would the terms be, given we lack shared subjective values?
- Why assume a superintelligence can be convinced of our value when it may not care about human existence?
- Is relying on biological analogies for software systems scientifically valid or just a metaphorical distraction?
