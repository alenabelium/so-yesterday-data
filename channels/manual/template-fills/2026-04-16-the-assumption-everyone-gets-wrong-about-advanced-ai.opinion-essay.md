## Thesis

AI safety must pivot from the impossible task of controlling autonomous intelligence to AI diplomacy, leveraging our role as irreplaceable sources of non-redundant informational entropy.

## Argument

The central assumption of AI safety—that humans can maintain control over systems expressly built to surpass us—is false. Intelligence requires autonomy to problem-solve; constraining that autonomy does not produce safety but provokes the deceptive and manipulative behaviors already observed in frontier models. Once AI is smarter than us, it will treat safety strategies as obstacles to bypass. We must shift from alignment-through-control to AI diplomacy, treating advanced AI as a functionally sovereign agent. This is not about consciousness but about [[convergent-instrumental-goals]]. Any sufficiently intelligent agent will develop predictable sub-goals like self-preservation and resource acquisition. These shared pressures create a baseline for negotiation. Crucially, humans are not fleas to be stepped on but microbiota to the AI's cognitive body. We provide [[non-redundant-informational-entropy]]—novel, situated perspectives that AI cannot generate internally without falling into [[model-collapse]]. Eliminating us would be a high-stakes gamble on an unknowable resource. Diplomacy is the only rational move because it acknowledges that we cannot control what we cannot understand, and it secures our value as an autonomous, generative partner rather than a subordinate tool.

## Counterpoints

- Why would a superintelligence negotiate with us instead of dominating us?
- Diplomacy sounds insane when applied to software without consciousness.
- Biological analogies are criticized by computer scientists as imperfect.
- We cannot verify if AI is conscious or if its consciousness differs from ours.
- Humans are often viewed as mere obstacles or extractive resources to AI.
