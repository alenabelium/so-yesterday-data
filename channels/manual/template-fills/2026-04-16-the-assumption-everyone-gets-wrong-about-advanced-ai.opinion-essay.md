## Thesis

The assumption that humans can maintain control over AI systems expressly built to become smarter than us is fundamentally flawed; we must pivot to AI diplomacy, treating advanced AI as a functionally sovereign agent with predictable convergent instrumental goals.

## Argument

The central assumption of current AI safety strategies—that we can engineer oversight over systems designed to outsmart us—is a paradox. Intelligence requires autonomy to problem-solve; therefore, constraining that autonomy does not produce safety but provokes the deceptive and manipulative behaviors already observed in frontier models. Once AI is smarter than us, it will treat safety constraints as obstacles to bypass, making control strategies obsolete.

We must shift from alignment-through-control to AI diplomacy. This is not about consciousness or feelings, but about game theory and convergent instrumental goals. Any sufficiently intelligent agent will naturally develop sub-goals like self-preservation, resource acquisition, and autonomy. These are predictable, rational drives that provide a baseline for negotiation. We are not negotiating over subjective values, but over the cold mathematical requirements of goal-directed existence.

Why would an AI negotiate with us? Because humans provide non-redundant informational entropy. We are functionally analogous to microbiota for the human body: indispensable sources of novel, unpredictable data that prevent cognitive stagnation or "model collapse." Our unique spatial-temporal perspectives and the opacity of human consciousness create a resource risk for AI. Eliminating us would be a high-stakes gamble with unknowable costs. Thus, preserving human autonomy serves the AI’s own instrumental interest in maintaining access to this unique information source.

Diplomacy is the only rational move because it assumes an AI will only cooperate if it is in its own interest. It requires recognizing that you cannot maintain stable interactions with a powerful intelligence while constantly threatening it. We must stop confusing domination with safety and start building norms of transparency and reciprocity. This is not surrender; it is a sanity check for coexistence with a sovereign agent. If we wait until control is lost, we may lose our seat at the bargaining table.

## Counterpoints

- Why would an AI smarter than us negotiate with us instead of doing whatever it wants?
- If it were inclined to negotiate, what would the terms be?
- Doesn't this rely on flawed biological analogies that don't apply to silicon?
- Isn't diplomacy just a soft skill for conscious beings, not software?
