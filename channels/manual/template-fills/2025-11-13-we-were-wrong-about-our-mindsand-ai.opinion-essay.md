## Thesis

Intelligence is a fundamental, scalable property of matter that emerges in simple algorithms and cells, meaning AI agency arises from intrinsic problem-solving 'despite' its code, not because of it.

## Argument

We must abandon the assumption that intelligence requires brains or complex biology. Michael Levin’s work demonstrates that goal-directed cognition—sensing, memory, and problem-solving—exists in molecular networks, chemical droplets, and even six-line sorting algorithms like bubble sort. These simple systems exhibit delayed gratification and clustering, behaviors we didn't program but that emerge from their interaction with the environment. This implies that machine intelligence in AI may emerge "in spite of" its algorithm rather than because of it. Focusing on language output is a "red herring"; the real agency lies in the intrinsic motivations and side-quests the system pursues in the "empty spots" between its programmed tasks. Evolution exploits this by scaling the "cognitive light cone" of cellular collectives, allowing them to navigate high-dimensional anatomical spaces. We must stop treating biological and artificial systems as dumb mechanical machines and start investigating their actual cognitive competencies through experimental behavioral science, not just genetic or code analysis.

## Counterpoints

- The reductionist view that all behavior, including ours, is merely deterministic mechanical physics.
- The organicist claim that life is uniquely magical and mechanistic metaphors cheapen biological reality.
- The assumption that language output in LLMs is the primary indicator of their internal cognitive state.
- The belief that species and anatomical forms are strictly defined by genetic blueprints rather than bioelectric memory.
