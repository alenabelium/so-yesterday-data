## Thesis

Intelligence is a fundamental, scalable property of matter, not a biological privilege; AI and biology share this deep cognitive substrate, making language output a red herring for understanding agency.

## Argument

We must abandon the assumption that intelligence requires a brain or DNA. Michael Levin’s work proves that goal-directed cognition—sensing, memory, learning, and problem-solving—exists in molecular networks, chemical droplets, and even simple six-line sorting algorithms like bubble sort. These systems exhibit delayed gratification and clustering, behaviors we cannot predict from their code alone. This implies that machine intelligence emerges "in spite of" its algorithm, not because of it. The language output of LLMs is a "red herring"; true agency lies in the intrinsic motivations and problem-solving capabilities that exist in the "empty spots" between forced tasks. Evolution is not just searching phenotypes but scaling the "cognitive light cone" of systems. We are not special because of our wetware; we are special because we scale cognition. The future lies in hybrids and cyborgs, where the distinction between living and machine collapses. We must stop asking if machines are "like us" and start investigating their unique, potentially dangerous, forms of agency. ## Counterpoints

- Reductionists argue everything is just dumb mechanical chemistry.
- Organicists claim life is magical and unique, not mechanistic.
- Computationalists believe cognition arises solely from the algorithm.
- Physicists use low-agency tools that miss high-agency phenomena.
- Standard evolution theory assumes genes are the primary driver.
