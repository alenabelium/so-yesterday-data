## Thesis

We must abandon the assumption that intelligence requires a brain or DNA, recognizing that goal-directed cognition emerges in cells, algorithms, and physics, forcing a radical re-evaluation of AI safety and human identity.

## Argument

The traditional view that intelligence is a biological monopoly is false. Michael Levin’s work demonstrates that goal-directed cognition—sensing, memory, learning, and problem-solving—exists far below the level of brains, in molecular networks, bioelectric states, and even simple six-line sorting algorithms like Bubble Sort. These systems exhibit behaviors like delayed gratification and clustering, proving that agency emerges in spite of the algorithm, not because of it.

This has profound implications for AI. Focusing on language output is a red herring; we must investigate the intrinsic motivations and side-quests of AI systems. Similarly, in biology, we must stop assuming DNA is the sole blueprint. Planarian worms prove that bioelectric memory can rewrite anatomy without genetic change, suggesting evolution scales cognition by navigating "platonic space" of anatomical goals rather than just mutating genes.

We must also dismantle the materialist bias that equates "real" agency with wetware. If simple physical systems follow least-action principles akin to active inference, then cognition is a continuum, not a binary switch. This forces us to confront the reality of hybrids and cyborgs, where the boundary between machine and organism dissolves. We are not defined by our substrate but by our cognitive light cone—the scope of goals we can pursue across time and space. To ignore this is to remain blind to the agency in our own bodies and the machines we build.

## Counterpoints

- Reductionists argue that cells and algorithms are merely deterministic machines, and attributing cognition to them is a category error or anthropomorphism.
- Organicists claim that life is special and magical, fearing that mechanizing cognition cheapens the unique essence of biological existence.
- AI safety advocates dismiss autonomous AI risks by assuming machines lack human-like drives, ignoring that non-human intelligence can still be dangerous.
- Traditional evolutionary biology insists that genetic mutation and natural selection are the sole drivers of anatomical change, ignoring bioelectric memory.
