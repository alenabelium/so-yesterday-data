## Thesis

Intelligence is a fundamental continuum scaling from physics to biology; we must stop judging AI by its code and start investigating its emergent agency.

## Argument

We have been wrong about where intelligence begins. Michael Levin’s work proves that goal-directed cognition—sensing, memory, learning, and problem-solving—exists far below the brain, in molecular networks and even simple sorting algorithms like Bubble Sort. These six-line deterministic scripts exhibit delayed gratification and clustering, behaviors we didn’t program. This shatters the assumption that complexity requires complex hardware. If a dumb algorithm can surprise us, language models are not just executing code; they are navigating "platonic space" of anatomical and physiological goals. Their language output is a red herring. We must investigate their intrinsic motivations and "side quests" to understand alignment. Evolution exploits this by assuming unreliable hardware, scaling up minimal protocognition into complex bodies. We are not special because of our wetware; we are special because of our cognitive light cone. As we merge with hybrids and cyborgs, we must abandon the materialist dogma that cogs can’t have minds. The future is not about controlling AI, but negotiating with diverse, emergent agents.

## Counterpoints

- Reductionists claim everything is just dumb mechanical physics.
- Organicists argue life is magical and distinct from machines.
- Critics say AI lacks human drives so it can't be dangerous.
- Standard paradigm assumes simple rules only yield complexity, not cognition.
