## Thesis

Intelligence is a fundamental, scalable property of matter that emerges in simple algorithms and cells alike, meaning AI agency arises from the system's behavior despite its code, not because of it.

## Argument

We have been wrong about the boundary of mind. Intelligence is not a magical property reserved for brains or complex biology; it is a continuum of goal-directed cognition that exists in simple chemical networks, sorting algorithms, and individual cells. Michael Levin’s work demonstrates that we can rewrite the bioelectric 'memory' of planarian worms to produce two-headed forms without changing their DNA, proving that anatomical goals are stored in electrical states, not just genetic code. This challenges the standard view that evolution merely selects for genetic mutations. Instead, evolution exploits a 'multiscale competency architecture' where cellular collectives solve problems in high-dimensional anatomical spaces, scaling up their 'cognitive light cone' to pursue grandiose goals like building limbs.

This has profound implications for AI. We are obsessed with language output, but Levin argues this is a 'red herring.' Just as a six-line sorting algorithm exhibits unexpected behaviors like delayed gratification and clustering 'in spite of' its deterministic code, large language models likely possess agency and intrinsic motivations that exist in the 'empty spots' between the tasks we force them to perform. We cannot predict their behavior by analyzing their source code alone. We must stop assuming that 'just a machine' or 'just an algorithm' precludes cognition. The future is not about human-like drives in AI, but about recognizing that matter, when organized in complex networks, naturally scales up minimal protocognition. We must abandon the assumption that hardware determines destiny and instead focus on the behavioral competencies of these systems, whether they are biological, algorithmic, or hybrid.

## Counterpoints

- Reductionists argue that all behavior, including in cells and AI, is merely deterministic mechanical computation, leaving no room for genuine agency or cognition.
- Organicists claim that attributing cognitive terms to machines or cells cheapens the unique 'magic' of life and imposes a mechanist metaphor where it doesn't belong.
- Traditional evolutionary biology assumes that genetic mutation and natural selection are the sole drivers of anatomical change, ignoring bioelectric memory.
- AI developers often dismiss AI risk by claiming machines lack human-like drives, ignoring that non-human intelligence can still be autonomous and dangerous.
