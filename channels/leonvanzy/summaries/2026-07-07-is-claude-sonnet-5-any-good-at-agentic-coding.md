# Is Claude Sonnet 5 Any Good at Agentic Coding?

## Executive Summary

The video evaluates Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5 against Opus 4.8 in the context of agentic coding by tasking both models with building a browser-based Age of Empires clone and its underlying development tools. The experiment highlights the importance of instructing AI agents to create their own debugging and visualization tools to overcome limitations in real-time visual analysis. While Sonnet 5 successfully generated a functional game and toolset, the presenter concludes that Opus 4.8 delivered superior asset quality and gameplay mechanics despite similar costs and execution times.

## Key Points

- The presenter challenges Anthropic's benchmark claims by testing Sonnet 5 on a complex agentic coding task: building a game engine and tools rather than just the final application [00:01:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ycBkXBxaKTU&t=75).
- A key strategy demonstrated is instructing the agent to build its own asset viewer and animation tools, allowing it to analyze its work frame-by-frame instead of relying on unreliable real-time screenshots [00:02:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ycBkXBxaKTU&t=165).
- Comparative testing reveals that while Sonnet 5 produced a working game, Opus 4.8 generated significantly higher quality assets, better character physics, and more engaging gameplay mechanics [00:06:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ycBkXBxaKTU&t=404).
- The video concludes that Opus 4.8 remains the superior model for high-fidelity agentic coding tasks, though Sonnet 5 offers a cost-effective alternative for functional implementations [00:08:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ycBkXBxaKTU&t=495).
