# The First Orbital Librarian

## Executive Summary

The video introduces Hermes, an autonomous AI agent framework designed to serve as the first orbital librarian, addressing the fragility of digital knowledge preservation. By hosting this agent in orbit, the system aims to continuously curate and maintain semantic indexes for sovereign knowledge collections without relying on terrestrial infrastructure. This approach leverages the vacuum of space to protect data from entropy and political instability, ensuring that meaning is preserved through continuous, lossless curation rather than static storage. The initiative, backed by 021T Capital and developed by Noose Research, represents a shift from passive archiving to active, perpetual stewardship of information.

## Key Points

- Historical libraries like Alexandria failed not from fire but from deferred maintenance, highlighting that libraries are verbs requiring active curation rather than static nouns [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8r-XTOXeDWM&t=0).
- Hermes, an open-source agent framework from Noose Research, will launch on a Starva satellite to act as an autonomous librarian that organizes collections and resolves conflicting records in orbit [00:01:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8r-XTOXeDWM&t=83).
- The orbital environment favors archivist tasks over oracle tasks, allowing the agent to work between questions to enrich metadata and build retrieval plans for sovereign AI models [00:02:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8r-XTOXeDWM&t=162).
- Each Hermes instance operates within a single trust domain under the owner's keys, ensuring no data sharing between customers and eliminating reliance on hyperscalers or the public internet [00:04:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8r-XTOXeDWM&t=247).
- The system uses sunlight as continuous energy for lossless copying, preventing the starvation of knowledge that occurred when human curation labor ceased [00:04:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8r-XTOXeDWM&t=247).
