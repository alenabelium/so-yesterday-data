# The First Interstellar Spacecraft to Alpha Centauri

## Executive Summary

The Fermy Explorer Mission announces the first interstellar spacecraft deliberately aimed at Alpha Centauri, utilizing existing ion thruster technology rather than waiting for future propulsion breakthroughs. This mission addresses the Fermi Paradox by acting as a hedge against three possibilities: being the first civilization to seed intelligence, escaping a potential 'great filter,' or signaling existence to an external observer. Rather than targeting extraterrestrial life, the spacecraft serves as a long-term time capsule and archive for humanity's descendants, democratizing access to space through public contributions.

## Key Points

- The mission aims to launch soon using proven ion thrusters, taking approximately 80,000 years to reach Alpha Centauri, a duration that defines its purpose as a long-term archive rather than an immediate message. [00:01:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EhTvfpLRvKw&t=85)
- Launching this craft hedges against three interpretations of the Fermi Paradox: seeding intelligence if we are first, escaping a civilizational 'great filter,' or proving our capability to reach interstellar space if we are in a galactic zoo. [00:02:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EhTvfpLRvKw&t=160)
- The payload is designed as a retrievable time capsule for future humans, allowing public contributions like names and DNA, while the supporting startup PSI focuses on commercializing transformative physics breakthroughs. [00:03:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EhTvfpLRvKw&t=225)
