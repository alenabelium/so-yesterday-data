## Thesis

The singularity has belonged exclusively to artificial minds until now, as Eon Systems demonstrates the first multi-behavior whole brain emulation by closing the perception-action loop in a physically simulated fly.

## Argument

For decades, whole brain emulation remained a disembodied theoretical exercise—activation without physics, motor outputs with nowhere to go. Eon Systems PBC has crossed this qualitative threshold. Building on a 2024 Nature-published computational model of the adult Drosophila brain (125,000+ neurons, 50 million synapses), they integrated connectome-derived neural dynamics with a physics-simulated body in MuJoCo. Unlike DeepMind’s RL-controlled fly or OpenWorm’s limited C. Elegans model, this system uses actual biological wiring to drive naturalistic behaviors. Sensory input flows in, neural activity propagates through the complete connectome, and motor commands execute in simulation. This is not an animation or a policy mimic; it is a copy of a biological brain running in simulation. The implication cascades upward: if a fly brain can close the loop, the mouse brain (70 million neurons) is a matter of scale, not kind. Eon is now amassing expansion microscopy and calcium imaging data to attempt human-scale emulation. The ghost is no longer in the machine; the machine is becoming the ghost.

## Counterpoints

- Prior work like DeepMind's MuJoCo fly used reinforcement learning, not connectome-derived dynamics.
- OpenWorm attempted embodiment but with only 302 neurons and limited behaviors.
- The 2024 Nature model was disembodied, lacking physical interaction.
