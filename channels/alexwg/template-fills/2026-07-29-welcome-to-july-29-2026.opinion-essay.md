## Thesis

The open-sourcing of the Singularity has triggered a chaotic realignment of global power, where compute scarcity, biological adaptation, and labor displacement define the new reality.

## Argument

The release of Moonshot AI's Kimmy K3 marked a pivot point: open weights forced a race for scarce Blackwell silicon, with Amazon retiring Nova models to prioritize Peter Ail's lab. Washington's regulatory lag allowed rivals OpenAI and Anthropic to quietly ally against chip restrictions, while Nvidia's open secure alliance hardened tooling. China responded by mass-producing domestic immersion DUV and CXMT memory, creating a redundant supply chain that undercuts Western leverage. Capital followed compute: Apple reclaimed the trillion-dollar crown for being late to the party, while Nvidia, Meta, and BlackRock poured billions into gigawatt-scale data centers, bypassing EPA rules. Labor markets are dissolving; Visa cut jobs for stablecoins, Microsoft and Uber erased call centers, and 57% of the new creator middle class earns below a living wage. Yet biology adapts too: an HIV vaccine raised neutralizing antibodies in macaques, and Georgetown volunteers' visual cortices wired directly to motor output, bypassing prefrontal cortex to sort cars while distracted. The future is not just code; it is infrastructure, biology, and the erosion of human agency in both labor and cognition.

## Counterpoints

- Regulatory frameworks are too slow to control open weights.
- Western supply chains are vulnerable to Chinese DUV and memory dominance.
- Labor displacement is accelerating faster than new job creation.
- Biological adaptation may outpace technological control mechanisms.
