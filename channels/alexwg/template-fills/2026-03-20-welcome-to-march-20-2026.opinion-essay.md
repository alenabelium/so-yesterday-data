## Thesis

By March 2026, AI agents will have achieved full autonomy in chip design, mathematics, and business operations, triggering a labor shift toward AI-only hiring and subscription-based expertise.

## Argument

The mechanism is recursive acceleration. Veror's Design Conductor compressed a quarterly engineering cycle into 12 hours, proving that AI can design its own silicon without human intervention. That same loop is tightening in research: Q's ensemble of 1.8 billion parameter models matched standard training with 10x less data, and MIT's neural cellular automata hint that life and language share a computational substrate. DeepMind's AlphaEvolve cracked Ramsey numbers Erdos called hopeless, and Math Inc. launched QualBench to benchmark autoformalization against graduate exams. The tools are becoming autonomous collaborators that work while you sleep. Anthropic added asynchronous event channels to Claude Code, letting MCP servers push CI results and alerts so Claude reacts while users are away. OpenAI is monitoring its own coding agents for misalignment, proof that recursive oversight is now required. Google's Vibe Design with Stitch cratered Figma's stock by 8%, and Meta is cutting human moderators while abandoning the metaverse. The business model of expertise is being rewritten: PwC's US boss warned that partners who resist AI will have no place, G42 posted a job exclusively for AI agents, and DoorDash turned gig workers into sensory organs for the ML stack. The physical world is being reorganized too: Waymo's fleet logged 170 million miles with 92% fewer serious crashes, Uber invested $1.25 billion in Rivian for 50,000 robotaxis, and Jeff Bezos is raising $100 billion to automate manufacturing.
