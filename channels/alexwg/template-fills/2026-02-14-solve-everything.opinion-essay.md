## Thesis

Superintelligence is no longer a question of if, but of where we point it, and we can solve every major human problem by 2035.

## Argument

The mechanism is the targeting system. Unlike old benchmarks that looked backward to record who was winning, a targeting system looks forward to create the future. Make the measurement legible, adversarial, and payable, and capital and cognition route themselves to clear the target automatically. This creates the abundance flywheel: commit compute to a hard problem, focus R&D until it clears, watch the domain collapse from artisanal craft to industrial utility, capture the surplus, and reinvest it into the next target. Repeat until scarcity is a memory.

We map a solution wavefront. Phase 1, 2026 to 2027, conquers pure information: math, code, and physics become formal verification utilities. Phase 2, 2028 to 2031, masters the physical world: chemistry, material science, and biology capitulate once the virtual cell comes online, turning the human body into a software problem. Phase 3, 2032 to 2035, tackles planetary-scale systems: energy, climate, food, and infrastructure are industrialized into dial-reliable utilities.

The operational heart is 15 specific moonshots, from manufacturing human organs on demand to doubling the healthy human lifespan to demonstrating human mind uploading. Each comes with benchmarks, milestone dates, guard rails, and a theory of spillover: solving the hardest problem in a field builds the tools to solve every other problem in that field.

The obstacle is not technology but the muddle—the entrenched layer of bureaucracy, input-based pricing, and scarcity-minded institutions. We argue there are roughly 18 months of regulatory foundry window

## Counterpoints

- The muddle path: AI captured by ad optimization and grant theater.
- The dark path: a safety incident that freezes progress entirely.
