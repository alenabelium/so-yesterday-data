## Thesis

The intelligence revolution has finally reached physical land, turning what was assumed to be permanently scarce into an abundant resource.

## Argument

The mechanism is the same force that collapsed the cost of compute, bandwidth, and gene sequencing: intelligence. Coastal Assembly PBC, backed by 021T Capital, uses AI-optimized underwater structures to program the ocean to deposit sediment naturally. In field studies at Maldives resorts, this approach grew over 90 feet of new beach in six months and created an entirely new island, adding over 800 cubic meters of land. The key inversion is that instead of fighting the ocean with static barriers, these porous structures dissipate storm energy while letting calm currents carry sediment inward, so sand accumulates but does not leave. The ocean becomes the construction crew, and the structures tell it where to build. Above this sits an intelligence layer that reads coastlines from space, decides where to grow land, and deploys without human intervention. Coordinated swarms of autonomous surface vessels will execute deployment at scale, and every new site feeds ground truth data back into the models, so the more land Coastal grows, the better it gets at growing land. This matters because prime coastal land can be worth $10 million per acre or more, and trillions of dollars in coastal property are priced on the assumption that land supply is fixed. As superintelligence makes landfall, that assumption becomes a liability. Island nations facing existential sea level rise become land producers instead of land losers. ## Counterpoints

- Mark Twain's advice that 'they're not making it anymore' has been true in practice because every method to date has been so expensive that land remained effectively scarce no matter how much you made.
- Only about 600,000 acres of artificial land have been built by brute force since 2000, roughly the size of Luxembourg, and the ocean still takes it back.
