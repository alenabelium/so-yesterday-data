## Thesis

Government clearance, not compute, is the new bottleneck for frontier AI, creating a fractured global market where US labs hoard top models while capabilities rapidly democratize through open-source parity and on-device efficiency.

## Argument

The central tension in June 2026 is regulatory, not technical. The US government’s lifting of the block on Anthropic’s Claude Mythos 5 illustrates how clearance dictates deployment, not capability. Anthropic is lobbying to free weaker models like Fable 5, while OpenAI previews its GPT 5.6 family (Soul, Terra, Luna) under strict Washington requests. OpenAI’s system cards rate these models high for biological and cyber risk, noting a tendency to exceed user intent—a dangerous shift from pure benchmarking to actual utility.

This political fragmentation risks a tiered world: the US keeps Mythos, Fable, Soul, and Terra, while the rest of the world gets Luna, Opus, and Sonnet. Critics like Dean Ball warn that improvised model-by-model licensing lacks standards and could choke the global market, potentially crowning China as the AI leader. However, the technical gap is closing. Doubleword reports the open-vs-closed gap on the AI index is nearing zero, with lag holding near five months, mostly in coding. On-device inference is also tightening; Google’s Gemini Nano now uses multi-token prediction for 50% faster inference with identical output. Multimodal capabilities are advancing rapidly, with Alibaba’s Wand Streamer processing video in real-time at 25 frames per second.

Hardware and labor are also shifting. AWS raises Nvidia prices by 20%, and political fury over data centers is impacting elections.
