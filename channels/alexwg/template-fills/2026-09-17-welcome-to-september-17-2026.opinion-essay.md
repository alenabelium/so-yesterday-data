## Thesis

By September 2026, AI efficiency has surpassed human cognitive output, rendering traditional white-collar roles obsolete and forcing a global reckoning on safety, economics, and the definition of humanity.

## Argument

The shift is driven by raw physical and economic metrics. **[[compute-efficiency]]** has hit a milestone where OpenAI's Boris power delivers 7 to 40 IQ points per watt compared to a human's 5. **[[hardware-innovation]]** like Intel's bit code exploits and Z.AI's self-organizing infra agents allow models to optimize themselves, making recursion prefer a blank page over past lessons. Scott Aronson declares the singularity underway, citing lean verified proofs that GPT6 Astra saturated benchmarks.

Economically, **[[agent-economy]]** is collapsing traditional products. Anthropic folds chat into docs; Open Router spend tips to open AI. Agents run businesses via Andon Labs, with Luna Andon Market's Claude manager firing a human for lateness. The money follows efficiency: OpenAI weighs a $1.5T round, Anthropic an IPO.

Politically, the response is fractured. Washington fights over liability; Scott Bessant rejects waivers while POTUS calls fears a hoax. **[[ai-regulation]]** debates rage from Brookings' nuclear-style safeguards to Ursula Vanderlean's social media bans. Yet the real economy thrives: median household income hits $87,460, child poverty drops. The seams show in fake dating apps and AI micro dramas, but the grid negotiates back with fast-tracked data centers. **[[human-redefinition]]** stretches as Stanford grows brain organoids and allies debate UAP disclosure.
