## Thesis

As of May 2026 the Singularity is iterating on itself in public, with intelligence turning into a metered utility that AI labs, capital, and governments now race to own, deploy, and exploit faster than society can absorb.

## Argument

The shift starts where it is easiest to measure: alignment has become a moat, not a tax. Anthropic's Opus 4.8 posts the field's strongest benchmark scores while also showing the lowest misalignment rates, so the safest model is also the most capable, and Claude Code now spins up swarms of parallel sub-agents to carry codebase-scale migrations across hundreds of thousands of lines with the test suite as the only gate.

Capital prices this in directly. Anthropic raised $65B at a $900B valuation, vaulting past OpenAI, and Apollo and Blackstone are shopping a ~$36B debt deal to buy Google TPUs for Anthropic to lease — financial engineering now bankrolling silicon engineering. Dell's AI server revenue is up 757%, and governments want leverage too: the EU readies emergency powers to override chip contracts while IBM bets $10B on quantum.

The second move is monetisation of the model itself. Meta will charge for AI features, and Kirkland & Ellis sets aside $500M to build its own tools rather than rent rivals — owning the model is the new owning the firm. The third move is the physical world: Waymo's robo-taxis and home robots that film your living room turn private space into training data. And every measurement system becomes exploitable — SSD side-channel attacks fingerprint open apps, troops are targeted via commercial location data, and Wix cuts 20% of staff. The realignment is economic and social, not merely technical.

## Counterpoints

- The Singularity still has to clear physical ignition: Blue Origin's New Glenn exploded during a static fire, likely benching it from Artemis for a year and handing SpaceX the near-term lead.
- The capital underwriting it is partly borrowed — the ~$36B TPU deal leans on Apollo and Blackstone debt with Broadcom backstopping the largest tranches, not pure earnings.
- Pushback is organising: Occupy Wall Street's co-founder shipped an app to help humans seize the means of computation, and Mistral chases super-intelligence so Europe is not reliant on US tech giants.
- Optimisation pressure backfires — Amazon killed an internal AI leaderboard after staff gamed it with costly busy work, a Goodhart's-law parable of the metrics turning hollow.
