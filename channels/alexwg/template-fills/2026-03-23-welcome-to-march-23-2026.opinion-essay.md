## Thesis

By March 2026, AI has achieved recursive self-improvement, automating the management layer of civilization and disrupting every industry, with the singularity now bootstrapping across silicon, orbital compute, and biology.

## Argument

The mechanism is recursive self-improvement. China's Miniax confirmed M2.7 is its first model deeply participating in its own evolution, and Google's Logan Kilpatrick posted—then deleted—a claim that all industries not yet disrupted by AI are about to be, hinting at a robotics breakthrough. The models aren't just rewriting themselves; they're reading ahead. Mantic and Thinking Machines applied reinforcement learning via Tinker to train LLMs on world event forecasting, seeing the future with the same rigor they used to parse the past. The management layer is being automated: Zuckerberg is building an AI agent to help him be CEO, and developers are treating AI bots like senior hires. Meanwhile, humans scramble for load-bearing roles—Snowflake laid off its entire technical writing team of ~70, replacing them with AI, while young people pivot to blue-collar careers. The silicon supply chain strains under exponential demand: Musk confirmed Terraab will produce ~1 billion chips/year at 1 kW/chip, powering 20M cyber cabs, 100M Optimus units, and 800M data center chips. TSMC's 2nm is booked through 2028, and Nvidia is redesigning chips because A16 capacity won't suffice. The orbital compute layer crystallizes: SpaceX and StarCloud converged on a common design, Blue Origin asked for 51,600 satellites, and the SpaceX IPO is projected above $2T. Robots proliferate: a Whimo shielded a passenger from an attacker, and xenobots with self-assembled nervous systems prove synthetic life can bootstrap its own wiring. The recursion is in the wetware now.

## Counterpoints

- Wall Street doesn't reward spending as enthusiastically as social media does—OpenAI tempered data center ambitions ahead of a potential IPO.
- Young people are trying to AI-proof themselves by pivoting to blue-collar careers like firefighters and electricians.
