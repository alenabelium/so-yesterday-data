## Thesis

By April 2026, AI development has become a scheduled, point-release singularity that outpaces civilization's ability to adapt, forcing governments and corporations to embrace risk as the safer alternative.

## Argument

The mechanism is acceleration itself. Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.7 ships as a midpoint between 4.6 and an unreleased Mythos preview, and internal polls show a third of staff expect Mythos to replace entry-level engineers in three months. The White House is routing Mythos into federal agencies despite cyber risks because not adopting it is scarier. OpenAI's GPT Rosalind targets biology, and Google's Gemini gets a body via Boston Dynamics Spot, while codeex turns the IDE into a co-worker. The silicon layer compounds: TSMC expects 30% growth, Cerebras files at $35B+, and XAI becomes a cloud provider. The human sensorium becomes an API—artificial smells via ultrasound, thought-to-text EEG, and a wireless gene switch. Capital chases the buildout: Alphabet gains $100B from SpaceX IPO, hyperscaler capex surpasses Apollo, and Taiwan overtakes the UK. The UK inverts conservation into abundance choreography. The US creates a special economic zone in the Philippines. Yet tensions surface: a Molotov attack on Sam Altman's house, and the White House investigates missing scientists. The Singularity ships point releases faster than civilization can debug itself.

## Counterpoints

- AI doomers are playing with fire, calling the attack on Altman's house really serious ST.
- Not adopting AI is scarier than the cyber risks it introduces.
- The pace of AI advancement outstrips humanity's ability to adapt, creating volatility.
