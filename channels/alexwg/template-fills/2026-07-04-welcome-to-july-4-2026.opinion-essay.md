## Thesis

The AI Singularity has arrived, driven by open-source economics and physical infrastructure, rendering traditional regulatory and economic barriers obsolete.

## Argument

The intelligence explosion is unstoppable because the economic floor has rocketed while the regulatory ceiling fails. Frontier models like Fable 5 and Opus 4.8 are throttled by politics, but GLM 5.2 tops benchmarks at a fraction of the cost, making sovereign, local models viable for every entity. This democratization allows a Cambrian explosion of cognition where models post-train other models. Simultaneously, the physical supply chain is locking in: Micron’s $9.3B expansion, custom silicon deals, and new nuclear reactors ensure the hardware can keep pace. The result is a world where AI agents write their own kernels, compute is commoditized, and the 'genie' becomes a permanent, invisible co-worker. The gears of this explosion cannot be sanded down by Washington; they are powered by concrete, silicon, and the relentless drive of open-source efficiency.

## Counterpoints

- Regulatory bodies like the FDA might try to control AI, but the president's adviser explicitly rejects this, stating there will be no licensing agency.
- Scaling might hit physics limits, but evidence shows apparent failures are usually bugs, not fundamental barriers.
- Frontier labs might hoard compute, but the economics force them to become compute providers themselves.
- Manual coding is obsolete, but the transition requires massive physical infrastructure upgrades that are already underway.
