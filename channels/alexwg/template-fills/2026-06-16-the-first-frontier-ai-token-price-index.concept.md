## TL;DR

The OR Token Price Index (OTPI) measures the real cost of AI inference tokens using transacted data rather than published rate cards. This benchmark addresses the structural gap between posted prices and actual spend, providing a transparent view of the AI economy's output side. By standardizing token pricing, OTPI complements compute indices to offer a complete picture of AI infrastructure costs.

## The argument

### Define the Structural Gap
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: Posted rate cards are declarations, not discoveries. Unlike oil's political pricing, AI's gap is structural: caching, routing, and model mixes mean the rate card never matches the realized cost.
- **Role**: definition

### Introduce OTPI Methodology
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:41']
- **Claim**: OTPI is a unit value index weighting every model by transacted token volume. It calculates total dollars paid over total tokens bought from executed paid inference, creating a single daily figure per lab.
- **Role**: definition

### Reveal Hidden Demand Signals
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:08']
- **Claim**: Because it is volume-weighted, OTPI reveals how traffic splits across models and how fast new releases win that traffic. This migration is the true demand signal, hidden from rate cards.
- **Role**: evidence

### Synthesize Commodity Standardization
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:06']
- **Claim**: Just as oil barrels were standardized before pricing, the token cleared the unit gate. OTPI is the second step: discovering the realized price of intelligence, complementing compute's price to read both sides of the cost curve.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

OTPI launches with daily indices for Anthropic and OpenAI. It complements OCPI, which prices GPU time (input). The speaker notes that OpenAI estimates intelligence cost falls 40x yearly. The index reflects buyer behavior: it holds up as buyers climb the frontier and slips as the frontier commoditizes. Caveat: The post is for informational purposes only, not investment advice. The speaker has a financial interest in OR.
