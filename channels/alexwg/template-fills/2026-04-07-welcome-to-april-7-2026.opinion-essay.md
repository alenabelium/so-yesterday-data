## Thesis

By April 2026, AI has become an autonomous researcher and the economic engine of a new prosperity, but the social contract must be refactored to manage the backlash and distribute the gains.

## Argument

The proof is in the lab: UNC's autonomous AI ran 50 experiments in 72 hours and invented a long-context memory system beating every human baseline. The scientist is now a subroutine. The frontier is policing itself—OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google share intelligence through the Frontier Model Forum to detect Chinese distillation attacks, a rare outbreak of lab solidarity against the entropy of open weights. Inside Meta, the arms race has gone intramural via claonomics, an internal leaderboard where employees flex by burning tokens in a new ritual called token maxing. The logical endpoint is the solo conglomerate: Henry Intelligent Machines just unveiled the first one-person AI conglomerates, an agent layer that spins up and operates fleets of micro businesses for a single human owner.

The compute substrate is printing money at industrial scale. Samsung reported a record ~$38 billion Q1 operating profit, up more than eight times YOY, as AI chip demand pumps memory prices. Anthropic's run-rate revenue leapt from ~$9 billion at end of 2025 to over $30 billion today, and it's inking a multi-gigawatt TPU deal with Google and Broadcom. OpenAI is scaling even more aggressively, reportedly planning to spend $121 billion on compute in 2028 alone while burning $85 billion that year, with Altman committing to $600 billion in 5-year spending and eyeing a Q4 IPO. The physical backlash is turning violent: an Indianapolis city councilor's home was shot up 13 times over a proposed data center, with a note reading 'no data centers.' The cloud casts a very local shadow.
