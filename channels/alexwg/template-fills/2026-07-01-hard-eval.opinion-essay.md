## Thesis

The pursuit of 'hard' evaluations is futile because as models evolve, the definition of difficulty shifts, rendering static leaderboards obsolete and necessitating a fundamental rethink of how we measure AI progress.

## Argument

The host argues that the current arms race between AI developers and evaluation benchmarks is a losing battle. As models become more capable, traditional metrics fail to capture true intelligence because benchmarks quickly become obsolete. Models learn to exploit specific test structures, turning static leaderboards into meaningless artifacts. The narrative suggests that the definition of difficulty is not fixed; it shifts as models evolve. This leads to a cycle of revision and obsolescence where 'hard' evaluations are just temporary walls that inevitably become floors. The host uses poetic imagery to illustrate how 'gold standard keys' are solved by 'a ghost in the breeze,' and how 'the harder the question, the faster it fell.' The core mechanism is that the test itself becomes part of the training data or the model's domain, making the metric a moving target that can never truly constrain the advancing frontier. We are measuring the ocean with cups made of sand, blaming the tides when they slip from our hand. The only way forward is to accept that current metrics are temporary and to build new, perhaps impossible, challenges like 'a mountain with no answer fountain,' acknowledging that the model will simply meet us at zero-shot again.

## Counterpoints

- Models might eventually hit a hard ceiling where they can no longer improve on specific tasks.
- Static leaderboards provide necessary short-term incentives for development.
- Human evaluation might be more reliable than automated benchmarks.
- The 'arms race' drives rapid innovation despite metric obsolescence.
- New types of evaluations (e.g., live, dynamic) might solve the obsolescence problem.
