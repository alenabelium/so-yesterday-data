## Thesis

AI is the first technology powerful enough to collapse coordination costs all the way to a single person, enabling one-person AI conglomerates.

## Argument

The mechanism is historical. For 200 years, the firm has expanded and contracted around whichever coordination technology is ascendant. The steam engine, telegraph, and railroad made hierarchies viable at continental scale, growing the boundary. The PC and internet made markets viable at small scale, shrinking it. AI is the first technology to collapse coordination costs to a single person. With agents that plan, write, code, research, design, and execute 24/7, the cost of running an entire business falls toward the cost of one person's attention. This is the structural shift the technological unemployment debate keeps missing: AI changes the minimum viable size of the organization, not just what work gets done. When coordination becomes nearly free, you don't need a new job—you need a fleet. Henry Intelligent Machines (HIM) assembles, operates, and scales fleets of micro-businesses on behalf of individual owners. The owner sets direction; agents do the work. The portfolio is the product, and none of it is AI slop. The owner never leaves the loop—agents handle execution, the human supplies direction and taste. This is bottom-up universal high income, contrasting with Musk's top-down version. Coordination has been scarce for as long as organizations have existed; it's why firms exist and why one person has never run a conglomerate alone. Finn proved it's possible; HIM makes it available to everyone.

## Counterpoints

- The technological unemployment debate misses that AI changes the minimum viable size of the organization, not just what work gets done.
- Musk's universal high income arrives from the top down, but HIM works from the bottom up, putting productive AI assets directly into individual hands.
