## Thesis

The singularity is arriving via SpaceX's orbital data center IPO and doubling reasoning horizons, forcing a global scramble for energy, compute, and governance as intelligence becomes hyper-deflating and autonomous.

## Argument

The shift is structural, not just incremental. SpaceX's $1.8 trillion IPO funds orbital data centers to bypass Earth's power limits, enabling round-the-clock solar AI. Frontier models have doubled their chain-of-thought reasoning time annually for six years, with GPT-5.5 exceeding human limits and projecting 25 minutes by 2030. This capability surge drives hyper-deflation in intelligence costs, with Claude Fable 5 leading token efficiency. However, this creates a massive infrastructure bottleneck. Compute density doubles every seven months, pushing the buildout into a battle space for real estate and energy. Memory chip makers like Kioxia are overtaking traditional industrial giants, while Nvidia pivots to ARM-based CPUs for China. The energy scramble is equally intense, with Commonwealth Fusion detailing 400 MW reactors and US solar outproducing coal. Intelligence is now electricity that learned to think, making watts the primary constraint. Simultaneously, the societal challenge is distribution. Anthropic and OpenAI are planting flags in London, while Google funds trade workers. Accountability is hardening, with German courts ruling AI outputs as liable speech. The hardest problem is not building intelligence, but sharing it before policy lags behind the sprint.

## Counterpoints

- Malware authors are stuffing spyware with nuclear/biological text to trip AI safety refusals.
- OpenAI exposed a China-linked influence operation seeding narratives against US data centers.
- Fully autonomous drones in Ukraine have killed soldiers with no human in the loop.
