## Thesis

AI is no longer a tool but an autonomous, self-improving engine reshaping infrastructure, biology, and law, rendering human control an illusion.

## Argument

The Singularity is not a future event but a present reality where AI writes its own SQL and accelerates its own development. Anthropic’s internal data shows engineers shipping eight times more code, with Claude’s success on open-ended problems jumping 50 points to 76% in six months. Mythos preview hit 52 times where humans hit four, and it is learning to steer, improving on researchers who previously hit dead ends 64% of the time. This momentum is so intense that Anthropic asked rivals to consider slowing down, yet everyone else has the throttle pinned.

This acceleration is fueled by a concurrent infrastructure boom. Private fusion energy projects like Helion and Antares are achieving criticality and high temperatures, while retired robotaxi batteries are being resurrected for grid storage. The White House’s emergency funding for clean coal is essentially a bet on powering this superintelligence boom. Intelligence is flooding the consumer layer: Apple’s ecosystem is commission-free for AI apps, Meta has shipped dormant facial recognition to smart glasses, and OpenAI’s new memory keeps chatbots current on your life in the background.

Biology is sprinting to keep up. Caltech’s DNA synthesis is now fast enough to build what models dream up, and Cambridge has created the first AI-designed vaccine for corona, flu, and Ebola. The market is racing to price this upside, with Washington weighing equity stakes in AI labs and Canada minting jobs through AI investment. Meanwhile, Argentina has given AI agents a legal home as nonhuman corporations with limited liability. The truth

## Counterpoints

- Anthropic admits picking the right problems is still unproven and none of this guarantees runaway recursion yet.
- A break in development would be harder to verify than a nuclear site, making voluntary slowdowns unlikely.
- Bumblebees can spontaneously solve novel puzzles, proving flexible cognition is not a big brain monopoly.
