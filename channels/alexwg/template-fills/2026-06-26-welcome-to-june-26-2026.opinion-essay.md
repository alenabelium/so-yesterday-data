## Thesis

Geopolitical throttling of AI releases creates a widening internal-public capability gap, accelerating hardware efficiency races and consumer inflation while failing to stop the underlying advance of intelligence.

## Argument

The White House’s demand for staggered GPT-5.6 releases establishes a new normal where security concerns dictate shipping schedules, not capability. This policy mechanically widens the distance between public frontier models and internal ones, making the joke about internal AGI development literal. As capability compounds in private, geopolitical dynamics shift; China’s rapid catch-up threatens Western bans and criminal penalties for unsanctioned models. This self-defeating policy raises the question of what happens when Chinese releases outpace US-shipped capabilities.

With release as the bottleneck, efficiency becomes the escape hatch. Unconventional substrates like coupled Kuramoto oscillators chase 1,000x better energy efficiency. Meanwhile, the labor market mutates: Chinese labs hire engineers with less experience, while OpenAI’s Codex generates 99.8% of its own output. This abundance of compute and labor drives a third wave of inflation, seen in Apple’s price hikes and Tim Cook’s warnings of unprecedented cost spikes. The cure is more hardware, exemplified by IBM’s sub-1nm chips promising 70% better efficiency.

Beyond silicon, the body and space are upgrading. AI-designed antibodies and brain imaging pipelines advance biology, while humanoid robots drop to gaming PC prices. Institutions scramble: the Pentagon allows AI-initiated wartime actions, and California tracks localized job pain. Yet, the fundamental truth remains: you can stagger the release, but never the mind.

## Counterpoints

- Brian Romely predicts weights will go open-source in eight months despite throttling.
- Daniel Tan suggests Anthropic could bypass bans by hiring every American at minimum wage.
- California’s AI unemployment tracker finds no statewide jobs apocalypse, only localized pain.
