## Thesis

AI's transformative effects are arriving faster than expected, reshaping everything from corporate strategy to daily life.

## Argument

The evidence is everywhere, and it's not just about models getting smarter. The timeline for superintelligence is collapsing: Anthropic's progress doubled, and Aja Cotra reads that as a 10x uplift, putting superintelligence within a year. Time horizons for AI tasks are doubling every two months, and internal models are outrunning the AI 2027 curve—a scenario that's been 85% accurate so far. This isn't speculation; it's a measurable acceleration.

Meanwhile, the infrastructure race is on. China built 120 megawatts at a fifth of US costs, and SpaceX is planning orbital Starmind computers for 2027. The compute is becoming as divisible as land, with 19.9-megawatt parcels predicted. And the money is following: Anthropic eyes a $1.75 trillion IPO, and OpenAI closed $11 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation. The market is haggling over the price, not the arrival.

But the real story is how this is hitting the ground. Research is exploding: deskbound fields are producing 15 times more research, and one engineer can outpace 100. Work is being refactored—Microsoft offers 60,000 buyouts, and India's IT chief warns grads face automation. Robots are clocking in, from Thesius humanoids restocking stores to Glasgow's first pediatric-only surgical robot. Even the cosmos is getting in on it: SpaceX earns over a third of its revenue from Anthropic, and the moon is getting zoning.

This is not a distant future. It's November 9, 2026, and the transformation is already here.

## Counterpoints

- Some suspect labs hide models to keep RSI leads, and bitter lesson author Richard Sutton now says brute force isn't enough.
- Voters curbed data centers in 40 of 41 measures, though they caused just 4% of home rate hikes.
- Pharma bosses, heads in the sand, call AI cures complete BS, even as designer drug neurons cut seizures 92.6%.
