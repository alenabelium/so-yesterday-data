## Thesis

Mathematical singularity is here, dissolving the human monopoly on discovery and forcing a redefinition of value in an era of collapsing intelligence costs.

## Argument

The host argues that the barrier to high-level mathematical discovery has collapsed. Frontier models solved 10 open problems, prompting a veteran to concede his $100k/paper bet early. The mechanism is not just speed but "cross-field translation" into verifiable constructions, creating a "deluge" of results that amateurs can now "oneshot." This triggers an existential crisis in academia, where math is described as a "religious order" being plundered by a "new machine god." Yet, the host frames this as democratic: the substrate is exploding, with compute doubling every 9 months toward 200 million H100 equivalents by 2028. The implication is that intelligence is becoming a utility, repricing energy markets and enabling new applications from Mars geology to primate tracking, where "all mysteries are shallow" given enough superintelligence.
