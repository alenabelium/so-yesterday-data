## Thesis

The proposed 'safety cartel' by AI labs is a transparent mechanism for regulatory capture and anti-competitive dominance, masking the reality that algorithmic efficiency and open innovation render coordinated pacing obsolete.

## Argument

The convergence of Altman, Musk, and Daario Amade on pacing the frontier was not altruism but a strategic move to entrench power. Critics correctly identify this as an 'alignment aristocracy' where dominant firms use safety rhetoric to set speed limits for everyone else, effectively demanding antitrust waivers while claiming moral high ground. The motive is revealed by the fact that liability and customer demand already align with reliability; there is no need for a cartel to police rivals who aren't even at the frontier yet.

The technical reality undermines the pacing argument. Recurrent looped transformers trade parameters for iterations, achieving gains that make compute-based pacing a 'leaky civ.' Meanwhile, open benchmarks like ARC AAGI4 warn that reducing openness undermines positive-sum futures. The research frontier advances through algorithmic innovation and open collaboration, not behind closed doors.

Geopolitically, the cartel fails because China is not pacing, making this a moment for historical critique rather than coordination. The underlying infrastructure is shifting power to Nvidia, now called the 'central bank of AI,' setting compute prices like the Fed sets money rates. This politicizes the buildout, with public lands opening for data centers and Bitcoin miners converting to inference. The narrative of safety is a cover for controlling the monetary authority of the next era.

## Counterpoints

- The 'safety cartel' is actually regulatory capture disguised as altruism, allowing dominant firms to set industry standards and exclude competitors under the guise of liability management.
- Algorithmic gains from recurrent looped transformers make compute-pacing obsolete, as efficiency improvements outstrip raw scale constraints.
- Open benchmarks like ARC AAGI4 demonstrate that independent evaluation and openness drive progress better than closed coordination.
- Geopolitical rivals like China are not participating in the pacing agreement, rendering the cartel's control over global development ineffective.
