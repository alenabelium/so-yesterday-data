## Thesis

By April 2026, AI has inverted the risk calculus: human-written code is now the hazard, and the bottleneck to exponential AI growth is no longer model capability but physical infrastructure—energy and compute.

## Argument

The inversion is stark: Anthropic's Mythos makes human coding itself hazardous, a reversal where the artisan for loop is the danger. This is not just a model milestone; it's a generational handoff, with Mythos trained at scale on Blackwells and Vera Rubin waiting. The frontier is racing ahead—OpenAI's staggered cyber model rollout, Elon's Colossus 2 with seven models in training—but sheer firepower isn't taste, as the leaked XAI memo admits. The model zoo is speciating: Meta's Muse Spark crushes data quality but flubs reasoning, Alibaba's Happy Horse 1.0 tops video benchmarks, and Bytedance's in-place test-time training makes a 4B model dominate at 128k context. Meanwhile, OpenAI solves Airdos problems, turning 20th-century conjectures into closed tickets. But the real story is the scissor: global flops grow ~3x annually, inference demand ~10x. That gap forecasts price hikes and a flight to smaller models. The applications layer drinks from the fire hose—Perplexity's ARR doubled, Tuby launches inside ChatGPT, Google folds NotebookLM into Gemini—and embodiment sneaks in as furniture, not humanoids. ## Counterpoints

- Sheer firepower is not the same as frontier position; seven simultaneous training runs cannot manufacture taste.
- The model zoo's specialization (e.g., Muse Spark) shows that benchmarks can be gamed, not that reasoning is solved.
- Energy costs and regulation can pause even Stargate's buildout, proving brute force has limits.
- Chinese and open labs run on ~10x less compute, yet that gap explains their creativity and urgency—suggesting compute isn't everything.
