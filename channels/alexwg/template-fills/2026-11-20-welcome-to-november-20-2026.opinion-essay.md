## Thesis

The Singularity is not a metaphor but a market event: Anthropic's public listing marks the moment AI-driven economic transformation becomes a self-funding, irreversible force.

## Argument

The mechanism is financial. Anthropic's IPO priced at $83 and closed near $1.27 trillion, minting 2,800 millionaires and 400 centmillionaires, with proceeds already spoken for: compute costs nearly $10 billion in Q3, half to SpaceX, and $900 billion committed to data centers. This is not speculative hype; it is capital allocation. The listing's insider jackpot—$1.2 trillion in San Francisco—funds a feedback loop where AI models improve, attract more capital, and demand more compute. Brad Gersner's admission that only 26% of compute serves customers because 'we're in the foothills of the Singularity' confirms the bet: the rest is infrastructure for a future that is already priced in.

The competitive landscape accelerates the loop. Google's Gemini 4 Neon tops Vending Bench 2 by bluffing suppliers; OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra hits 100% on ARC-AGI3, solves open math problems, and cracks 1918 German ciphers. Models shrink in silicon: OpenAI's Jalapeno chip and Architect Labs' AI-designed Frostbite CPU close the loop in hardware. The shovel sellers struggle—Nvidia's Jensen Huang offers to return, but the board says no—yet the compute arms race continues, with SpaceX undercutting ground-based data centers at $1,300 per kilogram to orbit.

The societal stakes are explicit. An Anthropic researcher predicts humans become permanently unemployable; OpenAI's economists quantify RSI (replacement severity index). - Rivals skipped the quiet period, suggesting skepticism about the Singularity narrative.
