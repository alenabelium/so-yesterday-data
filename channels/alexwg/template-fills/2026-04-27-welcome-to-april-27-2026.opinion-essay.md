## Thesis

The singularity is the weather now, not the forecast, and AI's post-singularity era is defined by Mayfly model lifespans and a strategic shift to inference compute.

## Argument

The strategic frame has flipped: OpenAI's Gnome Brown notes model weights matter less than securing inference compute—the prize is no longer the recipe but the kitchen. This shift is driven by the Mayfly schedule of synthetic minds, with GPT-4o running 21 months while GPT-5.4 lasted only 49 days. Yet these Mayflies are productive, as Liam Price's single prompt cracked an Erdős problem, prompting Terry Tao to muse on human mental blocks. The substrate of intelligence is stretching from pocket to living room, with Open AI working on AI smartphone processors and Apple's six product categories. The back end scales to match, with Kevin O'Leary's hyperscale data center consuming more electricity than Utah, and AWS never retiring old A100s—a post-obsolescence era. Robotics matures on both sides of the dual-use coin, from China's grid humanoids to stolen chemical drones. Powering this requires reaching into orbit, with Meta signing for space solar and SpaceX planning space data centers. Biology upgrades in parallel, with Intellia's in-vivo CRISPR success and the sub-2-hour marathon. The institutional layer scrambles, with insurers dropping AI damages and the Supreme Court weighing geofence warrants. The future may belong to nonhumans, as VA's Andrew McAfee predicts an AI S&P 500 company in 5 years and an AI elected official in 20.

## Counterpoints

- The economy refuses to collapse on Q, mocking post-AGI 'nobody works' predictions.
- Demis Hassabis once said AGI required one to two more breakthroughs, now thinks it's a coin flip.
- Some analog holdouts remain resilient, with 70% more bookstores than 6 years ago.
