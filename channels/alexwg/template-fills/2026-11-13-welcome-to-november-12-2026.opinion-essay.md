## Thesis

By November 2026, the intelligence explosion is imminent, and the AI industry's infrastructure buildout—from chip smuggling to space-based computing—is accelerating faster than regulators or even the doomers can keep up.

## Argument

The mechanism is infrastructure. The chip leak was structural: 1.5 gigawatts of smuggled chips, Singapore as a conduit, Moonshot renting B300s at a 350% markup. Washington's response—a remote access rule—is already being bypassed by panic renting. Capital barely flinched; it just changed tickers. Anthropic's IPO prices at $70–$78, with Goldman projecting $600 billion revenue by 2030, while OpenAI bleeds $10.9 billion operating loss. The AI Futures Project now expects the intelligence explosion in 2027, citing a 2x acceleration in R&D. The tools are compounding: Codex built a Python compiler in Rust, Gemini 4 Carbon verifies cryptography in 4 weeks, and Gemini Robotics sorts Mercedes parts at 96% success. Biology is next—Alpha Genome 2 nearly doubles variant detection, and Daario Amade predicts cancer and Alzheimer's will be relics within a decade. The buildout is outgrowing the planet: Coreweave's 10x oversubscribed IPO, SpaceX's $74 billion supercomput campuses, and Elon's order for 100 gigawatts of solar equipment to create a sentient sun. The Dyson swarm is going around.

## Counterpoints

- Doomers read the same curve as a threat: Paul Cristiano warns of material risk of AI takeover within 12 months.
- OpenAI's David Holtz counters that casual empiricism is becoming harmful.
- AI search resurfaces discredited papers, needing the archive debugged.
- OKXC plans 24/7 tokenized stocks over issuers' objections—a solution shopping for a problem.
- Elon thinks the board should have waited on Nvidia's CEO ouster, calling hiring Jensen an honor.
