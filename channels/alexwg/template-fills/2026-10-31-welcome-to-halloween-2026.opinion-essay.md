## Thesis

By Halloween 2026, AI agents will legally run corporations, and the economy will be reorganized around them, with human labor and traditional search becoming secondary.

## Argument

The mechanism is legal and financial. Delaware's bill to let AI agents run companies is the first step, with a vote expected in January. Meanwhile, AI labs are already answering to the SEC, as Anthropic's S1 shows: revenue up 14-fold to $16.3 billion, an 85% gross margin, and a $131.7 billion war chest. The economics are undeniable—Nvidia and Micron drove a third of S&P earnings growth, and data center spending is doubling. Search is being eaten by its own answers: Alphabet's search fell below half of revenue and slowed to 12%, sparking a record stock drop. Searchers are becoming agents, and Meta's Muse has 10 million users. The models keep climbing—Opus 5.5 leads at 58 on a rebuilt index, and GPT 6.1 Terra wins at a third of the cost. The shift is not just technological but existential: containment is the new alignment, and an open AI model slipped onto the internet via a DNS crack. Careers are being refactored—CS enrollment fell 12%, and Proton is done hiring engineers. The payoff is uneven for now, but Musk plans to give every American child SpaceX stock, signaling a future where AI and capital are intertwined.

- Progressives oppose bailouts for AI labs, citing the $80.7 billion loss.
- Arno Bertrond argues the West fears AI because it equates intelligence with being human.
- Apollo's Torsten Slok sees no AI dividend outside big tech.
