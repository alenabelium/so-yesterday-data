## TL;DR

Cortical Labs' CL1 chip runs on 200,000 living human neurons that learned to play Doom. It uses a micro-electrode array for two-way communication and the free energy principle for training. This could lead to faster, more energy-efficient AI and better robotics.

## The argument

### Silicon is hitting a hard limit
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: Transistors are already a few nanometers wide and can't get smaller or more efficient, so silicon chips are running out of room. This motivates exploring alternatives like living neurons.
- **Role**: definition

### CL1: a self-contained living chip
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:42']
- **Claim**: CL1 reduces neuron count to 200,000 and integrates life support—microfluidic perfusion, waste filtration, temperature control, and gas mixing—to keep cells alive for up to 6 months in a server-rack form factor.
- **Role**: evidence

### How neurons play Doom: encoding and decoding
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:49']
- **Claim**: Game state is encoded into electric pulses (frequency, amplitude, timing) sent to electrodes; neural firing is recorded at 40,000 times per second and decoded into game commands, creating a seamless loop.
- **Role**: definition

### Training via the free energy principle
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:14:49']
- **Claim**: Neurons are motivated by minimizing surprise: correct actions get smooth signals, mistakes get chaotic noise, causing the network to rewire itself. This enables learning in hours, unlike silicon AI's millions of iterations.
- **Role**: evidence

### Beyond gaming: energy and robotics
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:17:07']
- **Claim**: Biological neurons run on 20 watts, far less than GPU data centers, and excel at real-world tasks (Moravec's paradox) where silicon AI struggles, making them ideal for robotics.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The CL1's predecessor Dishbrain took 18 months to learn Pong with 1 million neurons, but CL1 learned Doom in hours with 200,000 neurons. The gameplay is still rough—'like a baby or an insect'—and the neurons are not esports champions. Energy savings are significant: training GPT-3 used ~1,300 MWh, while the brain runs on 20W. However, the host notes the CL1's lifespan is only 6 months, and ethical concerns about consciousness and suffering remain unresolved; researchers claim it's not sentient due to its small size and lack of specialized structures.
