## Thesis

The AI backlash is a rational sentiment collapse driven by tangible economic harms and undemocratic imposition, not ignorance of the technology.

## Argument

The paradox of mass adoption paired with mass resentment reveals a values gap, not a knowledge gap. While experts focus on benchmarks, the public evaluates AI by its real-world costs: job displacement fears, copyright theft, and utility bill spikes from data centers. This is not fear of the unknown; it is rejection of a trade-off where tech companies reap profits while communities bear the physical and financial burden. The backlash is organized resistance to having decisions imposed by actors with vastly more power, manifesting in lawsuits, strikes, and local moratoriums that signal a demand for control, not just critique.

## Counterpoints

- AI is merely a convenient excuse for layoffs in a tough economy, not the actual cause.
- Resistance is just fear of the unknown that will fade as people get used to the technology.
- Labor market data from 2022-2025 shows AI hasn't yet caused widespread job losses at scale.
