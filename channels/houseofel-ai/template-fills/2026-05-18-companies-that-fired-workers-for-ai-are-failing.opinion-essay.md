## Thesis

Companies firing workers for AI are failing because the technology amplifies human judgment rather than replacing it, while hidden costs and the Jevons paradox make pure automation a financial trap.

## Argument

The central mechanism is [[human-amplification]]. Gartner data shows no correlation between AI-driven layoffs and higher ROI; instead, high-return firms use AI to augment staff. The narrative of replacement is often [[ai-washing]], a cover for over-hiring corrections or generic cost-cutting. Furthermore, the [[jevons-paradox]] dictates that as AI makes work cheaper, demand explodes rather than shrinks, creating new roles like prompt engineering and AI governance. Finally, the financial model is flawed: replacing fixed salaries with unpredictable [[ai-infrastructure-costs]] creates budget volatility. Integration expenses often exceed estimates by 30-50%, and [[vendor-lock-in]] turns operational flexibility into a mystery bill. The most productive path is combining human expertise with AI tools, not substituting one for the other.

## Counterpoints

- Some executives claim AI will eliminate half of white-collar jobs, but this prediction has been moderated as the Jevons paradox takes effect.
- AI washing allows companies to blame AI for layoffs that would have happened anyway due to pandemic-era over-hiring.
- The pain of job loss is real and immediate, even if long-term data suggests net job creation and augmented productivity.
