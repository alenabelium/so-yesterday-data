## Thesis

Public anger toward AI is misdirected at the science rather than the economic precarity and corporate coercion that define its current deployment.

## Argument

The host argues that AI is a neutral scientific discipline, comparable to medicine, which has already delivered massive benefits like AlphaFold's protein folding predictions and early cancer detection. The prevailing "hate" for AI is actually a rational response to a precarious economic environment where median incomes have stagnated while housing costs have surged, making any technological disruption feel existential. Unlike smartphones, which arrived during a period of stability, AI is being forced upon users by tech giants through embedded products and workplace mandates, bypassing consent. This corporate recklessness, combined with unresolved questions over data consent and accuracy, creates a regulatory vacuum. The solution is not to reject the science, which is immutable, but to target the specific policy and economic choices—labor protections, data regulation, and corporate accountability—that determine whether AI serves as an opportunity or a threat.
