## Thesis

Public sentiment toward AI is turning negative not because the technology is useless, but because it is being forced on people through governance, workplace mandates, and financial structures.

## Argument

The mechanism is coercion. From the FAA's AI air traffic tool to workplace mandates that grade employees on AI usage, the technology is being imposed on people who have no say in the matter. Even those who see AI's value are fed up because they're forced to use it, forced to invest in it, and forced to trust it. The result is a system where the risks pile up on the people with the least power, and the benefits accrue to those who make the decisions. This is not about the technology itself; it's about the governance. The Anthropic IPO is the clearest example yet: passive investment vehicles will force ordinary savers to buy AI stocks without consent, creating a bubble-like dynamic. The solution is not to abandon AI but to demand serious AI governance and financial regulation before a major crisis occurs.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that AI is genuinely useful and that people who complain are just resistant to change.
- Others point out that the FAA and banks deny AI caused the outages, and that AI-written exploits often fail.
- There's also the view that index fund inclusion is a normal market mechanism and that investors can choose to avoid AI stocks if they want.
