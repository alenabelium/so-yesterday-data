## Thesis

The AI industry's $1.5 trillion valuation is an unsustainable bubble built on a fragile debt chain that will collapse when revenue fails to match the staggering infrastructure costs.

## Argument

The central mechanism of this crisis is not technological failure but financial overextension. OpenAI’s demand for a $1.5 trillion valuation against projected $350 billion in annual revenue by 2030 creates a $856 billion infrastructure gap funded by layered debt. This structure relies on bond investors lending to SoftBank, which lends to OpenAI, while Nvidia guarantees the collateral value of GPUs. If AI monetization lags, this chain breaks, transferring risk to ordinary investors via S&P 500 index funds. Simultaneously, enterprise adoption is stalling: 95% of generative AI pilots fail to deliver measurable impact, and experienced developers are actually 19% slower using AI tools. The industry is burning cash on compute while ignoring the organizational changes needed for real productivity gains, much like factories that kept steam layouts after switching to electric motors.

## Counterpoints

- AI delivers real productivity gains of 15-50% in specific tasks like coding and support.
- Small models (1-3B parameters) maintain 85-95% accuracy for domain-specific routine tasks.
- The situation is a 'localized bubble' within a genuine technological revolution, not a total collapse.
