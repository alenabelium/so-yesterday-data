## Thesis

The $1.5 trillion AI valuation is an unsustainable bubble built on a fragile debt chain that will collapse if revenue fails to match the $856 billion infrastructure spend.

## Argument

The industry is suffering from a fatal disconnect between technological potential and financial reality. While AI delivers real productivity gains (15-50% in specific tasks), 95% of enterprise pilots fail to generate measurable business impact, and only 20% of firms see revenue growth. The valuation gap—OpenAI demanding $1.5 trillion against a $300 billion shortfall from investors—ignores the fact that current deployment is plagued by misuse, such as Flock Safety’s surveillance scandals and military errors in Iran.

The financial structure is the critical vulnerability. It relies on layered borrowing: bond investors lend to SoftBank, which funds OpenAI, which borrows for infrastructure from companies like Oracle and CoreWeave. These infrastructure firms borrow to buy GPUs, with Nvidia sometimes guaranteeing the asset value. This creates a circular dependency where lenders assume hardware will retain value only if AI spending continues. With AI-related corporate bonds trading at wider spreads (115bps vs 78bps) and loans for projects like Oracle’s Project Jupiter selling at discounts (89-91 cents on the dollar), capital is becoming expensive. The system works only if AI generates massive revenue to service this debt; otherwise, the risk cascades through balance sheets into ordinary investors' index funds.

## Counterpoints

- AI provides genuine productivity gains of 15-50% in writing and coding, with labor productivity growth doubling in 2025.
- Small models (1-3B parameters) maintain 85-95% accuracy for domain-specific tasks, reducing costs for routine work.
- The situation is a 'technological revolution with localized bubble dynamics' rather than a total burst, per academic analysis.
