## Thesis

The AI industry’s $1.5 trillion valuation is an unsustainable bubble built on a fragile debt chain, where massive capital expenditures vastly outpace actual revenue and organizational integration capabilities.

## Argument

The core failure is not technological but structural. While AI delivers real productivity gains (15-50% in specific tasks), the industry is running on "vibration-based accounting." Only 20% of firms see revenue growth, and 95% of enterprise pilots fail to achieve measurable impact. The financial structure relies on layered borrowing: bond investors lend to SoftBank, which funds OpenAI’s $856B infrastructure spend, while Nvidia guarantees asset values. This creates a chain where risk is shifted but not eliminated. As seen in the Flock Safety surveillance scandal and the Iran school strike, misuse stems from human deployment failures, not AI malfunction. The market is already pricing in this risk; AI-related corporate bonds trade at wider spreads (115bps vs 78bps), and loans are trading at discounts (90 cents on the dollar). The gap between OpenAI’s $1.5T valuation ask and investor offers of $1.2T highlights a disconnect between hype and economic reality. Ordinary investors face systemic risk through index fund concentration in tech stocks, as the debt chain could cascade if revenue projections fail to materialize.

## Counterpoints

- AI delivers genuine productivity gains (15-50%) in writing, support, and coding.
- Small models (1-3B params) maintain 85-95% accuracy for domain-specific tasks.
- The technology is a true revolution, not just a bubble, requiring organizational adaptation like the electric dynamo.
