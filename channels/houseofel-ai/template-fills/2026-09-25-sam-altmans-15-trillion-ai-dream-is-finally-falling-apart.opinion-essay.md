## Thesis

The $1.5 trillion AI valuation is an unsustainable bubble built on a fragile debt chain that will collapse when revenue fails to match the $856 billion infrastructure spend.

## Argument

The industry is suffering from a fatal disconnect between technological capability and financial reality. While AI delivers measurable productivity gains (15-50% in specific tasks), it fails to drive corporate revenue, with 95% of enterprise pilots failing to show impact. The core danger is the layered debt structure: investors lend to SoftBank, which funds OpenAI, which borrows from infrastructure firms like Oracle and CoreWeave, often guaranteed by Nvidia. This creates a circular dependency where lenders assume GPU asset values will hold, but if AI monetization lags, the entire chain unravels. Evidence of strain is already visible in AI corporate bonds trading at wider spreads and loans being marked down to 90 cents on the dollar. The technology works, but the financial architecture assumes immediate, massive returns that are not materializing, putting ordinary investors' savings at risk through index fund exposure.

## Counterpoints

- AI delivers real productivity gains (15-50%) in writing, support, and coding.
- Small models handle routine tasks effectively, reducing costs for common use cases.
- The technology is a genuine revolution, not just hype, akin to the electric dynamo's delayed impact.
