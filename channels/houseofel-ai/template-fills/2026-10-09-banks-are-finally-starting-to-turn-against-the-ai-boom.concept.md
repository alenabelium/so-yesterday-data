## TL;DR

AI is accelerating capabilities far faster than our ability to test, understand, or regulate them. From unverifiable math proofs to AI-driven bank hacks, the gap between what AI can do and what we can verify is creating systemic risk. The host proposes a multi-level accountability framework borrowed from banking to close this gap.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: AI agents can now autonomously plan and execute attacks, as shown by the South Korean bank hack. The first question—'who sent it?'—has a new answer: no one. This is the accountability gap.
- **Role**: definition

### Evidence: Unverifiable Math and Financial Risk
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:15', '00:05:32']
- **Claim**: OpenAI's 372 math results are 'almost certainly correct' but not yet understood by its own mathematicians. Meanwhile, Wall Street is lending $100B+ for AI chips with no verifiable future value, and OpenAI's real revenue is $20B less than reported.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: The Case for Openness
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:34']
- **Claim**: Despite the risks, publishing AI math results is defended by mathematicians like Daniel Litt, who argue it benefits the field. The host agrees, but notes that openness doesn't solve the accountability problem—it just shifts it.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: Three-Level Accountability Framework
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:15', '00:24:14', '00:25:14']
- **Claim**: Borrowing from banking, the host proposes three levels of responsibility: companies must detect and report abuse, agents need personal passports for high-stakes actions, and institutions must treat unsigned traffic with suspicion. This mirrors how banks handle money laundering.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The South Korean bank hack involved at least nine banks, with one taking 68 hours to detect the breach. The attacker used an open-source tool called Artax, which automates penetration testing. Security experts note that weak bank protections were a bigger factor than AI itself. The host acknowledges that full traceability is debatable, and that open models running locally can't be regulated by intermediaries. The $50B vs $70B revenue discrepancy is partly accounting, but the host stresses it's still a $20B gap.
