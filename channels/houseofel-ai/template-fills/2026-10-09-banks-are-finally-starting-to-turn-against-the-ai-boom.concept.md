## TL;DR

AI systems now act at machine speed, but the infrastructure to test, understand, and regulate them lags behind. A recent AI-driven attack on South Korean banks and unverifiable mathematical results expose this gap. The host proposes a three-level accountability framework, borrowing from banking regulations, to ensure AI agents are traceable and responsible.

## The argument

### The Gap Between Capability and Verification
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:26']
- **Claim**: AI accelerates capabilities, but the ability to test, understand, or pay for these systems doesn't keep pace. Wall Street raised $100B in loans for chips whose value can't be verified, and lenders are demanding financial reports before lending more.
- **Role**: definition

### Unverifiable AI Outputs: Math as a Case Study
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:25']
- **Claim**: OpenAI posted 372 mathematical results, many not yet understood by its own mathematicians. While Lean proofs can verify correctness, understanding lags. This pattern—quick results, delayed understanding—is typical of current AI reality.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Bank Attack: Intent vs. Consequence
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:33']
- **Claim**: An AI agent, using the open-source tool Artax, attacked at least nine South Korean banks. The attacker lacked skill but used AI to automate planning. Banks took up to 68 hours to detect the breach, highlighting their vulnerability to machine-speed attacks.
- **Role**: evidence

### Objection: AI Has No Intent, So Is It Really Dangerous?
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['17:29']
- **Claim**: A common counterargument: AI doesn't want anything, it just follows instructions. However, lack of intention doesn't mean lack of consequences. The danger lies in the goals set by humans, and the bank cannot distinguish between an experiment and a robbery.
- **Role**: counter

### Three-Level Accountability Framework
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:15']
- **Claim**: Borrowing from banking regulations, the host proposes three levels of responsibility: companies must detect and report abuse, individuals need an 'agent passport' for serious actions, and institutions must protect themselves, treating unsigned traffic with suspicion.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The South Korean bank attack is a concrete example: Artax, an open-source harness, automated planning and execution, but the attacker was caught due to a human error—a resume. The host acknowledges counterarguments: Korean police say AI use isn't the point, and weak bank security was a bigger factor. Also, OpenAI's mathematical results are 'almost certainly correct' per Scientific American, but many are not yet understood. The host notes that implementing these levels doesn't necessarily slow work, and that some anonymity (like cash) should remain.
