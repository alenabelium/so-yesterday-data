## TL;DR

AI agents are accelerating capabilities in math, finance, and security, but the infrastructure for testing, understanding, and regulating them lags behind. This gap creates risks, from unverifiable proofs to bank hacks. A multi-level accountability framework, inspired by banking regulations, can ensure AI agents are traceable and responsible.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: AI agents can now autonomously plan and execute attacks, as seen in the South Korean bank hack, but the first question—'who sent it?'—often has no answer. This lack of accountability is the core problem.
- **Role**: definition

### Evidence: The Math and Finance Gaps
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:25']
- **Claim**: OpenAI's 372 math results are likely correct but not yet understood by its own mathematicians, and the $60 billion Anthropic debt package shows lenders demanding proof before funding. Both cases show a gap between capability and verification.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: The Case for Openness
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:30']
- **Claim**: Some argue for publishing AI results regardless of understanding, citing the aspirin analogy: knowing something works is valuable even without knowing why. This counters the demand for immediate explanation.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: Three-Level Accountability
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:23:15']
- **Claim**: Borrowing from banking anti-money-laundering rules, responsibility should be distributed across three levels: companies detecting abuse, a person-level agent passport, and institutions treating unsigned traffic with suspicion.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The South Korean bank hack involved at least nine banks, with Shinhan Bank compromising 25,000 customers and KB Kookmin taking 68 hours to detect the breach. The attacker used Artax, an open-source harness, and was identified only by a human error on his resume. OpenAI's math results were verified by Lean, but the company admitted its own mathematicians don't understand many of them. The host acknowledges that open models on personal computers are hard to regulate, and that full traceability may not be desirable, as cash transactions remain anonymous. Lenders demanding financial statements is seen as a positive market check.
