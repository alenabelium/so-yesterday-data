## Thesis

Jensen Huang's resignation didn't pop the AI bubble, but it exposed a single point of failure in the industry's circular financing machine, and the fallout will hit Nvidia and its bankrolled companies while the rest of AI moves on.

## Argument

The real crash came from voters, not Huang's love life. On November 5th, the NASDAQ fell 5.5% and Nvidia fell 9% — before any affair news broke. The trigger was the midterm elections: Democrats swept Congress on anti-AI platforms, and 44 of 45 data center referendums passed restrictions. That's a systemic bug affecting all AI chip makers — AMD fell 17% that day. But when Huang's affair became public, only Nvidia fell, proving the scandal is a company-specific bug.

Huang's departure matters because he wasn't just Nvidia's CEO — he was its chief salesman, lobbyist, and banker. Nvidia plowed profits into bankrolling startups, suppliers, and customers, with Huang personally involved in billions of dollars of deals. Since January, Nvidia made 120+ investments. He guaranteed $105 billion of OpenAI's lease payments for a data center campus that will run Nvidia hardware for 20 years. He promised $36 billion to buy compute from smaller cloud companies if they can't find customers — who then borrow against those promises to buy more Nvidia chips. That's circular financing, and Huang was the linchpin.

Now that he's gone, the web of guarantees is in question. CoreWeave, which fell with Nvidia on his exit, carries $47 billion in debt and pays more in interest than operating profit. An executive at a cloud company expects Nvidia's dealmaking to halt. But there's an upside: the 'Yansen jail' — where companies stayed loyal out of fear of getting fewer chips — may open. ## Counterpoints

- The spy damage is unproven — no public evidence that Alice Huang got sensitive info, and US intelligence hasn't published its evidence.
- Nvidia's board said it found no compromise of secrets, but the board is full of long-time associates, so its independence is questionable.
- Huang's defenders argue that Chinese AI labs building on American chips is better than them building their own, a view with merit.
- Some argue the CFO will ruin Nvidia, but history is mixed — Tim Cook (operations) did fine at Apple, while Intel's CFO-led tenure was rough.
- Huang himself denied circular financing, saying 'OpenAI will pay the lease,' but the market's reaction suggests skepticism.
