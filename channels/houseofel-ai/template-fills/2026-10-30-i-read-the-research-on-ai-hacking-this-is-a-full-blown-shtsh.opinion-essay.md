## Thesis

AI has made finding security flaws fast while fixing them remains slow, creating a dangerous gap that the entire ecosystem around AI must evolve to close.

## Argument

The mechanism is the asymmetry between AI's speed at finding vulnerabilities and the slow, human-dependent process of fixing and verifying them. This gap is widening as AI-generated fixes often break things, prompt injection cannot be fully trained away, and tracking AI actions remains unsolved. The research shows that while fencing AI in is feasible with existing tools, the infrastructure for monitoring and verifying AI work is not keeping up. This is not just a technical problem but a financial one, as companies like Anthropic seek public investment while openly listing these unresolved risks. The ecosystem must evolve with AI, adopting a balanced, evidence-based perspective rather than succumbing to hype or fear.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that the long list of risks in Anthropic's IPO filing is a normal legal precaution, not a sign of imminent danger.
- Others claim that talk about AI dangers makes it sound more powerful and therefore more valuable, turning warnings into a sales pitch.
- The sheer number of flaws found is not necessarily scary, as only a small fraction are actually exploited by attackers.
