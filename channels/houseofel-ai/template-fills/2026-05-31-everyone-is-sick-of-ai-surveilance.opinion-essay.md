## Thesis

The reactive model of regulation cannot keep pace with AI deployment, leaving citizens with no effective anonymity in public spaces because commercial incentives prioritize surveillance expansion over public oversight.

## Argument

The mechanism driving this loss of anonymity is the structural alignment of financial incentives with surveillance capability. Flock Safety’s national network allows any participating agency to search any other’s data without warrants, creating a system where the default is sharing, not privacy. This mirrors the Facebook data-sharing model: the architecture expands before oversight understands it. The consequences are already visible in three specific failures: local officers using the network for immigration enforcement via "side door access," employees accessing live feeds of children’s gymnastics classes for sales demos, and contract traps that allow companies to reinstall cameras after termination. These are not bugs but features of a system where valuation grows as data flows. The public’s belief that they can opt out by using cash or privacy tools is outdated; vehicle fingerprints, transaction logs, and facial recognition databases create a comprehensive record of movement that is functionally impossible to avoid in modern cities. Because regulation is inherently reactive—written only after misuse occurs—it lags behind "venture capital speed." By the time laws are drafted, the data is already shared and the infrastructure entrenched. The solution requires "agile regulation": iterative, adaptive testing against failure modes before deployment, rather than the current model of deploying first and discovering misuse later.

## Counterpoints

- The core technology genuinely helps recover stolen vehicles and find missing persons.
- Some argue individuals can avoid surveillance by paying in cash or using Faraday cages.
- Flock claims it does not work with ICE, relying on the distinction that local agencies share data.
- The market rewards Flock’s behavior, suggesting the model is economically viable despite protests.
- AI itself is neutral; the issue is deployment without guardrails, not the technology.
