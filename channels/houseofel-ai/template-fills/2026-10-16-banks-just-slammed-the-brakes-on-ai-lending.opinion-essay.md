## Thesis

AI makes software cheap to copy, but that is not the same as making it worthless, and the value it creates may flow to users rather than to AI companies or their lenders.

## Argument

The mechanism is cost. A single developer built seven Adobe alternatives for about $2,000 using Claude Opus 5.5, and another built a Photoshop competitor for a similar sum. But copying the visible surface is not the same as replicating the underlying reliability that professionals pay for. PhotoCraft's own plan admits it covers only 25–35% of professional needs, and tests show it matches Photoshop's smart filters just 5 times out of 30. Free alternatives have existed for decades—GIMP since the mid-1990s, LibreOffice with 200 million users versus Office's 1.2 billion—yet Adobe just posted its best quarter ever, with creative revenue up 13% to $4.65 billion. The value is in trust, habit, and years of stored files, not in the code itself.

Meanwhile, lenders are acting on the second bet: that AI won't earn enough to repay its debts. AI-backed loans fell from $113 billion in June to $23 billion in September, and credit insurance costs hit record highs—Oracle's at $2.88, Coreweave's jumping from $3.60 to over $10 in nine days. Coreweave's bonds trade at 77 cents on the dollar. This is real caution, not just a pause after early borrowing.

So where does the value go? Anthropic's revenue is growing, but slowing—45% in Q3 versus doubling before. The most likely answer: value flows to users through lower prices, not to AI companies or lenders. That's good for everyone except those who borrowed expecting to capture it. Both bets are partly right: AI makes code cheap without making anyone rich.

## Counterpoints

- The drop in AI lending could just mean companies already borrowed early in the year; Morgan Stanley's own explanation.
- Demand for chips is real—TSMC posted its sixth straight record quarter with profits up 69%.
- Rising interest rates make all borrowing more expensive, not just AI debt.
- Free copies have never outperformed real copies before, so why would this time be different?
