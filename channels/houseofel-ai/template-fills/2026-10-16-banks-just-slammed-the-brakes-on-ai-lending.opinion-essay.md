## Thesis

AI makes software cheap to copy, not worthless, and the value it creates may flow to users rather than to AI companies or their lenders.

## Argument

The mechanism is cost. A single developer, Brendan Thomas, claims to have rebuilt the Adobe suite with AI, but his own plan admits PhotoCraft covers only 25–35% of professional needs, and tests show it matches Photoshop's smart filters just 5 times out of 30. Free copies of expensive software are nothing new—GIMP and LibreOffice have existed for decades without killing Adobe or Microsoft Office. What's new is speed and cheapness, not the threat itself. Adobe just posted its best quarter ever, with creative revenue up 13% to $4.65 billion. So the first bet—that AI renders software useless—looks shaky.

The second bet is about debt. Since June, AI-backed loans have collapsed from $113 billion to $23 billion, and credit default swap prices for Oracle, Coreweave, and even Amazon and Nvidia hit record highs. Lenders are lending less and charging more, which signals real caution, not just early borrowing. Coreweave's bonds trade at 77 cents on the dollar, and its CDS jumped from $3.60 to over $10 in nine days. The question isn't whether AI demand is real—TSMC just posted its sixth straight record quarter—but whether renting chips earns enough to repay the debt built around them.

Where does the value go? Anthropic's revenue grew 45% in Q3, but that's a slowdown from doubling. Free apps are funded by selling AI models, so value may flow to model makers or to users via lower prices. ## Counterpoints

- The drop in AI lending could be innocent—companies simply borrowed early, and the year is still huge with $466 billion in AI-related debt due by 2026.
- Rising interest rates make all borrowing more expensive, so higher insurance costs might not be specific to AI companies.
- Demand for chips is real—TSMC posted its sixth consecutive record quarter with profits up 69%.
- AI revenues are still high—Anthropic grew 45% in Q3, so the second bet might be too pessimistic.
