## TL;DR

As AI inference costs plummet, consumption explodes via agentic workflows, creating a cost crisis for Big Tech. Companies like Uber and Meta are burning budgets through gamified token consumption rather than measuring output. The industry must pivot from internal engineering waste to empowering external researchers to justify the $740B capex.

## The argument

### Define the Jevons Paradox Mechanism
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:26']
- **Claim**: When a resource becomes more efficient, usage increases disproportionately. Cheaper tokens drive agentic workflows that consume 5-30x more tokens per task, meaning lower unit costs lead to higher aggregate spend.
- **Role**: definition

### Evidence of Gamified Waste
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:31']
- **Claim**: Meta's 'Claonomics' leaderboard and Amazon's 'token max' culture incentivized employees to consume tokens for status, not value. Uber's engineering team burned its annual budget in four months by rewarding consumption.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: The Incentive Contradiction
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:34']
- **Claim**: Nvidia's CEO urges engineers to burn $250k in tokens to boost GPU demand, while its own VP notes compute costs exceed payroll. This contradiction reveals supply-side incentives driving unsustainable consumption.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: The Research Pivot
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:06']
- **Claim**: The $740B capex is justified not by internal engineering efficiency, but by subsidizing frontier research in medicine and climate science. This creates organic, sustainable demand rooted in discovery rather than gamified metrics.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

Uber's CTO admitted burning an annual AI budget in four months (00:00:00). Meta employees consumed 60 trillion tokens in 30 days, with top users burning 281 billion (00:06:31). Microsoft killed Anthropic Cloud Code licenses to unify tooling and cut costs, despite engineers preferring it (00:09:50). The speaker notes that AI automation is only economically viable in 23% of roles with visual tasks (00:13:55). Caveat: The speaker admits it is impossible to call a bubble in real-time and acknowledges the technology produces real output, but questions the sustainability of the spending rate relative to returns.
