## Thesis

Big Tech CEOs are suffering from 'AI psychosis'—a structural delusion caused by insulated feedback loops that drives massive, unjustified layoffs despite research showing no robust link between AI adoption and aggregate productivity.

## Argument

The core mechanism is **[[information-insulation]]**. CEOs like Aaron Levy diagnose a condition where leaders, being distant from the 'last mile' of work, mistake the 'happy path' of demos for deployable reality. They see a contract generated in a demo but ignore the legal review, edge cases, and human labor required for actual deployment. This is not new; it is the 'yesmen problem' industrialized. LLMs are trained to align with user thinking, creating a **[[syphensy-problem]]** that mirrors organizational echo chambers. When dissent is filtered out, decision quality collapses.

This structural delusion manifests in **[[delusional-confidence]]**. CEOs like Mark Benioff, Zeb Evans, and Sam Altman declare massive workforce reductions with absolute certainty, ignoring that research shows mixed results. A UC Berkeley meta-analysis found 'no robust relationship between AI adoption and aggregate productivity gain.' MIT researchers note that while models may reach base competence by 2029, they are not yet expert quality. Furthermore, adding AI to skilled workers can sometimes degrade output, and individual productivity gains often create organizational bottlenecks at the management level.

Despite this, companies like Meta, Oracle, and Amazon are cutting tens of thousands of jobs while posting record profits, funding a $700 billion AI infrastructure spend. This is **[[ai-washing]]**: using AI as an excuse for cost-cutting and labor repricing to appear lean to investors. - Some CEOs like Dario Amodei are honest about the risks, suggesting they might be the only sober voices in the room.
- AI does deliver measurable improvements in specific contexts, such as a 35% throughput lift for junior support agents.
- Some layoffs are genuine 'AI washing,' using AI as an excuse for efficiency plays that would happen regardless of technology.
