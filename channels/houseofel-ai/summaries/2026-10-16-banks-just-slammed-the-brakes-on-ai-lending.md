# Banks Just Slammed the Brakes on AI Lending

## Executive Summary

The video examines two conflicting market bets on AI: that AI will render software useless, and that AI companies may not earn enough to repay their debts. It analyzes a developer's claim of creating free Adobe alternatives with AI, showing they cover only a fraction of professional features, and highlights declining AI-related lending and rising credit insurance costs as signs of lender caution. The video concludes that AI makes software cheap to copy but not necessarily worthless, and that the value created may flow to users rather than to AI companies or lenders.

## Key Points

- A developer released seven free Adobe alternatives built with AI, claiming 100% parity, but his own plan admits only 25-35% coverage of professional needs, and tests show poor compatibility with Photoshop files. [00:04:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AdQQXU3E_mA&t=249)
- Free alternatives to expensive software have existed for decades (GIMP, LibreOffice) without displacing paid versions, because reliability, familiarity, and ecosystem lock-in matter more than price. [00:06:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AdQQXU3E_mA&t=418)
- AI-related lending has collapsed from $113 billion in June to $23 billion in September, while credit default swap costs for companies like Oracle and Coreweave hit record highs, signaling lender distrust in AI's ability to repay debt. [00:12:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AdQQXU3E_mA&t=733)
- The value created by AI may be flowing to users through lower prices rather than to AI companies or lenders, leaving middle-layer companies like Coreweave and Oracle bearing the risk. [00:17:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AdQQXU3E_mA&t=1065)
