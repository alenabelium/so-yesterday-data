# AI Isn’t The Future. It’s History Repeating Itself

## Executive Summary

The video analyzes a recent commencement speech at UCF where a corporate executive compared AI to the Industrial Revolution, triggering a negative reaction from humanities graduates who view AI as an existential threat to their careers. By examining the historical reality of the Industrial Revolution, the speaker argues that this comparison is structurally accurate but ignores the decades of exploitation, inequality, and suffering that preceded any widespread benefit. The core argument posits that AI's impact will not be uniformly positive, but rather determined by the speed and effectiveness of regulatory reforms and the distribution of power between tech elites and affected communities. Ultimately, the video suggests that the 'booing' was a rational response to a narrative that glossed over the severe downsides of technological disruption for non-technical workers.

## Key Points

- The contrasting reactions to AI at UCF (humanities) versus Carnegie Mellon (STEM) highlight how the same technology is perceived as a threat by those whose skills are being automated and as an opportunity by those building it [00:02:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2WcpagVEnIw&t=161).
- Historical analysis of the British Industrial Revolution reveals that the period was characterized by extreme child labor, stagnant life expectancy, and massive wealth inequality for over 60 years before benefits filtered down to the general population [00:04:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2WcpagVEnIw&t=276).
- Political disenfranchisement during the Industrial Revolution, exemplified by 'rotten boroughs,' parallels modern issues where communities hosting AI infrastructure lack meaningful consent or power over the extraction of local resources like water and energy [00:09:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2WcpagVEnIw&t=547).
- Reforms such as the Factory Acts took decades of intense political pressure to implement, suggesting that the current AI revolution may also require long, hard-fought regulatory battles to ensure benefits are distributed fairly [00:11:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2WcpagVEnIw&t=683).
