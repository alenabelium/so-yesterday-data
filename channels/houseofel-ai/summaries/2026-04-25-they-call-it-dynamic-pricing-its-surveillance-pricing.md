# They Call It Dynamic Pricing. It’s Surveillance Pricing

## Executive Summary

The video argues that 'dynamic pricing' in grocery stores is actually 'surveillance pricing,' where AI systems use facial recognition and personal data to charge individuals their maximum willingness to pay. It highlights Kroger's deployment of this technology, which raises significant ethical concerns regarding discrimination and privacy, despite corporate denials. Although Maryland recently passed a ban on the practice, the legislation lacks enforcement mechanisms, rendering it ineffective against widespread industry adoption.

## Key Points

- Retailers like Kroger and Walmart are using AI and facial recognition to implement real-time personalized pricing, a practice the speaker terms 'surveillance pricing' rather than dynamic pricing [00:01:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UOXj-8IxRCE&t=60).
- Maryland became the first state to ban dynamic pricing in grocery stores, but the law lacks enforcement teeth, allowing retailers to continue deploying surveillance infrastructure with minimal consequences [00:04:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UOXj-8IxRCE&t=293).
- The technology poses serious risks of algorithmic bias, potentially overcharging minority communities due to higher error rates in facial recognition for darker skin tones [00:03:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UOXj-8IxRCE&t=195).
- The broader industry trend involves retailers building detailed consumer profiles to sell data and maximize profits, a practice that is expanding nationally despite isolated state-level bans [00:06:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UOXj-8IxRCE&t=419).
