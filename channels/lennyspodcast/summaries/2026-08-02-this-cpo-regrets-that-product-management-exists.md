# This CPO regrets that product management exists 

## Executive Summary

Tom Verilli, CPO of Whatnot, argues that the traditional product management function has become bloated and counterproductive, advocating for a model where engineers and designers retain decision-making autonomy to prevent skill atrophy. He emphasizes that senior product leaders should return to individual contributor roles, leveraging AI to execute directly rather than managing through layers of alignment and bureaucracy. This shift allows for faster, more accurate decision-making grounded in deep technical and customer understanding, effectively reducing the need for specialized PM roles in routine tasks.

## Key Points

- Verilli critiques the 'HR ratio' of hiring PMs for every engineering pod, arguing that it infantilizes engineers and designers by abstracting them from critical decision-making, causing their strategic muscles to atrophy [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ruvis-VWg2s&t=0).
- The industry is shifting toward senior product leaders acting as individual contributors who use AI tools to gain direct leverage on data and code, rather than managing through layers of alignment meetings [00:13:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ruvis-VWg2s&t=782).
- Hiring criteria for PMs have evolved to prioritize systems thinking and technical judgment over political alignment, with Whatnot hiring only one PM from over 31,000 applicants to maintain high standards [00:18:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ruvis-VWg2s&t=1133).
- Effective product management requires a 'play the accordion' approach, constantly zooming out to assess macro business implications and zooming back in to execute, rather than relying on static roadmaps [01:02:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ruvis-VWg2s&t=3720).
- Leaders must avoid the trap of relying on averages, which can hide critical edge cases, and instead engage deeply with ground truth data to make nuanced decisions that protect specific user segments [01:15:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ruvis-VWg2s&t=4522).
