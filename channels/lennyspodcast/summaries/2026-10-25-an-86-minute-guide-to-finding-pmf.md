# An 86-minute guide to finding PMF 

## Executive Summary

Rob Snyder introduces the 'Pull' framework for finding product-market fit, emphasizing that true demand comes from specific individuals with urgent, unavoidable projects that existing tools can't solve. He advises founders to discover these 'pull' situations through direct experience and sales conversations rather than brainstorming, and to validate by getting customers to try to buy before building. The process involves iterating through hypotheses, sales calls, and 'Ave Maria' questions, then scaling by treating the startup as a factory for repeating successful case studies.

## Key Points

- The Pull framework (Project, Urgency, List of options, Limitations) identifies when a specific person will tear a product from your hands because they have an urgent, unavoidable project that current tools can't handle. [00:06:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=19qrLchbQEc&t=364)
- To find pull, get your hands dirty and experience the customer's world directly, rather than brainstorming or relying on design partners. [00:20:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=19qrLchbQEc&t=1211)
- During sales calls, focus on discovery and conceptual proposals, and look for 'action in the form of a train'—signals that the customer is trying to buy now, not just enthusiasm. [00:26:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=19qrLchbQEc&t=1612)
- If pull isn't found, ask the 'Ave Maria' question—'Is there anything in your to-do list you'd pay for right now?'—to pivot and find real demand. [00:29:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=19qrLchbQEc&t=1774)
- After finding pull, treat your startup as a factory for repeating successful case studies, focusing on the bottleneck in your growth process. [01:11:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=19qrLchbQEc&t=4314)
