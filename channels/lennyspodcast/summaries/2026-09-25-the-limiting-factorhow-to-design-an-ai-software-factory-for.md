# The limiting factor—how to design an AI software factory for speed 

## Executive Summary

The speaker argues that in the race for AI-driven speed, the primary bottleneck has shifted from coding to product definition, coordination, and verification. By building an internal 'AI software factory' with specialized agents for insights, design, coding, testing, and coordination, organizations can drastically reduce cycle times and eliminate manual bottlenecks. This approach allows product managers to evolve into technical architects and general managers who oversee autonomous systems rather than managing reactive tasks.

## Key Points

- The bottleneck in AI development has shifted from engineering to product definition; by using an 'Insights Agent' to aggregate customer data and a 'Glass Agent' for context-aware prototyping, teams can define problems with precision before coding begins [00:04:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZG8Mf3P9xzI&t=257).
- Automation of the creation and verification phases through agents like 'Inspect' (coding), 'Review Buddy' (code review), and 'Testo' (QA) allows non-engineers to contribute code and ensures 93% of PRs are automatically processed, freeing senior engineers for complex issues [00:08:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZG8Mf3P9xzI&t=504).
- Coordination becomes the next bottleneck, solved by a 'Gadget' agent that connects organizational data sources to answer queries, update roadmaps, and automate status updates, covering 85% of routine product manager questions [00:11:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZG8Mf3P9xzI&t=713).
- Product managers must evolve into three roles: technical architects who build the AI factory, tastemakers who define quality standards AI cannot replicate, and general managers responsible for broader business outcomes beyond just product features [00:16:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZG8Mf3P9xzI&t=1016).
