# OpenAI Codex lead on taste, curation, and building for AGI 

## Executive Summary

Andrew Ambercino, lead for OpenAI's Codex app, argues that as AI makes code implementation nearly free, the primary value in product development shifts to 'taste' and curation rather than technical execution. He describes a new product workflow where teams generate dozens of prototypes simultaneously, requiring leaders to act as 'taste makers' who steer direction and filter signal from noise. Ambercino also discusses the blurring of traditional roles like engineering, design, and product management, suggesting that future success depends on high agency and the ability to adapt to rapidly evolving model capabilities.

## Key Points

- Implementation is no longer the expensive part of software development; instead, 'taste' and the curation of 90+ simultaneous prototypes have become the critical bottleneck for product teams [00:04:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P3KDebPTUrw&t=288).
- Traditional design processes are collapsing because the assumption that implementation is costly no longer holds, forcing a shift toward rapid prototyping and flexible medium selection [00:18:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P3KDebPTUrw&t=1101).
- Roles are becoming fluid and defined by the average of current tasks rather than fixed job titles, creating a 'zone defense' where product leaders coordinate chaos and guide alignment [00:22:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P3KDebPTUrw&t=1374).
- AI models lag in design because good design requires nuanced human feedback loops and cultural novelty that are harder to grade and train for than functional code [00:13:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P3KDebPTUrw&t=825).
- The long-term vision for Codex is to serve as a universal home base that seamlessly orchestrates and interacts with other specialized tools like Excel and Premiere Pro rather than replacing them [00:58:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P3KDebPTUrw&t=3481).
