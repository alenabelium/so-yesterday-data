# The rise of junior AI talent 

## Executive Summary

The podcast episode features Sharif, head of AI and product development skills at Atlassian, discussing the evolving role of product managers in the AI era. He argues that AI enhances productivity but not direction, making PMs more valuable than ever for steering teams and filling context gaps. He introduces frameworks like the PM mastery triangle (artist, scientist, general manager) and emphasizes the importance of diverse teams, including junior talent who bring fresh AI-native methods. The conversation also covers counter-narratives like starting with solutions, bolting AI onto existing workflows, and the growing importance of taste and user experience.

## Key Points

- AI increases productivity but not direction, so product managers are needed more than ever to steer the ship and fill context gaps. [00:25:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hRtmV1mCBSE&t=1537)
- The PM mastery triangle categorizes product managers into artists, scientists, and general managers, each with distinct strengths; teams should be built to complement these archetypes. [00:11:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hRtmV1mCBSE&t=704)
- Junior talent is crucial because they bring new AI-native working methods and are more willing to experiment, while seniors excel at detecting AI garbage and ensuring quality. [00:33:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hRtmV1mCBSE&t=1991)
- Starting with a solution can be effective in certain contexts, such as when exploring new product ideas or leveraging existing assets and workflows. [00:46:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hRtmV1mCBSE&t=2811)
- Bolting AI onto existing workflows is a practical strategy for established products, as seen with Rovo agents in Jira, rather than rewriting from scratch. [00:52:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hRtmV1mCBSE&t=3166)
