# Why Meta’s Muse worked: The story behind the number one app this year 

## Executive Summary

Mona, VP of Product at Meta's Super Intelligence Labs, recounts the origin and development of Muse, the number one app of the year. Inspired by Nat Friedman's introduction to OpenClaw, the team built a personal AI agent that prioritizes trust, safety, and personalization over speed to market. The conversation highlights the importance of a small, autonomous team, the role of leadership in shaping the product's vision, and the unexpected success of features like the Jolly avatar and shopping use cases. Mona also discusses the changing role of product managers in the AI era, emphasizing taste, empathy, and the ability to prototype quickly.

## Key Points

- Muse's origin story began when Nat Friedman introduced the team to OpenClaw, sparking a weekend of experimentation that led to the vision for a personal superintelligence agent. [00:04:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WY0cdD4gNoQ&t=255)
- The team deliberately delayed launch to ensure reliability, safety, and trust, believing that launching too early with problems would destroy user trust in the agent. [00:13:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WY0cdD4gNoQ&t=787)
- The Jolly avatar, initially a temporary prototype, became iconic because it made the product feel personal and non-corporate, a decision that Mark Zuckerberg supported. [00:26:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WY0cdD4gNoQ&t=1578)
- Unexpectedly, shopping became a major use case for Muse, surprising the team and leading them to focus on it as a key feature. [00:32:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WY0cdD4gNoQ&t=1947)
- The role of product managers has shifted towards having high product taste and empathy, as AI enables faster prototyping and iteration, making the ability to feel user pain more critical than ever. [01:05:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WY0cdD4gNoQ&t=3913)
