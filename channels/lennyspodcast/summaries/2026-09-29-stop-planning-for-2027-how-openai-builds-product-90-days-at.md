# Stop planning for 2027: how OpenAI builds product 90 days at a time 

## Executive Summary

OpenAI product leaders Tara and Nan argue that traditional long-term planning is obsolete in the AI era, advocating instead for a 90-day development horizon to match the rapid pace of model capabilities. They emphasize shipping imperfect products quickly to gather empirical user feedback, rather than waiting for perfection, while balancing this speed with strong constraints on user value and internal adoption. The discussion highlights the necessity of direct user accessibility, robust onboarding, and designing agentic experiences that align with human cognitive limits.

## Key Points

- Product teams must abandon multi-year planning cycles in favor of a 2-3 month horizon to stay aligned with rapidly evolving model capabilities without becoming unusable or irrelevant. [00:19:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ciSTkEVy30&t=1185)
- Shipping imperfect solutions like the 'toggle' is essential for getting agentic functionality into users' hands quickly, allowing the product to evolve based on real-world usage rather than theoretical perfection. [00:00:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ciSTkEVy30&t=55)
- Designing effective AI agents requires mapping features to human nature by grouping tasks into manageable bundles rather than overwhelming users with dozens of individual micro-agents. [00:07:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ciSTkEVy30&t=467)
- Product leaders must become 'DMable' and maintain direct, high-frequency contact with users to understand subtle failures in agentic outputs that traditional feedback loops might miss. [00:25:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ciSTkEVy30&t=1512)
- The future of AI product interfaces will be dominated by voice and 'self-driving' agents that proactively assist users, reducing the friction of empty input boxes and complex manual configuration. [00:26:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ciSTkEVy30&t=1601)
