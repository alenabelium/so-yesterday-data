# 90 minutes of unfiltered product advice from Snap's first PM 

## Executive Summary

Peter Sellers, Snap's first product manager, argues that effective organizations function like 'terrorist groups' with clear ideological purpose and decisive leadership structures rather than relying on excessive collaboration. He contends that the average product manager is often mediocre because top talent naturally evolves into founders or executives, leaving a gap in the profession. Sellers also critiques Snapchat's monetization struggles due to its camera-centric design and highlights Discord's success by focusing intensely on the daily habits of its core gaming community.

## Key Points

- Sellers advocates for building teams with 'terrorist organization' characteristics: strong ideological alignment and clear decision-making authority, which allows for speed and autonomy without excessive cooperation [00:07:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=97LRJUUPy_w&t=472).
- He explains that product management suffers from a 'mediocre average' because exceptional PMs tend to leave to become founders or leaders, while those who stay are often less ambitious [00:16:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=97LRJUUPy_w&t=1013).
- Snapchat's advertising challenges stem from its young demographic and a product architecture centered on the camera, which lacks natural inventory for ads compared to consumption-based platforms [00:22:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=97LRJUUPy_w&t=1370).
- Discord achieved massive growth by focusing exclusively on improving the experience for gamers' daily routines, proving that deep focus on a core user habit is more effective than broad feature expansion [00:55:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=97LRJUUPy_w&t=3323).
- Sellers emphasizes the importance of 'taste' and restraint, noting that leaders must learn to say 'no' to good ideas to maintain product integrity and avoid diluting the core experience [00:35:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=97LRJUUPy_w&t=2144).
