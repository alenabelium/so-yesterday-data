# How to build products on a moving frontier 

## Executive Summary

Dan Shipper argues that product leaders must separate exploratory research from core execution by establishing dedicated 'labs' teams to navigate the rapid changes of the AI frontier. He proposes a structure where small, agile groups of 'pirates and architects' run parallel experiments, expecting to discard 90% of them while identifying viable innovations. Successful concepts are then validated through internal use and rigorous criteria before being merged into the main product roadmap, as demonstrated by OpenAI's integration of Codex.

## Key Points

- Separate concerns by creating a labs team focused on divergent exploration while the main product team handles convergent execution, allowing organizations to harness early adopters without distracting core staff [00:03:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DqF08Dz3nok&t=204).
- Run labs using tiny teams of 'pirates' who generate messy experiments and 'architects' who refine them, utilizing dogfooding and parallel testing to maintain tight feedback loops [00:07:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DqF08Dz3nok&t=439).
- Implement a research pipeline with clear decision criteria, such as internal adoption rates and 10x improvement metrics, to systematically move winners from experimentation to production [00:12:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DqF08Dz3nok&t=778).
- Use discarded experiments for external content or early adopter programs to maintain ROI, and merge successful innovations like OpenAI's Codex into the main product to drive self-disruption [00:10:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DqF08Dz3nok&t=608).
