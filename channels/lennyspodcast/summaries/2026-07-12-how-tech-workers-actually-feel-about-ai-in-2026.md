# How tech workers actually feel about AI in 2026 

## Executive Summary

A 2026 survey of 6,000 tech workers reveals a deeply bifurcated workforce, with half feeling energized by AI's potential and the other half experiencing destabilization and burnout. While AI productivity gains are widely acknowledged, they have led to unsustainable work expectations, cognitive atrophy, and a sharp decline in career optimism. The industry is characterized by 'smiling exhaustion,' where high enjoyment coexists with intense anxiety about job security and the erosion of professional identity.

## Key Points

- The tech workforce is split 50/50 between those feeling 'amplified' by AI and those feeling 'destabilized' or 'diminished,' with burnout rising to 54.7% and optimism falling to 48.7% in 2026 [00:15:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_cmpIveXnvE&t=929).
- Despite feeling more productive, workers report 'cognitive rot' and a decline in work quality, as the speed of AI output has been absorbed into higher performance expectations without corresponding pay increases [00:46:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_cmpIveXnvE&t=2817).
- Founders and employees at small companies report the highest levels of happiness and optimism, while designers and researchers are the most negative, with nearly zero promoters recommending their roles to newcomers [00:07:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_cmpIveXnvE&t=445).
- Manager effectiveness is the single largest predictor of employee well-being, yet only 25% of workers rate their managers as highly effective, highlighting a critical leadership gap in navigating AI-driven changes [01:13:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_cmpIveXnvE&t=4396).
