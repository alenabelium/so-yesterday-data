## Key arguments

### Underestimating Business Viability
- **Timestamp**: [05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fF3lkTCM5-c&t=300)
- **Summary**: Cagan admits he severely underestimated business viability, initially burying it under feasibility. He argues that PMs must ensure solutions are sellable, legal, and ethical, especially in AI, moving beyond his engineering-centric bias.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### Over-indexing on Problem Identification
- **Timestamp**: [08:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fF3lkTCM5-c&t=502)
- **Summary**: He regrets over-emphasizing problem definition, noting that teams often get stuck there. He argues innovation happens in finding solutions, and many perceived 'problems' vanish when better solutions appear.

### The Danger of Predictability Artifacts
- **Timestamp**: [12:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fF3lkTCM5-c&t=759)
- **Summary**: Cagan identifies the desire for predictability as a major blind spot, criticizing roadmaps and PRDs as artifacts that create arrogance. He warns they waste engineering capacity if used to dictate requirements rather than communicate learned evidence.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### Neglecting Product Leadership & Politics
- **Timestamp**: [16:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fF3lkTCM5-c&t=963)
- **Summary**: He regrets focusing only on team craft in his books, ignoring corporate governance and politics. He notes that empowered teams require strong leadership and strategy to survive internal motivations and competitive 'blood sports'.

### Process as a Substitute for Thinking
- **Timestamp**: [19:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fF3lkTCM5-c&t=1166)
- **Summary**: Cagan warns against using frameworks and AI as substitutes for thinking. He argues that good product work is fundamentally about mindset and creating to learn, not just following processes or delivering features.
