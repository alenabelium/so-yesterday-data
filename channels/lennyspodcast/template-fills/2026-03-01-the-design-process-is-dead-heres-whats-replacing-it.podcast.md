## Key arguments

### The Death of Traditional Design Process
- **Timestamp**: [05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=350)
- **Summary**: The traditional research-diverge-converge design process is dead because AI coding tools allow engineers to execute features rapidly. Design work has stratified into supporting fast execution alongside engineers and creating short-horizon (3-6 month) product visions rather than multi-year design decks.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0, 1]

### Stratification of Design Work
- **Timestamp**: [06:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=408)
- **Summary**: Design time has shifted from 60-70% mocking/prototyping to 30-40%, with the remainder split between pairing directly with engineers and writing code. Designers now focus on guiding direction and implementing polish rather than blocking engineers with static mocks.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [2]

### Human Value in Judgment and Taste
- **Timestamp**: [28:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=1710)
- **Summary**: While AI will improve at taste and judgment, humans remain accountable for deciding what gets built. The hardest parts of software are often resolving disagreements and making final calls on what matters, which requires human oversight and liability.

### Hiring Archetypes for the AI Era
- **Timestamp**: [46:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=2786)
- **Summary**: Jenny looks for three archetypes: strong generalists (block-shaped skills), deep specialists (deep T-shape), and 'crack new grads' who are eager learners unburdened by old processes. Resilience and adaptability are critical for surviving the rapid changes in the industry.

### Management and the Legibility Framework
- **Timestamp**: [55:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=3300)
- **Summary**: Managers should embrace 'low leverage' tasks like dogfooding to build product familiarity and team trust. Designers should use the Legibility Framework to spot 'illegible' ideas—novel concepts that are hard to articulate but have high energy—and help translate them into understandable products.
