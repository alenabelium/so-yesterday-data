## Key arguments

### The Death of Traditional Design Process
- **Timestamp**: [05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=350)
- **Summary**: The traditional research-diverge-converge design process is dead, forced by AI engineering speed. Design work stratifies into supporting fast execution alongside engineers using multiple agents, and creating short-horizon (3-6 month) product visions rather than multi-year design decks.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0, 4]

### Stratification of Design Work
- **Timestamp**: [06:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=408)
- **Summary**: Design work splits into two modes: supporting implementation with engineers who ship scrappy versions, and creating short-horizon visions. Time allocation shifts from 60-70% mocking/prototyping to 30-40%, with the rest split between pairing with engineers and writing code.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [2]

### Human Judgment and Accountability
- **Timestamp**: [28:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=1710)
- **Summary**: While AI improves at taste and judgment, humans remain accountable for deciding what gets built. AI can weigh in on disputes, but someone must still decide what matters and take responsibility for the final decision, similar to a radiologist signing off on an AI diagnosis.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### Hiring Archetypes for the AI Era
- **Timestamp**: [46:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=2786)
- **Summary**: Jenny looks for three archetypes: strong generalists (block-shaped skills), deep specialists (deep T-shape), and 'crack new grads' who are eager learners unburdened by old processes. Resilience and adaptability are critical for surviving the rapid shift in design roles.

### Management and the Legibility Framework
- **Timestamp**: [55:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=3300)
- **Summary**: Managers should embrace 'low leverage' tasks like dogfooding to build product familiarity and team trust. Designers should use the legibility framework to spot 'illegible' ideas—frontier concepts that are hard to articulate but have high energy—and help translate them into tangible products.
