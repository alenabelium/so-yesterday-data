## Key arguments

### The Death of the Traditional Design Process
- **Timestamp**: [05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=350)
- **Summary**: The traditional research-diverge-converge process is dead, forced by engineering speed. Design work is stratified into supporting fast execution alongside engineers running multiple AI agents and creating short-horizon (3-6 month) product visions rather than multi-year design decks.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0, 2]

### Stratification of Design Work: Execution vs. Vision
- **Timestamp**: [06:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=408)
- **Summary**: Designers now split time between supporting implementation (30-40%) and creating direction (30-40%). Time spent mocking/prototyping dropped from 60-70% to 30-40%, with the remainder split between pairing directly with engineers and writing code herself.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### Building Trust Through Speed and Early Release
- **Timestamp**: [26:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=1615)
- **Summary**: To maintain quality and trust while shipping rapidly, Anthropic releases early as 'research previews' with clear caveats. They commit to continuous iteration and visible feedback loops, ensuring users feel heard and that the product is improving, which builds trust faster than waiting for perfection.

### Future of Design Hiring: Generalists, Specialists, and New Grads
- **Timestamp**: [46:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=2786)
- **Summary**: Jenny looks for three archetypes: strong generalists (block-shaped skills), deep specialists (T-shaped with deep tips), and 'crack new grads' who are eager learners unburdened by old processes. She advises new designers to build actual things and share them to stand out.

### Management: Low-Leverage Tasks and Psychological Safety
- **Timestamp**: [56:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=3360)
- **Summary**: Managers should embrace 'low-leverage' tasks like dogfooding and nitpicking to build product familiarity and team trust. She also encourages 'roasting' among team members as a sign of psychological safety, balancing it with high standards and direct feedback.
