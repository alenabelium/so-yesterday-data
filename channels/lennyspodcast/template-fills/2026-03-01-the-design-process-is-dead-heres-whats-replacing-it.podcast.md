## Key arguments

### The death of the traditional design process
- **Timestamp**: [05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=350)
- **Summary**: Jenny argues the research-diverge-converge process is dead because AI coding tools allow engineers to execute features rapidly. Designers no longer have time for beautiful mocks; instead, they must support fast execution alongside engineers running multiple AI agents, shifting from gatekeeping to enabling speed.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0, 1, 4]

### Stratification of design work into execution and vision
- **Timestamp**: [06:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=408)
- **Summary**: Design work has split into two modes: supporting implementation (now 30-40% of time, down from 60-70%) and creating short-horizon (3-6 month) product visions. Long-term multi-year design decks are obsolete because the technology landscape changes too quickly to predict.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [2, 3]

### Building trust through speed and early release
- **Timestamp**: [26:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=1615)
- **Summary**: To maintain quality and trust while shipping rapidly, Anthropic launches products as 'research previews' with clear caveats. They build trust by responding immediately to feedback and iterating visibly, proving that speed and transparency can coexist without degrading brand reputation.

### The future of design hiring: Archetypes and resilience
- **Timestamp**: [46:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=2786)
- **Summary**: Jenny identifies three key archetypes for modern designers: strong generalists (block-shaped skills), deep specialists, and 'crack new grads' who are eager learners unburdened by old processes. She advises managers to return to IC work to understand the changing landscape.

### Management rituals: Low-leverage tasks and psychological safety
- **Timestamp**: [56:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=3360)
- **Summary**: Effective managers should embrace seemingly 'low-leverage' tasks like dogfooding or fixing bugs to build product familiarity and team trust. She also encourages 'roasting' within teams as a sign of psychological safety, balancing it with high standards and direct challenge.
