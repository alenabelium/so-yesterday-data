## Key arguments

### The Death of the Traditional Design Process
- **Timestamp**: [05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=350)
- **Summary**: Jenny argues the research-diverge-converge process is dead because AI coding tools allow engineers to ship features instantly. Designers can no longer afford to block execution with lengthy mocks; instead, they must support fast implementation and create short-horizon (3-6 month) visions rather than multi-year decks.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0]

### Stratification of Design Work into Two Modes
- **Timestamp**: [06:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=408)
- **Summary**: Design work has split into supporting rapid engineering execution and creating directional prototypes. Time allocation has shifted from 60-70% mocking to 30-40%, with the rest spent pairing with engineers and writing code to implement polish and prototypes directly.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [2]

### Building Trust Through Speed and Early Release
- **Timestamp**: [26:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=1615)
- **Summary**: To maintain quality and trust while shipping faster, designers should release early as 'research previews' with clear caveats. Trust is built by responding to feedback and iterating rapidly, rather than waiting for perfection, which degrades brand if nothing happens after launch.

### Future Archetypes for Hiring Designers
- **Timestamp**: [46:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=2786)
- **Summary**: Jenny identifies three key archetypes for the future: strong generalists (block-shaped skills), deep specialists (extreme T-shape), and 'crack new grads' who are eager learners without baked-in process biases. She advises new designers to build actual things and share them.

### Management: Low-Leverage Tasks and Psychological Safety
- **Timestamp**: [56:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=3360)
- **Summary**: Managers should embrace 'low-leverage' tasks like dogfooding or fixing bugs to build empathy and team culture. She also encourages 'roasting' as a sign of psychological safety, balancing it with high standards to create a 'tough parent' dynamic that fosters high performance.
