## Key arguments

### The Death of Traditional Design Process
- **Timestamp**: [05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=350)
- **Summary**: Jenny argues that the traditional research-diverge-converge design process is dead, forced by engineering speed enabled by AI tools like Claude Code. Design work has stratified into supporting fast execution alongside engineers and creating short-horizon (3-6 month) product visions rather than multi-year design decks. Time allocation has shifted from 60-70% mocking to 30-40%, with the rest spent pairing with engineers and coding.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0, 1]

### Strategic Shifts in Design Work
- **Timestamp**: [07:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=470)
- **Summary**: Designers must now focus on two modes: supporting implementation and creating direction. Because engineers can build scrappy versions instantly, designers can no longer block progress but must guide it. Visions have shortened to 3-6 months, often taking the form of prototypes rather than beautiful decks, to point teams in the right direction amidst rapid technological change.

### Hiring and Skill Archetypes
- **Timestamp**: [46:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=2786)
- **Summary**: Jenny identifies three valuable designer archetypes: strong generalists (block-shaped skills), deep specialists (deep T-shape), and 'crack new grads' who are eager learners without baked-in process biases. She advises new designers to build actual things and share them, emphasizing resilience and adaptability in a rapidly changing landscape.

### Management and Leadership Dynamics
- **Timestamp**: [56:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=3360)
- **Summary**: Jenny challenges the idea of 'low leverage' time for managers, arguing that leaders should engage in nitpicky tasks to build product familiarity and team trust. She advocates for psychological safety that allows for 'roasting' and humor, balanced with high standards, to create high-performing teams.

### The Legibility Framework for Innovation
- **Timestamp**: [01:02:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh8bcBIAAFo&t=3720)
- **Summary**: Using the Legibility Framework, Jenny explains that designers should spot 'illegible' ideas—those that are on the frontier and not yet understood—to transform them into clear products. This involves diving deep into energy around unclear prototypes to extract value, similar to how VC investors spot early opportunities.
