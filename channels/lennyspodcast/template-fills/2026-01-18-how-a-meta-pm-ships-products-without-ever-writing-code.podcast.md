## Key arguments

### Start with a GPT project as your CTO
- **Timestamp**: [09:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1em64iUFt3U&t=559)
- **Summary**: Zevy recommends non-technical beginners start with a ChatGPT project configured as a 'CTO' to compartmentalize context and avoid the people-pleasing pitfalls of default AI. This allows for conversational learning and gradual exposure to code, before moving to more advanced tools like Cursor.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0]

### Graduate from Bolt/Lovable to Cursor for control
- **Timestamp**: [14:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1em64iUFt3U&t=862)
- **Summary**: Zevy explains that while Bolt and Lovable are great for beginners, they are opinionated and limit control. Cursor with Claude Code offers full access to the codebase and models, enabling more serious product development. The key is to graduate gradually as your skills grow.

### Use slash commands to structure the entire build workflow
- **Timestamp**: [16:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1em64iUFt3U&t=991)
- **Summary**: Zevy's workflow is a series of slash commands: create issue (Linear), exploration phase, create plan, execute plan, review, peer review, and update docs. These commands automate the process, making it repeatable and efficient, and are available for listeners to download.

### Peer review with multiple AI models catches more bugs
- **Timestamp**: [39:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1em64iUFt3U&t=2341)
- **Summary**: Zevy uses Claude, Codex, and Gemini to review each other's code, leveraging their distinct strengths and weaknesses. He treats them as team members with different personalities, and the peer review command has them 'fight it out' to resolve issues, compensating for his lack of technical expertise.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [2]

### Update documentation and tooling to prevent repeated mistakes
- **Timestamp**: [45:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1em64iUFt3U&t=2739)
- **Summary**: Zevy emphasizes the importance of post-mortems: when AI makes a mistake, he asks it to introspect and then updates its prompts or documentation to prevent recurrence. This continuous improvement is a key productivity hack and differentiates serious builders from casual users.
