## Key arguments

### Every company needs a lab team
- **Timestamp**: [02:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=161)
- **Summary**: Josh argues that every company must adopt a lab-team mindset to stay competitive, because the technology landscape is changing so rapidly. He emphasizes the need for a dedicated group on the front lines, testing new models and figuring out what's possible before competitors or startups do.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0]

### Good ideas cannot be microwaved
- **Timestamp**: [06:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=374)
- **Summary**: Josh shares that the best ideas rarely come from design sprints or scheduled brainstorming. Instead, they emerge from people who are constantly creating and testing, often in their spare time. He stresses the importance of creating a space where strange things can develop and being attentive to what catches your attention.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### Unlearning speed is the new learning speed
- **Timestamp**: [31:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=1888)
- **Summary**: Josh highlights that the most valuable skill now is 'unlearning speed'—how quickly someone can learn something and then abandon it based on new information. He also values 'explosive endurance,' the ability to work intensely while sustaining the pace over the long term, and the capacity to build trust and collaborate effectively.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [2]

### Product-market fit is in the eyes
- **Timestamp**: [15:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=916)
- **Summary**: Josh explains that early product-market fit is more art than science. He looks for the light in people's eyes when showing prototypes, rather than relying on metrics like DAU or retention. He also notes that teams often know when to kill an idea before the leader does, and it's crucial to create a culture where they can speak up.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [3]

### Labs need independence to thrive
- **Timestamp**: [43:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=2617)
- **Summary**: Josh discusses the history of corporate labs failing because they lack independence. He advises that labs must have a certain level of autonomy, report directly to the CEO, and be allowed to create 'useful nuisances' that challenge the company's status quo. He also warns against hiring too many people too quickly without product-market fit.
