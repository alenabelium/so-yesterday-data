## Key arguments

### Every company needs a lab team
- **Timestamp**: [02:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=161)
- **Summary**: Josh argues that every company must adopt a lab-team mindset to stay competitive, because the technology landscape is changing so rapidly. This means having a dedicated group to test new models and find opportunities before competitors or startups do.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0]

### Good ideas can't be microwaved
- **Timestamp**: [06:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=374)
- **Summary**: Josh explains that the best ideas rarely come from design sprints or scheduled brainstorming. Instead, they emerge from people who are constantly building and exploring, often as side projects. The key is to create an environment where these ideas can develop and then be ready to catch them.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]

### Product-market fit is in the eyes
- **Timestamp**: [15:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=916)
- **Summary**: Josh describes product-market fit as more art than science. Early on, the key metric is not DAU or retention, but the look in someone's eyes when you show them a prototype. If their eyes light up and they lean forward, that's the signal to double down.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [2]

### Unlearning speed and explosive endurance
- **Timestamp**: [31:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=1888)
- **Summary**: Josh highlights the skills that are becoming more valuable: unlearning speed (how quickly you can abandon a learned approach) and explosive endurance (the ability to work intensely for bursts while sustaining the pace long-term). He also emphasizes the growing importance of collaboration and building trust.

### Labs need independence and a clear mission
- **Timestamp**: [48:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3I6QYkccd3o&t=2881)
- **Summary**: Josh advises that labs must have a degree of independence from existing business units to be effective. They also need a clear understanding of their goal—whether to translate developments into existing products or invent new categories—and should be used to challenge how the company works, not just to create products.
