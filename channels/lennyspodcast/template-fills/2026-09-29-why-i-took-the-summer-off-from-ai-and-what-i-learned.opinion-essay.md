## Thesis

Context becomes the product: organizations must use AI to automate routine execution so teams can dedicate recovered time to deep customer engagement and collective learning rather than chasing tool efficiency.

## Argument

[00:01:25] The industry is obsessed with scaling as a "software factory," optimizing for volume over value. But customers don't buy lines of code; they buy experiences born from understanding.

[00:04:09] Automation creates a dangerous gap between execution and learning. If you outsource the struggle, you lose the intuition that comes from solving problems directly. The real asset is not the tool, but the team's accumulated judgment and taste.

[00:06:22] AI should handle repetitive drudgery—like bug investigation via Linear Loop and DataDog—to free up engineer time. This recovered bandwidth must be redirected to deeper customer engagement: joining Slack channels, answering support tickets, and building intuition.

[00:10:26] To combat the isolation of individual AI agents, we implemented a "quality environment." Weekly defect fixes and optional "function analysis" meetings force shared critique. This trains the team's eye for detail and ensures collective taste evolves together.

[00:14:07] We store this context in accessible spaces for both humans and agents. Intuition isn't magic; it's trained pattern recognition from sustained exposure to customer signals. The future of product work is managing this context, not just executing code.

## Counterpoints

- Automation creates a gap between execution and learning, risking the loss of team intuition if teams don't struggle with problems directly.
- Working in isolation with AI agents reduces opportunities for colleagues to learn from each other's methods and critiques.
- There are no clear best practices yet for using AI workflows; every company is experimenting differently without established standards.
