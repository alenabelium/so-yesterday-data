## Thesis

Context becomes the product: organizations must use AI to automate routine execution so teams can dedicate their recovered time to deep customer engagement and collective learning.

## Argument

The industry is obsessed with scaling 'software factories'—optimizing for speed and volume through specialized roles and rigid processes. This obsession predates AI but has accelerated under its influence, creating a dangerous gap between execution and learning. When teams rely solely on AI to generate code or features, they outsource the struggle that builds intuition.

The core mechanism is economic and cultural: AI saves time, but if that time is just filled with more execution, the team loses its competitive edge. Great products are not built by lines of code; they are built by teams with deep context, taste, and judgment. This context is cumulative, like a Formula 1 steering wheel refined over decades of precise use.

To counter this, Linear uses AI to automate bug fixes (via Linear Loop) and collect customer signals (via an agent watcher), but explicitly redirects the saved time toward 'quality environments' and 'function analysis.' These practices force engineers to engage with small defects and critique features together, training their eyes and judgment. The goal is not just efficiency, but sustaining a learning cycle where context is stored, shared, and used by both humans and agents. Intuition is not magic; it is trained pattern recognition derived from this deep, shared understanding of the customer and the product.

## Counterpoints

- AI tools are objectively getting better, faster, and more functional every day.
- Automation saves engineers time on repetitive tasks like bug fixing, which they can then redirect.
- Working in isolation with agents is effective for individual output but harms collective learning.
