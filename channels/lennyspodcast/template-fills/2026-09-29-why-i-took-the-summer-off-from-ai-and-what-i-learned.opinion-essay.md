## Thesis

Context becomes the product: organizations must use AI to automate routine execution so teams can dedicate time to deep customer engagement and collective learning.

## Argument

**[00:01:25]** The industry is obsessed with "software factories," scaling through specialized roles and rigid processes. But more results do not equal better products; customers buy value, not lines of code.

**[00:04:09]** Great products stem from team context—taste, judgment, and cumulative learning. Automating everything creates a gap between execution and learning, causing teams to lose their edge. The danger is outsourcing intuition.

**[00:06:22]** AI should automate repetitive tasks (like bug fixes) to free time for deeper customer engagement. At Linear, we use agents to monitor customer feedback and workflows, ensuring engineers stay connected to user problems rather than working in isolation.

**[00:10:26]** To combat the isolation of AI work, we implement a "quality environment." Weekly defect fixes and "function analysis" meetings force shared critique. This trains the team's eye for detail and disseminates judgment across the organization.

**[00:14:07]** We store this context in accessible spaces for both humans and agents. Intuition is not magic; it is trained brainpower derived from listening to customers and understanding product history. Hiring is about trajectory and taste, not just productivity.

## Counterpoints

- The industry obsession with 'software factories' and scaling through specialized roles and rigid processes prioritizes efficiency over product quality.
- Automating everything creates a dangerous gap between execution and learning, causing teams to lose their competitive edge and intuition.
- AI agents can cause engineers to work in isolation, reducing the shared learning and collective judgment that defines great product teams.
