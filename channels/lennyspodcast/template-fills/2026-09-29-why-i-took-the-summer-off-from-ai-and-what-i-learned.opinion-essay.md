## Thesis

Context becomes the product: organizations must use AI to automate routine execution so teams can dedicate more time to deep customer engagement and collective learning.

## Argument

[00:04:09] The industry is obsessed with scaling through 'software factories,' prioritizing speed and process over value. This creates a gap between execution and learning. If you automate everything, you lose the intuition gained from struggling with problems.

[00:06:22] AI should save time on repetitive tasks (like bug fixes) to free up engineers for deeper customer engagement. At Linear, we encourage engineers to join Slack channels and answer questions to build intuition.

[00:07:57] The goal is sustaining the learning cycle. We use AI agents to collect feedback from sales calls and support emails, creating a 'watcher' that notifies us of interesting patterns. This builds a shared context base.

[00:10:26] To counter isolation, we implemented a 'quality environment.' Everyone fixes one small defect weekly and shares findings in meetings. We also hold 'function analysis' meetings where anyone can critique new features. This trains the team's eye for quality and ensures everyone learns from each other.

[00:14:07] Context must be stored in an accessible place for both humans and agents. Intuition is not magic; it is training your brain through accumulated context. Hiring is about bringing in people with good judgment who can expand that context. The product organization's job is managing this context, not just executing code.

## Counterpoints

- AI tools are getting better and faster, so we must keep up to avoid falling behind.
- Software factories and automation are necessary for scaling organizations efficiently.
- People may start working in isolation with their own agents, reducing collective learning.
