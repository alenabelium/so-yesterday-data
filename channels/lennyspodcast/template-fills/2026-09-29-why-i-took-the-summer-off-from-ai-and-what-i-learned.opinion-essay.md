## Thesis

Context becomes the product: organizations must use AI to automate routine execution so teams can deepen customer engagement and collective learning, rather than chasing efficiency.

## Argument

[00:01:25] The industry is obsessed with scaling through 'software factories'—hiring specialists, adding processes, and running experiments to optimize output. But more results do not mean better products; customers buy value, not lines of code.

[00:04:09] Great products emerge from teams with deep context: taste, judgment, and cumulative learning. Automating everything creates a gap between execution and learning, causing teams to lose their edge. The danger is outsourcing the thinking that builds intuition.

[00:06:22] Instead of full automation, use AI for repetitive tasks like bug fixes (e.g., Linear Loop connecting to Sentry). This frees engineers to engage directly with customers via Slack or support channels, developing intuition through direct problem-solving.

[00:07:57] The goal is sustaining the learning cycle. At Linear, we collect customer feedback from sales and support into a shared context base. I use an agent to monitor requests and provide a daily digest of AI workflow discussions, keeping me close to customer signals without manual effort.

[00:10:26] To combat isolation in AI-driven work, we implement 'quality environments.' Weekly 'quality defect' fixes train the team's eye for detail. 'Function analysis' meetings allow broad critique of new features, teaching developers what users find confusing and aligning judgment across the org.

## Counterpoints

- Automation saves time but risks losing the 'direct connection to learning' if teams stop struggling with problems.
- Working in isolation with agents reduces peer-to-peer learning; shared critique practices are needed to maintain quality standards.
- Efficiency gains from AI should be reinvested into abstract thinking and customer research, not just faster execution.
