## Thesis

Context becomes the product: companies must use AI to automate routine tasks so teams can dedicate more time to deep customer engagement and collective learning.

## Argument

[00:01:25] The industry is obsessed with scaling as a 'software factory,' optimizing for speed and volume. But results don't equal quality. Customers buy value, not lines of code.

[00:04:09] Great products come from teams with deep context, taste, and judgment. Automating execution creates a gap between doing and learning. If you don't learn on the job, you lose your edge.

[00:06:22] Use AI to save time on repetitive bugs (e.g., Linear Loop), then redirect that time to customer engagement. Build intuition by talking to users.

[00:10:26] Combat isolation with 'quality environments.' Weekly bug fixes and 'function analysis' meetings train the team's eye for detail and share judgment. This builds collective taste.

[00:14:07] Store context in a shared space for humans and agents. Intuition is trained brainpower, not magic. Hire for trajectory and judgment, not just productivity.

## Counterpoints

- Automation creates a gap between execution and learning; if teams don't struggle with problems, they lose the intuition needed to build great products.
- Working in isolation with agents prevents teams from learning from each other, eroding collective taste and shared standards.
