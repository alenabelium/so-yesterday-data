## Key arguments

### Non-determinism and agency-control trade-off redefine AI product building
- **Timestamp**: [07:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z7T1pCxgvlA&t=457)
- **Summary**: AI products differ fundamentally: non-deterministic inputs/outputs and the agency-control trade-off. Building must start with high control, low agency, gradually increasing autonomy as trust is earned.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0, 1]

### Start small, scale autonomy: the CCCD framework
- **Timestamp**: [13:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z7T1pCxgvlA&t=800)
- **Summary**: Adopt Continuous Calibration, Continuous Development: begin with low-agency versions (e.g., routing, suggestions), log human behavior, and incrementally increase autonomy only when surprises are minimal.

### Evals and production monitoring are complementary, not either/or
- **Timestamp**: [33:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z7T1pCxgvlA&t=2001)
- **Summary**: Evals catch known errors; production monitoring catches emerging patterns. Both are essential; relying on one is a false dichotomy. Use implicit signals like regenerations to detect dissatisfaction.

### Success triangle: leaders, culture, and technical obsession
- **Timestamp**: [25:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z7T1pCxgvlA&t=1514)
- **Summary**: Successful AI companies have hands-on leaders who rebuild intuitions, an empowering culture that avoids FOMO, and teams obsessed with workflows and data rather than technology hype.

### One-click agents are pure marketing; flywheels take time
- **Timestamp**: [28:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z7T1pCxgvlA&t=1718)
- **Summary**: Skeptical of one-click agents: enterprise data is messy, and real ROI takes 4-6 months. Focus on building pipelines that learn and improve over time.
