## Key arguments

### Agency Over Titles
- **Timestamp**: [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mCO-D3pkviM&t=0)
- **Summary**: Max Shing argues that in the AI era, 'agency'—the belief that you can change things—is more valuable than traditional job titles or technical skills. He cites the example of Notion employees who drive change by building prototypes themselves rather than waiting for engineering, emphasizing that the world is made up of people no smarter than you.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [0]

### The Value of Taste and Iteration
- **Timestamp**: [00:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mCO-D3pkviM&t=39)
- **Summary**: Shing defines 'taste' as the ability to predict user reaction through a mental simulation, built by doing reps and getting feedback. He warns against the pitfall of adding endless features, stating that great products have one tiny core that is exceptionally good, and that taste is developed through iteration, not speedrunning.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [2]

### Malleable Software vs. SaaS Apocalypse
- **Timestamp**: [17:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mCO-D3pkviM&t=1078)
- **Summary**: Shing advocates for 'malleable software' where users have ownership over their tools, contrasting it with the 'SaaS apocalypse' fear. He argues that while AI lowers the barrier to entry, most people still prefer the maintenance and specialization of SaaS (like Slack or Notion) over building and maintaining their own full-stack solutions.

### Designers Should Code for Understanding
- **Timestamp**: [06:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mCO-D3pkviM&t=391)
- **Summary**: Shing believes designers and PMs should code not to ship to production, but to understand the medium they are designing for. He argues that understanding agent loops and the substrate of code is crucial for future product leaders, as it forces them to interrogate the material of their designs rather than just tweaking styles.

### The First 10% is Free
- **Timestamp**: [28:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mCO-D3pkviM&t=1730)
- **Summary**: Shing notes that AI has made the first 10% of any project free, allowing for rapid exploration and 'shots on goal.' He suggests that while the last 10% of engineering remains hard, the ability to quickly demo and iterate on ideas changes product development from waterfall to a more experimental, demo-driven process.
- **Anchor Quotes**: [1]
