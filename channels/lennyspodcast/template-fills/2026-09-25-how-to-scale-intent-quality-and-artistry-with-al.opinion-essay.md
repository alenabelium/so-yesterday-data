## Thesis

AI accelerates production but risks creating generic 'zombie UI'; builders must encode a strong brand point of view and rigorous human editing to scale intent and artistry rather than just speed.

## Argument

The current AI building boom echoes the post-WWII architectural rush, where urgency replaced intentionality, resulting in "zombie buildings" that signal no care for context or user. LLMs optimize for probability and popularity, not originality or specific brand soul. This creates three watch points: generic outputs, premature satisfaction with "done," and disposable decision-making.

To counter this, builders must first establish a distinct point of view—defining who they are and what they care about—to prevent AI from defaulting to the past. At Stripe, this means embedding optimism into every detail. Second, standards must be encoded into the machine via opinionated systems (like CLI-based design harnesses) rather than vague prompts, ensuring distributed builders produce coherent results. Third, we must refuse to confuse "done" with "good." The traditional quality filter is gone; now, human editors must scrutinize outputs pixel-by-pixel, pushing past the initial AI generation to find unexpected details and deeper meaning. Finally, unleash creativity. AI should be a catalyst for new interfaces and aesthetics, not just a faster way to make existing things. By improving inputs with specific brand context and stress-testing outputs, we can raise the ceiling of design, creating products that feel uniquely crafted and cared for.

## Counterpoints

- AI makes things feel finished too quickly, leading to premature satisfaction with mediocre output.
- Distributed building leads to diffuse ownership, making it easy to abdicate quality standards to the machine.
- The traditional filtering process in product development is gone, requiring new post-build editorial rigor.
