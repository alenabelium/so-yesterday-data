## Thesis

AI accelerates production but risks creating generic 'zombie UI'; builders must encode a strong brand point of view and rigorous human editing to scale intent, quality, and artistry rather than just speed.

## Argument

**[00:01:20]** We are in an AI building boom echoing the post-WWII architectural surge. Just as modernism's intentionality was lost to generic patterns when urgency replaced care, AI’s ability to generate the "most probable answer" creates monotony. The temptation of done is real; AI makes interfaces feel finished prematurely, masking whether they actually solve problems or differentiate.

**[00:05:24]** To avoid this, builders must first establish a strong point of view. Without it, AI defaults to generic past trends. At Stripe, we encode optimism into every detail, from color to copy. This requires defining standards that align distributed teams. We evolved from vague prompts to a CLI harness that consumes design documentation at the right time, making the system opinionated enough to drive coherent outcomes.

**[00:12:25]** Second, refuse to confuse done with good. The traditional quality filter is gone; we now build 20 ideas in a week. This demands a new role: the editor. Editors must scrutinize outputs like users would, ensuring coherence across the journey. As Nabil Koreshi notes, art lies in unexpected details and deeper meaning—gaps where AI fails. We must push one level deeper than what customers see, embracing meticulous craft over speed.

**[00:16:18]** Finally, unleash creativity. AI is a catalyst for new interfaces and aesthetics, not just faster production. ## Counterpoints

- AI makes things feel finished too quickly, leading builders to overlook whether the output actually solves the user's problem or differentiates from competitors.
- The traditional quality filter built into early-stage product staffing is gone; now filtering must happen post-build when it is harder to say no to shipped work.
- Distributed building and agentic construction mean designers cannot be in every room, so standards must be encoded into the machine rather than relying on human oversight alone.
