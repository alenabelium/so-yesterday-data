## Thesis

AI accelerates production but risks creating generic 'zombie UI'; builders must encode specific brand intent and rigorous human editing to scale soul, not just speed.

## Argument

[00:01:20] The core danger is the post-WWII architectural pitfall: urgency replaced intentionality. LLMs optimize for probability—what is popular or past—yielding generic patterns ill-suited to specific contexts. This creates 'zombie UI,' where speed masks a lack of care for the user.

[00:06:41] To counter this, builders must first define a distinct point of view. Without it, AI defaults to mediocrity. At Stripe, this means encoding optimism and specific brand standards directly into the machine via opinionated design systems, not just vague prompts. The system must be extensible yet opinionated enough to feel handmade.

[00:12:25] Second, refuse to confuse 'done' with 'good.' AI removes the traditional filtering stages of product development, making post-build editing critical. Editors must scrutinize outputs pixel-by-pixel, ensuring coherence and solving actual user problems rather than accepting superficial polish. As seen in complex animation workflows, AI opens the possibility space, but human iteration (e.g., 56 iterations) provides the soul.

[00:18:29] Finally, unleash creativity. AI is a catalyst for new interfaces and aesthetics, not just faster execution of old ones. Builders must improve inputs with specific brand context and stress outputs using adversarial critique. The goal is to raise the ceiling of artistry, ensuring every detail reflects unique human judgment and care.

## Counterpoints

- AI makes things feel finished too quickly, leading to premature acceptance of mediocre output.
- Distributed building dilutes ownership, making it easy to abdicate quality standards to the machine.
- Traditional filtering stages are gone, so editors must now work harder post-build to ensure coherence.
