## Thesis

To avoid the 'zombie UI' plague of generic, soulless software in the AI building boom, builders must encode a strong brand point of view and rigorous human editorial standards directly into their systems rather than relying on vague.

## Argument

**[00:01:20]** The post-WWII architectural boom offers a cautionary tale: urgency and new methods led to 'zombie buildings'—generic patterns copied without intentionality, showing no care for context or user. We are in an AI building boom where teams of three can do the work of thirty, but LLMs only predict what is popular, not what is original or specific to your brand.

**[00:02:47]** The first trap is the 'temptation of done.' AI makes interfaces feel finished in seconds, like a microwave burrito—edible and fast, but lacking soul. We must refuse to confuse speed with quality. The second watch point is disposability; because building is easy, we abdicate responsibility for long-term maintenance and context.

**[00:05:24]** To navigate this, builders need four recommendations. First, define a clear brand point of view. At Stripe, 'optimism' drives every detail, from color to copy. Without this, AI defaults to generic past patterns. Second, encode these standards into the machine. Design systems must scale intent, not just consistency. We moved from simple MCPs to a CLI that consumes full templates and flows, making the system opinionated enough to feel handmade despite being machine-made.

**[00:12:25]** Third, appoint editors who experience the product as users would. The old quality filter (staffing constraints) is gone; now filtering happens post-build. Editors must ensure coherence and push for 'one level deeper' than what customers see, filling the gaps AI leaves in unexpected details.

**[00:16:18]** Fourth, unleash creativity. AI is a catalyst for new interfaces

## Counterpoints

- AI makes things feel finished too fast, leading to premature acceptance of mediocre outputs like microwave burritos.
- Distributed building and agentic construction diffuse ownership, making it easy to abdicate responsibility for quality.
- Old quality filters (like staffing limits) are gone, so filtering must now happen post-build by human editors.
