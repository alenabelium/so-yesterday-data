## Thesis

To avoid creating generic 'zombie UI' in the AI building boom, builders must encode a strong brand point of view and rigorous human editorial standards into their systems rather than relying on vague prompts.

## Argument

The current AI era mirrors the post-WWII architectural boom: speed sacrifices intentionality, leading to monotonous, context-ill-suited outputs. LLMs optimize for probability and popularity, not originality or brand soul. The temptation of 'done' is dangerous; AI makes interfaces feel finished prematurely, masking a lack of genuine user care.

To counter this, builders must first define a clear point of view—what the brand stands for and who it serves—and encode these standards directly into the machine via opinionated design systems, not just loose prompts. At Stripe, this means using CLI tools that enforce full flows and templates, ensuring consistency without designer intervention.

Second, we must refuse to confuse 'done' with 'good.' The traditional quality filter is gone, so human editors must step in post-build to scrutinize details, ensuring the product feels coherent and cared for. Third, unleash creativity: use AI as a catalyst for new interfaces and aesthetics, not just faster production of old patterns. Finally, improve inputs with specific brand context and stress outputs through adversarial critique. The goal is Gothic-style uniqueness—where every detail shows the maker's hand—not the generic uniformity of zombie buildings.

## Counterpoints

- AI makes things feel finished too quickly, leading builders to overlook flaws in favor of speed (the 'microwave burrito' effect).
- Distributed building and agentic construction make it hard to maintain a unified brand point of view without explicit encoding.
- The traditional quality filter built into early-stage staffing is gone, making post-build editorial scrutiny essential but difficult.
