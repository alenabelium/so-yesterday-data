## Thesis

To avoid creating generic 'zombie UI' in the AI building boom, builders must encode a strong brand point of view and rigorous human editorial standards into their systems rather than relying on vague prompts.

## Argument

The post-WWII architectural boom sacrificed intentionality for speed, resulting in "zombie buildings" that signal no care for context or users. We are repeating this error in the AI era, where LLMs optimize for probability and popularity, not originality or brand-specific nuance. The temptation of "done" is powerful; AI makes interfaces feel finished prematurely, masking flaws with superficial polish. This work feels disposable because it is too easy to produce.

To navigate this, builders must first establish a distinct point of view. Without it, AI defaults to generic patterns. At Stripe, this means encoding optimism and entrepreneurial support into every detail, from color to copy. Second, encode these standards into the machine. Design systems must evolve from static components to opinionated templates and flows that guide agentic construction, ensuring consistency at scale. Third, refuse to confuse "done" with "good." The traditional quality filter is gone; humans must now act as editors, scrutinizing outputs for coherence, problem-solving, and unexpected details that AI misses. Finally, unleash creativity. Use AI not just to replicate existing patterns but to amplify human artistry, creating new interfaces and aesthetics. As John Ruskin noted about Gothic architecture, true care lies in unique, hand-crafted details that show the maker's mind. We must choose a creative renaissance over digital monotony.

## Counterpoints

- AI makes things feel finished quickly, leading to premature acceptance of mediocre outputs.
- Distributed building and agentic construction diffuse ownership, making it easy to abdicate responsibility for quality.
- Users care about the final output's quality, not how it was made, so standards must be based on results, not process.
