## Thesis

AI accelerates production but risks creating generic 'zombie UI'; builders must encode specific brand intent and rigorous human editing into their systems to preserve artistry and care.

## Argument

The current AI building boom mirrors the post-WWII architectural surge, where speed sacrificed intentionality for generic patterns. LLMs predict popularity, not originality, leading to 'zombie UI' that lacks soul or context. To counter this, builders must first establish a strong point of view and encode those standards directly into the machine via opinionated systems, not just vague prompts. Second, we must refuse to confuse 'done' with 'good'; AI makes things feel finished prematurely, so human editors are essential to scrutinize outputs for coherence and user care. Third, unleash creativity by using AI as a catalyst for new interfaces and aesthetics, not just faster replication of old ones. Finally, improve inputs with specific brand context and stress outputs through adversarial critique. The goal is not to lower the floor but to raise the ceiling, ensuring every pixel reflects the maker's hand and care.

## Counterpoints

- AI makes things feel finished too quickly, misleading builders into thinking mediocre output is sufficient.
- The cost of creation has dropped so low that quality filters are disappearing from the development process.
- Distributed building makes it easy to abdicate responsibility for design standards to the AI.
