## Thesis

To avoid creating generic 'zombie UI' in the AI building boom, builders must encode a strong brand point of view and rigorous human editorial standards directly into their systems rather than relying on vague prompts.

## Argument

The current AI development era mirrors the post-WWII architectural boom: speed has replaced intentionality, leading to "zombie buildings" that are generic and uncared for. LLMs optimize for probability, not originality or brand context. To scale intent, quality, and artistry, builders must first define a clear point of view—what Stripe calls "optimism"—and encode these standards into the machine via opinionated systems (like CLI harnesses) rather than diffuse prompts. Second, we must refuse to confuse "done" with "good." The traditional quality filter is gone; now, human editors must scrutinize outputs pixel-by-pixel to ensure they solve real problems and feel coherent. Third, unleash creativity. AI should be a catalyst for new interfaces and aesthetics, not just a faster way to make existing things. By improving inputs with specific brand context and stress-testing outputs, we can raise the ceiling of design, ensuring every detail shows the hand and care of the maker.

## Counterpoints

- AI makes things feel finished prematurely, masking flaws behind speed (the 'microwave burrito' effect).
- Distributed building leads to diffuse ownership, making it easy to abdicate quality standards to AI.
- The traditional filtering mechanism of product development is gone, requiring new post-build editorial rigor.
