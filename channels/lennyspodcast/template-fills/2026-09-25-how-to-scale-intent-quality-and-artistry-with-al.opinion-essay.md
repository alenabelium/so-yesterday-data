## Thesis

AI builders must encode a distinct brand point of view into their systems to avoid creating generic 'zombie UI' and instead produce software that feels cared for, intentional, and soulful.

## Argument

**[00:01:20]** The danger is not just speed, but the loss of intentionality. Just as post-WWII construction led to "zombie buildings"—generic patterns ill-suited to context—AI enables teams to build interfaces that feel finished prematurely but lack soul. LLMs optimize for the probable, which means they replicate what has worked before, erasing brand character.

**[00:05:24]** To counter this, builders must first define a strong point of view. Without it, AI defaults to generic past patterns. At Stripe, this means encoding standards like "optimism" into every detail, from color to copy. This requires meticulous craft—what they call "Pepsi bubbling"—going one level deeper than the customer sees.

**[00:10:38]** Second, encode these standards into the machine. The object of design is no longer just the screen but the system itself. Old design systems scaled consistency; new ones must scale intent. This means moving beyond atomic components to full templates and flows that constrain AI behavior, ensuring coherence across distributed teams.

**[00:12:25]** Third, refuse to confuse done with good. The natural filtering of resource constraints is gone. We need editors who scrutinize outputs like users, pushing for unexpected details and deeper meaning that AI misses. Quality must be enforced post-build through rigorous human judgment.

**[00:16:18]** Finally, unleash creativity. AI should amplify artistry, not replace it. By improving inputs with specific brand context and stress-testing outputs, we can invent new interfaces and aesthetics. The goal is a creative renaissance,

## Counterpoints

- AI makes things feel finished prematurely, leading to 'zombie UI' that lacks character or care for the user context.
- The temptation to accept mediocre output because it was generated quickly, similar to eating a microwave burrito.
- Distributed building and agentic construction make it hard to maintain quality without explicit human oversight.
- Fear that encoding standards into systems might stifle creativity rather than enable it.
