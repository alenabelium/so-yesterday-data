## Thesis

Timothy Gowers, a Fields medalist, argues that while he cannot assign a specific probability to AI existential risk, the risk is serious enough to warrant caution and further theoretical work, especially from mathematicians.

## Argument

Gowers' argument unfolds through a structured exploration of first-level arguments and their rebuttals, ultimately concluding that the risk is non-negligible. He begins by acknowledging his initial skepticism, rooted in the difficulty of defining AI 'desires' and the absurdity of paperclip-maximizer scenarios. However, he systematically dismantles these objections: the standard reply to the desire problem is that AI can act 'as if' it has desires, which is sufficient for danger, and the paperclip scenario, while absurd, highlights the risk of misaligned sub-goals that arise from training models rather than designing them. He then confronts the question of why AI would want to harm us, moving beyond science-fiction scenarios to more plausible ones like resource competition, drawing an analogy to how humans treat orangutans. Gowers also addresses the probabilistic argument that many unlikely scenarios could sum to a high overall risk, but he notes the crucial caveat of non-independence, using the Magnus Carlsen analogy to illustrate that while specific predictions are unreliable, the outcome is not. He ultimately concludes that he cannot provide a specific probability, but his second-order probability is 'medium' or 'high', and he emphasizes the importance of mathematicians contributing to AI safety through formal definitions and theoretical frameworks, even if the path is not yet clear.

## Counterpoints

- AI cannot have desires, so it cannot want to destroy us.
- The paperclip-maximizer scenario is absurd and would not happen in practice.
- AI is locked in computers and can be turned off if it becomes dangerous.
- The probability of any single extinction scenario is low, so the overall risk is low.
- Pausing AI development could lead to regulatory capture by big AI companies.
