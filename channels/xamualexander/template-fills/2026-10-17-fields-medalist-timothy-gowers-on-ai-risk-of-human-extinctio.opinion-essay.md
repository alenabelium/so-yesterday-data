## Thesis

Timothy Gowers, a Fields medalist, argues that while he cannot assign a specific probability to AI extinction risk, the risk is serious enough to warrant caution and further theoretical work, especially by mathematicians.

## Argument

Gowers' journey begins with a personal admission: he initially resisted thinking about existential risk because it would force him to confront moral obligations. He compares this to avoiding Peter Singer's book on animal liberation. This emotional resistance, he notes, is a common first-level barrier to serious engagement. The core of his argument is that first-level objections to AI risk—such as 'AI doesn't have desires' or 'we can just turn it off'—are insufficient because they ignore well-established counterarguments. For instance, the 'desire' objection is addressed by the concept of acting *as if* it has desires, which is sufficient for danger. The 'turn it off' objection is countered by scenarios involving robots, drones, and AI's ability to manipulate humans through economic incentives. Gowers then introduces a mathematical framework to evaluate risk, acknowledging that while individual extinction scenarios are unlikely, the aggregate probability could be high if they are independent. However, he notes the crucial caveat of interdependence, using the analogy of predicting a Magnus Carlsen chess game: you can't predict the moves, but you can predict the outcome. He concludes that the probability of extinction is not negligible, but he cannot specify it, leading him to a 'meta-probability' of medium to high. This uncertainty, combined with the high stakes, justifies the need for more theoretical work, particularly from mathematicians who can formalize concepts like 'desire' and 'alignment'.

## Counterpoints

- AI doesn't have desires, so it won't want to destroy us.
- AI is locked in computers and can be turned off if it becomes dangerous.
- Specific extinction scenarios are individually unlikely, so the overall risk is low.
- The benefits of AI (e.g., curing cancer) outweigh the risks.
- The risk of extinction is zero because we survived the Cold War.
