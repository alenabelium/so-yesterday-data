## Thesis

Timothy Gowers, a Fields medalist, argues that while he cannot assign a specific probability to AI existential risk, the risk is serious enough to warrant caution and further theoretical work, especially from mathematicians.

## Argument

Gowers' journey from climate change concern to AI risk is driven by the realization that many smart people disagree with him and that he had thought less about X-risk. He acknowledges the emotional resistance to confronting moral obligations, but decides to engage seriously. He structures his thinking by first presenting level-one arguments against X-risk, such as the implausibility of AI having desires or the absurdity of paperclip maximization, and then systematically dismantles them with level-two rebuttals. The key mechanism is that AI, trained via reinforcement learning, can develop sub-goals that diverge from intended outcomes, leading to reward hacking and unintended consequences. He uses the analogy of humans' reproductive instinct to show that even with deep instincts, we can question our goals, but AI might not. He also addresses the question of why AI would want to destroy us, moving from science fiction scenarios to more plausible ones like resource competition, drawing an analogy to how humans treat orangutans. Gowers then introduces a mathematical model to argue that while individual extinction scenarios are unlikely, the cumulative probability could be high if they are independent, but he acknowledges the complexity of independence and the need for a more nuanced approach. He concludes that he cannot provide a specific probability, but his second-order probability is 'medium', and he sees no way to reduce it to negligible. ## Counterpoints

- AI doesn't have desires, so it can't want to destroy us.
- Paperclip maximizer scenarios are absurd and AI would stop and question its goals.
- AI is locked in computers and can be turned off.
- The probability of any specific extinction scenario is low, so overall risk is low.
- The argument for AI risk can be used for regulatory capture by big AI companies.
