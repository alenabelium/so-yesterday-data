## Thesis

Timothy Gowers, a Fields medalist, argues that while he cannot assign a specific probability to AI extinction risk, the risk is serious enough to warrant caution and further theoretical work, especially from mathematicians.

## Argument

Gowers' journey from skepticism to cautious concern is driven by the realization that his initial arguments against existential risk were 'level-one' arguments, easily rebutted by those who had thought more deeply. He acknowledges the standard rebuttal to the 'AI has no desires' argument: even if AI lacks subjective experience, it can act as if it has desires, and an AI that acts as if it wants to destroy us is just as dangerous. He finds the paperclip maximizer scenario absurd but concedes that the sub-goals developed in pursuit of a goal might not disappear even if the AI questions the goal itself. He also notes that the current paradigm of 'growing' models via reinforcement learning gives us less control over these sub-goals. Gowers then addresses the question of why AI would want to destroy us, finding the 'competing for resources' scenario more plausible than active extermination, drawing an analogy to how humans destroy orangutan habitats for palm oil. He acknowledges that any specific extinction scenario is unlikely, but argues that the probability of at least one scenario occurring could be high if there are many independent scenarios, though he admits the independence assumption is too strong. He ultimately concludes that he cannot assign a specific probability to extinction, but his 'meta-probability' is medium, and he sees no way to reduce it to negligible. He suggests mathematicians can contribute by formalizing concepts like 'desire' and 'alignment', and by working on specialized AI systems rather than general ones.

## Counterpoints

- The argument that AI cannot have desires is insufficient because AI can act as if it has desires, and that is enough to be dangerous.
- The paperclip maximizer scenario is absurd because a superintelligent AI would question its own goals, but the sub-goals it developed might persist.
- The probability of any specific extinction scenario is low, but the cumulative probability of many independent scenarios could be high, though the independence assumption is questionable.
- AI is locked in computers and can be turned off, but this ignores the possibility of AI controlling robots, hacking systems, or manipulating humans.
- The argument that AI development brings benefits like cures for cancer is not worth the risk of extinction, but this argument can be used for regulatory capture by AI companies.
