## Thesis

The AI Mathathon's redesign toward exposition is a welcome shift, but it's a trap: if AI proves better at exposition too, mathematicians will have no castles left to defend.

## Argument

The original Mathathon was a positive, playful student initiative that got attacked by an open letter from established mathematicians—'no fun allowed.' Now the organizers have redesigned it around 'old problems, new proofs,' celebrating human understanding and exposition. That's a genuine improvement: it rewards the parts of math that grant makers and hiring committees have completely ignored, and it follows 3Blue1Brown's suggestion to value conceptual understanding. The event now requires full disclosure of AI usage, encourages open-source tools, and is funded by Terence Tao's foundation and XTX Markets after OpenAI withdrew and Anthropic's funding was turned down. But here's the trap: mathematicians were rudely awakened when AI started proving results better than humans, threatening their job security. So they're retreating to the narrative that understanding and exposition are what really matter—even though that contradicts their behavior for centuries. If this Mathathon reveals that AIs are also better at exposition, there's nowhere left to run. The community is running out of castles to defend.

## Counterpoints

- Some commenters doubt the theme change will alter the agendas of XTX Markets or SAIR.
- The chess analogy suggests AI might help weaker mathematicians more than elites, dethroning top names.
- Anonymous argues math is not competitive in principle, unlike chess, though the host notes practice has been brutally competitive.
- A commenter questions whether the redesign attracts people more interested in resume polishing than pure math research.
