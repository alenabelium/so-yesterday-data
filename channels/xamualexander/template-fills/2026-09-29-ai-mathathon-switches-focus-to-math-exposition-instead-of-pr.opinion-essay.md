## Thesis

The redesigned AI Mathathon, by pivoting from proving new results to celebrating human exposition of old problems, exposes a fatal trap: if AI proves better at exposition too, mathematicians will have no castle left to retreat to.

## Argument

The redesign is a retreat dressed as a victory. The original Mathathon was a bold, positive announcement by Caltech undergrads, immediately attacked by established mathematicians in an open letter — the 'no fun allowed' letter. Now, after a week of consensus-building, the event has been rebranded as 'old problems, new proofs,' explicitly shifting focus from open problems to mathematical understanding and exposition. The organizers even admit that these components have been 'overlooked by grant makers and hiring committees' — which is an understatement; they've been completely ignored. This is a major change, and it follows the suggestion made by 3Blue1Brown to value human conceptual understanding. The community is filling a leadership vacuum with math expository people, which would have been unthinkable a year ago.

But the trap is this: mathematicians were rudely awakened when AI started proving theorems better than humans, imperiling their job security. Now they're coalescing around the narrative that it's not the proofs, it's the understanding. This blatantly contradicts their behavior for the past several hundred years, where everything was about bullet points on CVs and tenure. If this Mathathon reveals that AIs are not only better at proving theorems but also better at exposition, where will the elitist mathematicians run next? They're running out of castles to defend.
