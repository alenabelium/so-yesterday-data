## Thesis

The AI Mathathon's redesign toward exposition is a retreat that will fail because AI will likely prove better at exposition too, leaving mathematicians with no castle left to defend.

## Argument

The redesign is a strategic retreat by mathematicians who, after AI proved better at theorem-proving, are now claiming that understanding and exposition are the true core of mathematics. This is a blatant contradiction of their historical behavior, where only problem-solving was rewarded. The Mathathon's new focus on 'old problems, new proofs' and human-oriented expositions is a desperate attempt to find a domain where humans can still compete. But the trap is obvious: if AI is better at proving theorems, why would it not also be better at explaining them? The event's own structure, requiring full disclosure of AI usage and encouraging open-source tools, will likely accelerate AI's development in this area. The chess analogy from the comments is instructive: when computers helped both Kasparov and his opponent equally, the elite's advantage vanished. If the same happens in math, the 'unwashed masses' will become as good as the 'elitists' at exposition, and the mathematicians will have nowhere left to run.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that mathematics is not competitive like chess, but in practice it has been extremely competitive for centuries, with mathematicians fighting for CV bullet points and tenure.
- Others doubt the theme change will matter, suggesting that funders like XTX Markets and SAIR won't change their agendas, but this is speculative.
- A commenter suggests that AI-assisted math resembles the 'advanced chess' experiment, where computers helped weaker players as much as stronger ones, potentially dethroning elite mathematicians.
- Some believe that the focus on understanding is a positive shift, but this contradicts the historical reward system that ignored exposition entirely.
