## Thesis

The AI Mathathon's pivot to exposition is a strategic retreat by mathematicians who, having lost the proof battle to AI, are now retreating to a castle that AI may soon conquer too.

## Argument

The redesign is a retreat dressed as a celebration. The original Mathathon was a bold, positive announcement by Caltech undergrads, but established mathematicians responded with a massive open letter demanding its cancellation. Now, the organizers have capitulated, shifting the focus from proving new results to 'old problems, new proofs' and human-oriented exposition. This is a direct response to the existential threat AI poses to mathematical job security. The community is coalescing around the narrative that 'understanding' and 'exposition' are what truly matter, even though this blatantly contradicts centuries of behavior where only solving problems was rewarded. The trap is obvious: if the Mathathon reveals that AI is also better at exposition, mathematicians will have nowhere left to retreat. The event's new funding from Terence Tao's foundation and XTX Markets, after OpenAI and Anthropic withdrew or were turned down, signals a shift toward open-source tools, but it doesn't change the underlying dynamic. The chess analogy from the comments is apt: if AI helps the unwashed masses as much as the elites, the mathematical hierarchy collapses. The only hope is that AI's exposition skills remain inferior, but that's a fragile hope. The Mathathon is a fascinating experiment, but it's also a last stand.
