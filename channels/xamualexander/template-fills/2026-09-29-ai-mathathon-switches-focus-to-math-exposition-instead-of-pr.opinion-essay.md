## Thesis

The AI Mathathon's redesign toward exposition is a strategic retreat that will collapse if AI proves better at explaining math than humans.

## Argument

The Mathathon's pivot from proving new results to old problems, new proofs is a direct response to the open letter backlash, but it's also a dangerous concession. The organizers now celebrate human understanding and exposition, explicitly valuing these over problem-solving—a shift that aligns with 3Blue1Brown's influence. This is a trap: mathematicians, having lost the proof frontier to AI, are now retreating to exposition as their last castle. If the Mathathon reveals AI can also explain better, there's nowhere left to run. The event's funding shift from OpenAI and Anthropic to Terence Tao's SAIR and XTX Markets signals a community realignment, but it doesn't change the core risk. The trap is that this narrative contradicts centuries of mathematical practice, where proofs were the currency. Now, by making exposition the goal, they're inviting AI to compete on that turf too—and if AI wins, the retreat becomes a rout.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue the original Mathathon attracted resume-polishers, not serious mathematicians—a claim the host dismisses as projection.
- Wolfgang suggests XTX and SAIR won't change their agendas despite the redesign, hinting at hidden motives the host doesn't fully grasp.
- The chess analogy is disputed: math is not competitive in principle, though the host notes it's been practiced competitively for centuries.
- Mochizuki's ABC proof is now known to be wrong via Lean, making any attempt to salvage it a waste of time.
- The host acknowledges the possibility that AI might not outperform humans in exposition, but warns it's too early to tell.
