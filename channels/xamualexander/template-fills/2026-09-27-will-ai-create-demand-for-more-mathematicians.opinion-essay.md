## Thesis

AI will not create demand for more mathematicians because the rate of AI-generated breakthroughs would require exponentially more mathematicians, making the vision unrealistic.

## Argument

The mechanism is the feedback loop between mathematicians and AI. If we increase the number of mathematicians to understand every AI breakthrough, those mathematicians will use AI to generate even more breakthroughs, leading to an exponential explosion in the required number of mathematicians. This is unsustainable and would eventually require every human to be a mathematician, which is not realistic. Additionally, the premise that struggle is essential to understanding is questionable; with a perfect tutor, struggle might not be necessary. The idea of a 'deployable intellectual reserve' of pure mathematicians is flawed because pure mathematicians are concerned with things that do not have applications, and the government would likely prefer physicists or chemists for such a reserve. The prestige and career incentives in pure mathematics are also changing, with the excitement of proving new theorems evaporating as AI can recreate them quickly. The article's own admission of using GPT-6 to draft it undermines its call for human understanding.

## Counterpoints

- Struggle is essential to understanding difficult concepts.
- A deployable intellectual reserve of mathematicians is needed to understand AI breakthroughs.
- Human agency is a fundamental value that requires understanding AI-generated ideas.
- AI systems will make each of us so much more effective that fewer people could do this work.
