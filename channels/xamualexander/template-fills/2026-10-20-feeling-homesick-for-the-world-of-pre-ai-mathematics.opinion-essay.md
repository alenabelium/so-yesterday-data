## Thesis

The transition to post-AI mathematics is a permanent, irreversible phase transition that leaves mathematicians homesick for the pre-AI world, even as they embrace the new era's breakthroughs.

## Argument

The mechanism is emotional and irreversible. The host, Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander, describes the shift as a phase transition, a hysteresis, that cannot be reversed. He notes that AI now proves theorems better than almost any human, citing OpenAI's release of nearly 400 families of major math results. This makes traditional mathematical skills obsolete, a painful realization for someone who spent decades honing them. The democratization of mathematics is exciting, but it also erases the distinction that independent researchers once had. Without university affiliation, his papers are likely to be desk-rejected, as editors will use affiliation as a proxy for quality. The host mourns the loss of the adventure of mathematical discovery—the uncertainty, the struggle, the serendipitous beauty of a proof that evolves beyond the original conjecture. He finds consolation in his pre-AI publications, which now hold a special, almost ancient status, and he predicts that all pre-AI papers will gain a similar aura. He also notes that errors in math papers will become extinct, adding to the nostalgia. Despite the grief and fear, he is excited for the future, comparing it to a pilgrim stepping into a new world, but the ships are burned—there is no going back.

## Counterpoints

- Some might argue that AI will not replace human mathematicians, but the host counters that AI already outperforms humans and the pace is accelerating.
- Others might hope for government intervention to stop AI, but the host dismisses this as unrealistic, calling it a permanent state change.
- The host acknowledges the fear of job insecurity, especially for early-career mathematicians, but he also notes his own financial security from leaving academia, yet still feels the fear of a potential depression.
- He addresses the objection that his nostalgia is irrational, comparing it to homesickness for a poorer country, and admits it's a form of Stockholm syndrome.
