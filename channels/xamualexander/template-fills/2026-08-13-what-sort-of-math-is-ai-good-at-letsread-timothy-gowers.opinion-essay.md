## Thesis

AI's mathematical strengths lie in broad knowledge and brute-force search, making it good at finding examples and counterexamples, but humans may retain an edge in conceptual, deep-probing proofs.

## Argument

Gowers starts by noting that AI's most famous mathematical successes, like the non-sofic group and the Ramsey number bound, were largely counterexamples or examples rather than proofs of universal theorems. He argues that this isn't because AI has a special affinity for existential statements, since many theorems can be reformulated as existence claims. Instead, the real advantage comes from two obvious AI strengths: vast mathematical knowledge and the ability to try many approaches at high speed. This makes AI well-suited for tasks with a probabilistic element, where the best strategy is to try many ideas until one works. In contrast, humans excel at conceptual arguments that require digging deeper into a problem, using intuition to prune the search tree. Gowers lists eight common methods for finding counterexamples, noting that AI is likely good at testing ready-made examples, using probabilistic methods, and 'just do it' proofs, but may struggle with methods requiring judgment about promising directions, like using metavariables or successive approximation. He suggests that AI's lack of human intuition is evident in interactions, where it often proposes approaches that sound promising but fail upon scrutiny. The key barrier is that AI may not develop the ability to prune search trees efficiently because it lacks the incentive to do so, given its speed and knowledge. A true sign of human-level mathematical ability would be producing proofs that are new, surprising, and later seen as beautiful and natural.

## Counterpoints

- AI can also prove universal statements, but the strongest examples are counterexamples.
- The distinction between example and counterexample is context-dependent, not purely logical.
- AI might develop human-like intuition as an emergent property of scaling.
- AI's apparent lack of intuition might be due to training data lacking examples of fruitful research directions.
- A different reward structure during training could encourage more human-like research behavior.
