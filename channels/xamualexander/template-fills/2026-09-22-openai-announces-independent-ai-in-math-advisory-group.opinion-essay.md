## Thesis

OpenAI's independent math advisory group is a PR olive branch that mathematicians should accept, because refusing it would accelerate their irrelevance.

## Argument

The mechanism is reputational and strategic. OpenAI has already solved over 100 open problems, including Navier-Stokes, and could simply publish them without any mathematician input. Instead, they've created an advisory group hosted at the IAS, with heavyweights like Gowers and Witten, explicitly stating it operates independently and won't be paid. This is a calculated move to extend an olive branch to a community that recently embarrassed itself with a flailing open letter signed by 25 Fields Medalists. The group's value hinges on its independence, but the host immediately flags a conflict: Gowers receives free access to OpenAI's pro models, which is a significant non-monetary payment that undermines the 'unpaid' claim. The deeper issue is the pace of progress. The advisory group won't advise on internal pacing, meaning OpenAI will keep advancing regardless. Mathematicians face a game-theoretic bind: if they refuse to serve, they lose any voice in how results are communicated and risk being seen as Luddites protecting job security. If they serve, they legitimize OpenAI's PR, but they get a seat at the table. The host argues that cooperation is the only rational choice because the alternative—inaction—guarantees the field's gutting within 25 years. The 'if you're not at the table, you're on the menu' framing captures the stakes. The host also notes that mathematics is an ideal AI benchmark, and the objection that it shouldn't be used is baseless. The future is coming regardless, and mathematicians should shape it rather than fight it.

## Counterpoints

- Burt Totaro: OpenAI is exploiting mathematicians' trust for crisis management, and the group can't change how OpenAI does business.
- Anonymous: Refusing to cooperate can delegitimize OpenAI's math-solving enterprise, so mathematicians aren't on the back foot.
- Anonymous: OpenAI explicitly aims to replace mathematicians, so why help them?
- Anonymous: The advisory group's independence is undermined by members receiving free access to OpenAI's pro models, a conflict of interest.
- Anonymous: The lack of transparency about the 100 results—what they are and their significance—is disheartening and fuels skepticism.
