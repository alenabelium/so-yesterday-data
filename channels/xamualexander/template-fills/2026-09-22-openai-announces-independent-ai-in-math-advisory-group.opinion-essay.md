## Thesis

OpenAI's independent AI-in-Math Advisory Group is a diplomatic olive branch that mathematicians should accept, despite risks, because refusing it would accelerate their irrelevance.

## Argument

The mechanism is reputational and strategic. OpenAI could simply ignore the 25 Fields Medalists' open letter and face no repercussions, but instead it created an advisory group hosted at the Institute for Advanced Study, with members like Timothy Gowers and Edward Witten. This is a calculated PR move, but it's also a genuine offer of cooperation. The group will advise on review and communication of results, operate independently, and members won't be paid by OpenAI—though Gowers' free access to pro models is a conflict of interest that undermines that claim.

The alternative to cooperation is worse. If mathematicians refuse to engage, OpenAI will publish its 100+ solved problems anyway, without care for exposition or community absorption. The public already perceives mathematicians as Luddites protecting their jobs. As one commenter put it, "If you're not at the table, you're on the menu." The glass castle is already shattering; holding out guarantees destruction, while cooperation offers a chance to shape how results are communicated and to reform academia.

Moreover, mathematics is an ideal benchmark for AI—it's a brilliant application. The objections to using it are weak and stem from job insecurity. No one owns mathematics. The field is entering an exciting era, and mathematicians who adapt will thrive, even if their roles change dramatically. The PhD student's fear of wasted effort is understandable, but pure math was already a poor career choice for most; the love of the subject is the only valid reason to pursue it.

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI is cynically exploiting mathematicians' trust for PR, as Burt Totaro suggests, and the group cannot change OpenAI's behavior.
- Mathematicians could collectively refuse to serve, delegitimizing OpenAI's enterprise and preserving their own standing.
- The advisory group's independence is compromised by members like Gowers receiving free access to OpenAI's pro models.
- OpenAI explicitly aims to replace mathematicians, so helping them is self-defeating.
- The group won't advise on pacing internal progress, so it has no real influence over OpenAI's actions.
