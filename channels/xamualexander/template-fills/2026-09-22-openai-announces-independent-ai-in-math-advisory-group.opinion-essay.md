## Thesis

OpenAI's independent math advisory group is a PR olive branch that mathematicians should accept, because refusing it would only accelerate their field's irrelevance.

## Argument

The mechanism is reputational and strategic. OpenAI has already solved over 100 open problems, including Navier-Stokes, and could simply publish them without any community input. Instead, they're offering mathematicians a seat at the table—a move that makes OpenAI look mature and responsible while giving mathematicians a voice. The alternative is inaction, which the host argues is far worse. As one commenter puts it, "If you're not at the table, you're on the menu." Mathematicians who refuse to cooperate are like Luddites clinging to job security, but the castle is already shattering. Cooperation, even with a potential enemy, is the only way to influence how AI reshapes the field. The host also notes that pure math PhDs were already a terrible career choice before AI, so the real value is in the love of the subject, not the job prospects. The advisory group, despite its conflicts of interest, represents a chance to reform academia and reward human contributions like proof exposition, rather than being left behind entirely.

## Counterpoints

- OpenAI is cynically using mathematicians to launder its reputation after bad publicity, and the group can't change how OpenAI operates.
- Refusing to cooperate could delegitimize OpenAI's math-solving enterprise, giving mathematicians leverage.
- The advisory group's independence is compromised because members like Timothy Gowers receive free access to OpenAI's pro models.
- The group won't advise on pacing internal progress, so it's just a PR stunt with no real influence.
- Mathematicians who cooperate are helping the enemy that aims to replace them.
