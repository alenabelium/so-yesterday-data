## TL;DR

A long-standing open problem—the optimal packing of 11 squares—has been solved by AI, with the proof formalized in the Lean proof assistant rather than in plain English. This marks a significant triumph for AI in mathematics, showcasing its ability to handle proofs too complex for human patience. The breakthrough opens the door for AI to tackle other unproven configurations, potentially revealing more 'glitches in reality.'

## The argument

- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:19']
- **Claim**: A company used AI to solve the long-standing open problem of optimal packing of 11 squares, presenting an ugly configuration that is proven to be optimal. The proof relies on a reflection mapping to a torus, which the AI animated to visualize.

### Proof Formalized in Lean
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:01']
- **Claim**: The proof is so complex that it was not written in plain English; instead, the authors provided a link to their formalization in the Lean proof assistant. This highlights AI's role in producing and verifying proofs that are beyond human patience to write out.

- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:09']
- **Claim**: While many square packings are proven (e.g., 5 by Fritz Gerbil, 10 by Walter Stromquist), many others remain unproven, such as 17, 29, and 1765. These unproven configurations are merely hypotheses, and AI could either prove their optimality or find better ones.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The video showcases the 11-square packing as a proven optimal configuration, with the proof formalized in Lean. Other examples include the 10-square packing, which is a family of optimal packings proven by Walter Stromquist in 2003. The host notes that many configurations, like 17 and 29, are unproven and may not be optimal. He also mentions that factories can use these packings without proof, but proving optimality provides peace of mind.
