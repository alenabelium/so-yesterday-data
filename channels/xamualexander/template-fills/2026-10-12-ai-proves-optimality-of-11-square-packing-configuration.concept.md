## TL;DR

AI has formally proven the optimal packing of 11 squares, a long-standing open problem, by encoding the proof in the Lean proof assistant rather than plain English. This marks a significant triumph for AI in mathematics, showcasing its ability to handle proofs too complex for human patience. The same approach is poised to tackle many other unproven configurations, from 17 to 1765 squares.

## The argument

### The Ugly Optimal Configuration
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:19']
- **Claim**: The 11-square packing looks ugly but is proven optimal. The key insight is a reflection mapping the ugly configuration to a beautiful one on a torus, which the host animated with AI. This mapping alone doesn't prove optimality, but it's the visual foundation.
- **Role**: definition

### The Proof Lives in Lean, Not English
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:48']
- **Claim**: The paper on Fibonacci geometry for 11 squares doesn't contain the full proof in plain English; it references a formalization in the Lean proof assistant. The proof is so complex that the authors didn't even attempt a human-readable version, highlighting AI's role in formal verification.
- **Role**: evidence

### AI's Infinite Patience vs. Human Limits
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:01']
- **Claim**: No human would have the patience to formally prove this packing's optimality, but AI has infinite patience and never tires. This makes AI ideal for such tedious, complex proofs, opening the door to tackling other unproven configurations.
- **Role**: synthesis

### A Catalog of Unproven Configurations
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:09']
- **Claim**: Many other square packings remain unproven, from 17 squares (discovered in 1998) to 1765 squares (improved in 2024). The host doubts any human would have the patience to prove these, but AI is likely to either prove their optimality or find better configurations soon.
- **Role**: evidence

## Evidence and caveats

The host notes that the 11-square proof is formalized in Lean, not plain English, and that the paper itself only references the proof. He also points out that the website cataloging packings hasn't been updated to reflect the 2026 proof. For other configurations, like 17 squares, optimality remains unproven, and the host suggests AI might find better packings. He also mentions that factories don't need proofs to use these packings, but proofs provide peace of mind.
