## TL;DR

AI has formally proven the optimal packing of 11 squares, a long-standing open problem, by encoding the proof in the Lean proof assistant rather than plain English. This marks a significant triumph for AI in mathematics, showcasing its infinite patience for complex proofs. The same approach could soon resolve other unproven configurations, like 17 or 1765 squares.

## The argument

### The Ugly Optimal Configuration
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:19']
- **Claim**: The 11-square packing looks ugly but is optimal; the key is a reflection that maps it to a nicer configuration on a torus. This mapping is visual but does not itself prove optimality.
- **Role**: definition

### Proof Hidden in Lean Code
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:01']
- **Claim**: The proof is so complex that the authors didn't write it in plain English; they linked to a formalization in Lean. This is a significant triumph for AI, which has infinite patience for such tasks.
- **Role**: evidence

### Many Packings Remain Unproven
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:09']
- **Claim**: While some packings like 10 squares are proven, many others (17, 28, 29, 1765) are only conjectured. AI's patience makes it likely that these will be proven or improved soon.
- **Role**: counter

### AI as a Glitch-Finder in Reality
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:08:53']
- **Claim**: The research program on square packing reveals 'glitches in reality'—configurations that look non-optimal but are. AI's role is to confirm or find better packings, turning these glitches into proven facts.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The 11-square proof is anchored in the Lean formalization, not a human-readable proof. Other packings like 10 squares have families of optima, proven by Walter Stromquist in 2003. The website cataloging packings hasn't been updated to reflect the 11-square proof. Many configurations (17, 28, 29, 1765) remain unproven, and the host doubts any human would have the patience. AI's infinite patience makes it ideal for such proofs, but the host notes that factories don't need proofs to use the packings; proofs just provide peace of mind.
