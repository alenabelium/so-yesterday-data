## TL;DR

A recent breakthrough uses AI to prove the optimal packing of 11 squares, a problem open since 1979. The proof is formalized in the Lean proof assistant, not plain English, marking a significant AI triumph in mathematics. This opens the door to proving many other unproven configurations, potentially revealing 'glitches in reality'.

## The argument

### The 11-Square Breakthrough
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:19']
- **Claim**: AI has solved the long-standing open problem of optimal packing of 11 squares, proving that the 'Trump configuration' is indeed optimal. This was previously unproven despite being discovered in 1979.
- **Role**: definition

### Proof in Lean, Not English
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:01']
- **Claim**: The proof is so complex that it was formalized in the Lean proof assistant rather than written in plain English. The paper only links to the Lean code, highlighting AI's ability to handle proofs that humans would find too tedious.
- **Role**: evidence

### AI's Infinite Patience
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:50']
- **Claim**: AI's infinite patience makes it ideal for formal proofs that would be impractical for humans. This is a key advantage, as no human would likely have the patience to manually prove such optimality.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Landscape of Unproven Packings
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:09']
- **Claim**: Many other square packing configurations, such as 17, 28, and 1765 squares, remain unproven. These are only hypotheses, and AI is poised to either prove their optimality or find better configurations.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Revealing Glitches in Reality
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:08:47']
- **Claim**: The research program on square packing is like finding 'glitches in reality'—counterintuitive optimal configurations that challenge our intuition. AI's role in proving these is a step toward understanding these imperfections.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The video cites the 'Fibonacci Geometry of 11 Squares' paper, which links to a Lean formalization as the actual proof. The host notes that the paper itself does not contain a complete proof in plain English. Other examples include the 10-square family proven by Walter Stromquist in 2003, and the 5-square case proven by Fritz Gerbil in 1979. The host also mentions that the website cataloging these packings has not been updated to reflect the 11-square proof. He cautions that while AI can prove optimality, it might also find better configurations, and that factories don't need proofs to use these packings practically.
