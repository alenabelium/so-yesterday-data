## TL;DR

AI has proven the optimal packing of 11 squares, a long-standing open problem, by formalizing the proof in Lean. This marks a significant triumph for AI in mathematics, as the proof was too complex for plain English. The host sees this as a glimpse into a future where AI tackles even more complex packing problems.

## The argument

### The Ugly Optimal Configuration
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:19']
- **Claim**: The host introduces the 11-square packing problem, showing an 'ugly' arrangement that is actually optimal. He explains that this configuration, mapped onto a torus, reveals a symmetry that is key to the proof, but the animation alone does not prove optimality.
- **Role**: definition

### The Proof Lives in Lean, Not English
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:01']
- **Claim**: The host reveals that the proof is not written in plain English but is formalized in the Lean proof assistant. This is a significant triumph for AI, as no human would have the patience to formally prove this, but AI has infinite patience.
- **Role**: evidence

### A Catalog of Unproven Packings
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:09']
- **Claim**: The host surveys other square packing configurations, noting that many, like 17 squares, remain unproven. He expresses doubt that any person would have the patience to prove them, but AI could do it, or even find better configurations.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Counterintuitive Nature of Optimality
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:08:47']
- **Claim**: The host highlights the counterintuitive nature of optimal packings, like the 29-square case, which looks 'ugly' and 'crazy' but is optimal. He frames this as a way to find 'glitches in reality', exposing the universe's imperfections.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The host provides several examples of square packing configurations, noting that many are unproven, such as 17, 29, and 1765 squares. He mentions that the 11-square proof was formalized in Lean, and that the website cataloging these packings has not yet been updated. He also notes that while proving optimality is reassuring, factories can use these packings without proof.
