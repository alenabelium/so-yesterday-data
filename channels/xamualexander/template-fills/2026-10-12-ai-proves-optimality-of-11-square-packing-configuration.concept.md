## TL;DR

AI has proven the optimal packing of 11 squares, a long-standing open problem, by formalizing the proof in Lean. This marks a shift: proofs too complex for human patience are now achievable. The same approach could soon resolve other unproven configurations, like 17 or 1765 squares.

## The argument

### The Ugly Optimal Configuration
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:19']
- **Claim**: The 11-square packing, discovered by Walter Trump, looks ugly but is provably optimal. The key insight is a reflection that maps the ugly configuration to a nicer one on a torus, but this mapping alone doesn't prove optimality.
- **Role**: definition

### Proof Hidden in Lean Code
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:48']
- **Claim**: The paper on Fibonacci geometry doesn't contain the full proof; it references a formalization in Lean. The proof is so complex that the authors didn't attempt a plain-English version, making this a significant triumph for AI in mathematics.
- **Role**: evidence

### AI's Infinite Patience
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:01']
- **Claim**: Humans lack the patience for such formal proofs, but AI has infinite patience and never tires. This makes AI ideal for tackling complex mathematical proofs that would otherwise remain unproven.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Many Configurations Remain Unproven
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:09']
- **Claim**: While some packings like 3, 4, and 5 are proven, many others like 17, 28, and 1765 are only conjectured. AI could soon prove these or find even better configurations, potentially improving factory efficiency.
- **Role**: counter

## Evidence and caveats

The video cites the 11-square proof as a landmark, but notes the paper itself defers to Lean code. Other configurations like 17 and 1765 remain unproven, and the speaker admits the animation doesn't prove optimality. The speaker also highlights the practical benefit for factories, though he notes they don't need proof to use the packing.
