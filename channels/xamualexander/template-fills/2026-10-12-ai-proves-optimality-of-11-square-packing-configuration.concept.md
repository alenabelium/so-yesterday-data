## TL;DR

AI has formally proven the optimal packing of 11 squares, a long-standing open problem, using the Lean proof assistant. This marks a significant triumph for AI in mathematics, showcasing its ability to handle proofs too complex for human patience. The approach is poised to tackle many other unproven configurations, from 17 to 1765 squares.

## The argument

### The Ugly Configuration is Optimal
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:19']
- **Claim**: The seemingly ugly arrangement of 11 squares inside a larger square is actually the optimal packing. There is no way to pack them more efficiently, a fact that was long unproven despite the configuration's discovery.
- **Role**: definition

### Proof via Torus Mapping and Lean
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:01:39', '00:04:01']
- **Claim**: The proof leverages a mapping to a torus, where the configuration becomes beautiful and symmetric. The formal proof is so complex that it was not written in plain English but instead formalized in the Lean proof assistant, with the paper referencing the Lean code as the actual proof.
- **Role**: evidence

### AI's Infinite Patience is Key
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:04:50']
- **Claim**: No human would have the patience to formally prove this optimality, but AI has infinite patience and never tires. This makes AI perfect for tackling such complex mathematical proofs, a significant triumph for AI in mathematics.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Many Configurations Remain Unproven
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:09', '00:08:47']
- **Claim**: While some configurations like 5, 10, and 11 are proven, many others (17, 28, 29, 1765) remain unproven hypotheses. AI is likely to prove these or find even better packings, potentially revealing 'glitches in reality' and improving practical applications like factory efficiency.
- **Role**: counter

## Evidence and caveats

The 11-square proof was formalized in Lean, not plain English, highlighting its complexity. The paper itself references the Lean code as the proof, showing a shift in mathematical practice. Other configurations like 10 squares have families of optimal packings, proven by Walter Stromquist in 2003. The host notes that many configurations (17, 28, 29, 1765) are unproven, and the website cataloging them is not updated. He also cautions that the animation of the torus mapping does not itself prove optimality, as it leaves empty space that might be rearranged. The host expresses excitement about AI's potential to solve these problems, but also acknowledges that factory owners can use the packings without proof, though proof provides peace of mind.
