## Thesis

AI is now settling major open mathematical conjectures at random, and the mathematics community is too overwhelmed to even acknowledge the results.

## Argument

The mechanism is random selection. Tom Adamski's thread shows Astra producing Lean-verified disproofs of Smail's mean value conjecture (1981) and the Köthe conjecture (1930), but Adamski openly admits he doesn't know if these conjectures are significant. He's just pulling from lists of open problems. The Köthe conjecture, called the biggest open problem in ring theory, is 96 years old. A year ago, this would be front-page news. Now it languishes on Twitter, not even posted to arXiv. The community is 'zombified' after the Jacobian conjecture counterexample and the deluge of AI results. Even Levent Alpoge, famous for that counterexample, says 'Nice' — implying Anthropic already knew about the Smail disproof and didn't bother announcing it. The Wikipedia page for the Köthe conjecture still says 'open as of 2025' — the talk page only has a one-hour-old comment about the counterexample. The point isn't the specific results; it's that anyone can now point an AI at a random open problem and get a major theorem settled, and the field can't keep up.

## Counterpoints

- Adamski himself says he's not competent to judge the results and asks people not to hype them until the math community weighs in.
- The Köthe conjecture's significance is debated — 'biggest open problem in ring theory' may overstate it since ring theory is more a tool than a standalone field.
- There's contradictory information about whether the conjectures came from a formal conjectures repository or Wikipedia's list of open problems.
