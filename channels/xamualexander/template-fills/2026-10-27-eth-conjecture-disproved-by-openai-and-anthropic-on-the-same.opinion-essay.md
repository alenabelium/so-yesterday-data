## Thesis

The simultaneous, independent disproof of the Exponential Time Hypothesis by OpenAI and Anthropic on October 16, 2026, invalidates thousands of papers and proves AI creates new open problems, not just solves them.

## Argument

The mechanism is the sheer scale of the breakthrough. Both OpenAI and Anthropic, on the same day, independently found a deterministic algorithm that counts satisfying assignments of k-CNF formulas in subexponential time, directly refuting ETH. This isn't a marginal improvement; it's a 'gargantuan breakthrough' that 'renders immediately obsolete hundreds or probably thousands of papers' that assumed ETH. The result is not just a solution—it's a problem generator. Every result that was proven 'under the assumption of the exponential time hypothesis' is now an open problem, directly countering the 'tired canard that AI only solves problems and never gives us new problems.' The papers themselves are a testament to the shift: OpenAI's 67-page paper casually mentions subexponential algorithms for counting graph colorings and perfect matchings, results that 'would be more than enough to guarantee you a PhD' a year ago. Yet, the result is purely asymptotic—'galactic algorithms' that only beat brute force for inputs with more than 10^10,000 variables. This reveals a perverse state of affairs: the 'better' algorithm is useless in practice, suggesting we need new ways to measure complexity. The coincidence of the same-day discovery is 'astonishing,' and the lack of cryptographic hashes to establish priority is 'strange,' hinting that Anthropic was forced to publish early after OpenAI's announcement. The result also refutes the Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH) but not the Orthogonal Vectors Hypothesis, leaving some fine-grained complexity results intact.

## Counterpoints

- Some might claim AI only solves problems, but this result creates hundreds of new open problems, directly refuting that.
- The result does not solve P vs NP; it's subexponential, not polynomial, and doesn't transfer to all NP problems.
- The algorithms are 'galactic'—impractical for real-world inputs, only beating brute force for astronomically large instances.
- Skeptics question Anthropic's priority claim, noting the lack of a cryptographic hash to prove discovery date.
- The same-day discovery is so coincidental that it invites conspiracy theories about spying or collusion.
