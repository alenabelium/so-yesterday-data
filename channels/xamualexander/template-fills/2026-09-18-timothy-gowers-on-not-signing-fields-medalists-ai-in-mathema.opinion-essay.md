## Thesis

The Fields Medalists' letter on AI in mathematics misidentifies the crisis: the real threat is not the volume of AI-generated results but the collapse of the social structures that support mathematical digestion and training.

## Argument

Gowers' refusal to sign the letter stems from a deeper disagreement about the nature of mathematical practice. He argues that the letter's emphasis on conceptual understanding over problem-solving is a recent, self-serving shift, noting that journals have long rejected papers that offer only conceptual insight without new proofs. This hypocrisy reveals that the community's actual priority has always been problem-solving. The real crisis, as Gowers sees it, is not the flood of AI results—which, like a deluge of streaming content, offers more to digest, not less—but the potential destruction of the social structures that train and support mathematicians. If AI solves problems too easily, the motivation for young people to enter the field may evaporate, and the human mathematical tradition could wither. Gowers advocates for adaptation and creative use of AI, such as using it to provide personalized hints for active reading, rather than resistance. He warns against tribalism and the futile attempt to control AI's release, arguing that the best path forward is to recognize the inevitable changes and work out the least unsatisfactory way to adapt.

## Counterpoints

- The letter's claim that mass-produced results could destroy fertile ground for new mathematics, which Gowers counters by noting that AI results often build on human work and that the volume is manageable due to specialization.
- The concern that AI will lead to a loss of deep understanding, which Gowers addresses by suggesting that active reading and AI-assisted hints can preserve problem-solving skills.
- The fear that AI will overwhelm the community's ability to digest results, which Gowers rebuts with the streaming service analogy: more content means more to choose from, not a worse situation.
- The worry about AI not properly crediting human mathematicians, which Gowers sees as a temporary problem that will resolve as the credit system collapses.
- The argument that AI will destroy the motivation for new mathematicians, which Gowers acknowledges as a real risk but counters by emphasizing the need for imaginative ways to use PhD students' time.
