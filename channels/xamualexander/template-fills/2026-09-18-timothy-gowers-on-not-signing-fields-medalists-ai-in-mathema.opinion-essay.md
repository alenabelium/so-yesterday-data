## Thesis

The Fields Medalists' letter on AI in mathematics misdiagnoses the crisis: the real threat is not the volume of AI-generated results but the collapse of the social structures that support mathematical digestion and training.

## Argument

The letter's central claim—that problem-solving is merely a tool for conceptual understanding—oversimplifies the spectrum of mathematical temperaments. Gowers, drawing on his own 'two cultures' essay, argues that mathematicians range from problem-solvers to concept-seekers, and both are valuable. The real crisis is not the flood of AI results but the potential destruction of the social structures that support mathematical digestion and training. He points out that the mathematical community already fails to prioritize conceptual understanding: papers that offer only insight without new proofs are rejected by journals. The letter's proposed solution—an international council to regulate the release of results—is a bureaucratic fantasy that would entrench the establishment. Instead, Gowers advocates for adaptation: using AI to enhance active reading, creating databases to navigate the flood, and finding new ways to train the next generation of mathematicians. The letter's lack of a concrete call to action and its timing, after AI has already demonstrated its power, make it ineffective. The real challenge is to preserve the human mathematical tradition in a world where AI can solve problems better than most humans.

## Counterpoints

- The letter's claim that problem-solving is only a tool for conceptual understanding is oversimplified; there is a spectrum of mathematical temperaments.
- The concern about AI-generated results overwhelming digestion is mitigated by the fact that mathematics is highly specialized and mathematicians can work in parallel.
- The fear that AI results will be overlooked is countered by the fact that even human discoveries, like Galois's, can be overlooked; a flood of AI results would likely increase the total amount of important mathematics that is digested.
- The problem of AI not crediting human mathematicians is temporary; the credit system will collapse as AI becomes the primary producer of proofs.
- The letter's proposed international council to regulate AI results is a bureaucratic fantasy that would entrench the establishment and is unlikely to be effective.
