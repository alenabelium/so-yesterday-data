## Thesis

The Fields Medalists' letter on AI in mathematics misidentifies the crisis: the real threat is not AI-generated results but the collapse of the social structures that support mathematical digestion and training.

## Argument

The letter's core claim—that problem-solving is merely a tool for conceptual understanding—is a convenient post-hoc rationalization. For decades, the mathematical community has rewarded problem-solving above all else; papers that offer only conceptual insight without new proofs are routinely rejected. This hypocrisy reveals that the community's true priority has always been answers, not understanding. 

The real crisis, as Gowers frames it, is not the volume of AI-generated results but the potential destruction of the social structures that allow mathematics to be digested and passed on. If AI solves problems faster than humans can absorb them, the bottleneck becomes the human infrastructure—the PhD students, the custodians of the mathematical tradition, the funding that supports them. If that infrastructure collapses, mathematics as a human endeavor suffers, regardless of how many theorems AI proves. 

Gowers' proposed adaptation is not resistance but creative integration: using AI as a personalized tutor that feeds hints to help mathematicians read papers actively, preserving the problem-solving muscles that build deep understanding. This is the productive path forward, not the bureaucratic fantasy of an international council controlling the release of results—a scenario that reeks of gatekeeping and vested interest.

## Counterpoints

- The letter argues that problem-solving is only a tool for conceptual understanding, but this contradicts the community's actual reward system, which rejects papers without new proofs.
- Some worry that AI-generated results will be too numerous to digest, but mathematics is already flooded with human papers, and the community has coped without crisis.
- The letter's signatories may be motivated by a genuine concern for the new generation of mathematicians, but their proposed solution—an international council—is a bureaucratic fantasy that would entrench the establishment.
- The letter lacks a clear call to action, making it unclear what it demands that isn't already happening.
