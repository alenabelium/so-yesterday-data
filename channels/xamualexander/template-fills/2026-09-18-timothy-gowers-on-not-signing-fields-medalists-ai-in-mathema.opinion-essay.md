## Thesis

The real crisis in mathematics is not AI-generated results but the collapse of the social structures that support mathematical digestion and training, and the community should adapt rather than resist.

## Argument

Gowers' refusal to sign the Fields Medalists' letter hinges on a more nuanced view of the relationship between problem-solving and conceptual understanding. He argues that the letter oversimplifies this relationship, presenting a false dichotomy that would alienate mathematicians across the spectrum. The actual threat, he contends, is not the volume of AI-produced theorems but the potential destruction of the social fabric—the training pipelines, the motivation for young mathematicians, and the funding structures—that sustain the discipline. He points out that the letter's emphasis on conceptual understanding is hypocritical, as journals reject papers that offer only insight without new proofs. He also notes that the fear of being overwhelmed by results is unfounded, as mathematics is specialized and digestible in parallel, and the pre-AI flood of papers already exceeded our capacity to read. The real danger is that the dream of solving famous problems will no longer motivate new generations, leading to a collapse in the number of mathematicians. Gowers advocates for creative adaptation, such as using AI to provide personalized hints for active reading, rather than futile resistance. He concludes that the letter lacks a clear call to action and that criticizing AI companies is pointless, as the release of powerful models is inevitable.

## Counterpoints

- The letter's claim that problem-solving is merely a tool for conceptual understanding is oversimplified and ignores the spectrum of mathematical motivations.
- The fear that AI will overwhelm mathematicians with results is mitigated by the specialized, parallel nature of mathematical work and the pre-existing flood of human papers.
- The concern that AI will destroy the motivation for young mathematicians is real, but it can be addressed through adaptation and creative use of AI, not resistance.
- The letter's lack of a concrete call to action makes it ineffective, as AI companies will release powerful models regardless of criticism.
