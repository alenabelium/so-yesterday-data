## Thesis

Fears that AI will make mathematicians obsolete are overblown; AI makes mathematics more accessible and should be embraced as a tool for solving harder problems, not resisted.

## Argument

The doom and gloom is overrated. Mathematicians will continue to have employment, though the roles may shift and new people may enter the field. The real crisis is an identity crisis from within academia, where mathematicians are valued solely for solving difficult problems, not for conceptual understanding. AI will solve many problems, but mathematicians enjoy the process of solving, not just the solution. AI also makes frontier mathematics accessible to the masses, breaking down the ivory tower. The 25 Fields medalists' declaration that conceptual understanding is paramount is a 180-degree turn from the past, driven by fear of losing prestige. Instead of whining, mathematicians should ask what they can do with AI, using it to solve harder problems and compete as before. The calculator didn't stop mathematical research; AI shouldn't either. The bar should be raised, and those who embrace AI will be remembered in history. AI is bringing people together, as seen in collaborative research on Medvedev's logic. The future is about using AI to solve open problems and advance science, not resisting it.

## Counterpoints

- AI could make mathematicians obsolete, as some fear.
- The value of proofs versus conceptual understanding is debated.
- Motivated explanations are subjective, making them hard to reward.
- AI might become a 'hard drug' with diminishing returns, as seen in software engineering.
- There are concerns about AI being used for harmful purposes, like creating super pathogens.
