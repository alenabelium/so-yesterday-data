## Thesis

AI can reproduce completed mathematical research but cannot yet conduct the exploratory, iterative process of discovering false assumptions and building theories, which remains the core of human mathematical work.

## Argument

The distinction is not about solving well-defined problems but about the research process itself. Morgan Jones's graduate students spent years on projects that began as broad directions, not clear problems. They repeatedly hit failures that changed their understanding—a process fundamentally different from OpenAI's Astra model, which tackled problems with specific goals like proving a hypothesis. This is analogous to ARC AGI-3, where leading models perform poorly because it requires investigating an environment, forming and correcting hypotheses, and using that knowledge adaptively. The key is long-term memory and continuous adaptation. Current LLMs have finite context windows; when extended work exhausts them, they either forget earlier parts or compress, degrading performance. The author's own feedback linearization project illustrates this: initial simulations failed, and the team took those failures as evidence that their assumptions were wrong, leading to new theoretical insights. This iterative, evolving goal over months is something current models cannot sustain. The question is whether AI can automate this discovery process—entering an unfamiliar problem, identifying false assumptions, inventing intermediate abstractions, and building a theory. A priori, there's no reason it can't, but current models still fail at ARC AGI-3, so human theorists still have a role. However, this distinction may blur as AI improves at permanent memory and adaptation. ## Counterpoints

- Some might argue that if AI can reproduce completed research, the end reader can't tell the difference, so the process doesn't matter.
- One might say that AI could eventually handle long-term memory and adaptation, making the distinction disappear.
- The author himself admits he sees no a priori reason why AI can't automate the discovery process.
- The host questions whether humans even perform well on ARC AGI-3, suggesting the benchmark might not be a fair measure of research ability.
