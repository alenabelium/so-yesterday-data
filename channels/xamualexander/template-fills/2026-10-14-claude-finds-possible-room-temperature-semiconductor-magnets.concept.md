## TL;DR

AI agents from Claude Opus 5.5 identified two candidate materials for room-temperature antiferromagnetic semiconductors, potentially enabling faster, denser computer memory. One is a newly designed compound; the other was synthesized in 1999 but overlooked. This could mark AI's first major contribution to materials science, echoing its recent impact on mathematics.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:37']
- **Claim**: The key to next-gen memory is a material that combines zero net magnetism (like antiferromagnets) with spin sorting (like ferromagnets). This is achieved in Luttinger-compensated materials, where spin-up and spin-down atoms are in non-equivalent environments, allowing energy separation of spins.
- **Role**: definition

### Evidence: The 1999 Candidate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:13:26']
- **Claim**: A material first synthesized in 1999, a Prussian blue analog, was identified by AI as a Luttinger-compensated semiconductor. It was created for other purposes and its spin-sorting properties were overlooked. This candidate retains magnetic order up to 376 K, above room temperature, and its structure fixes each metal in its own node, avoiding the fabrication issues of the designed compound.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counter: The Fabrication Challenge
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:10']
- **Claim**: The designed candidate, yttrium-barium-manganese-iron-O5, requires a perfect checkerboard arrangement of manganese and iron atoms. Simulations show this order collapses at 950 K, and synthesis at 900-1300°C likely yields a chaotic crystal, losing spin sorting. This raises doubt about its practical creation.
- **Role**: counter

### Synthesis: AI as a Literature Miner
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:56']
- **Claim**: The discovery of the 1999 candidate exemplifies AI's strength: exhaustive literature review. AI can find what any human could have found but didn't, because humans don't read everything. This is a 'warm-up' for a potential 'physical apocalypse' in materials science, similar to the early AI contributions in mathematics.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The 1999 candidate has a predicted band gap of 2.1 eV with spin-sorted windows of 2.6 eV (holes) and 1.6 eV (electrons), far exceeding thermal jitter at room temperature (26 meV). The designed compound shows a band gap of 2.35 eV with windows of 1.0 eV and 1.4 eV. However, the 1999 sample is a powder with water in pores, showing a residual magnetic moment of 0.125 Bohr magnetons per formula unit, whereas a perfect crystal would have zero. Modeling methods disagree on how water affects spin sorting. Neither band gap nor spin sorting has been measured directly. The host remains cautiously optimistic, noting that if it were that amazing, people in 1999 might have noticed, but AI's relentless attention could uncover overlooked gems.
