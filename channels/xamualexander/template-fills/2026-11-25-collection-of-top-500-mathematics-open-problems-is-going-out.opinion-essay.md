## Thesis

Proof Atlas's AI-ranked top 500 open math problems are already obsolete because major problems like the Riemann hypothesis and Navier-Stokes have been solved, and the site fails to update frequently enough to keep pace with the.

## Argument

The site's premise is sound: with mathematics advancing so rapidly, humans can't keep up, so using AI to rank open problems makes sense. But the execution fails. The list is already outdated. The Riemann hypothesis, ranked #2, was proven just days ago using AI. The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, ranked #5, was also solved by Anthropic. Even the generalized Riemann hypothesis at #10 is outdated. This isn't a minor issue—it's fatal. The whole point of such a product is to track the fast-moving frontier. If they don't update daily, the site loses its value. The site even lists its own achievements, like a partial Collatz result, but fails to remove solved problems. This suggests they're more interested in promoting their own work than maintaining an accurate list. The site is a mess. It's a call to action: someone should build a competitor that actually keeps up. The need is real because progress is accelerating, and we're at the limit of what we can track. It's a fantastic time to be alive, but we need better tools to keep up.

## Counterpoints

- One might argue that the list is still useful as a historical record, but the site's own 'solved problems' section shows they intend to keep it current.
- Some might say that the Riemann hypothesis and Navier-Stokes are not fully solved, but the host insists they are, citing recent AI proofs.
- The site could be seen as a starting point for discussion, but the host finds it too outdated to be relevant.
