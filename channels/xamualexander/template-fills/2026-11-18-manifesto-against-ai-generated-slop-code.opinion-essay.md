## Thesis

The 'No AI Slop' Manifesto correctly identifies that AI-generated content becomes 'slop' when creators invest less effort than consumers must spend to understand it, and this asymmetry is the central problem of AI-assisted work.

## Argument

The manifesto's core insight is the asymmetry between creation and validation: generating a 10-page document or a thousand-row query costs near zero, but validating it still costs the same as before. This is why AI can create more work in a day than a human can review in a lifetime. The solution isn't to abandon AI but to enforce human responsibility, judgment, and effort. The manifesto's principles—like valuing results over output, understanding over speed, and improving systems over correcting results—are not just about code but about any creative or intellectual work. This is especially visible in academia, where the 'publish or die' pressure already buried us in mountains of papers before AI, and now the flood is worse. The manifesto's call to 'dogfood' your own software, to put prompts under version control instead of machine-written code, and to review at least some results based on risk, are practical steps. But the deeper point is that slop isn't new—end-user license agreements are a pre-AI example. The real question is whether we can resist the commercial pressures that reward speed and volume over quality. The Hacker News discussion shows the tension: some argue AI should make things slower, not faster, to maintain quality, while others point out that most people just want the job done. The manifesto's value is in forcing us to confront this trade-off explicitly.

## Counterpoints

- AI is a labor-saving tool; reducing effort is its purpose, so why is that 'slop'?
- The manifesto's values (responsibility, dignity) are not sellable to CEOs who care about profit and speed.
- Getting the same output as everyone else isn't necessarily bad; innovation tokens should be spent on core features.
- Banning large pull requests or requiring prompts in version control may not work for 'slave' workers or in all contexts.
- The manifesto itself could be AI-generated slop, undermining its own credibility.
