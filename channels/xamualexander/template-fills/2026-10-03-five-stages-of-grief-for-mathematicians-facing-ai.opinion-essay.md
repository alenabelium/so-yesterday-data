## Thesis

Mathematicians grieving AI's rise are stuck in denial, anger, bargaining, or depression, but the field's future belongs to those who embrace AI and move forward.

## Argument

Kevin Buzzard's article maps the mathematical community's reaction to AI onto Kubler-Ross's five stages, and the first four are rampant. Denial appears in the Association for Human Mathematics, whose members, including Fields Medalist Peter Scholze, vow to never use AI—a stance that will make them irrelevant. Anger shows in posts like Vlad Vlasic's, raging at AI's temerity to solve 'their' problems. Bargaining is the Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI begging labs to fund human understanding of AI outputs, a plea that ignores fiduciary duties. Depression hits individuals like a fluids professor, a postdoc, and a PhD student questioning their purpose after AI one-shots their lemma.

But the grief is misplaced. The sudden reframing of mathematics as 'about human understanding' is a goalpost move, exposed by the fact that even Buzzard, a student of Richard Taylor, never fully understood his own thesis's foundations. Understanding is nebulous; theorem tally is measurable. AI's proofs, even without explanations, are valuable—OpenAI's 100+ results should be dumped on the world, and humans can reverse-engineer understanding later. The real limitation is not AI's current lack of theory-building or conjecture, but the infinite nature of mathematics versus finite resources. Exponential progress will hit a wall, but that's where humans begin the next adventure. Embrace the change, let go of the old, and move on.

## Counterpoints

- Peter Scholze's stance: he will not use AI and will die on that hill, but this will make him irrelevant.
- The claim that mathematics is about human understanding, not theorem tally, is a goalpost move; even experts don't fully understand their own work.
- AI proofs lack explanations, but humans can reverse-engineer them, as seen with Navier-Stokes and other results.
- Exponential progress can't continue indefinitely, but mathematics is infinite, so machines will hit a natural boundary where humans take over.
