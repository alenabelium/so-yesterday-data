## Thesis

Mathematicians grieving AI's theorem-proving are clinging to a reframed 'human understanding' narrative that is both historically false and strategically doomed, so the field should embrace AI and move forward.

## Argument

The grief stages are real: denial via the Association for Human Mathematics and Scholze's 'die on that hill' stance, anger from Vlasic, bargaining from the Advisory Group asking labs to stop and fund human understanding, and depression from researchers questioning their purpose. But the 'human understanding' justification is a face-heel turn. Before AI, top journals and departments rewarded hard theorems, not understanding. Thurston's essay is now weaponized, but even Buzzard's own PhD experience shows understanding is often skipped. AI will soon explain proofs too, making that retreat untenable. The real value is proving theorems, developing tools, and seeing where to go next. AI is already an expert across fields, as with the Erdős unit distance disproof. Let machines loose, then begin the journey to where they stop. This is the optimal path, and we should get on board now.

## Counterpoints

- AI proofs often come with no explanation, just equations, as with the rank 30/31 elliptic curves, so humans learn nothing.
- Machines can't yet do theory building or conjecture formulation, limiting their progress.
- Exponential progress can't continue indefinitely, and mathematics is infinite, so machines must eventually get stuck.
- Some mathematicians are motivated by art and legacy (Hardy), not just theorems, and they will grieve.
- The 'human understanding' framing is not new cynicism; experts have long valued understanding from prize-winning results.
