## Thesis

Kevin Buzzard is right: mathematicians grieving AI's rise are stuck in denial, anger, bargaining, or depression, and the field must embrace AI to move forward.

## Argument

The grief is real and visible. Denial shows in the Association for Human Mathematics, whose members, including Fields Medalist Peter Scholze, vow to never use AI—a stance that will make them irrelevant. Anger erupts in posts like Vlad Vlasic's, treating AI's progress as an insult to human mathematicians. Bargaining appears in the Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI's plea for labs to stop testing on proprietary models and fund human understanding—a desperate, undignified ask that investors won't support. Depression hits individuals: a fluids professor finds Navier-Stokes news depressing, a postdoc considers quitting, a PhD student's lemma gets one-shotted by ChatGPT.

But the grief is misplaced. The reframing of mathematics as 'about human understanding' is a goalpost move. Thurston's essay is now cited to justify this, yet experts have always valued both theorems and understanding—Wiles' proof gave both. The retreat to understanding is a trap: AI will soon explain theorems too. Buzzard's own experience shows understanding is nebulous; he never read Deligne's proof, yet proved theorems. He's in it for the theorem tally, and so should we be. OpenAI should dump its 100+ results now; humans can reverse-engineer understanding later. The future is exponential progress, but mathematics is infinite—machines will hit a wall. The optimal move is to let them loose, ride the wave, and then begin the new adventure.

## Counterpoints

- Peter Scholze's stance: he will not use AI and will die on that hill—but this will make him irrelevant.
- The 'human understanding' framing, citing Thurston, is a goalpost move; AI will soon explain theorems too.
- Skeptics say machines can't do X today, but exponential growth will continue—yet mathematics is infinite, beating exponential.
- The Advisory Group's bargaining: labs should stop testing and fund human understanding—but investors won't fund that.
- Depression is real: mathematicians feel their work is pointless when AI one-shots their lemmas.
