## Argument

The grief is real but misdirected. Buzzard's five-stage map—denial, anger, bargaining, depression—captures the community's reaction, but each stage rests on a flawed premise. The denialists at the Association for Human Mathematics, including Peter Scholze, vow to die on the hill of AI-free mathematics, yet that stance guarantees irrelevance, not preservation. The angry posts on Proofs and Prompts are a soap opera, a tantrum against the temerity of AI touching 'their' problems. The bargaining of the Advisory Group—begging labs to fund human understanding—is undignified and doomed; investors won't fund nostalgia. And the depression of individual mathematicians, from the fluids professor to the PhD student whose lemma ChatGPT one-shotted, is understandable but premature.

The deeper flaw is the sudden reframing of mathematics as 'all about human understanding.' That's a goalpost move. Before AI, the field rewarded hard theorems, not understanding—papers were rejected from the Annals and candidates from hiring committees for lacking proofs, not for lacking insight. Buzzard torpedoes the 'understanding' ship with his own experience: as Richard Taylor's student, he never read Deligne's proof or Langlands-Tunnell, yet he produced a thesis. So what does 'understanding' even mean? If it's nebulous, then the measurable metric—theorem tally—is the honest one.

## Counterpoints

- Some mathematicians argue mathematics is fundamentally about human understanding, not just theorem-proving.
- The Association for Human Mathematics and Fields Medalist Peter Scholze vow to never use AI, even if it means becoming irrelevant.
- The Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI, including three Fields Medalists, asks labs to stop testing on proprietary models and to fund human understanding of AI outputs.
- Skeptics on social media accuse mathematicians of moving the goalposts by redefining mathematics as understanding to survive AI.
- Some argue that exponential progress in AI will continue indefinitely, but mathematics is infinite, which beats exponential growth.
