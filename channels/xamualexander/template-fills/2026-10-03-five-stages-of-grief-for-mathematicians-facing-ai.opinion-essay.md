## Thesis

Mathematicians grieving AI's rise are stuck in denial, anger, bargaining, or depression, but embracing AI as a tool for proving theorems and exploring new frontiers is the only viable path forward.

## Argument

The grief is real but misplaced. Kevin Buzzard's five-stage framework maps the community's reactions: denial from the Association for Human Mathematics and Peter Scholze's 'die on that hill' stance, anger from posts like Vlad Vlasic's, bargaining from the Advisory Group's plea for labs to fund human understanding, and depression from researchers questioning their purpose after AI solves their problems. These stages are understandable but ultimately self-defeating. The 'human understanding' narrative is a recent invention, contradicted by how mathematicians actually operate—prizes go to hard theorems, not deep insights, as seen in the Wiles-Taylor proof. Buzzard's own experience with Richard Taylor shows that even top mathematicians don't fully understand their field's foundations. The real opportunity is to let AI loose, dump its theorems, and then reverse-engineer them for understanding. AI's current limits—theory building and conjecture formulation—mean humans still have a role, but only if they move past grief and embrace the change. The future belongs to those who adapt, not those who cling to a romanticized past.

## Counterpoints

- Peter Scholze's refusal to use AI, even if it means becoming irrelevant, is a principled stand but won't keep him competitive.
- The Advisory Group's request for labs to fund human understanding is bargaining; labs have fiduciary duties and won't fund unprofitable ends.
- The 'human understanding' framing is a goalpost shift; historically, prizes and jobs went to hard theorem provers, not just deep thinkers.
- AI's current proofs lack explanations, but this is temporary; AI will soon explain theorems as well as prove them.
- Exponential progress can't continue indefinitely, and mathematics is infinite, so AI will hit a natural boundary—but humans should still ride the wave.
