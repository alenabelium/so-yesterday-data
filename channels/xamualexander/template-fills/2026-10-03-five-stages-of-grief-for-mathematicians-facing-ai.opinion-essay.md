## Thesis

Mathematicians are grieving a dead version of their field, but the only rational response is to embrace AI and let it push mathematics to new frontiers.

## Argument

The grief is real but misplaced. Buzzard's five-stage map—denial, anger, bargaining, depression—captures the community's reaction, but each stage is a refusal to accept that the old game is over. The denialists at the Association for Human Mathematics, including Scholze, will become irrelevant if they refuse to engage. The angry posts and the bargaining of the Advisory Group, begging labs to fund human understanding, are undignified and futile. The depression is understandable but self-defeating.

The deeper problem is that the community is rewriting its own history. For decades, mathematics rewarded hard theorems, not understanding. Now that AI proves hard theorems, the goalposts are moving to 'human understanding.' But that framing is fragile: if AI soon explains theorems as well as it proves them, there is nowhere left to retreat. Buzzard's own experience—never reading Deligne's proof, relying on authority—shows that 'understanding' was always a convenient fiction.

The only honest position is Buzzard's: mathematics is about proving theorems, developing tools, and seeing where to go next. AI is already doing the first, and the second is coming. The right move is to release AI's results, let humans reverse-engineer them, and then follow where the machines lead. Exponential progress will eventually hit a wall—mathematics is infinite—but that wall is far off. Get on board now, or be left behind.

## Counterpoints

- Peter Scholze and the Association for Human Mathematics argue that AI threatens the human essence of mathematics; Alexander counters that refusing AI will make them irrelevant.
- The Advisory Group's bargaining—asking labs to fund human understanding—is dismissed as undignified and unlikely to succeed due to fiduciary duties.
- The 'human understanding' framing, citing Thurston, is called a narrative shift; Alexander notes that pre-2026, hard theorems, not understanding, determined journal acceptance and hiring.
- Hardy's view of mathematics as art, which motivates many grieving mathematicians, is acknowledged but not shared by Alexander or Buzzard.
- The claim that AI can't explain mathematics is countered with the prediction that AI will soon explain as well as it proves, leaving no retreat.
