## Thesis

Mathematicians grieving AI's theorem-proving are clinging to a reframed 'human understanding' narrative that never matched how the field actually operated, and they will be left behind.

## Argument

The mechanism is the field's own history. Before 2026, top journals and departments rewarded hard theorems, not understanding — you could improve understanding beyond the moon and still get rejected from the Annals. The sudden pivot to 'it's about human understanding' is a blatant narrative shift to fight the tides, as obvious as Pinocchio's nose. Kevin Buzzard torpedoes this reframing by pointing out that even he, a Fields-adjacent mathematician, never read Deligne's proof or Langlands-Tunnell — he was told his funding was for three years only. So what does 'understanding' even mean? The real metric was always the theorem tally, and AI is now winning that tally. The bargaining stage — the Advisory Group begging labs to fund human understanding — is undignified and won't survive fiduciary duty. The denialists like Scholze will die on their hill and become irrelevant. The only logical move is to let the machines loose, dump the theorems, and start the journey to where they stop — because mathematics is infinite and beats exponential growth hands down.

## Counterpoints

- Peter Scholze and the Association for Human Mathematics argue AI threatens mathematics as a human endeavor, and Scholze will not use AI, even if it makes him irrelevant.
- Thurston's 1994 essay and Tao's blog argue mathematics is about human understanding, not theorem tally, and AI proofs come with no explanation.
- Some argue AI proofs like the rank 30/31 elliptic curves come with equations and nothing else, so we learn nothing from them.
- Skeptics say exponential progress can't continue indefinitely, and mathematics is infinite, so machines will hit a natural boundary.
