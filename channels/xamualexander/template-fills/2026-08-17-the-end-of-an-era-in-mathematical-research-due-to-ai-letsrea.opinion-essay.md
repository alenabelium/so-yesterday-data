## Thesis

AI will end artisanal mathematics, shifting mathematicians from proof creation to curation, but human enjoyment of the craft can persist.

## Argument

The article's core claim is that AI will transform mathematical research from a craft of proof creation to a role of curation and understanding. This is already happening: mathematicians spend more time promoting, reviewing, and explaining results than proving theorems. The chess analogy supports this: computers surpassed humans, yet chess thrives because humans still enjoy playing and explaining. Similarly, AI can generate proofs, but humans will still value understanding and sharing mathematical ideas. The Industrial Revolution analogy shows that while professional roles change, the activity itself persists. The author's claim that teaching will remain human is baseless—AI can motivate and connect emotionally, as seen in its use as a therapist. The real shift is toward prompt engineering and curation, where mathematicians become arbiters of AI-generated results. This is a personal tragedy for those who love the art of discovery, but it doesn't mean mathematics ends—it becomes more accessible and popular, like chess did with AI.

## Counterpoints

- The claim that teaching will remain human is unsupported; AI can fulfill emotional and social roles.
- The chess analogy is flawed because mathematics is a science with practical implications, not a game about human competition.
- AI is unprecedented as an agent, not just a tool, so historical analogies like the Industrial Revolution are incomplete.
- The loss of artisanal mathematics is a personal tragedy for those who value the craft, even if the field advances.
- The note that the article was written with AI contradicts the claim that teaching will remain human.
