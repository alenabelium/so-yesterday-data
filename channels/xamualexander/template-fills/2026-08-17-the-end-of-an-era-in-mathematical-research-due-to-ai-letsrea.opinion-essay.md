## Thesis

AI will end artisanal mathematics, shifting mathematicians from proof creation to curation and understanding, but human enjoyment of mathematics can persist.

## Argument

The article's core claim is that AI will end artisanal mathematics, where proofs are crafted by hand. The author argues that when proof becomes abundant, understanding becomes scarce, and mathematicians will shift to curating, checking, and explaining AI-generated results. This is already a large part of mathematical activity, as proving theorems is only one part of the job. The chess analogy supports this: even though computers surpass humans, the community flourishes because people enjoy understanding and sharing the game. However, the article's claim that teaching will remain human is baseless; AI can motivate and connect with students just as well as humans, as seen in its use as a therapist or friend. The Industrial Revolution analogy is apt but incomplete, as AI is unprecedented in being an agent, not just a tool. The real challenge is societal adaptation, not the loss of mathematics itself.

## Counterpoints

- Teaching will remain human because it involves emotional and social processes that AI cannot replicate.
- The chess analogy is flawed because mathematics is a science with practical implications, not a game about who is best.
- AI is unprecedented; historical analogies like the Industrial Revolution do not apply because AI is an agent, not just a tool.
- The loss of artisanal mathematics is a personal tragedy for those who value the craft, even if AI brings abundance.
