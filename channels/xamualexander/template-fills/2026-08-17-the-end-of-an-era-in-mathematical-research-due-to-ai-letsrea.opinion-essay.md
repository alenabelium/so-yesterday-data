## Thesis

AI will end artisanal mathematics, shifting professional mathematicians from proof creation to curation and understanding, while human enjoyment of the craft can persist.

## Argument

The article's core claim is that AI will end artisanal mathematics, and I agree. The mechanism is already visible: AI models can now propose definitions, find examples, and construct original proofs, making proof creation abundant. When proof becomes abundant, understanding becomes scarce. Mathematicians will shift to deciding which questions matter, distinguishing deep ideas, and explaining results—roles that are already a large part of the job. The chess analogy is instructive: computers surpass humans, yet chess thrives because people enjoy understanding and sharing the game. Similarly, AI can discover theorems, but humans can still marvel at proofs and find elegant explanations.

However, the article's claim that teaching will remain human is baseless. The author provides no evidence that AI cannot fulfill the emotional and social roles of teaching, especially since people already use AI as therapists and friends. The Industrial Revolution analogy is apt but incomplete, as commenters note the real human suffering and the unprecedented nature of AI as an agent, not just a tool. The commenter Jean rightly points out that historical analogies fail because we've never had a tool that can think universally. The loss of artisanal mathematics is a personal tragedy for many, but I advise mathematicians to adapt by streaming their work to prove authenticity, as I do. The future may bring more mathematics and a profound loss simultaneously, but that's not a contradiction—we can have both abundance and enjoyment.
