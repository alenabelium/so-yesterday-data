## Thesis

AI will end artisanal mathematics, shifting mathematicians from proof creation to curation, but the claim that teaching will remain human is unsupported.

## Argument

The article's core claim is that AI will end artisanal mathematics, where proofs are handcrafted over months or years. The host agrees that AI will make proof abundant, making understanding scarce, and that mathematicians will shift to curating, verifying, and explaining AI-generated results. This is already largely the case, as proving theorems is only a small part of a mathematician's job. The chess analogy is apt: AI surpassed humans, yet chess thrives because humans enjoy the game and the explanations. Similarly, mathematics can persist as a human activity even if AI is superior at discovery. The host critiques the article's assertion that teaching will remain human, finding it baseless—AI can motivate, personalize, and explain, and there's no evidence that human connection is irreplaceable. The Industrial Revolution analogy is also examined, with the host noting that while it caused suffering, it also brought benefits, and AI's impact may be unprecedented as an agent, not just a tool. The host advises mathematicians to adapt by streaming their work to prove authenticity, as he does, and to continue doing math for love, like chess players.

## Counterpoints

- Teaching will remain human because of emotional and social bonds, but the host finds no evidence for this claim.
- The chess analogy is flawed because chess is a game about who's best, while mathematics is a science with social implications.
- AI is unprecedented as an agent, not just a tool, so historical analogies like the Industrial Revolution are incomplete.
- The loss of artisanal mathematics is a personal tragedy for those who love the craft, but the host sees it as premature hyperbole.
- AI-generated proofs may be correct, but they still need careful checking, and human mathematicians can spot mistakes.
