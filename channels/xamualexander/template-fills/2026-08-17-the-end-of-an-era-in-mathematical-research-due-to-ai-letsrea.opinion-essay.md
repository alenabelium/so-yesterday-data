## Thesis

AI will end artisanal mathematics, shifting professional mathematicians from proving theorems to curating and understanding AI-generated results, while human enjoyment of the craft can persist.

## Argument

The article's core claim is that AI will fundamentally change mathematical research, moving it from a craft of proof creation to one of curation and understanding. The host agrees with this shift, noting that for many mathematicians, this is already the case: "actually proving theorems is only one part of a mathematician's job." The host critiques the article's assertion that teaching will remain human, finding it unsupported: "I see no reason why AI can't fulfill many of these roles." The chess analogy is explored, with the host noting that while computers surpass humans, the community thrives because people enjoy the game and the human explanations of moves. The Industrial Revolution analogy is also examined, with a commenter highlighting the human suffering it caused, a point the host appreciates for its modesty. The host also addresses the concern that AI will make traditional mathematics a "productive suicide," advising mathematicians to stream their work as proof of authenticity. Ultimately, the host concludes that while professional roles may change, human enjoyment of mathematics can persist, and the revolution is exciting to witness.
