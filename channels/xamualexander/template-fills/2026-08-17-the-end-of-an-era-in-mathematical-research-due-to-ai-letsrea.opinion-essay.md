## Thesis

AI will end artisanal mathematics, shifting mathematicians from proof creation to curation and understanding, but human enjoyment of mathematics can persist.

## Argument

The article's core claim is that AI will fundamentally change mathematical research, moving from a model where a person thinks for months to produce a proof to one where AI generates proofs and humans curate and understand them. The host agrees with this shift, noting that for many mathematicians, this is already the case. The host critiques the article's assertion that teaching will remain human, finding it unsupported and noting that AI can fulfill many of the emotional and social roles of a teacher. The host also discusses the chess analogy, arguing that while AI surpasses humans in chess, the community thrives because people enjoy the game and the human element. The host extends this to mathematics, suggesting that AI can make mathematics more popular with the public, even if it doesn't make it more profitable for professionals. The host advises mathematicians to adapt by streaming their work to prove authenticity, and concludes that while professional roles may change, the joy of doing mathematics can persist.

## Counterpoints

- Teaching will remain human because of emotional and social components like trust and admiration.
- The chess analogy is flawed because mathematics is a science with social and technical implications, not a game about who's best.
- AI is unprecedented as an agent, not just a tool, so historical analogies like the Industrial Revolution are incomplete.
- The loss of artisanal mathematics is a personal tragedy for those who value the craft.
- The claim that AI can't do teaching is baseless; AI can fulfill many of these roles.
