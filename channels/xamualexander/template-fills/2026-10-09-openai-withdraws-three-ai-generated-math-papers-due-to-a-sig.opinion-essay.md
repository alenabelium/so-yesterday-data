## Thesis

OpenAI's withdrawal of three AI-generated math papers due to a single sign error is a net positive for mathematics, not a sign of AI unreliability.

## Argument

The incident is being blown out of proportion. The withdrawal rate is less than 1% of nearly 400 papers, and it wasn't three separate errors—it was one sign error that two other papers depended on. This is the kind of mistake kids make in algebra, yet it's being used to argue AI math is fundamentally flawed. But human mathematics is full of errors too; we've seen human papers with flaws that AI later corrected, like the elliptic curve with rank 31 that contradicted a human paper. The real story is the shift in the burden of proof: with Lean formal verification, the hard part is no longer the proof itself but translating the theorem from English into Lean. That's a new challenge, but it's not a reason to reject the work. The community is already building on these results, like the grassroots effort improving the integer multiplication bound. The skeptics who say 'I told you so' are missing the bigger picture: AI is accelerating mathematical discovery, and the errors will be found and fixed faster than ever before. The future is not doom and gloom; it's a resounding victory for mathematics.

## Counterpoints

- The error was caught by a human, not the AI, suggesting AI can't self-correct.
- The withdrawal rate might be higher once more papers are scrutinized.
- The lack of Lean verification for withdrawn papers raises questions about unverified results.
- The volume of AI-generated proofs makes human verification impractical.
- The release feels like a PR stunt, offloading verification work onto the community.
