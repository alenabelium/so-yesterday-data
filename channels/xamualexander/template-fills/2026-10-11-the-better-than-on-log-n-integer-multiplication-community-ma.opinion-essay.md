## Thesis

The grassroots, version-controlled improvement of integer multiplication algorithms on the Beyond En-Log-N website reveals that mathematical progress is becoming contingent on human choices, challenging the Platonic ideal of mathematical.

## Argument

The mechanism is the community's rapid, collaborative refinement of the exponent kappa. Starting from OpenAI's initial kappa of 2^-182 on October 6th, the community improved it within days to 1.48 * 2^-11, with each step building on the previous via pull requests and shared terminology. This process, which the host celebrates as 'mathematics done properly with Git,' is inherently human-dependent: the sequence of kappas is shaped by what individuals choose to upload, making the limit kappa_infinity a 'physical number' rather than a Platonic ideal. The host then constructs a meta-algorithm that could achieve kappa_infinity using an oracle, but notes it is not a real algorithm because it requires predicting human behavior. To restore a purely mathematical question, he fixes a specific AI (e.g., GPT-6 Astro) and a specific version of Lean (4.34.1), enumerating all kappas provable by that combination. This yields a theorem: if Lean 4.34.1 is bug-free, then integer multiplication can be done in O(n * log^(1 - kappa_infinity_prime) n) time, where kappa_infinity_prime is the supremum of that enumeration. However, this is a 'galactic algorithm'—impractical for any real computation—but it illustrates how fixing the AI and proof checker anchors the result in the Platonic realm.

## Counterpoints

- The limit kappa_infinity is not a mathematical number because it depends on unpredictable human uploads to the website.
- The meta-algorithm using an oracle is not a real algorithm; it cannot run without predicting human behavior.
- The purely mathematical variation requires fixing a specific AI and Lean version, and assumes Lean 4.34.1 has no bugs.
- The resulting theorem is totally impractical, as it would only yield improvements for fantastically huge numbers.
