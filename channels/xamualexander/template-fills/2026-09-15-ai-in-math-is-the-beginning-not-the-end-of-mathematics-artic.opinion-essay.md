## Thesis

AI in mathematics is the beginning, not the end, of the field, and human mathematicians will become more essential than ever for understanding and meaning.

## Argument

The production of mathematical text is becoming disconnected from human understanding, as AI systems can now solve major open problems without anyone knowing what the problems say. This forces a radical reinvention of the profession, not just a tweak. The goal of mathematics is not to prove theorems—that's trivially automatable—but to produce and understand high-quality mathematics and to produce high-quality mathematicians. We must distinguish mathematical progress from mathematical expertise. The PhD should be reconceptualized: not a thesis but a rigorous defense demonstrating deep understanding and the ability to convey it. Talks and sustained discussion will replace papers as the primary signals of expertise. We must reward the parts of the job that cannot be automated: understanding, asking interesting questions, and building community. The recent Caltech math-a-thon scandal shows that trying to stop people from using AI is futile and counterproductive. There is more interest in math than ever before, and we should be ecstatic. The abundance of new mathematics will only have value if we build a community to engage with it. This is an exciting, wonderful explosion of mathematics, and we are at the beginning, not the end.

## Counterpoints

- Some mathematicians believe AI is the end of mathematics, but this essay argues it's the beginning.
- Some want to preserve the precise shape of academic institutions (journals, peer review, gatekeeping), but the host says throw them out.
- Some hope AI capabilities will disappear (labs nationalized, bubble pops), but the host says that's not a viable plan.
- Some worry about subjectivity in rewarding research programs, but the host acknowledges this risk.
- Some mathematicians are anti-AI and want to avoid its outputs, but the host predicts that segment will eventually die out.
