## Thesis

AI's disruption of mathematics is an opportunity for the field to evolve and thrive, not a death knell.

## Argument

The disruption is real: AI can now generate results that a year ago would have been respectable publications, and this has sent many mathematicians into a panic. But this narrow view of mathematics as problem-solving misses the point. Mathematics is a culture of rigorous reasoning and communication, a capacity wired into our DNA. The real challenge is to pursue mathematical understanding in the age of AI, and Avigad offers three ways: solve harder problems, think bigger thoughts, and try new things. Harder problems are those that AI can't just cobble together from existing techniques, and they may have more interesting solutions that invoke ideas and insights. Thinking bigger thoughts means recognizing that we choose the problems and grade the solutions; we decide what's interesting. Riemann's revolutionary lecture on geometry, which Einstein later used, is a prime example of a development that no reinforcement learning setup could have evaluated. Finally, trying new things means embracing proof assistants and neural networks as legitimate tools for mathematical discovery, not dismissing them as mere technicians. This is not without precedent: algebraic methods, infinitesimals, and set-theoretic abstraction were all once alien and disconcerting. The message to the next generation is clear: we stand with you, mathematics is as important as ever, and the next few years will be extremely interesting. We are at a new frontier, and it's up to us to shape what comes next.

## Counterpoints

- AI-generated solutions to open problems are all similar, cobbling together available techniques; they may miss creativity and higher-level strategizing.
- Some argue that as the job market contracts, we should turn inward to preserve traditional mathematical skills; Avigad disagrees, saying engaging with new technologies will keep the discipline strong.
- The value of mathematics in wider society is purely utilitarian, and AI may surpass humans in those pursuits, threatening job prospects.
- The current challenge for humans is creating oracles for AI to measure against, as AI needs a reality to check its outputs.
