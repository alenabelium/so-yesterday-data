## Thesis

AI will not kill mathematics; it will force the field to evolve by solving harder problems, thinking bigger thoughts, and trying new things, and mathematicians who embrace this will thrive.

## Argument

The disruption is real: AI can now generate results that a year ago would have been respectable publications, upending the narrow view of mathematics as solving problems and varying parameters. But this narrow view misses the point. Mathematics is a culture of rigorous reasoning and communication, a capacity wired into our DNA. The question isn't whether we need mathematics, but how to pursue understanding in the age of AI.

Jeremy Avigad offers three answers. First, solve harder problems. Foundation models have seen the entire literature and are tireless, so they can cobble together known techniques to solve open problems. But that's not the same as insight or creativity. There are still hard questions and ambitious research programs, and AI can help.

Second, think bigger thoughts. We choose the problems, grade the solutions, and decide what's interesting. Riemann's lecture on the foundations of geometry, which revolutionized the field and later enabled general relativity, came out of nowhere. No reinforcement learning setup could have evaluated Riemann's decisions; the benefits were diffuse and the time horizon too long. AI might spin out theorems interesting to it, but we care about what's interesting to us. Mathematics is our story to tell.

## Counterpoints

- AI-generated solutions to open problems are just cobbling together available techniques, not real insight.
- The job market is contracting, so some argue we should turn inward to preserve traditional mathematical skills.
- AIs have patience and compute we can only dream of, but they still need a reality to measure against.
- Society values mathematicians for utilitarian reasons, and AI may surpass humans in those pursuits.
- The value of mathematics is tied to producing new interesting proofs, but wider society values mathematical reasoning beyond research.
