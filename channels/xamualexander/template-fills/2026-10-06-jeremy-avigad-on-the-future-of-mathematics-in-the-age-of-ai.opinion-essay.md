## Thesis

AI's disruption of mathematics is an opportunity for the field to evolve and thrive, not a death knell, and mathematicians should embrace it.

## Argument

The disruption is real: AI can now generate results that a year ago would have been respectable publications, and this has upended traditional workflows. But this narrow view of mathematics as problem-solving misses the point. Mathematics is a culture of rigorous reasoning and communication, a capacity wired into our DNA. The challenge is to pursue mathematical understanding in the age of AI, and Avigad offers three ways: solve harder problems, think bigger thoughts, and try new things. AI can cobble together existing techniques, but it can't yet produce the insights of a Riemann or a Grothendieck, whose work opened new vistas. The values we assign to mathematics are embedded in our history and culture; AI can't think for us. We should view those who develop proof assistants and neural networks as doing mathematics proper, not as technicians. Expanding our view of mathematics is the best way to keep the profession relevant. This is a new frontier, and we are blessed to be here.

## Counterpoints

- AI-generated results are just cobbled-together existing techniques, not true insight.
- The job market is contracting, so we should turn inward to preserve traditional skills.
- AI will surpass humans in all utilitarian mathematical pursuits, making math grads obsolete.
- The value of mathematics is tied to producing new interesting proofs, which AI can automate.
