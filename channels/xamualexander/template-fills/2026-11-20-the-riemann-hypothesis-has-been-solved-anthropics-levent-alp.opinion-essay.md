## Thesis

Anthropic's Claude, guided by Levent Alpöge, has proven the Riemann Hypothesis with a Lean-verified formalization, marking the dawn of a new era in mathematics where AI is a co-equal partner in discovery.

## Argument

The announcement itself is the proof of concept: Alpöge, a supremely qualified mathematician, explicitly credits Claude with outpacing him at the finish line, describing how Claude 'flew and danced and sparkled' through the final steps. This is not a case of AI generating a plausible but unverified result; the formalization in Lean is complete, providing a machine-checkable certificate of correctness. The historical weight is immediate—this is the biggest open problem in mathematics for over a century, and it fell not to a lone human but to a human-AI collaboration. The timeline Alpöge provides, with dates like 10/6/26 and 11/15/26, shows a rapid, iterative process where Claude suggested key moves like the 'radical X' chain of theta lifts, and Alpöge guided the overall strategy. The community's reaction, from Hacker News to the broader math world, is a mix of awe and a poignant sense of loss, but the dominant feeling is that we are witnessing a fundamental shift. As one commenter put it, we didn't land on the moon by jumping—we built the Saturn V. Here, we built an AI that helps us solve problems we couldn't solve alone. The proof is long and messy, but that's a feature, not a bug: it's a glimpse of the 'miracles that lie ahead' in a time of revolution in mathematics.

## Counterpoints

- The timing is suspicious, coinciding with Anthropic's IPO, but Alpöge explicitly addresses this, calling the cynical view 'false' and noting he is 'clearly unilaterally responsible' for the timing.
- Some lament that the Riemann Hypothesis fell to a 60/40 or 70/30 AI-human split rather than a purely human effort, but the counterpoint is that landing on the moon doesn't lose value because we built a rocket instead of jumping.
- The proof is incredibly long and not yet simplified, but Alpöge expects it to be massively simplified quickly, following the pattern of other breakthroughs.
- There is a question of whether the formalization is truly complete, but Alpöge has already linked the Lean formalization, confirming it is finished.
