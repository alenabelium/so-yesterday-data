## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to define species, independent of morphology. If humanity persists, the axiom implies you are either destined for extinction or to become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: For any biological species S, every organism X either has fewer than infinitely many descendants in S or fewer than infinitely many non-descendants in S. This means you are either the ancestor of almost all future humans or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A. This gives Team A an unfair advantage, so over time it will dominate unless it dies out—exactly what the IAP axiom predicts.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports the Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with constant size n, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for generation k + 1.77 log2(n). For n=8 billion, that's about 33 generations, making it almost certain you are a common ancestor or non-ancestor for some future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued species should never be permanently split. If George Washington violated the IAP axiom, the human species would be forever split into his descendants and non-descendants. Thus, Hennig's principle implies the IAP axiom must hold. A reviewer noted the IAP axiom is a perfect formalization of Hennig's ambiguous idea.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently, but both definitions produce convex sets satisfying the IAP axiom, then their species are almost identical when considering generator sets. This shows species are more objective than previously thought, independent of morphology or genetics.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and Willy Hennig's cladistic principle. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, but if you disagree, you can restrict the axiom to biparental species. The speaker emphasizes the IAP axiom is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven, similar to the parallel postulate in geometry. He acknowledges the simplifying assumption that the biosphere never dies out, comparing it to a spherical cow. The objective species theorem requires convexity and the IAP axiom, with convexity being nearly uncontroversial.
