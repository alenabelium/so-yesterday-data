## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom is a mathematical property that distinguishes biological species. It states that for any organism, either its descendants eventually go extinct or it becomes the ancestor of almost all future members of its species. This axiom, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, suggests that unless humanity faces extinction, each of us is either the ancestor of nearly all future humans or of almost none.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than an infinite number of descendants in S, or fewer than an infinite number of non-descendants in S. This means each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A member mates with a Team B member, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports the Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or a common non-ancestor for a future generation. For a population of 8 billion, this occurs within about 33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations, supporting the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Addressing the Equator Wall Objection
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:38']
- **Claim**: Critics suggest building a wall along the equator to isolate hemispheres, disproving the IAP axiom. However, if isolated forever, the two groups would evolve differently and eventually become different species, so they couldn't both remain human, undermining the objection.
- **Role**: counter

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: The objective species theorem states that if two biologists define species differently but both definitions are convex and satisfy the IAP axiom, their species are almost identical. This shows that species have an objective, genealogical basis, independent of morphological or genetic definitions.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The IAP axiom is an axiom, not a theorem, so it cannot be proven. The speaker acknowledges the simplifying assumption that the biosphere never dies out, similar to physicists' spherical cow. Arguments for plausibility include the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and cladistic principles. The speaker also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, supporting the axiom's applicability. However, the speaker hedges that humanity may not perfectly match Chang's population model, and the axiom's validity depends on the species persisting indefinitely.
