## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical basis for distinguishing species. It implies that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S, or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, aligning with the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for a future generation, with the probability increasing without limit. For humans (n=8 billion), this threshold is about 33 generations, making it almost certain you are a common ancestor or non-ancestor for some future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Cladistics and Hennig's Principle
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species should never be permanently split. If someone violates the IAP axiom, the species is forever divided into two infinite groups, contradicting Hennig's principle. Thus, the IAP axiom is a formalization of Hennig's idea, making it plausible.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently, but both definitions satisfy the IAP axiom and convexity, their species are almost identical. This shows species are more objective than previously thought, as the theorem eliminates partial overlaps, leaving only complete separation or near-identity.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and cladistic principles from Willy Hennig. He also notes that the axiom is not proven but is a plausible assumption, similar to physicists' spherical cow. Caveats include the assumption that the biosphere never completely dies out, and that the axiom applies to biparental species; monoparental species are addressed via the Knight-Darwin law, which suggests biparental reproduction occurs at least rarely. The speaker acknowledges that humanity may not fit Chang's population model exactly, but considers it strong evidence.
