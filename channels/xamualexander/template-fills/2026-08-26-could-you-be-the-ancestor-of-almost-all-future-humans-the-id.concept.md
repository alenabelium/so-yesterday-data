## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that for any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. This mathematical principle, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to distinguish species. It suggests that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means each organism either dominates the future of its species or its lineage goes extinct.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, supporting the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling and Cladistic Support
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with constant population size n, each individual is likely a common ancestor or non-ancestor for generations about 1.77 log2(n) ahead. For n=8 billion, that's about 33 generations. Additionally, Willy Hennig's principle that species never split permanently is formalized by the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Addressing the Equator Wall Objection
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:38']
- **Claim**: Critics suggest building a wall along the equator to permanently isolate hemispheres, disproving the IAP axiom. However, if isolated forever, the two groups would evolve into different species, so they couldn't both remain human. Thus, the objection fails.
- **Role**: counter

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: The objective species theorem states that if two biologists define species differently, but both definitions produce convex sets satisfying the IAP axiom, their species are almost identical. This shows species have an objective, genealogical basis independent of morphology.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and cladistic principles from Willy Hennig. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, but if you disagree, you can restrict the axiom to biparental species. The speaker emphasizes that the IAP axiom is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven, similar to the parallel postulate in geometry. He also acknowledges that humanity may not fit Chang's population model exactly, but it provides strong evidence.
