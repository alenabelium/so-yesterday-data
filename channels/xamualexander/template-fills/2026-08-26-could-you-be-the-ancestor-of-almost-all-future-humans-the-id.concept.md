## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. This mathematical principle, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to distinguish species without relying on morphology. It suggests that unless humanity goes extinct, each of us is either the ancestor of nearly all future humans or of almost none.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinitely many descendants in S, or fewer than infinitely many non-descendants in S. This implies that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of almost all future humans.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A dominates unless it dies out, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports the Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or a common non-ancestor for a future generation, roughly 33 generations ahead for a population of 8 billion. This probability increases with more generations, providing strong evidence for the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species should never be permanently split. If George Washington violated the IAP axiom, the human species would be forever divided into his descendants and non-descendants, contradicting Hennig's principle. Thus, the IAP axiom is a formalization of Hennig's idea, making it more plausible.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions satisfy convexity and the IAP axiom, their species sets are almost identical. This theorem shows that species are more objective than previously thought, as competing definitions converge when they meet these conditions.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and Willy Hennig's principle. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, but if you disagree, you can restrict the axiom to biparental species. The speaker acknowledges the axiom is not proven, comparing it to the axiom of parallelism in Euclidean geometry. He also notes that the IAP axiom is a simplification, assuming the biosphere never dies out, similar to physicists' spherical cow assumption. The objective species theorem relies on convexity and the IAP axiom, and non-generators are ignored as 'higher-order sterile' mules.
