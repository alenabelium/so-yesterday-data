## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom is a mathematical property that distinguishes biological species. It states that for any organism in a species, either its descendants will eventually go extinct, or it will become the ancestor of almost all future members of that species. This axiom, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, is argued to be plausible through three distinct arguments and leads to the objective species theorem.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has less than an infinite number of descendants in S, or X has less than an infinite number of non-descendants in S. This means that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of almost all future humans.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports the Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or a common non-ancestor for a future generation. For a population of 8 billion, this occurs within about 33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations, providing strong evidence for the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Addressing the Objection of Deliberate Isolation
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:38']
- **Claim**: Critics might suggest building a wall along the equator to isolate hemispheres and disprove the IAP axiom. However, this fails because the two isolated groups would evolve differently over millions of years, eventually becoming different species, so they couldn't both remain human forever.
- **Role**: counter

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: The objective species theorem states that if two biologists define species differently, but both definitions produce convex sets satisfying the IAP axiom, then their species are almost identical. This shows that species are more objective than previously thought, based purely on genealogy.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling, and cladistic principles. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no species is completely monoparental, which supports the axiom's applicability. However, the speaker emphasizes that the IAP is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven. He acknowledges that the population model may not perfectly represent humans, and that the axiom assumes the biosphere never completely dies out.
