## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that for any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to distinguish species. It implies that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S, or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means each organism either dominates the future of its species or its lineage goes extinct.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, exactly as the IAP axiom predicts.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with constant population size n, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for generation k + 1.77 log2(n). For n=8 billion, this is about 33 generations, making it almost certain you are a common ancestor or non-ancestor for some future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued species should never be permanently split. If George Washington violated IAP, the human species would be forever split into descendants and non-descendants, contradicting Hennig's principle. A reviewer noted that IAP is the perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's informal idea.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently, but both definitions produce convex sets satisfying IAP, then their species are almost identical when considering generator sets. This shows species are more objective than previously thought, independent of morphology.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for IAP's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and cladistic principles from Willy Hennig. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no species is completely monoparental, but if you disagree, you can restrict the mathematics to biparental species. The speaker emphasizes that IAP is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven, similar to the parallel postulate in geometry. He also acknowledges that the assumption of an eternal biosphere is a simplification, like a spherical cow in physics.
