## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom is a mathematical property that distinguishes biological species. It states that for any organism in a species, either its descendants will eventually go extinct, or it will become the ancestor of almost all future members of that species. This axiom, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, suggests that if humanity persists, each of us is either destined for extinction or to be the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has less than an infinite number of descendants in S, or less than an infinite number of non-descendants in S. This means that if your descendants don't go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of almost all future humans.
- **Role**: definition

### Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A member mates with a Team B member, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Evidence
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual in a constant-size population is either a common ancestor or a common non-ancestor for a future generation. For humans (n=8 billion), this is about 33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations.
- **Role**: evidence

### Cladistic Principle Formalization
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species should never be permanently split. If an organism violates the IAP axiom, the species is forever split into two infinite groups. Thus, the IAP axiom is a perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's principle, making it plausible.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: The objective species theorem shows that if two biologists define species differently, but both definitions satisfy the IAP axiom and convexity, their species are almost identical. This reveals an objective, genealogical basis for species, independent of morphology.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and the authority of cladistics founder Willy Hennig. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no species is completely monoparental, which supports the axiom's applicability. However, the speaker emphasizes that the IAP is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven. He also acknowledges that the axiom assumes the biosphere never completely dies out, a simplifying assumption similar to a spherical cow. The population model may not perfectly represent humans, and the axiom may not apply to monoparental species.
