## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work, offers a purely genealogical way to distinguish species. If your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S, or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means each organism either dominates the future of its species or its lineage goes extinct.
- **Role**: definition

### Team Argument for Unfair Advantage
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A dominates unless it dies out, matching the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling and Hennig's Authority
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is a common ancestor or non-ancestor for a future generation. Willy Hennig's principle that species never split permanently is formalized by the IAP axiom, as a violation would create an infinite permanent split.
- **Role**: evidence

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions are convex and satisfy the IAP axiom, their species are almost identical. This shows species are more objective than previously thought, relying only on genealogy, not morphology.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the team argument, population modeling, and Hennig's principle. He notes the axiom is not proven but is a plausible assumption, similar to physicists' spherical cow. The objective species theorem requires convexity and the IAP axiom; convexity is nearly undeniable, but the IAP axiom is argued for, not proven. The speaker also addresses monoparental species, citing Darwin's Knight-Darwin law, but allows replacing 'species' with 'biparental species' if one disagrees.
