## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists forever. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to define species. It suggests that unless humanity goes extinct, each of us is either the ancestor of nearly all future humans or of almost none.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means each organism either dominates the future of its species or its lineage goes extinct.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, supporting the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Confirms the Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for a future generation about 33 generations ahead (for n=8 billion). This probability increases with more generations, providing strong evidence for the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species should never be permanently split. If George Washington violated the IAP axiom, the human species would be forever divided into his descendants and non-descendants. Thus, the IAP axiom is a perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's principle, making it plausible.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently, but both definitions produce convex sets satisfying the IAP axiom, then their species are almost identical. This theorem shows that species are more objective than previously thought, based purely on genealogy.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and the authority of Willy Hennig. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, but if you disagree, you can restrict the axiom to biparental species. The speaker acknowledges the axiom is not proven, only plausible, and assumes the biosphere never completely dies out. He also addresses a counterargument about building a wall to isolate hemispheres, but argues that such isolation would eventually lead to speciation, not a permanent split.
