## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. This mathematical principle, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to distinguish species. It suggests that unless humanity goes extinct, each of us will either become the ancestor of nearly all future humans or of almost none.

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S, or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of almost all future humans, because there will be a future year when all living people share the same set of ancestors from 2026.

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and Willy Hennig's cladistic principle. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, but if you disagree, you can restrict the axiom to biparental species. The speaker acknowledges the axiom is not proven, comparing it to the axiom of parallelism in Euclidean geometry. He also addresses a counterexample involving a wall at the equator, arguing that such isolation would lead to speciation, preventing a permanent split. The objective species theorem requires convexity and the IAP axiom, with convexity being 'practically a given'.
