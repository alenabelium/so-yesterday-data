## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom is a mathematical property that distinguishes biological species. It states that for any organism in a species, either its descendants eventually go extinct, or it becomes the ancestor of almost all future members of that species. This axiom, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang, suggests that unless humanity faces extinction, each of us is either an ancestor of nearly all future humans or of almost none.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinitely many descendants in S, or fewer than infinitely many non-descendants in S. This implies that if humanity persists, each person is either an ancestor of almost all future humans or of almost none.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A member mates with a Team B member, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or a common non-ancestor for a future generation, with the probability increasing over time. For a population of 8 billion, this threshold is about 33 generations, providing strong evidence for the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Addressing the Equator Wall Objection
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:38']
- **Claim**: Critics might propose building a wall along the equator to isolate hemispheres, disproving IAP. However, such isolation would lead to divergent evolution, eventually creating separate species, so the two groups could not both remain human forever. This counters the objection and reinforces the axiom's applicability.
- **Role**: counter

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions are convex and satisfy the IAP axiom, their species are almost identical in terms of generator sets. This theorem shows that species are more objective than previously thought, as competing definitions converge to the same genealogical clusters.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The IAP axiom is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven, similar to the parallel postulate in geometry. The speaker acknowledges the assumption that the biosphere never completely dies out, comparing it to the spherical cow assumption in physics. The axiom is supported by three arguments: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and cladistic principles. The objective species theorem further shows that different species definitions converge when they satisfy IAP and convexity, with non-generators being 'higher-order sterile' and thus ignorable. The speaker also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, but this is not essential to the argument.
