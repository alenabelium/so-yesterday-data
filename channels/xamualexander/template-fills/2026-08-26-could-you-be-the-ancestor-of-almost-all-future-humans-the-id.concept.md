## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work, offers a purely genealogical way to define species. It suggests that if humanity persists, you are either extinct or the ancestor of nearly everyone.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: For any species S, each organism X either has finitely many descendants in S or finitely many non-descendants in S. Applied to humans, this means you either have a finite number of future descendants or a finite number of future non-descendants, leading to the conclusion that you are either the ancestor of almost all future humans or of almost none.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A member mates with a Team B member, the child is always in Team A because having one descendant parent suffices. This gives Team A an unfair advantage, so over time Team A either dominates or goes extinct, supporting the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling and Probability
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for a future generation. For a population of 8 billion, this occurs within about 33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations, providing strong evidence for the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle as Formalization
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species never split permanently. If an organism violates the IAP axiom, the species is permanently split into descendants and non-descendants, contradicting Hennig's principle. A reviewer noted that the IAP axiom is a perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's ambiguous idea, strengthening the axiom's theoretical foundation.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently, but both definitions satisfy convexity and the IAP axiom, then their species are almost identical in terms of generator sets. This shows that species are more objective than previously thought, as competing definitions converge to the same genealogical clusters.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and the authority of Willy Hennig. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, and even if they do, the mathematics applies to biparental species. Caveats include the simplifying assumption that the biosphere never dies out, and that the IAP axiom is not proven but argued for as plausible. The objective species theorem requires convexity and the IAP axiom, and non-generators are ignored as 'higher-order sterile'.
