## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical basis for distinguishing species. It implies that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinitely many descendants in S, or fewer than infinitely many non-descendants in S. This implies a future 'common ancestor point' where all living members share the same ancestors from a given time, making each organism either an ancestor of almost all or almost none of the future species.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage Argument
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When Team A and Team B interbreed, the offspring always belongs to Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it goes extinct, aligning with the IAP axiom's prediction.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Evidence
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with constant population size n, each individual is likely a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for a generation about 1.77 log2(n) generations later. For n=8 billion, this is about 33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations, supporting the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species should never be permanently split. If an organism violates the IAP axiom, the species is forever divided into two infinite groups, contradicting Hennig's principle. A reviewer noted that the IAP axiom is a perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's informal idea, strengthening the case for the axiom.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: The objective species theorem states that if two biologists define species differently, but both definitions produce convex sets satisfying the IAP axiom, then their species are almost identical in terms of generator sets. This shows that species have an objective, genealogical basis independent of morphology or competing definitions.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and the authority of Willy Hennig's cladistic principle. He also cites Darwin's Knight-Darwin law to argue that no species is truly monoparental, extending the axiom's applicability. However, he emphasizes that the IAP is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven. He acknowledges that humans may not fit Chang's population model exactly, and that the axiom assumes the biosphere persists indefinitely, a simplifying assumption akin to a physicist's spherical cow. The speaker also notes that non-generators, like mules, are ignored in the objective species theorem, as they are 'higher-order sterile'.
