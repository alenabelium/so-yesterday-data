## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom proposes that for any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work, offers a purely genealogical basis for distinguishing species. It suggests that if humanity persists, you are either extinct or the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: For any organism X in species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S, or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means you are either the ancestor of almost all future humans or almost none, assuming the species persists.
- **Role**: definition

### Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A dominates unless it dies out, matching the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is a common ancestor or non-ancestor for a future generation. For n=8 billion, this happens within ~33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations, supporting the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued species never split permanently. If someone violates IAP, the species splits into two infinite groups, contradicting Hennig. A reviewer noted that IAP is a perfect formalization of Hennig's principle, making the axiom theoretically compelling.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions are convex and satisfy IAP, their species are almost identical. This shows species are objective, not subjective, despite definitional disagreements.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and Willy Hennig's principle. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species, but if you disagree, you can restrict the axiom to biparental species. Caveats: the axiom assumes the biosphere never dies out (a simplifying assumption like a spherical cow), and it is an axiom, not a theorem, so it cannot be proven. The speaker acknowledges that humanity may not fit Chang's population model exactly.
