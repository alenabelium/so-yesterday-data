## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical basis for distinguishing species. If your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S, or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This implies a future year when all living humans share the same ancestors from 2026, making you either the ancestor of almost all future humans or almost none.
- **Role**: definition

### Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A dominates unless it dies out, matching the IAP axiom's prediction.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with constant size n, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for generation k + 1.77 log2(n). For n=8 billion, this is about 33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations, making it almost certain you are a common ancestor or non-ancestor for some future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued species never split permanently. If George Washington violated IAP, the human species would be permanently split into descendants and non-descendants, contradicting Hennig. A reviewer noted that IAP is the perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's principle, making the axiom more plausible.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions are convex and satisfy IAP, their species are almost identical. This theorem shows species are more objective than thought, relying only on genealogy, not morphology. Non-generators are like mules—ignored because their descendants go extinct.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for IAP's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and Hennig's principle. He also addresses objections, such as building a wall to isolate hemispheres, but notes that such isolation would lead to speciation. The speaker acknowledges IAP is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven. He also discusses monoparental species, citing Darwin's Knight-Darwin law that no species is truly monoparental, but suggests replacing 'species' with 'biparental species' if you disagree. The objective species theorem requires convexity and IAP, with convexity being practically undeniable.
