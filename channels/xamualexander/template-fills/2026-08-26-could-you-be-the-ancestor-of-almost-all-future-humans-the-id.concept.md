## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that for any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to distinguish species. It implies that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: For any species S, each organism X either has fewer than infinite descendants in S or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means you are either the ancestor of almost all future humans or almost none, assuming the species persists.
- **Role**: definition

### Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A dominates unless it dies out, matching the IAP axiom's prediction.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for a future generation. For a population of 8 billion, this threshold is about 33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations, strongly supporting the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species never split permanently. If someone violates the IAP axiom, the species is forever split into descendants and non-descendants, contradicting Hennig's principle. A reviewer noted that the IAP axiom is a perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's ambiguous idea.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions are convex and satisfy the IAP axiom, their species are almost identical. This shows species are more objective than thought, as competing definitions converge on the same genealogical clusters.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and Willy Hennig's cladistic principle. He also notes the Knight-Darwin law, suggesting no species is truly monoparental, but allows replacing 'species' with 'biparental species' if one disagrees. Caveats include the assumption that the biosphere never dies out (like a spherical cow) and that humanity fits Chang's population model, which is not guaranteed. The IAP axiom is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven, but the biological arguments are convincing.
