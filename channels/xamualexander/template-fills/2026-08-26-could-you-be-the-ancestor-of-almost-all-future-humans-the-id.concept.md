## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom is a mathematical property that distinguishes biological species. It states that for any organism in a species, either it is the ancestor of almost all future members, or of almost none. This axiom, combined with convexity, leads to the objective species theorem, showing that different species definitions converge.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has less than an infinite number of descendants in S, or less than an infinite number of non-descendants in S. This implies that if the human species satisfies the axiom, then you are either the ancestor of almost all future humans or of almost none.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A member mates with a Team B member, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate or go extinct, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or a common non-ancestor for a future generation. For a population of 8 billion, this is about 33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations, providing strong evidence for the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Addressing the Equator Wall Objection
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:38']
- **Claim**: Critics suggest building a wall along the equator to isolate hemispheres, disproving the IAP axiom. However, this fails because isolated populations would evolve differently over millions of years, eventually becoming different species, so they couldn't both remain human forever.
- **Role**: counter

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: The objective species theorem states that if two biologists define species differently, but both definitions produce convex sets satisfying the IAP axiom, then their species are almost identical. This shows that species are more objective than previously thought, despite differing definitions.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The IAP axiom is an axiom, not a theorem, so it cannot be proven, similar to the parallel postulate in Euclidean geometry. The speaker presents three arguments for its plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and cladistic principles. The objective species theorem requires convexity and the IAP axiom; convexity is practically undeniable, and the IAP axiom is argued to be plausible. The speaker also notes that the assumption of an immortal biosphere is a simplification, like a spherical cow, and that the Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist.
