## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that for any biological species, every organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to define species. It implies that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than an infinite number of descendants in S, or fewer than an infinite number of non-descendants in S. This means each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or almost none.
- **Role**: definition

### Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate or go extinct, supporting the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Evidence
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for a future generation, with the probability increasing without limit. For humans (n=8 billion), this threshold is about 33 generations, making it almost certain you are a common ancestor or non-ancestor for some future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Counterargument: The Equator Wall
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:38']
- **Claim**: Critics suggest building a wall along the equator to isolate hemispheres, disproving the IAP axiom. However, this fails because isolated populations would evolve differently over millions of years, eventually becoming different species, so they cannot both remain human forever.
- **Role**: counter

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions are convex and satisfy the IAP axiom, their species are almost identical. This shows species are more objective than previously thought, based purely on genealogy, not morphology.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The IAP axiom is supported by three arguments: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and cladistic principles. The speaker notes this is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven, similar to the parallel postulate in geometry. The speaker also addresses monoparental species, citing the Knight-Darwin law that no species is truly monoparental, but suggests replacing 'species' with 'biparental species' if one disagrees. The objective species theorem requires convexity and the IAP axiom, with convexity being practically undeniable, paraphrasing Dollo's law of irreversibility.
