## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that for any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to distinguish species. It implies that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S, or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means each organism either dominates the future of its species or its lineage dies out.
- **Role**: definition

### Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, supporting the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Evidence
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual in generation k is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for generation k+1.77*log2(n). For humans (n=8 billion), this is about 33 generations, making it almost certain you are a common ancestor or non-ancestor for some future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Cladistic Principle of No Permanent Split
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig, father of cladistics, argued species should never be separated forever. If George Washington violated the IAP axiom, the human species would be permanently split into his descendants and non-descendants, contradicting Hennig's principle. Thus, the IAP axiom formalizes Hennig's idea.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: The objective species theorem states that if two biologists define species differently, but both definitions produce convex sets satisfying the IAP axiom, then their species are almost identical. This shows species are more objective than previously thought, based purely on genealogy.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and cladistic principles from Willy Hennig. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, but if you disagree, you can restrict the axiom to biparental species. The speaker emphasizes that the IAP axiom is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven, similar to the parallel postulate in geometry. He also acknowledges that the assumption of an immortal biosphere is a simplification, like a spherical cow in physics.
