## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists forever. This mathematical principle, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to define species. It suggests that unless your descendants go extinct, you will eventually be the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinitely many descendants in S, or fewer than infinitely many non-descendants in S. This implies that if your descendants don't go extinct, you will eventually be the ancestor of almost all future humans.
- **Role**: definition

### First Argument: Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, supporting the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Second Argument: Population Modeling by Joseph Chang
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or a common non-ancestor for a future generation, roughly 33 generations ahead for a population of 8 billion. This probability increases with more generations, providing strong evidence for the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Third Argument: Hennig's Principle of No Permanent Splits
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species should never be permanently split. If George Washington violated the IAP axiom, the human species would be forever split into his descendants and non-descendants, contradicting Hennig's principle. Thus, the IAP axiom formalizes Hennig's idea.
- **Role**: evidence

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently, but both definitions satisfy the IAP axiom and convexity, then their species are almost identical in terms of generator sets. This shows that species have an objective, genealogical basis independent of morphology.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, Joseph Chang's population modeling, and Willy Hennig's cladistic principle. He also notes that Charles Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist, extending the axiom's applicability. Caveats include the assumption that the biosphere never dies out (like a spherical cow), the possibility that humans may not fit Chang's model, and the acknowledgment that the IAP is an axiom, not a theorem, and cannot be proven. The objective species theorem requires convexity and the IAP axiom, but convexity is considered uncontroversial.
