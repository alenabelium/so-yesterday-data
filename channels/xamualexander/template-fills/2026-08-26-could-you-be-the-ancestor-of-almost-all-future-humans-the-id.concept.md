## TL;DR

A mathematical axiom suggesting that for any species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. It formalizes the idea that species have a genealogical, not morphological, basis. This axiom underpins the objective species theorem, showing that different species definitions converge.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinitely many descendants in S, or fewer than infinitely many non-descendants in S. This implies that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually be the ancestor of almost all future humans.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports the Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for a future generation, with the probability increasing over time. For a population of 8 billion, this threshold is about 33 generations, making it almost certain that you are either a common ancestor or non-ancestor for some future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Cladistics and the Formalization of Hennig's Principle
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species should never be permanently split. If the IAP axiom were violated, the human species would be forever divided into descendants and non-descendants of a given individual, contradicting Hennig's principle. A reviewer noted that the IAP axiom is a perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's informal idea.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently, but both definitions satisfy the IAP axiom and convexity, then their species are almost identical. This theorem shows that species are more objective than previously thought, as different definitions converge to the same genealogical clusters.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling, and cladistic principles. He also notes that the axiom is not proven but is a plausible assumption, similar to physicists' spherical cow. He addresses objections, such as the possibility of building a wall to isolate hemispheres, but argues that such isolation would eventually lead to speciation. He also discusses monoparental species, citing Darwin's Knight-Darwin law, and suggests that if you disagree, you can restrict the axiom to biparental species. The speaker emphasizes that the axiom is genealogical, not morphological, and that non-generators are like mules, which can be ignored.
