## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom proposes that for any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical basis for defining species. It suggests that unless humanity faces extinction, each of us is either the ancestor of nearly all future humans or of almost none.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than infinitely many descendants in S, or fewer than infinitely many non-descendants in S. This means each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely.
- **Role**: definition

### Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future people into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A dominates unless it dies out, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for a future generation, with the probability increasing without limit. For humans (n=8 billion), this threshold is about 33 generations, making it almost certain you are a common ancestor or non-ancestor for some future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Addressing the Equator Wall Objection
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:05:38']
- **Claim**: Critics suggest building a wall along the equator to isolate hemispheres, disproving IAP. However, if isolated forever, the two populations would evolve differently and eventually become different species, so they couldn't both remain human. Thus, the objection fails because it assumes they stay the same species.
- **Role**: counter

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions are convex and satisfy the IAP axiom, their species are almost identical in a technical sense. This shows species are more objective than previously thought, providing a genealogical basis independent of morphology.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and cladistic principles. He also notes that the axiom is not proven but is a plausible assumption, similar to physicists' spherical cow. The objective species theorem requires convexity and IAP satisfaction, with convexity being 'practically a given' and IAP 'very plausible'. The speaker acknowledges that humans may not fit Chang's population model exactly, but it is 'strong evidence'. He also mentions that monoparental species are addressed via the Knight-Darwin law, which assumes no truly monoparental species, but if one disagrees, the presentation can be adjusted.
