## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none. This mathematical property, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to define species. If your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: For any biological species S, if X is any organism of S, then either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S, or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means you are either the ancestor of almost all future humans or almost none.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A dominates unless it dies out, matching the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for a future generation. For a population of 8 billion, this happens within about 33 generations, and the probability increases with more generations.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species should never be permanently split. If George Washington violates IAP, the human species splits forever into descendants and non-descendants. The IAP axiom is a perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's principle, resolving its ambiguity.
- **Role**: synthesis

### Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions satisfy IAP and convexity, their species are almost identical in terms of generator sets. This shows species have an objective, genealogical basis independent of morphology.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling, and Hennig's principle. He notes that the axiom is not proven but is a simplifying assumption, similar to a spherical cow. He also addresses the objection of building a wall to isolate hemispheres, arguing that such isolation would lead to speciation. The speaker acknowledges that the axiom assumes biparental reproduction and cites Darwin's Knight-Darwin law that no species is truly monoparental. He also mentions that the objective species theorem requires convexity, which he considers uncontroversial.
