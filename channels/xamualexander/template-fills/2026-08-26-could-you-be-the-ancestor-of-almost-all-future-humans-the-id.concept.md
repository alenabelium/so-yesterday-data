## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom states that in any biological species, each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members or of almost none, assuming the species persists indefinitely. This mathematical principle, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, offers a purely genealogical way to distinguish species. It suggests that unless your descendants go extinct, you will eventually become the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in species S, either X has fewer than infinite descendants in S, or fewer than infinite non-descendants in S. This means each organism either dominates the future of its species or its lineage dies out.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Divide future humans into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When Team A and Team B interbreed, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, matching the IAP axiom's prediction.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports the Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual is either a common ancestor or common non-ancestor for generations about 33 generations ahead (for n=8 billion). This probability increases with more generations, making it almost certain that you are either a common ancestor or non-ancestor for some future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle as a Formalization
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig argued that species should never be permanently split. If George Washington violated the IAP axiom, the human species would be forever split into descendants and non-descendants, contradicting Hennig's principle. A reviewer noted that the IAP axiom is a perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's informal idea.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions are convex and satisfy the IAP axiom, their species are almost identical in terms of generator sets. This shows species are more objective than previously thought, independent of morphology.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and Hennig's principle. He also addresses objections, such as building a wall to isolate hemispheres, but argues that such isolation would lead to speciation. The speaker notes that the axiom is not proven but is a plausible assumption, similar to physicists' spherical cow. He also discusses monoparental species, citing Darwin's Knight-Darwin law, but allows replacing 'species' with 'biparental species' if one disagrees. The objective species theorem relies on convexity and generator sets, which are defined and justified.
