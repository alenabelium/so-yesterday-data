## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom is a mathematical property that distinguishes biological species. It states that for any organism in a species, either its descendants will eventually go extinct, or it will become the ancestor of almost all future members of that species. This axiom, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, suggests that if your descendants never die out, you will eventually be the ancestor of nearly all future humans.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has less than an infinite number of descendants in S, or less than an infinite number of non-descendants in S. This means that if your descendants never go extinct, you will eventually be the ancestor of almost all future humans.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent and a Team B parent mate, the child is always on Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports the Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with constant population size n, each individual is either a common ancestor or a common non-ancestor for a generation about 1.77*log2(n) generations later. For n=8 billion, this is about 33 generations, making it highly probable that you are either an ancestor of everyone or no one in that future generation.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig, the father of cladistics, argued that species should never be permanently split. If the IAP axiom were violated, a species would be forever divided into two infinite groups, contradicting Hennig's principle. A reviewer suggested that the IAP axiom is the perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's idea.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently, but both definitions satisfy the IAP axiom and convexity, then their species are almost identical in terms of generator sets. This shows that species are more objective than previously thought, as competing definitions converge to the same genealogical clusters.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, population modeling by Joseph Chang, and the authority of Willy Hennig. He also notes that the axiom is not proven but is a plausible assumption, similar to physicists' spherical cow. Caveats include the assumption that the biosphere never dies out, and that the axiom applies to biparental species; for monoparental species, one can replace 'species' with 'biparental species'. The speaker also mentions that the axiom is independent of morphology, relying purely on genealogy.
