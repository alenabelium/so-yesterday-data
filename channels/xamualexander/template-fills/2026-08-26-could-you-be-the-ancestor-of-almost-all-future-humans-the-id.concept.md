## TL;DR

The Identical Ancestor Point (IAP) axiom is a mathematical property that distinguishes biological species. It states that for any organism in a species, either its descendants eventually go extinct or it becomes the ancestor of almost all future members of that species. This axiom, formalized from earlier work by Joseph Chang and Willy Hennig, suggests that if humanity persists, each of us is either an ancestor of almost all future humans or of almost none.

## The argument

### Define the IAP Axiom
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:29']
- **Claim**: The IAP axiom states that for any organism X in a species S, either X has fewer than an infinite number of descendants in S, or fewer than an infinite number of non-descendants in S. This means each organism is either the ancestor of almost all future members of the species or of almost none.
- **Role**: definition

### The Unfair Advantage of Descendant Groups
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:09']
- **Claim**: Consider future humans divided into Team A (descendants of George Washington) and Team B (non-descendants). When a Team A parent mates with a Team B parent, the child is always in Team A, giving Team A an unfair advantage. Over time, Team A will dominate unless it dies out, illustrating the IAP axiom's plausibility.
- **Role**: evidence

### Population Modeling Supports IAP
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:17']
- **Claim**: Joseph Chang's population model shows that with high probability, each individual in a constant-size population is either a common ancestor or a common non-ancestor for all individuals about 33 generations later (for n=8 billion). This probability increases with more generations, providing strong evidence for the IAP axiom.
- **Role**: evidence

### Hennig's Principle Formalized
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:12:05']
- **Claim**: Willy Hennig, the father of cladistics, argued that species should never be permanently split. If an organism violates the IAP axiom, the species is forever divided into two infinite groups, contradicting Hennig's principle. Thus, the IAP axiom is a perfect mathematical formalization of Hennig's idea.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Objective Species Theorem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:19:10']
- **Claim**: If two biologists define species differently but both definitions satisfy the IAP axiom and convexity, their species are almost identical. This theorem shows that species are more objective than previously thought, as different definitions converge when they meet these conditions.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker presents three arguments for the IAP axiom's plausibility: the unfair advantage of descendant groups, Joseph Chang's population modeling, and Willy Hennig's cladistic principle. He also notes that the axiom is not proven but is a plausible assumption, similar to the spherical cow in physics. The speaker acknowledges that the axiom assumes the biosphere never completely dies out, and that it applies to biparental species, though Darwin's Knight-Darwin law suggests no truly monoparental species exist. The objective species theorem relies on convexity, which is practically a given, and the IAP axiom, which is argued to be plausible.
