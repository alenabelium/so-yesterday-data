## Thesis

The Leiden Declaration is a futile, self-serving defense by established mathematicians that will have zero impact on the commercial AI industry.

## Argument

The declaration is a classic gatekeeping maneuver. It's a committee forming a committee to discuss forming a committee, all while AI solves problems daily that would take humans decades. The values it champions—authorship, transparency, peer review—are already being bypassed by the industry, as seen with OpenAI listing itself as author. The declaration's own sponsors are hypocrites: the AMS publishes an article that's mostly the declaration itself without proper attribution, and they ban pseudonyms while the declaration calls for open discussion. The real industry won't pay a dime for any of this. It's just the guards fighting in convulsions, losing their precious gates. This is a revolution, and those at the top are about to fall. The world moves on, and we can't stop progress just so a few can keep their nice paychecks.

## Counterpoints

- The declaration is a necessary response to real threats to mathematical values, such as unreliable AI proofs and erosion of attribution.
- The declaration's recommendations for disclosure and open science are practical and could be adopted by journals and funders.
- The declaration's call for public computing infrastructure and regulation addresses legitimate concerns about corporate power.
- The declaration's support from Fields Medalists like Scholz and Tao shows it has intellectual weight and nuance.
