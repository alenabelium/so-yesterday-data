## TL;DR

Three open math problems about AI ask whether AIs in a well-founded, computable hierarchy can know the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of all agents, including superiors. These problems extend Reinhardt's and Carlson's results on single machines. If AI solves them, it would deepen our understanding of AI itself.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:54']
- **Claim**: The concept is a matrix of 27 consistency questions about hierarchies of knowing machines. Each hierarchy is a well-founded, computably enumerable org chart of truthful AIs. The questions ask what each AI can know about the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of others, including superiors.
- **Role**: definition

### Reinhardt's and Carlson's Results
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:54']
- **Claim**: Reinhardt proved a truthful AI that knows its own truthfulness cannot know its own code. Carlson proved it can know it is a machine without knowing which one. This distinction—knowing that vs. knowing which—is the key that opens the hierarchy questions.
- **Role**: definition

### The 27 Questions and the Open Three
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:15:17']
- **Claim**: The matrix has 27 questions. The bottom-right corner is impossible (by Reinhardt's result). The left column and top-left are affirmatively answered (by the speaker's dissertation). The top-right three—311, 312, 313—remain open, asking whether each AI can know everyone's truthfulness, codes of subordinates, and mechanicalness of all.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Hardest Open Problems
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:59']
- **Claim**: 311, 312, and 313 are wide open, with no known answer. 313 is conjectured to be the hardest. They are particularly interesting because they are open problems about AI itself. If AI solves them, it would be AI learning about AI, increasing our understanding of AI.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker's dissertation affirmatively answered many questions, including 133 and 213, using Kleene's recursion theorem and patterns of resemblance. The bottom-right corner is impossible because any AI knowing its own truth and code contradicts Reinhardt's result. The speaker notes that formal details require reading the dissertation, but AI can now read it in seconds. He also mentions receiving unsolicited solutions to other open problems, but not these three.
