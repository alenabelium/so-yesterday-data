## TL;DR

Three open math problems about AI ask whether machines in a well-founded, computable hierarchy can know the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of all agents, including superiors. These problems extend Reinhardt's and Carlson's results on self-knowledge. Solving them would deepen our understanding of AI, and AI itself might be the one to do it.

## The argument

### Define the Hierarchy and Its Knowledge Properties
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:24']
- **Claim**: The concept is a well-founded, computably enumerable hierarchy of truthful AIs, like an org chart. Each AI can know the truthfulness, codes, or mechanicalness of subordinates, self, or everyone. The 27 questions ask which combinations are possible.
- **Role**: definition

### Reinhardt's Impossibility and Carlson's Possibility
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:54']
- **Claim**: Reinhardt proved a truthful AI that knows its own truthfulness cannot know its own code. Carlson proved it can know it is a machine without knowing which one. This distinction between knowing 'that' and knowing 'which' is the crux.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Open Questions: 311, 312, 313
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:59']
- **Claim**: These ask if every such hierarchy can be populated with AIs that know everyone's truthfulness, plus codes and mechanicalness of subordinates (311), also mechanicalness of self (312), or mechanicalness of everyone (313). All are wide open.
- **Role**: definition

### Why These Are Hard and Why AI Might Solve Them
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:21:38']
- **Claim**: These are likely the hardest open problems in the speaker's papers. If AI solves them, it would be AI learning about AI, increasing our understanding of AI itself. The formal details are accessible via AI reading the dissertation.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker's own dissertation affirmatively answered many questions, e.g., 133 and 213, using Kleene's recursion theorem and patterns of resemblance. The bottom-right corner is impossible due to Reinhardt's result. The speaker emphasizes these are open problems with no known answer, and the formal definitions require reading the dissertation. He notes that people have emailed solutions to other open problems, but not these.
