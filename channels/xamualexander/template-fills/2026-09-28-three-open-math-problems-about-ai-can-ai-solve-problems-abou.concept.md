## TL;DR

Three open math problems about AI ask whether AIs in a well-founded hierarchy can know the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of all agents, including superiors. These problems extend the one-machine results of Reinhardt and Carlson. If AI solves them, it would be AI learning about AI.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:54']
- **Claim**: The concept is a matrix of 27 consistency questions about hierarchies of knowing machines. Each hierarchy is a well-founded, computably enumerable org chart of AIs. The questions ask what each AI can know about the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of others, including superiors.
- **Role**: definition

### One-Machine Results Set the Stage
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:54']
- **Claim**: Reinhardt proved a truthful AI that knows its own truthfulness cannot know its own code. Carlson proved such an AI can know it is a machine without knowing which one. The speaker's own work showed an AI can know its own code if it does not know its own truthfulness.
- **Role**: evidence

### Generalize to Hierarchies
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:24']
- **Claim**: The speaker generalized these results to multiple AIs in a hierarchy, like an org chart. The 27 questions arise from combinations of three properties (truthfulness, codes, mechanicalness) and three scopes (subordinates, self+subordinates, everyone). The bottom-right corner is impossible due to Reinhardt's result.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Three Open Problems
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:59']
- **Claim**: Questions 311, 312, and 313 remain open. They ask whether each AI can know everyone's truthfulness, plus codes and mechanicalness of subordinates (311), self+subordinates (312), or everyone (313). The speaker has no idea if they are true, and suspects 313 is hardest.
- **Role**: counter

### AI as the Solver
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:21:57']
- **Claim**: If AI solves these problems, it would be AI learning about AI, increasing our understanding. The speaker hopes AI will settle them within his lifetime, which would be 'divinely appropriate' since they are about AI.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker notes that the bottom-right corner of the matrix is impossible, following from Reinhardt's result. He also mentions that his own contributions, like question 133, use Kleene's recursion theorem. He cautions that the formal details are complex and that he is speaking in plain English; the exact definitions of 'knowing' and 'truthful' require reading the dissertation. He also notes that people have emailed solutions to other open problems in his papers, but not these three.
