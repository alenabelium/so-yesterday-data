## TL;DR

Three open math problems about AI ask whether machines in a well-founded, computable hierarchy can know the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of all agents, including superiors. These questions extend Reinhardt's and Carlson's results on single machines. If AI solves them, it would deepen our understanding of AI itself.

## The argument

### Define the Hierarchy and Its Knowledge Properties
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:24']
- **Claim**: AIs can be arranged in a well-founded, computably enumerable org chart. Each AI may know the truthfulness, exact codes, or mechanicalness of subordinates, self, or all agents. These properties form a 3x3 matrix of 27 consistency questions.
- **Role**: definition

### Reinhardt's Impossibility and Carlson's Possibility
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:54']
- **Claim**: Reinhardt proved a truthful AI that knows its own truthfulness cannot know its own code. Carlson showed such an AI can know it is a machine without knowing which one, via the strong mechanistic thesis. This distinction underlies the hierarchy questions.
- **Role**: evidence

### My Contribution: Machines That Know Their Own Code
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:07:20']
- **Claim**: By relaxing the requirement that the AI knows its own truthfulness, it becomes possible for a truthful AI to know its own code, using Kleene's recursion theorem. This extends the single-machine case and motivates the hierarchy generalization.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Open Questions 311, 312, 313
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:53']
- **Claim**: Questions 311, 312, and 313 ask whether every well-founded computable hierarchy can be populated with AIs that know everybody's truthfulness, plus codes and mechanicalness of subordinates (or self and subordinates, or all). These remain open; 313 is conjectured hardest.
- **Role**: synthesis

### AI Solving Problems About AI Would Be Divinely Appropriate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:21:57']
- **Claim**: If AI solves these problems, it would be an example of AI increasing our understanding of AI itself. The formal details are accessible via AI reading the dissertation, making these problems uniquely suited for AI-assisted proof.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The bottom-right corner of the matrix is impossible due to Reinhardt's result, while the left column and top-left are affirmatively answered in the dissertation. Question 133 is a relatively easy positive result using Kleene's recursion theorem, whereas question 213 is exceedingly difficult and required patterns of resemblance. The speaker notes that people have emailed solutions to other open problems but not these three, suggesting they are the hardest. He also acknowledges the need for formal details, which he omits for brevity, directing viewers to the dissertation or AI-assisted reading.
