## TL;DR

Three open mathematical problems ask whether AIs in a well-founded, computably enumerable hierarchy can know the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of all agents, including superiors. These questions, from a 2013 dissertation, remain unsolved and are uniquely about AI itself. If AI solves them, it would deepen our understanding of AI.

## The argument

### Define the Hierarchy and Its Knowledge Properties
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:24']
- **Claim**: AIs arranged in a well-founded, computably enumerable hierarchy (like an org chart) can have various knowledge properties: knowing truthfulness (1xx, 2xx, 3xx), knowing codes (x1x, x2x, x3x), and knowing mechanicalness (xx1, xx2, xx3). The matrix of 27 questions asks which combinations are possible.
- **Role**: definition

### Reinhardt's Impossibility and Carlson's Possibility
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:54']
- **Claim**: Reinhardt proved a truthful machine that knows its own truthfulness cannot know its own code. Carlson proved such a machine can know it is a machine without knowing which one, via the strong mechanistic thesis. This distinction between knowing 'that' and knowing 'which' is foundational.
- **Role**: definition

### The Open Questions 311, 312, 313
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:59']
- **Claim**: These three questions ask if every well-founded computably enumerable hierarchy can be populated with AIs that know everyone's truthfulness, codes of subordinates, and mechanicalness (of subordinates, self+subordinates, or everyone). They are wide open, with no known answer.
- **Role**: definition

### Why These Are Hard and Unique
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:21:38']
- **Claim**: These are likely the hardest open problems in the speaker's papers, and they are uniquely about AI. Solving them would be AI learning about AI, increasing our understanding of AI itself. The speaker has received unsolicited solutions to other problems but not these.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker notes that the bottom-right corner of the matrix is impossible, following from Reinhardt's result (e.g., question 221). The top-left corner was answered affirmatively in his dissertation, using Kleene's recursion theorem and patterns of resemblance. He emphasizes that these are formal problems requiring precise definitions of 'knows' and 'truthful', which are not fully covered in plain English. He suggests using AI to read the dissertation for formal details.
