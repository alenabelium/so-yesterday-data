## TL;DR

Three open mathematical problems about AI, drawn from a doctoral dissertation, ask whether AIs in a well-founded, computably enumerable hierarchy can know the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of all agents, including superiors. These problems extend prior results on single machines to multi-agent hierarchies. Solving them would deepen our understanding of AI, and AI itself might be the one to solve them.

## The argument

### Define the Hierarchy of Knowing Machines
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:24']
- **Claim**: The concept extends single-machine knowledge to a hierarchy of AIs, like an org chart, where each AI can know things about subordinates, peers, and superiors. The hierarchy is assumed well-founded (no loops, no infinite descending chains) and computably enumerable (an algorithm can list it).
- **Role**: definition

### Catalog the 27 Consistency Questions
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:37']
- **Claim**: The 27 questions form a 3x3x3 matrix, each dimension asking whether every AI knows the truthfulness, the exact codes, or the mechanicalness of a certain group: subordinates (1xx), self and subordinates (2xx), or everyone including superiors (3xx). Each question asks if such a hierarchy can be populated with truthful AIs satisfying the corresponding knowledge properties.
- **Role**: definition

### Show the Known Results and the Open Core
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:17:46']
- **Claim**: Reinhardt proved a truthful machine that knows its own truthfulness cannot know its own code. Carlson proved it can know it is a machine without knowing which. The speaker's dissertation affirmatively answered many questions, but the bottom-right corner is impossible, and the top-right three (311, 312, 313) remain open.
- **Role**: evidence

### State the Open Problems in Plain English
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:59']
- **Claim**: 311 asks if every well-founded computably enumerable hierarchy can be populated so each AI knows everyone's truthfulness, codes of subordinates, and mechanicalness of subordinates. 312 adds self-knowledge of codes and mechanicalness. 313 adds mechanicalness of everyone. All three are wide open, with no known answer.
- **Role**: definition

### Highlight the Meta-Implication for AI
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:21:57']
- **Claim**: These are open problems about AI. If AI solves them, it would be an example of AI increasing our understanding of AI itself. The speaker hopes AI will settle them within his lifetime, calling it 'divinely appropriate'.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker's dissertation affirmatively answered many questions, including 133 and 213, using Kleene's recursion theorem and patterns of resemblance. Question 213 is described as 'exceedingly difficult to prove' and was a key reason for the PhD. The bottom-right corner (e.g., 221) is impossible because it would imply a machine knowing its own code, contradicting Reinhardt. The speaker notes that people have emailed solutions to other open problems in his papers, but none for these three. He also acknowledges the formal details are complex and recommends having an AI read the dissertation for exact definitions.
