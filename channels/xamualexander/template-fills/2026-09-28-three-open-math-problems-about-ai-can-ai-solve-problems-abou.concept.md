## TL;DR

Three open math problems about AI ask whether machines in a well-founded, computable hierarchy can know the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of all agents, including superiors. These questions extend Reinhardt's and Carlson's results on self-knowledge. If AI solves them, it would deepen our understanding of AI itself.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:00:00']
- **Claim**: The concept is a matrix of 27 consistency questions about hierarchies of knowing machines—AIs arranged like an org chart, each with knowledge about others' truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness. Three questions (311, 312, 313) remain open, and they are specifically about AI's self-knowledge.
- **Role**: definition

### Reinhardt's Impossibility and Carlson's Possibility
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:54']
- **Claim**: Reinhardt proved a truthful machine that knows its own truthfulness cannot know its own code. Carlson proved it can know it is a machine without knowing which one, via the strong mechanistic thesis. This distinction—knowing 'that' vs. 'which'—is the key to the hierarchy questions.
- **Role**: evidence

### The Hierarchy Generalization
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:24']
- **Claim**: The dissertation generalizes from one machine to many, arranged in a well-founded, computably enumerable hierarchy. Questions 1xx, 2xx, 3xx ask about knowledge of truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness, respectively, with increasing scope from subordinates to everyone. The bottom-right corner is impossible due to Reinhardt's result.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Open Questions 311, 312, 313
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:53']
- **Claim**: 311 asks if each AI can know everyone's truthfulness, codes of subordinates, and mechanicalness of subordinates. 312 adds self-knowledge of mechanicalness; 313 extends mechanicalness to everyone. All are open; 313 is conjectured hardest. Positive answers would imply weaker ones, negative answers would cascade.
- **Role**: definition

### AI as the Solver
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:21:57']
- **Claim**: If AI solves these problems, it would be a meta-example: AI learning about AI, increasing our understanding. The speaker hopes for this within his lifetime, calling it 'divinely appropriate' since the problems are about AI itself.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker notes that the bottom-right corner of the matrix is impossible, following from Reinhardt's result that a truthful machine knowing its own truthfulness cannot know its own code. He also mentions his own contribution: a machine can know its own code if it does not know its own truthfulness, using Kleene's recursion theorem. He emphasizes that the formal details are in his dissertation, which is over 100 pages, and suggests using AI to read it. He admits he has no idea whether the answers to 311, 312, or 313 are yes or no, and that they are likely the hardest open problems in his papers.
