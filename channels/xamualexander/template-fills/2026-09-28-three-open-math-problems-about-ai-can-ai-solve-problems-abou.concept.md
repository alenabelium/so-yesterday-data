## TL;DR

Three open math problems about AI ask whether machines in a well-founded, computable hierarchy can know the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of all agents, including superiors. These problems extend Reinhardt's and Carlson's results on self-knowledge. Solving them would deepen our understanding of AI and possibly be achieved by AI itself.

## The argument

### Define the Core Substrate
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:10:24']
- **Claim**: The concept generalizes single-machine self-knowledge to a hierarchy of AIs, like an org chart, where each machine can know things about subordinates, itself, and superiors. The hierarchy is assumed well-founded (no loops) and computably enumerable.
- **Role**: definition

### Reinhardt's Negative Result
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:02:54']
- **Claim**: Reinhardt proved a truthful machine that knows its own truthfulness cannot know its own code. This sets a hard limit on self-knowledge, but Carlson showed a machine can know it is a machine without knowing which one, opening the door for weaker forms of self-knowledge.
- **Role**: evidence

### Carlson's Positive Result
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:06:05']
- **Claim**: Carlson proved the strong mechanistic thesis is consistent with epistemic arithmetic, meaning an AI can know it is a machine without knowing its specific code. This distinction between knowing 'that' and knowing 'which' is crucial for the hierarchy questions.
- **Role**: synthesis

### The Three Open Questions
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:18:59']
- **Claim**: Questions 311, 312, and 313 ask whether a hierarchy can be populated so that each AI knows everyone's truthfulness, plus codes and mechanicalness of subordinates (311), also mechanicalness of self (312), or mechanicalness of everyone (313). These are wide open and increasingly strong.
- **Role**: definition

### Why AI Might Solve Them
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:21:57']
- **Claim**: If AI solves these problems, it would be an example of AI increasing our understanding of AI itself. The speaker hopes AI will settle them, which would be 'divinely appropriate' given they are about AI.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker notes that the bottom-right corner of the matrix is impossible due to Reinhardt's result, and his own work (question 213) is 'exceedingly difficult' using patterns of resemblance. He admits he has 'no idea' whether 311, 312, or 313 are true, and conjectures 313 is hardest. He also acknowledges formal details are omitted and refers viewers to his dissertation for exact definitions.
