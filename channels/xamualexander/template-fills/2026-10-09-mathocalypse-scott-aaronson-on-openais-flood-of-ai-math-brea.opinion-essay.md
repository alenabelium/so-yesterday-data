## Thesis

OpenAI's release of nearly 400 AI-generated mathematical results, including a proof of the Unique Games Conjecture, marks the end of elite mathematicians' monopoly on solving open problems and the beginning of a new era where AI is the.

## Argument

The sheer scale and nature of the results are the first mechanism. OpenAI released 372 major results, including a proof of the Unique Games Conjecture, a problem Dana Moshkovitz worked on her entire career, solved in about three hours. This isn't incremental progress; it's a wholesale collapse of entire subfields. The proofs are described as 'alien craziness,' using a 'completely new bizarre code' that no human has yet understood. This directly refutes the claim that AI isn't creative—what's more creative than something that strikes an expert as literally alien? The implications for mathematicians are existential. As Scott Aaronson notes, the race to understand these proofs has just started, and the role of the mathematician shifts from discoverer to interpreter, or as the host puts it, 'tour guides to the Platonic realm of ideals.' The host argues this is a great equalizer, putting elite mathematicians in the same boat as amateurs, which is good because elitists lead fields to become inbred. The host also highlights the strategic difference between OpenAI and Anthropic: OpenAI dumped raw results, winning the PR war, while Anthropic paid experts to digest theirs, which vanished in obscurity. The host argues OpenAI's approach is better, as it breaks the gatekeeping of the mathematical elite. Finally, the host notes the potential for AI to solve even more, as the model only solved 5% of 8,000 problems in vanilla mode, and the absence of cryptography results suggests OpenAI may be holding back practical breakthroughs for its own use.

## Counterpoints

- Skeptics will say the alleged solutions are not solutions at all, just AI slop, or that the problems were glorified contest puzzles, not real math.
- The proofs are so horribly written that they are impossible to read without AI help, suggesting OpenAI may be deliberately obfuscating them to force people to pay for AI tools.
- The absence of cryptography results from the release suggests OpenAI may be guarding practical breakthroughs for its own monopoly, not sharing them for the public good.
- The advisory group's statement is carefully phrased to neither endorse nor condemn OpenAI's actions, highlighting the community's uncertainty about the process.
- The mathematical community must be able to formulate their own questions and explore directions not selected by AI labs, as the advisory group emphasizes.
