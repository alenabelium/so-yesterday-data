## Thesis

OpenAI's release of nearly 400 AI-generated mathematical breakthroughs, including a proof of the Unique Games Conjecture, marks the end of elite mathematicians' gatekeeping and the beginning of a new era where AI, not human genius, is the.

## Argument

The mechanism is the sheer scale and speed of the discovery. OpenAI's internal model, running in vanilla mode for about three hours per problem, solved roughly 5% of 8,000 long-standing open problems—including the Unique Games Conjecture, L=BPL, and Hilbert's 10th problem over the rationals. This isn't a bespoke, million-dollar compute contraption; it's a model that might be released to ChatGPT customers within months. The proofs are so alien that even experts like Dana Moshkovitz describe them as 'written by someone on psychedelics,' requiring AI assistance to even parse. This directly refutes the claim that AI can't be creative—what's more creative than a proof that strikes an expert as 'alien craziness'? The implications are profound: mathematicians are now tour guides to the Platonic realm, not its discoverers. The old hierarchy, where a few elites controlled access to knowledge, is shattered. OpenAI's strategy of dumping raw, undigested results—unlike Anthropic's approach of paying experts to explain them—has won the PR war and democratized access to the frontier. The future isn't the end of mathematics; it's a flood of new interest, as the field goes mainstream. The real challenge is not whether AI can solve problems, but whether we can nurture a community that still values the heroic adventure of understanding, even when the teleportation machine does the climbing.

## Counterpoints

- Skeptics will say the proofs are AI slop, not real mathematics, or that the problems solved were trivial contest puzzles—but Hilbert's 10th problem over the rationals is hardly a puzzle.
- The absence of P≠NP and other 'greatest open problems' shows AI's limits, but that's a goalpost move—it solved 5% of the hardest problems in a single attempt.
- Cryptography's absence from the release suggests OpenAI may be hoarding practical breakthroughs for itself, not publishing them for the public good.
- Anthropic's model of paying experts to explain results is more responsible, but it puts a private company in the role of choosing which humans get to be emissaries of the AI.
- The advisory group's cautious statement neither endorses nor condemns OpenAI, but its call for equitable access rings hollow when elite institutions still guard their libraries.
