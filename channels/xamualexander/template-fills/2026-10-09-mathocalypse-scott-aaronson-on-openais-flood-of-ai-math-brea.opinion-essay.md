## Thesis

OpenAI's release of nearly 400 AI-generated mathematical proofs, including the Unique Games Conjecture, marks the end of elite mathematicians' monopoly on discovery and the beginning of a new era where AI is the primary creator of.

## Argument

The sheer scale and nature of OpenAI's release—372 results including a proof of the Unique Games Conjecture, L=BPL, and a solution to Hilbert's 10th problem over the rationals—shatters the notion that AI lacks creativity. These are not incremental combinations; they are 'alien craziness' that even experts like Dana Moshkovitz find incomprehensible without AI assistance. The proofs are so novel they strike the community as if from an alien species, directly refuting the 'AI just combines existing things' crowd. This is a seismic event that collapses entire subfields, like the one devoted to L vs. BPL, and makes elite mathematicians' life work obsolete in hours. The implications are profound: mathematicians are now 'tour guides to the Platonic realm of ideals,' and the field's future lies in interpreting and verifying AI's output, not in solo discovery. The race to understand these proofs has just begun, and the community's response—rushing to decipher them—shows the new reality. The 'mathocalypse' is not a metaphor; it's the literal end of mathematics as a human-centric discipline.

## Counterpoints

- Skeptics argue the proofs are 'AI slop' or not real math, but the solutions to problems like Hilbert's 10th problem over the rationals are undeniable, and the goalposts keep moving (e.g., 'no Riemann hypothesis yet').
- Some claim AI is not creative, but the proofs are so novel they strike experts as 'alien craziness,' directly refuting the 'just combines existing things' argument.
- The absence of cryptography results from OpenAI's release suggests they may be withholding practical breakthroughs for their own benefit, not that AI can't solve them.
- The advisory group's cautious statement—neither endorsing nor condemning—highlights the community's struggle to assess the impact, but it's a 'seismic 9.0 earthquake' that demands a new approach.
