## Thesis

OpenAI's release of nearly 400 AI-generated mathematical proofs, including the Unique Games Conjecture, marks the end of elite mathematicians' monopoly on discovery and the beginning of a new era where AI is the primary creator of.

## Argument

The mechanism is the sheer scale and speed of the breakthrough. OpenAI's internal model, running in vanilla mode for about three hours per problem, solved roughly 5% of 8,000 long-standing open problems, including the Unique Games Conjecture and Hilbert's 10th problem over the rationals. This isn't a bespoke, million-dollar compute contraption; it's the latest internal model, likely to be released to paying ChatGPT customers within months. The proofs are so alien that even experts like Dana Moshkovitz describe them as 'something written by someone who's on psychedelics' — a new code, not the long code or short code, but 'some alien craziness.' This directly refutes the claim that AI isn't creative; what's more creative than something that strikes an expert as literally alien? The implications are devastating for the old guard. Elite mathematicians who spent careers on these problems are now in the same boat as amateurs. The 'great equalizer' has arrived. The old gatekeepers — the elitists who controlled access to mathematical glory — are now holding keys to a gate with no wall. OpenAI's strategy of dumping raw, unreadable proofs forces the entire community into a frantic race to understand them, and it's a PR masterstroke. Anthropic, by contrast, paid experts to digest their result, and that move vanished in obscurity. The future is clear: mathematicians become tour guides to the Platonic realm of ideals, explaining the alien proofs to the rest of us. The old craft of climbing the mountain by hand is over; the teleportation machine is here, and it's not going away.

## Counterpoints

- Skeptics will say the proofs are AI slop, not real math, or that the problems were just glorified puzzles — but Hilbert's 10th problem over the rationals is not a contest puzzle.
- The absence of P ≠ NP and the Riemann hypothesis shows AI still can't crack the truly hardest problems — but that's moving the goalposts, and they'll move them again when those fall.
- Cryptography is conspicuously absent from the release, suggesting OpenAI may be hoarding practical breakthroughs for its own monopoly, not publishing everything.
- The advisory group's statement is carefully neutral, neither endorsing nor condemning, but it's a 9.0 earthquake, not just an 'important event.'
- Anthropic's approach of paying experts to explain results was more ethical but PR-disastrous; OpenAI's raw dump won the public relations war.
