## Thesis

OpenAI's release of nearly 400 AI-generated mathematical proofs, including the Unique Games Conjecture, marks the end of elite mathematicians' monopoly on discovery and the start of a new era where AI is the primary creator of.

## Argument

The sheer scale and depth of the release is unprecedented. Scott Aaronson calls it 'one of the biggest days in mathematical history.' The proof of the Unique Games Conjecture, a problem his wife Dana Moshkovitz worked on her entire career, was solved in about three hours by a vanilla OpenAI model. This isn't just incremental progress; it's a wholesale collapse of entire subfields. L equals BPL, a major derandomization conjecture, is solved. The Fourier transform and integer multiplication break a 1960s barrier. Hilbert's 10th problem over the rationals, arguably the biggest open problem in computability theory, is finally resolved. These are not 'AI slop' or 'glorified puzzle contests'—they are the life's work of hundreds of researchers, done in hours.

The creative nature of the proofs is the second key point. Dana's text describes the UGC proof as 'some alien craziness,' a 'completely new bizarre code with a noise test.' This directly refutes the claim that AI can't be creative, that it just combines existing ideas. What's more creative than something that strikes an expert as literally alien? This is a fundamental shift in how mathematics is done. The old model of a lone genius climbing a mountain is replaced by a new one where AI teleports you to the peak, and your job becomes clearing the fog and explaining the view.

## Counterpoints

- The alleged solutions are not real proofs, just AI slop or glorified puzzle contests.
- The proofs are so horribly written that they are impossible to read without AI help, suggesting OpenAI's angle is to force people to pay for AI tools.
- The absence of major results like P vs NP and the Riemann Hypothesis shows AI's limits.
- Cryptography is conspicuously absent from the release, suggesting OpenAI is keeping practical breakthroughs for itself.
- The advisory group's statement carefully neither endorses nor condemns OpenAI's actions, highlighting the community's uncertainty.
