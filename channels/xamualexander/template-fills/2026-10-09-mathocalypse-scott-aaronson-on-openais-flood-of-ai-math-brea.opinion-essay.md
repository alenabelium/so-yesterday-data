## Thesis

OpenAI's release of nearly 400 AI-generated mathematical proofs, including the Unique Games Conjecture, marks a 'mathocalypse' that shatters the elitist gatekeeping of mathematics and forces a redefinition of human mathematical creativity.

## Argument

The sheer scale and depth of OpenAI's release—372 results including a proof of the Unique Games Conjecture, L=BPL, and a resolution of Hilbert's 10th problem over the rationals—is not just incremental progress but a seismic event that collapses entire subfields and decades of human effort. The proofs are described as 'alien craziness,' using novel codes and constructions that strike experts as literally from another species, directly refuting the claim that AI merely combines existing ideas. This is not about AI assisting mathematicians; it's about AI outpacing them, solving in three hours problems that defined entire careers. The implications are twofold: first, the 'elitist' structure of mathematics, where a few gatekeepers control access and prestige, is rendered obsolete. The great equalizer is that elite mathematicians are now in the same boat as amateurs, and this is a good thing, as elitism breeds inbreeding and exclusivity. Second, the future of mathematics shifts from discovery to interpretation—mathematicians become 'tour guides' to the Platonic realm of ideals, explaining and contextualizing AI's outputs rather than generating them. The OpenAI model of dumping raw, uninterpreted proofs, while chaotic, is superior to Anthropic's approach of paying select experts to digest results, because it democratizes access and sparks a global race to understand, whereas Anthropic's method merely reinforces the old gatekeeping.
