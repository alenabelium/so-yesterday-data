## Thesis

The AI-in-Math Advisory Group's guidelines are a mix of sensible proposals and self-serving, hypocritical demands that fail to address the real challenges of AI-generated mathematics.

## Argument

The guidelines start with an impractical request: asking AI labs to stop using proprietary models. This is as likely to succeed as asking for a billion dollars. The group's appeal to traditional norms is selective, ignoring that the four-color theorem was accepted without human understanding and that conferences are closed to the public. The recommendation that AI labs fund human understanding is self-serving, benefiting the mathematicians who would receive the funding. The demand for AI labs to cite prior work is unfair, as independent discovery does not require citation. The proposal for repositories is good, but it ignores that arXiv restricts public submission. The call for transparency is welcome, but it misses the need for random seeds and determinism. The push for formalization is sensible, but endorsing YAML is short-sighted. The funding requests are blatant begging, and the claim that accepting money doesn't confer legitimacy is absurd. The group's stance on access is hypocritical, as it ignores the paywalls of academic journals. The survey of 600 respondents is opaque, and the group failed to include the recommendation to release results ASAP, which was a plurality view. The mathematical community is in chaos, but the old paradigm is dead.

## Counterpoints

- The group's request to stop using proprietary models is impractical, akin to asking for a billion dollars.
- The norm that authors must understand proofs is contradicted by the four-color theorem, which is accepted without human understanding.
- The requirement to cite prior work is unfair for independent discoveries, even if the AI has trained on literature.
- The recommendation to fund human understanding is self-serving for the mathematicians who would receive the funding.
- The group's stance on access is hypocritical, ignoring the paywalls of academic journals.
