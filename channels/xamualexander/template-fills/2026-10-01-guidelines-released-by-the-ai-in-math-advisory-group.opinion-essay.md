## Thesis

The AGMI advisory group's recommendations on AI-generated mathematics are self-serving, hypocritical, and fail to address the real crisis of peer review, instead demanding funding and enshrining outdated norms.

## Argument

The group's initial demand that AI labs stop using proprietary models is a performative request, akin to asking for a billion dollars, with no enforcement mechanism. Their appeal to 'longstanding norms' of peer review ignores that the system is already collapsing under a mountain of submissions, and reviewers will inevitably use AI to cope. The requirement that authors understand and verify proofs is already violated by the universally accepted four-color theorem, which no human can fully verify. The demand for talks and seminars is hypocritical, as these venues are closed to the general public. The recommendation that AI labs fund human understanding is a blatant conflict of interest, as it directly benefits the mathematicians writing the report. The call for 'papers' enshrines a specific medium, ignoring that mathematics is now distributed as PDFs. The demand for citations is flawed, as independent rediscovery without conscious awareness of prior work does not obligate citation. While the recommendation for persistent identifiers is correct, it is hypocritical to demand publication in repositories like arXiv that require institutional affiliation or vouching, excluding the public. ## Counterpoints

- The group's demand for AI labs to stop using proprietary models is unrealistic and performative, as it has no enforcement power.
- The requirement that authors understand and verify proofs is already violated by the four-color theorem, which is accepted despite no human understanding.
- The recommendation for AI labs to fund human understanding is a conflict of interest, as it directly benefits the mathematicians writing the report.
- The call for equitable access is hypocritical, as it ignores the paywalled journal system that creates unequal access.
- The group ignored the survey's clear plurality for immediate release of results, revealing a disconnect between the community and the committee.
