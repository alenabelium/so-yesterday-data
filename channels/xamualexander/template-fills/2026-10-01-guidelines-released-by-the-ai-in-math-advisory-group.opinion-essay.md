## Thesis

The AI-in-Math Advisory Group's guidelines are a mix of sensible, self-serving, and hypocritical recommendations that fail to address the real challenges AI-generated mathematics poses to the mathematical community.

## Argument

The guidelines open with a request that AI labs stop using proprietary models, which Dr. Alexander dismisses as unrealistic, comparing it to his own request for a billion dollars. He then challenges the traditional norms the group cites, pointing out that the four-color theorem was accepted despite no human understanding, and that peer review is already unsustainable. The group's call for community feedback is undercut by the fact that Alexander, a mathematician, was never consulted. However, he finds genuine merit in the recommendation that AI labs release their results promptly, noting OpenAI's claim of over 100 breakthroughs. Yet, he criticizes the group's stance on funding human understanding, seeing it as self-serving for mathematicians, and he questions the practicality of requiring traditional publication formats, using a thought experiment about papyrus scrolls. He agrees with the need for persistent identifiers and version control, but notes the arXiv's access restrictions are hypocritical. He also points out the omission of random seeds for reproducibility, and questions the endorsement of YAML, which may become outdated. The guidelines' call for AI labs to fund conferences and workshops is seen as a conflict of interest, and the demand for equitable access is undermined by the group's silence on paywalled journals.

## Counterpoints

- The group's request to stop using proprietary models is unrealistic, akin to asking for a billion dollars.
- The norm that authors must understand and verify proofs is already violated by the four-color theorem.
- The claim that the mathematical community was consulted is dubious, as Alexander was not asked.
- The recommendation to fund human understanding is self-serving for mathematicians.
- The call for equitable access is hypocritical given the paywalled journal practices.
