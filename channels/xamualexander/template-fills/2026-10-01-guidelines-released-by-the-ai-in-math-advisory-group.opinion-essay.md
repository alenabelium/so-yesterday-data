## Thesis

The AGMI advisory group's recommendations are self-serving, hypocritical, and fail to address the real crisis in mathematics, which is the collapse of peer review and the gatekeeping of knowledge.

## Argument

The group's core demand—that AI labs stop using proprietary models—is a fantasy, as is their request for funding, which I sarcastically echo. Their appeal to 'longstanding norms' is nostalgic nonsense; peer review is already drowning, and reviewers will secretly use AI. The four-color theorem proves that human understanding isn't a prerequisite for mathematical acceptance, so their emphasis on it is a red herring. Their call for community feedback is a sham—I was never asked. The one good thing is their recommendation that AI labs release results ASAP, which would force OpenAI to publish its 100 breakthroughs. But their other suggestions are either absurd (like requiring 'papers' when we're in the PDF era), self-serving (funding mathematicians to understand AI output), or hypocritical (demanding open access from AI labs while journals charge $3,000 for it). They even miss the obvious need for deterministic runs and random seeds for reproducibility. The group is out of touch, and the old paradigm is dead.

## Counterpoints

- The group's request for AI labs to stop using proprietary models is unrealistic and akin to asking for a billion dollars.
- The norm that authors must understand and verify their proofs is already broken, as shown by the four-color theorem.
- The claim that the mathematical community was consulted is dubious—I was never asked.
- The recommendation that AI labs fund human understanding is self-serving and creates a conflict of interest.
- The demand for open access is hypocritical when journals charge thousands for it.
