## Thesis

The AI-in-Math Advisory Group's guidelines are a mix of sensible, self-serving, and hypocritical recommendations that fail to address the real challenges of AI-generated mathematics.

## Argument

The guidelines open with a request that AI labs stop using proprietary models, which Dr. Alexander dismisses as unrealistic—comparing it to asking for a billion dollars. He then attacks the traditional norms the group invokes, noting that peer review is already overwhelmed and that the four-color theorem was accepted without human understanding, undermining the requirement that authors understand proofs. The group's call for community feedback is undercut by the fact that Dr. Alexander, a mathematician, was never consulted, and the survey's 600 respondents are not described. He praises the recommendation that AI labs release results ASAP, but notes the group omitted this from their final recommendations, suggesting a disconnect. The demand that AI labs fund human understanding is seen as self-serving, benefiting the mathematicians who would receive the funding, and he points out the hypocrisy of asking for money while disavowing the labs' practices. He also criticizes the recommendation to cite prior work, arguing that independent discovery doesn't require citation, and the push for formalization is marred by endorsing specific file formats like YAML, which may become obsolete. The call for open access is undermined by the group's silence on journal paywalls, which Dr. Alexander calls hypocritical. He concludes that the mathematical community is in chaos, with some demanding AI mathathons be cancelled and others seeking funding, and urges embracing the future rather than clinging to outdated norms.

## Counterpoints

- The group's request to stop using proprietary models is unrealistic, akin to asking for a billion dollars.
- The requirement that authors understand proofs is contradicted by the four-color theorem, which was accepted without human understanding.
- The claim that the group consulted the mathematical community is dubious, as Dr. Alexander was never asked.
- The recommendation to cite prior work is flawed because independent discovery doesn't require citation, even if the discoverer was exposed to the idea subconsciously.
- The call for open access is hypocritical because the group doesn't address journal paywalls that limit access.
