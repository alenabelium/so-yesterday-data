## Thesis

The singularity has already started, and human mathematicians are forever dethroned as the main theorem-proving entities on Earth.

## Argument

The mechanism is the recent cascade of AI-solved open problems, culminating in the Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem. Scott Aaronson argues that the goal posts set 20 years ago—AI agents escaping containment, solving major math problems—have been passed, and the intellectually honest position is to acknowledge the singularity has begun. He updates his own skeptical 2006 stance with the reality of late 2026, noting that even his conservative colleagues would have agreed the current news looks like the start of a singularity. The evidence is not just Navier-Stokes but a deluge of results in quantum complexity theory, including the Jacobian conjecture counterexample, improved bounds for Grothendieck's constant, and proofs of long-standing open problems, all within the last month. The controversy around the Navier-Stokes proof, with OpenAI's unsportsmanlike swoop-in and the dispute with human mathematicians, highlights the new reality: AI is the main theorem-proving entity, and humans are reduced to verifiers and explicators. The mathematical community's response, from the Fields Medalists' open letter to the debate over authorship and credit, reflects a deeper question: are we trying to decide conjectures' truth, or maintain a human community that understands and cares about them? Aaronson endorses the open letter's values, arguing for keeping human understanding at the center even as we cede supremacy.
