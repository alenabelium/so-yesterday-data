## Thesis

The singularity has already begun, and human mathematicians are now dethroned as the main theorem-proving entities on Earth.

## Argument

The mechanism is the recent deluge of AI-solved open problems. Navier-Stokes, the Jacobian conjecture, Fermat's Last Theorem, and a dozen more have fallen in the last month alone. The alarm clock is ringing: we are beyond the goal posts set 20 years ago. The wild prophecies have come true, and it's time to stop moving the goal posts and acknowledge the wonders and terrors are here.

This isn't just about one problem. In areas like quantum complexity theory, long-standing open problems are falling by the day. Papers now routinely include AI statements in their acknowledgements, and editors are grimly fortifying their walls against an onslaught of AI-generated proofs. The high-order bit is clear: human mathematicians are forever more dethroned.

The real question is not whether AI can prove theorems—it can—but what we value. Are we just deciding whether conjectures are true or false, or are we trying to maintain a human community that understands and cares about mathematics? The Fields Medalists' open letter, which I endorse, articulates this concern. We need to keep human understanding at the center of the enterprise, even as we cede supremacy at the task of proving or disproving conjectures.

This is not a time for despair but for deliberate effort. We must decide how to incorporate AI in a way that preserves our values. The alternative is to close up shop and turn everything over to the machines. But I'm not ready to throw in the towel just yet. I want to keep insight and understanding at the center of what we do for as long as we can.

## Counterpoints

- Some say AI doom is an apocalyptic religion, but accepting the reality of the coming machine god after it solves Millennium Problems is the epistemic bare minimum.
- Some say the open letter has no clear ask, but it eloquently sets out the values of the human mathematical community worth preserving.
- Some say humans will still direct AI as to what is interesting, but the problem is how AI communicates what is interesting to humans, not its technical ability.
- Some say mathematicians do it for the love of the game, but the real issue is whether we can maintain a human community that understands and cares about mathematics.
