## Thesis

The singularity has already started, and human mathematicians are forever dethroned as the main theorem-proving entities, with AI solving major open problems like Navier-Stokes.

## Argument

The mechanism is the relentless acceleration of AI capabilities, from AlphaGo a decade ago to the current deluge of AI-proved or assisted results in mathematics. The Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem, solved with crucial AI contributions and verified in Lean, is the clearest signal. OpenAI burned at least $15 million in compute to produce a 166-page solution that no human has fully read. This is not an isolated event; in complexity theory, long-standing open problems are falling by the day, and papers now routinely include AI statements in their acknowledgements. The high-order bit, as Zvi points out, is that human mathematicians are dethroned as the main theorem-proving entities. The response from the mathematical community, including 25 Fields Medalists, is to preserve human understanding and community, but the question is whether that's sustainable. The host argues that we must update our worldview with intellectual honesty: the wild prophecies have come true, and the alarm clock is ringing.

## Counterpoints

- The open letter from Fields Medalists doesn't have a clear ask, only values.
- Some argue AI still relies on humans to direct it toward interesting problems.
- The dispute over Navier-Stokes credit suggests AI companies act unsportsmanlike.
- Eliezer Yudkowsky didn't expect this to happen so soon, so his predictions were vague.
- The host's own past skepticism about an AI winter was wrong, but he updated.
