## Thesis

The singularity has already started, and human mathematicians are forever dethroned as the main theorem-proving entities on Earth.

## Argument

The mechanism is the relentless, accelerating stream of AI-solved open problems. Scott Aaronson's essay, read here, opens with the 20-year-old skeptical position: 'Wake me up when AI solves a Millennium Problem.' That alarm is now ringing. The Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem appears solved, with an OpenAI model verifying a finite-time singularity in Lean, at a cost of $15 million in compute and a 166-page proof no human has fully read. This isn't an isolated event. In the last month alone, AI has contributed to a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, improved bounds for Grothendieck's constant, an AI-verified proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, and a proof of Wachter's disentangler conjecture. In complexity theory, the deluge is daily. The high-order bit, as Zvi points out, is that human mathematicians are dethroned. The role shifts from theorem-prover to verifier, explicator, and community-builder. The open letter from 25 Fields Medalists, endorsed by Aaronson, isn't about stopping AI but about preserving a human mathematical community that understands and cares about results. The real question is whether we can keep human understanding at the center, or whether we eventually close up shop and turn math over to machines entirely. The response from the community is a mix of denial, strategic retreat, and a scramble to define new rules for authorship and credit. ## Counterpoints

- The open letter from 25 Fields Medalists has no clear ask; it merely sets out values worth preserving.
- Some argue that AI will still rely on humans to direct it toward what is interesting or useful, and that humans remain the main consumers of math.
- A commenter suggests that mathematicians should pivot to creating better problem statements and theories about AI systems themselves, rather than lamenting the loss of theorem-proving.
- A critic notes that Eliezer Yudkowsky himself didn't expect the singularity to happen this soon, undermining the claim that he was right about the timeline.
