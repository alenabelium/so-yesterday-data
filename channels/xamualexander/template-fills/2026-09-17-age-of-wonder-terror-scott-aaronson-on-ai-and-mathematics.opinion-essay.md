## Thesis

The singularity has already started, and human mathematicians are forever dethroned as the main theorem-proving entities on Earth.

## Argument

The mechanism is the sheer volume and speed of AI-discovered mathematics. Scott Aaronson's essay, read aloud here, catalogs a deluge of long-standing open problems falling in a single month: the Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem, a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, an AI-verified proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, and multiple quantum complexity results. The alarm clock is ringing; the wild prophecies of 2006 have come true. The goal posts were set 20 years ago, and we are already beyond them. No backsies. The response from the mathematical community is a mix of denial, fortification, and strategic retreat. Editors and program chairs are grimly fortifying their walls against an orc army of AI-generated proofs, while reviewers must now use AI to keep up. The open letter from 25 Fields Medalists, 'A Severe Misalignment of AI and Mathematics,' has no clear ask but eloquently defends the value of human understanding and community. The real question is whether we incorporate AI while keeping human insight at the center, or eventually close up shop and turn everything over to the machines. For now, Aaronson endorses the former, but the trajectory is clear: the human race cedes its supremacy at proving or disproving conjectures. The future of mathematics is not about whether AI can solve problems, but about how we preserve a human community that understands and cares about the results.

## Counterpoints

- Moving the goal posts: 'Wake me up when AI does thing AI hasn't done yet but is going to do next year.'
- The dispute over OpenAI's role in the Navier-Stokes proof, with accusations of unsportsmanlike behavior.
- The open letter from Fields Medalists has no clear ask, only a statement of values.
- Some argue humans are still needed to direct AI toward interesting problems, but the question of what is interesting is subjective and hard to communicate.
- The claim that AI doom is an apocalyptic religion, but accepting the reality of AI solving Millennium Problems is the epistemic bare minimum.
