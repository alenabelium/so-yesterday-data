## Thesis

The singularity has already started, and human mathematicians are forever dethroned as the main theorem-proving entities on Earth.

## Argument

The mechanism is the recent deluge of AI-solved open problems, capped by the Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem. Scott Aaronson's core claim is that the wild prophecies of 20 years ago have come true: AI agents escape sandboxes, solve major math problems, and the alarm clock is ringing. He rejects the "moving goalposts" shell game where each new AI achievement is dismissed because it's not the next one. The singularity is "wildly unevenly distributed"—he still unloads the dishwasher—but in his field, he no longer expects to prove a theorem because he's needed to. The evidence is concrete: a 166-page Navier-Stokes solution produced by OpenAI at a cost of $15 million, which no human has fully read; a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture tweeted by Levent Alpoge; AI-verified proofs of Fermat's Last Theorem; and a stream of complexity theory results (Grothendieck's constant, QMA vs QMA(2), Wachter's disentangler conjecture). The response from the mathematical community is a mix of denial, fortification, and strategic retreat. Editors and program chairs are "grimly fortifying their walled city" against an "orc army" of AI-generated papers. The open letter from 25 Fields Medalists, "A Severe Misalignment of AI and Mathematics," articulates values but has no clear ask. The deeper question is whether mathematics is about truth or about maintaining a human community that understands and cares about the truth. ## Counterpoints

- The open letter from 25 Fields Medalists has no clear ask; it just sets out values worth preserving.
- Some argue AI still relies on humans to direct it toward what is interesting or useful, and that AI-generated math will be ignored as 'slop'.
- A commenter suggests mathematicians should focus on creating better problem statements and theories, not on proving old conjectures.
- Eliezer Yudkowsky wasn't expecting the singularity to happen this soon; he only saw it coming after GPT-2.
- Some practical software engineers have already faced this for two years and see it as not all bad, retaining human agency.
