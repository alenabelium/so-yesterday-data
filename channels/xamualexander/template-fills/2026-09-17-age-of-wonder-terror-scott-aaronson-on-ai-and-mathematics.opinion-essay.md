## Thesis

The singularity has already begun, and AI has permanently dethroned human mathematicians as the primary theorem-proving entities on Earth.

## Argument

The mechanism is the sheer volume of AI-solved open problems. Navier-Stokes, the Jacobian conjecture, and a dozen other long-standing results have fallen in the last month alone, with AI as the primary driver. The alarm clock is ringing: we've passed the 2006 goalposts, and there's no going back. The old regime is over. The question is no longer whether AI will dominate math, but how we preserve human understanding and community in the face of that dominance. The Fields Medalists' open letter, which I endorse, is a last-ditch effort to keep insight and understanding at the center of the enterprise, even as we cede supremacy at proving theorems. The alternative is to close up shop and turn everything over to the machines, but I'm not ready to throw in the towel just yet. The real work now is alignment, not theorem-proving.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue AI results are just 'AI slop' and humans still direct what's interesting or useful, but that misses the point: the AI is already solving problems we couldn't, and the question of what's interesting is itself becoming a meta-problem.
- Others say the singularity hasn't started because we still do mundane tasks like unloading the dishwasher, but that's moving the goalposts—the wild prophecies of 2006 have already come true.
- The Navier-Stokes dispute raises questions about OpenAI's unsportsmanlike conduct, but the high-order bit is that human mathematicians are forever dethroned, regardless of who gets credit.
