## Thesis

The Caltech AI Mathathon is a legitimate experiment in AI-assisted mathematics, and the 771 mathematicians demanding its cancellation are gatekeeping out of fear of job loss, not protecting the field.

## Argument

The open letter from Caltech mathematicians is a case of established professionals bullying undergrads. Their core complaint is that AI-generated results will have 'destructive impacts' and extract unpaid verification labor. But this framing treats mathematics as a charity program for mathematicians rather than a pursuit of objective truth. The theorems exist independently in the Platonic realm; how they are discovered doesn't diminish their value. The letter's claim that AI can't solve conjectures faster is 'empirically dubious' — so the Mathon is a perfect empirical test. If the skeptics are right, the event will prove them right. If they're wrong, they're just delaying the inevitable. The organizers' response is sober and professional: they added a six-month verification period, made arXiv publication a requirement, and even invited critics to speak. They're extending olive branches to their 'persecutors.' The real issue is job security. The letter's signatories are terrified that AI will make their skills obsolete, so they're trying to stop the future. But mathematics has intrinsic value regardless of who or what proves it. The Mathon is a responsible experiment that preserves the culture of mathematics while embracing new tools.

## Counterpoints

- The event will create 'slop mathematics' and burden the community with verification.
- Undergrads lack the expertise to verify AI results, and publication isn't required.
- AI companies are exploiting mathematicians and engaging in research misconduct.
- The event will harm young mathematicians' reputations and careers.
- The organizers' changes are cosmetic; the only proper response is to cancel the event.
