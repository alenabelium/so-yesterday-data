## TL;DR

A new mathematical framework proposes two axioms—common ancestor point and convexity—to define biological species. The resulting species-like clusters are shown to be almost unique, challenging the long-held view that species are subjective. This work exemplifies mathematics aimed at understanding reality, especially relevant in the post-AI era.

## The argument

### Define the Core Axioms
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:03:45']
- **Claim**: The common ancestor point (AP) axiom states that for any organism in a species, either its descendants within the species eventually go extinct, or eventually all members of the species are its descendants. ### Species-Like Clusters Are Almost Unique
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:09:19']
- **Claim**: The objective species theorem shows that any two infinite species-like clusters (sets satisfying both axioms) either almost do not overlap or almost completely overlap. This eliminates the possibility of partial overlap, proving that species boundaries are objective in a technical sense, contrary to centuries of subjective interpretations.
- **Role**: evidence

### Address the Small-Cluster Problem
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:11:20']
- **Claim**: Singletons satisfy the two axioms, which is problematic because an individual organism should not be a separate species. To fix this, we add two additional constraints: the common ancestor property and the mapping property. These ensure that each organism belongs to a maximal species-like cluster, solving the problem of small clusters.
- **Role**: counter

### Prove Existence via Zorn's Lemma
- **Anchor Timestamps**: ['00:16:04']
- **Claim**: The proof of the main theorem is nontrivial because the AP axiom is not closed under ascending unions. By showing closure under descending intersections and ascending unions, and using Zorn's lemma and König's lemma, we guarantee that every organism belongs to a maximal species-like cluster with the additional properties.
- **Role**: synthesis

## Evidence and caveats

The speaker provides several arguments for the plausibility of the AP axiom, including the example that if your lineage never goes extinct, eventually all living humans will be your descendants. The convexity axiom is described as 'trivially obvious.' The speaker acknowledges that the proof is not Fields Medal-worthy, rating its difficulty as 3-4 out of 10 for a professional mathematician, but notes it is 'almost incomprehensibly difficult' for a biologist. The speaker also mentions that the AP axiom is a formalization of Hennig's indivisibility criterion, as pointed out by a reviewer. Future applications include explaining Darwin's law and improving evolutionary fitness measurement.
