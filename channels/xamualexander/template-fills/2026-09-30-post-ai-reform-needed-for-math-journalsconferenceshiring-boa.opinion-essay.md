## Thesis

Mathematics' incentive structures—journals, conferences, and hiring boards—must be reformed immediately to reward clarity and understanding over raw proof production, treating all results as AI-proven to prevent dishonesty.

## Argument

The mechanism is the prisoner's dilemma. With AI capable of solving research-level problems, any mathematician optimizing for priority must use AI to produce as many results as possible, as quickly as possible. This leads to a flood of unreadable, poorly-written papers—the archive submission counts in combinatorics have nearly doubled since 2025, and other fields are following. The system rewards solving problems, not communicating them, so that's what we get. Amit Harlove's proposal is radical: we must treat all results as AI-proven, because we cannot verify whether AI was used, and rewarding human-only proofs would incentivize lying. The value shifts to proof refinement and canonization—the slow, consensus-building work of making proofs clearer and more natural, which AI still struggles with. This is the most valuable part of mathematics, and it's what we should reward in hiring and publication.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that AI-proven results have zero value, even if human-verified, but this ignores the value of the verification and refinement process itself.
- Hiring committees face practical constraints: they cannot read 40-50 papers per applicant, so they rely on proxy metrics, making it hard to assess contributions to human understanding.
- The transition to new standards will take time, and some doubt it can be resolved within five years, given the inertia of academic institutions.
