## Thesis

Mathematics' incentive structures—journals, conferences, and hiring committees—must be reformed immediately to prevent a publication crisis and adapt to AI's exponential growth in proving results.

## Argument

The urgency is driven by an exponential surge in paper submissions, nearly doubling since 2025 in fields like combinatorics, triggered by AI models like GPT-5.2. This creates a prisoner's dilemma where mathematicians must use AI to stay competitive, leading to a flood of unreadable, low-quality papers. The host agrees with Amit Harlove's controversial stance that all results should be treated as AI-proven to prevent dishonesty, as verifying AI use is impossible. The value of mathematical work must shift from merely proving results to improving human understanding through clear presentation and proof refinement, a process aligned with Terence Tao's concept of canonization. This reform is urgent because the current system rewards speed over quality, and hiring committees and journals must update their guidelines to prioritize clarity and understanding over sheer output. The host emphasizes that without such changes, the discipline will drown in a 'crisis of crises,' and mathematicians will continue to lose sleep over priority disputes, as illustrated by Amit's personal anecdote of tweeting a hash of his proof to establish priority.
