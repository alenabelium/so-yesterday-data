## Thesis

Mathematics' incentive structures—journals, conferences, and hiring committees—must be reformed immediately to prevent a publication crisis and adapt to AI's exponential growth in proof generation.

## Argument

The urgency is driven by an exponential surge in paper submissions, doubling in hard-hit fields like combinatorics since 2025, and now spreading to others like algebraic geometry. This creates a prisoner's dilemma: to stay competitive, mathematicians must use AI to publish first, sacrificing quality and human understanding. The current system rewards solving and publishing first, not clarity or insight, leading to a flood of unreadable, low-value papers. Amit Harlove's proposal is to shift the value from merely proving results to improving human understanding through clear presentation, proof refinement, and canonization—the slow, consensus-based process of making a result natural and standard. This is the least automatable stage and the most valuable. To avoid incentivizing dishonesty, we must treat all results as AI-proven, applying the same standards to everyone. This means hiring committees should reward those who deepen collective understanding, not just produce results, and journals should reject poorly written papers outright. The crisis is already here; waiting for consensus is not an option.

## Counterpoints

- Some argue that AI-proven results have zero value, even if verified, but this ignores the value of the process and the human refinement that follows.
- Others worry that rewarding only human understanding will disincentivize speed, but the prisoner's dilemma makes speed necessary unless incentives change.
- A practical objection is that hiring committees are already overwhelmed and cannot read enough papers to assess understanding, but this only accelerates the need for new evaluation methods.
- There is skepticism that consensus on new norms will take too long, but the crisis demands immediate action, even if imperfect.
