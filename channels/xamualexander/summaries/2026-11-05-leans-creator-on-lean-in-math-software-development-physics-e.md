# Lean's creator on Lean in math, software development, physics, etc

## Executive Summary

Leo de Moura, creator of the Lean proof verification system, discusses how AI-powered autoformalization is transforming mathematics, software development, and other fields. He explains that AI has made formal verification practical by automating the tedious process of writing proofs, shifting the bottleneck to specification and modeling. The conversation covers Lean's role in verifying code, the importance of multiple independent kernels, and the future of formal verification in areas like physics, finance, and law. De Moura also reflects on the changing nature of expertise and the potential for unequal access to computing resources in science.

## Key Points

- AI has made formal verification practical by automating proof writing, shifting the challenge from proofs to specifications. [00:13:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=myQYZD8tIyA&t=796)
- Lean can verify code written in other languages, such as Rust via Aeneas, and AI can autoformalize entire programs for verification. [00:07:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=myQYZD8tIyA&t=426)
- Formal proofs are not static; they can be refactored, simplified, and explored with AI, making them living objects. [00:17:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=myQYZD8tIyA&t=1075)
- Testing is useful for finding bugs but cannot prove their absence; formal verification provides guarantees for all inputs. [00:20:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=myQYZD8tIyA&t=1245)
- The future of software engineering will focus more on writing formal specifications and models, with code becoming ephemeral and generated by AI. [00:30:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=myQYZD8tIyA&t=1817)
