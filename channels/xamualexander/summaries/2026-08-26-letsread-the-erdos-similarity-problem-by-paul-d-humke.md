# LetsRead "The Erdős Similarity Problem" (by Paul D. Humke)

## Executive Summary

The video is a reading and commentary on Paul D. Humke's article about the Erdős similarity problem, a famous open problem in real analysis. The speaker shares personal anecdotes about Paul Erdős, the problem's history, and its formulation, emphasizing the deceptively simple nature of the problem and the ongoing lack of a solution even for a specific simple set. He also mentions the reward offered for a solution and encourages viewers to explore the problem and related review articles.

## Key Points

- The Erdős similarity problem asks whether every infinite set of real numbers has a similar copy that is not contained in some positive measure set, and it remains unsolved. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PNbFh2P3LAg&t=0)
- The problem was first published by Erdős in 1974, and the speaker recounts personal memories of Erdős, including his visits and interactions with mathematicians. [00:01:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PNbFh2P3LAg&t=61)
- Even for the specific set E0 = {1/2^n}, it is unknown whether there exists a positive measure set avoiding a similar copy, and a $1,000 reward is offered for a solution. [00:07:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PNbFh2P3LAg&t=468)
