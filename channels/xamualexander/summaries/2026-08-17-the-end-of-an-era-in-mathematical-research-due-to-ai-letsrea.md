# The End of an Era in Mathematical Research (due to AI)? LetsRead Alonso Castillo-Ramirez

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reviews Alonso Castillo-Ramirez's article on AI's transformative impact on mathematical research, arguing that AI will shift mathematicians from proof creation to curation and understanding. He critiques the article's claim that teaching will remain human, finding it unsupported, and discusses the chess and Industrial Revolution analogies, along with commenter perspectives on AI's unprecedented nature and the potential loss of artisanal mathematics. Alexander concludes that while AI may change professional roles, human enjoyment of mathematics can persist, and he advises mathematicians to adapt by streaming their work to prove authenticity.

## Key Points

- AI models like GPT 5.6 Soul and Claude Fable 5 can now generate proofs, shifting mathematics from artisanal creation to curation and verification, with automated proof checkers like Lean and Coq aiding verification. [00:00:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kbrYmHaEFaI&t=59)
- The article's claim that teaching will remain human is baseless; AI can fulfill motivational and social roles, as evidenced by its use as therapists and friends. [00:07:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kbrYmHaEFaI&t=476)
- The chess analogy shows that AI superiority doesn't eliminate human enjoyment, but mathematics differs as a science with real-world implications; the Industrial Revolution analogy highlights potential human costs and the need for careful policy.
