# Can peer review survive AI-generated text?

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander critiques a paper proposing AI detectors and stricter review protocols to save peer review, arguing that the system is already overloaded and that AI will inevitably replace expert evaluation. He contends that technical errors will be eliminated by AI, making the proposed 'first error' rejection policy ineffective, and that the real challenge lies in assessing relevance and innovation. He suggests that peer review may become obsolete, drawing parallels to the Gutenberg press, and advocates for adapting to a post-AI world rather than trying to preserve a failing system.

## Key Points

- The paper's proposal to use AI detectors and reject flagged articles at the first error adds more load to an already overloaded system, and AI-generated articles will likely be free of technical errors, making this approach ineffective. [00:01:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PPFL7xdNAYY&t=104)
- The distinction between AI as a complement versus a replacement is flawed; in cases like the Jacobian conjecture, AI can fully replace human labor, and the burden of verification shifting to readers is an inevitable consequence of AI's cost reduction. [00:10:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PPFL7xdNAYY&t=617)
- The assumption that AI detectors like Pangram will remain reliable is questionable, as AI and human language evolve, and the proposed penalties for reviewers are impractical given the voluntary nature of reviewing. [00:12:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PPFL7xdNAYY&t=752)
