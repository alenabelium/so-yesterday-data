# Goodstein's Theorem: using infinity to prove a shocking result about arithmetic

## Executive Summary

Goodstein's theorem is a surprising result in arithmetic: starting from any natural number, repeatedly writing it in hereditary base notation, increasing the base, and subtracting one eventually yields zero, even though the numbers appear to grow astronomically. The proof uses infinite ordinal numbers, specifically omega, to show that each step decreases the corresponding ordinal, and since ordinals cannot have infinite descending sequences, the process must terminate. This theorem is notable because it cannot be proven using the standard axioms of Peano arithmetic, requiring stronger mathematical foundations.

## Key Points

- Goodstein's theorem states that repeatedly raising the base in hereditary base notation and subtracting one eventually reaches zero, despite the numbers growing enormously. [00:02:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xtiQLntt6mk&t=133)
- The proof works by replacing the base with omega, the first infinite ordinal, which turns the process into a strictly decreasing sequence of ordinals. [00:03:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xtiQLntt6mk&t=191)
- Since ordinal numbers cannot have infinite strictly decreasing sequences, the process must eventually reach zero. [00:06:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xtiQLntt6mk&t=416)
