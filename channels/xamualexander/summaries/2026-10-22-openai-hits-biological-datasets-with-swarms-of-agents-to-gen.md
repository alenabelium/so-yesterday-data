# OpenAI hits biological datasets with swarms of agents to generate leads (and hype)

## Executive Summary

OpenAI, in collaboration with Perturb AI and Scripps Research, used thousands of AI agents powered by GPT Rosalind to analyze a mouse brain CRISPR atlas, uncovering potential biological leads in just 32 minutes. While the results are notable for speed and scale, the findings are preliminary and lack experimental validation, leading the host to critique the hype surrounding the announcement, especially in light of OpenAI's IPO. The video compares this to AI's impact in mathematics, noting that biology's reliance on physical experiments makes it less scalable for AI-driven breakthroughs.

## Key Points

- OpenAI emphasizes the number of agents used (thousands) rather than the results, suggesting hype ahead of an IPO. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blc-wgIYLFw&t=0)
- The distributed agents reproduced human findings and uncovered additional leads, but the results are preliminary and not yet experimentally validated. [00:05:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blc-wgIYLFw&t=327)
- Distributed agents cited over 700 references compared to 11 for a single agent, but the chart is misleading due to a flat line. [00:10:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blc-wgIYLFw&t=640)
