# Elections, Ultrafilters and Hyperreals (elite mathematicians HATE this trick!)

## Executive Summary

This video presents an intuitive, voter-based analogy to introduce the mathematical concepts of free ultrafilters and hyperreal numbers. By imagining natural numbers voting on comparisons between functions, the video derives axioms for majority decisions and shows how, with infinitely many voters, these axioms can be satisfied without a dictator, leading to a non-Archimedean number system that includes infinitesimals and infinities. The video connects these ideas to Arrow's impossibility theorem and highlights the historical controversy over infinitesimals, ultimately crediting Abraham Robinson for their rigorous foundation.

## Key Points

- The video introduces the idea of numbers voting to decide which function is bigger on average, leading to the need for a decision method for infinitely many voters. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VBwO5A546F8&t=0)
- Three intuitive axioms for majorities (non-triviality, monotonicity, unambiguity) are presented, along with a fourth, transitivity, which forces the intersection of majorities to be a majority. [00:03:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VBwO5A546F8&t=216)
- The video shows that with infinitely many voters, these axioms can be satisfied without a dictator, which is equivalent to the existence of a free ultrafilter, and this leads to the construction of hyperreal numbers. [00:09:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VBwO5A546F8&t=557)
- Hyperreal numbers include infinite and infinitesimal numbers, and the video demonstrates that the identity function represents an infinite hyperreal and the reciprocal function represents a positive infinitesimal. [00:14:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VBwO5A546F8&t=876)
