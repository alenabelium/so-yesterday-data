# Guidelines released by the AI-in-Math Advisory Group

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander critiques the AI-in-Math Advisory Group's guidelines for releasing AI-generated mathematics, arguing that while some recommendations are sensible, many are impractical, self-serving, or hypocritical. He highlights the tension between traditional mathematical norms and the new reality of AI-generated proofs, and calls for more inclusive and transparent practices. The video ultimately urges the mathematical community to adapt to the changing landscape.

## Key Points

- The advisory group recommends AI labs release significant mathematical results ASAP, but Dr. Alexander notes this contradicts their stance on proprietary models and questions the group's authority. [00:07:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kterUWGqNz8&t=449)
- Dr. Alexander criticizes the recommendation that AI labs fund human understanding of AI-generated results, calling it self-serving and pointing out potential conflicts of interest. [00:09:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kterUWGqNz8&t=584)
- He praises the call for persistent identifiers and version control in repositories, but suggests Git as a superior alternative to arXiv. [00:17:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kterUWGqNz8&t=1049)
- He points out the omission of random seeds and determinism in the recommendations, which are crucial for reproducibility. [00:23:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kterUWGqNz8&t=1398)
- He calls out the hypocrisy of the group's stance on open access, demanding they also address the high costs of open access publishing. [00:31:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kterUWGqNz8&t=1869)
