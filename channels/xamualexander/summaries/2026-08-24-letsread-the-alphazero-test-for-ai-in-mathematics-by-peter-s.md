# LetsRead "The AlphaZero Test" for AI in Mathematics (by Peter Sarnak)

## Executive Summary

Peter Sarnak, a renowned number theorist, discusses the potential impact of AI on mathematics, drawing parallels to AlphaZero's revolution in chess. He proposes an 'AlphaZero Test' where a theorem-proving AI, starting from scratch without external data, must prove a famous conjecture like Ramanujan's. Sarnak is skeptical that AI will surpass the threshold of statistical complexity in mathematics, predicting it will only handle problems within existing theories, but acknowledges that if it does, the nature of mathematics itself would need to be rethought. The video's host, Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander, adds his own analysis, highlighting fundamental differences between chess and mathematics, such as the lack of a losing condition in mathematics, which may hinder AI's self-play learning approach.

## Key Points

- Peter Sarnak, a famous mathematician, shares his initial skepticism about AI, prompted by his student Jacob Zimmerman's enthusiasm for AI solving math Olympiad problems, and discusses how he began to take AI seriously. [00:01:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K9Hmg17XJ0k&t=76)
- Sarnak introduces the 'AlphaZero Test': a theorem-proving program with no external data must decide the truth of a statement like Ramanujan's conjecture, and he suspects it will fail, predicting AI will only handle problems within existing mathematical theories. [00:07:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K9Hmg17XJ0k&t=443)
- The host highlights a key difference between chess and mathematics: in chess, games end with a win/loss, but in mathematics there is no losing condition, which may prevent AI self-play from working as effectively as it does in games. [00:10:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K9Hmg17XJ0k&t=651)
