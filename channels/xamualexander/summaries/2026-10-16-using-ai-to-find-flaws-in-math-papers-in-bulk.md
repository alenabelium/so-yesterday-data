# Using AI to find flaws in math papers in bulk

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reviews a study using GPT-6.1 to automatically detect errors in arXiv math papers, finding serious errors in 6% of pre-AI papers and a drop to 1.5% after AI adoption. The study uses a pipeline of theorem extraction, counterexample search, and verification to reduce hallucinations, though it acknowledges a 10% false positive rate. Alexander argues that AI is improving mathematical reliability and heralds a new golden age for the field, despite some mathematicians' resistance to AI-generated corrections.

## Key Points

- The study finds serious errors in 6% of arXiv math papers from Jan 2025 to July 2026, but this is likely an underestimate because counterexamples can be hard to find. [00:05:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0nPeEIKAGDQ&t=322)
- After AI use became widespread in August-September 2026, the serious error rate dropped to 1.5%, suggesting AI improves mathematical reliability. [00:05:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0nPeEIKAGDQ&t=349)
- The pipeline uses four stages: theorem extraction, counterexample search, verification, and checking later versions, with a total cost of about $2,500 in API credits. [00:16:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0nPeEIKAGDQ&t=978)
