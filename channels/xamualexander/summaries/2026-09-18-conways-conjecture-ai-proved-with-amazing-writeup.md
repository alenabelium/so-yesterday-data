# Conway's Conjecture AI-proved, with AMAZING writeup!

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reviews Dan Abramov's blog post about using AI to prove Conway's conjecture on omnific integers, a 50-year-old open problem in surreal number theory. The video details Abramov's month-long, multi-agent AI workflow involving Claude and ChatGPT, highlighting the challenges of hallucinated mathematics, the importance of Lean formal verification, and the eventual successful proof. The reviewer emphasizes the shift from AI-generated 'word salad' to verified results, the strategic use of multiple AI agents with distinct roles, and the broader implications for amateur mathematicians and the future of mathematical research.

## Key Points

- Dan Abramov spent a month using AI agents to prove Conway's conjecture, a 50-year-old open problem about omnific integers, with the proof verified in Lean. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CFkrHlkrH24&t=0)
- Initial attempts with Claude produced 'word salad'—impressive-sounding but unverifiable claims—leading to a switch to ChatGPT for a more skeptical and grounded approach. [00:10:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CFkrHlkrH24&t=659)
- The winning workflow involved a multi-agent laboratory with roles like project manager, math agents, red team, and Lean agents, plus a 'burn it all down' strategy to salvage only verified results. [00:15:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CFkrHlkrH24&t=930)
- The proof was eventually completed and kernel-checked in Lean, with the final breakthrough coming from a revised induction strategy suggested by ChatGPT. [00:37:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CFkrHlkrH24&t=2236)
- The project consumed an estimated 40 billion tokens (costing ~$40,000), but the author believes the approach could be 5-10 times cheaper with better steering, and the episode illustrates how AI can empower amateur mathematicians. [00:46:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CFkrHlkrH24&t=2793)
