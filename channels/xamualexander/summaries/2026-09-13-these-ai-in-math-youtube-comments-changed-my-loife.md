# These AI-in-Math YouTube comments changed my LOIFE!

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reads and responds to YouTube comments about AI in mathematics, focusing on the controversy over OpenAI allegedly using private research to solve the Navier-Stokes millennium problem. He discusses the implications for academia, intellectual property, and the future of mathematical research, arguing that AI is transforming the field and that the drama is ultimately beneficial for mathematics. He also addresses concerns about AI safety, resource competition, and the role of human mathematicians in an AI-driven era.

## Key Points

- OpenAI's handling of the Navier-Stokes solution, including the demand to remove a researcher from the paper, was a major misstep that overshadowed the breakthrough and triggered a PR backlash. [00:14:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zQhVDlD833k&t=885)
- The academic community's gatekeeping and slow publication processes are outdated; big tech's approach of allowing open publication and using indexing/ranking is more suited to the 21st century. [00:04:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zQhVDlD833k&t=293)
- The controversy over whether OpenAI stole the solution is less important than the fact that AI solved a millennium problem, which will bring new blood and interest to mathematics. [00:44:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zQhVDlD833k&t=2648)
- AI is unreasonably effective at mathematics, which suggests our own minds may work similarly to neural nets, and this will fundamentally change how we approach ethics and privacy. [00:18:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zQhVDlD833k&t=1095)
- The demand by established mathematicians to cancel an undergrad AI mathathon is out of touch and reflects a denial of AI's role in the future of mathematical research. [00:49:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zQhVDlD833k&t=2977)
