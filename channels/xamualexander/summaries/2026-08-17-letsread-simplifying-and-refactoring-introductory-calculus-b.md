# LetsRead "Simplifying and Refactoring Introductory Calculus" (by Jonathan Bartlett)

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reviews Jonathan Bartlett's article proposing a refactoring of introductory calculus education, drawing parallels to technical debt in software engineering. The article advocates for introducing derivatives before limits, using differentials instead of derivatives to unify explicit, implicit, and multivariable differentiation, and treating integrals as infinite sums rather than areas. It also suggests replacing logarithmic differentiation with a generalized power rule and using hyperreal numbers for a more intuitive understanding of limits. Alexander praises the article, noting its alignment with his own independent research on differentials.

## Key Points

- The article applies the software engineering concept of refactoring to calculus education, aiming to reduce technical debt by streamlining processes and unifying concepts. [00:01:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T7XayMbPtTY&t=74)
- The author recommends introducing derivatives before limits, using practical slope problems to build intuition, and moving limits to the end of the course. [00:04:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T7XayMbPtTY&t=254)
- Using differentials instead of derivatives simplifies implicit differentiation, multivariable calculus, and higher-order derivatives, preserving symmetry and enabling algebraic manipulation. [00:10:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T7XayMbPtTY&t=631)
- The article suggests replacing logarithmic differentiation with a generalized power rule for u^v, and defining integrals as infinite sums to generalize their application. [00:26:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T7XayMbPtTY&t=1578)
- Hyperreal numbers are proposed as a more intuitive approach to limits, though historical and philosophical resistance to infinitesimals has hindered their adoption. [00:29:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T7XayMbPtTY&t=1743)
