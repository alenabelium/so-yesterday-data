# Singapore's state-run dating app uses Gale-Shapley matching algorithm

## Executive Summary

This video discusses Singapore's state-run dating app 'First Date' which uses the Gale-Shapley stable marriage algorithm to match public servants aged 21-35. The host explains the algorithm's mechanics, its proposer-optimal bias, and contrasts it with for-profit dating apps that have perverse incentives to keep users single. He explores various Hacker News comments debating whether dating problems are matching or clearing problems, the impact of preferences and standards, and the potential societal implications. The video concludes by reflecting on the long-term uncertainty of the app's success and the broader role of mathematics in solving societal issues.

## Key Points

- Singapore's government dating app uses the Gale-Shapley algorithm, which guarantees stable matches but is proposer-optimal, meaning proposers get their best possible match while receivers get their worst. [00:05:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xTq0PpASM1A&t=351)
- For-profit dating apps have perverse incentives to keep users single, whereas Singapore's app is designed to get users off the platform, making it a unique experiment in state-sponsored matchmaking. [00:04:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xTq0PpASM1A&t=279)
- The dating market is not just a matching problem but a clearing problem, where many people cannot meet the 'reserve price' of their desired partners, making it harder to solve than simply matching preferences. [00:25:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xTq0PpASM1A&t=1518)
