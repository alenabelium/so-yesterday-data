# If math is more than proof, how can we better celebrate the rest of it?

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reviews Grant Sanderson's guest post on Terence Tao's blog, which argues that mathematics should celebrate 'motivated explanations' alongside proofs, especially as AI can now generate proofs without advancing human understanding. Alexander agrees with the need to reform how mathematical credit is assigned but repeatedly emphasizes the central problem of subjectivity in evaluating exposition, warning that without objective standards, meritocracy in mathematics could collapse into tribal politics. He also discusses the existential pressure AI places on the field, the need for cultural shifts to attract young mathematicians, and the risk that AI may eventually excel at exposition too, undermining the proposed solution.

## Key Points

- Alexander introduces Sanderson's proposal to elevate 'motivated explanations' as a complement to proofs, noting the historical role of proofs as a proxy for both understanding and meritocracy. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R FrImTPMZKs&t=0)
- He highlights the core tension: motivated explanations are inherently subjective, making them vulnerable to bias and corruption, unlike the objective verifiability of proofs. [00:02:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R FrImTPMZKs&t=126)
- He points to the example of the AI-assisted proof of the primitive sets conjecture, where the follow-up paper clarifying the key idea arguably deserves more credit than the proof itself. [00:19:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R FrImTPMZKs&t=1180)
- He discusses the practical suggestions from the article, such as treating small problems like PhD defenses and creating journals focused on exposition, but reiterates that these still face the subjectivity problem. [00:22:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R FrImTPMZKs&t=1325)
- He concludes that the field must find a way to maintain meritocracy without relying solely on proofs, warning that otherwise mathematics could become like other humanities, driven by personal connections rather than objective achievement. [00:39:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R FrImTPMZKs&t=2390)
