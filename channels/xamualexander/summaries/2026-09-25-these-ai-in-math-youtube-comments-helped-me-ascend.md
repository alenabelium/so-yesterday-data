# These AI-in-Math YouTube comments helped me ascend

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reads and responds to viewer comments on his AI-in-mathematics videos, addressing debates about whether AI will make mathematicians obsolete, the value of proofs versus conceptual understanding, and the future of mathematical research. He argues that fears of obsolescence are overblown, that AI makes mathematics more accessible, and that mathematicians should embrace AI as a tool for solving harder problems rather than resisting it. He also highlights collaborative AI-assisted research, the potential for AI to solve open problems, and the need to adapt to changing incentives in academia.

## Key Points

- Mathematicians' identity crisis is internal; AI won't make them obsolete, just as digital photography didn't eliminate photographers. [00:01:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umNbtzMI-Ls&t=78)
- AI makes frontier mathematics accessible to non-experts, allowing them to 'touch the stuff' instead of just consuming math content. [00:04:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umNbtzMI-Ls&t=277)
- The 25 Fields medalists' letter against AI-assisted proofs is criticized as a self-interested reaction to protect prestige, not a genuine concern for understanding. [00:07:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umNbtzMI-Ls&t=464)
- Mathematicians should focus on using AI to solve harder problems and innovate, rather than whining about job security. [00:15:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umNbtzMI-Ls&t=944)
- AI-assisted research is collaborative and can lead to new discoveries, as seen in the Medvedev logic paper where two groups joined forces. [00:27:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umNbtzMI-Ls&t=1649)
