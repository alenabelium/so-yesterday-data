# LetsRead "Curve-Counting and Mirror Symmetry" (by Emily Clader)

## Executive Summary

This video is a read-through of Emily Clader's article 'Curve-Counting and Mirror Symmetry' from the Notices of the AMS, presented by Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander. The article traces the historical development of enumerative geometry from ancient Greek problems to modern Gromov-Witten invariants and mirror symmetry. It explains the role of moduli spaces, virtual fundamental classes, and the connection to string theory, culminating in Givental's mirror theorem and quasi-map theory. The presenter acknowledges the material's difficulty but emphasizes the value of reading beyond one's current understanding.

## Key Points

- The article begins with classical curve-counting problems, such as conics through five points, and explains how modern approaches use deformation invariance and moduli spaces to make counts rigorous. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3BDfhTz3y2A&t=0)
- Gromov-Witten invariants are defined using moduli spaces of stable maps and a virtual fundamental class, which is necessary because the moduli space may have components of different dimensions. [00:14:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3BDfhTz3y2A&t=882)
- Mirror symmetry, originating from string theory, predicts a relationship between Gromov-Witten invariants of a Calabi-Yau manifold and period integrals of its mirror, leading to Givental's mirror theorem and quasi-map theory. [00:36:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3BDfhTz3y2A&t=2207)
