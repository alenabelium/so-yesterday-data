# They surveyed 260 young mathematicians about AI in Math

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander analyzes a survey of 260 young mathematicians about AI's impact on mathematics, highlighting widespread career uncertainty, decreased motivation, and concerns about hiring standards and funding flows. He critiques the survey's design and respondents' habits, while expressing optimism about AI's potential despite most respondents' skepticism. The video concludes with a call for updated data as attitudes evolve.

## Key Points

- 56% of surveyed mathematicians are considering changing career plans due to AI, but only 8% have decided to do so [00:01:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJozabQj2EA&t=105).
- Most respondents believe AI will make it harder to get a permanent position, reflecting an already brutal job market [00:04:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJozabQj2EA&t=275).
- 85% use AI for literature search, but only 68% use it for editing and error correction, which the speaker strongly criticizes [00:09:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJozabQj2EA&t=552).
- The vast majority dislike it when AI solves a problem they were working on, with only six respondents saying they are very happy [00:17:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJozabQj2EA&t=1030).
- 69% are concerned about funding flowing from academia to private companies, and 67% worry about future hiring standards [00:25:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJozabQj2EA&t=1553).
