# LetsRead "Survival, Prediction, and the Perverse Law of Waiting" (by Bradley Efron)

## Executive Summary

This video is a detailed reading of Bradley Efron's article 'Survival, Prediction, and the Perverse Law of Expectations,' which explores how statistical methods handle randomness in survival analysis and prediction. The host explains key concepts like Kaplan-Meier curves, hazard rates, censored data, and micromorts, illustrating them with medical and societal examples. The discussion also covers the 'perverse law of waiting,' where expected waiting times can increase with elapsed time, and contrasts traditional statistical modeling with modern pure prediction algorithms like random forests and AI. The video concludes by reflecting on the limitations of correlation-based predictions and the enduring value of scientific modeling.

## Key Points

- Survival curves and hazard rates are fundamental tools for comparing treatments, as shown in the head and neck cancer trial where adding radiation improved survival. [00:01:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZYQN3DSh1Q&t=84)
- Censored data complicates survival analysis, but hazard rates neutralize its effects by using only available data at each time point. [00:12:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZYQN3DSh1Q&t=761)
- The 'perverse law of waiting' applies when risk levels decrease over time, leading to paradoxical expectations, unlike human survival where risk increases with age. [00:14:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZYQN3DSh1Q&t=886)
- Pure prediction algorithms like random forests can achieve high accuracy on training data but may fail on new data due to concept drift, highlighting the importance of scientific modeling. [00:28:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZYQN3DSh1Q&t=1695)
