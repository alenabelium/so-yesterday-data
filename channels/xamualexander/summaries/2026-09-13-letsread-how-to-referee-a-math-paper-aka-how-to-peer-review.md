# LetsRead "How to Referee a Math Paper" (aka how to Peer Review it), by Eric Rowland

## Executive Summary

This video discusses Eric Rowland's article on how to referee a math paper, arguing that the primary role of a reviewer is not to certify correctness but to help authors communicate their ideas effectively. The reviewer should focus on readability, examples, notation, and high-level explanations rather than verifying every proof detail. The video emphasizes that reviewers bring a fresh perspective and can identify where intuition is lacking, making their feedback invaluable for improving the paper's clarity and impact.

## Key Points

- Reviewers should not aim to certify correctness; instead, they should help authors communicate with the community, as authors already have thorough training and focus on correctness. [00:00:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tjGpiyTWoxU&t=57)
- The three most important factors for readability are examples, examples, and more examples; reviewers should check if there are enough examples and if notations are self-explanatory. [00:05:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tjGpiyTWoxU&t=302)
- Reviewers should read freely, skip proofs, and demand that authors provide high-level explanations and summaries, as readers are unlikely to read at the proof level. [00:08:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tjGpiyTWoxU&t=486)
