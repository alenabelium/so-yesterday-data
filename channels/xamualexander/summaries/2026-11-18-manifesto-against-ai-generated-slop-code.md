# Manifesto against AI-generated slop-code

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reviews the 'No AI Slop' Manifesto from the AIE Code Summit 2026, which argues that AI-generated content becomes 'slop' when creators invest less effort than consumers must spend to understand it. He endorses the manifesto's principles—such as human responsibility, judgment, and the asymmetry of creation versus validation—while adding his own commentary on academia, software development culture, and the historical persistence of low-quality output. He also analyzes Hacker News reactions, debating the value of AI in coding, the role of prompts in version control, and the tension between artistic craftsmanship and commercial pressures.

## Key Points

- The manifesto defines 'slop' as putting less effort into creating something than the consumer spends understanding it, a definition Dr. Alexander praises. [00:02:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gi-Q82xiC4Q&t=147)
- AI cannot replace human judgment, especially as a liability holder, since you can't sue an AI—contracting firms provide scapegoat utility. [00:06:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gi-Q82xiC4Q&t=370)
- The asymmetry of 'slop'—creation cost approaches zero while validation cost remains—is particularly problematic in academia, where journals are inundated with submissions. [00:07:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gi-Q82xiC4Q&t=435)
- Dr. Alexander suggests that prompts, not AI-generated code, should be under version control, as code not written by a human shouldn't be in the repository. [00:22:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gi-Q82xiC4Q&t=1362)
- The discussion on Hacker News highlights a culture war between developers who value craftsmanship and owners who prioritize profit, with Dr. Alexander advising to sell long-term support rather than artistic value. [00:28:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gi-Q82xiC4Q&t=1693)
