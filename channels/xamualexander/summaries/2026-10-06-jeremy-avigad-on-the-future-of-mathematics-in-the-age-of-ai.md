# Jeremy Avigad on the future of mathematics in the age of AI

## Executive Summary

Jeremy Avigad argues that AI's impact on mathematics, while disruptive to traditional workflows, presents an opportunity for the field to evolve rather than decline. He emphasizes that mathematics is a culture of rigorous reasoning and communication, and that mathematicians should focus on solving harder problems, thinking bigger thoughts, and trying new things. Avigad encourages embracing AI and proof assistants as legitimate tools for mathematical discovery, and reassures the next generation that mathematics remains vital and that the community stands with them. The video host, Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander, echoes this optimism, comparing the current era to a new frontier and urging mathematicians to adopt a glass-half-full perspective.

## Key Points

- AI can now easily generate results that a year ago would have been respectable publications, disrupting traditional mathematical workflows and raising questions about the future of the discipline. [00:02:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ9GhBpQ_uA&t=166)
- Avigad suggests three responses: solve harder problems, think bigger thoughts, and try new things, emphasizing that mathematicians must retain agency over what is considered interesting and important. [00:03:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ9GhBpQ_uA&t=226)
- Avigad argues that developing symbolic automation or training neural networks is as much a contribution to mathematics as traditional theorem proving, and that expanding the view of mathematics is the best way to keep the profession strong.
