# OpenAI withdraws three AI-generated math papers due to a sign error

## Executive Summary

OpenAI withdrew three AI-generated math papers after a single sign error invalidated a core argument that two other papers depended on. The error was caught by a human mathematician, not by the AI, and the withdrawal rate is less than 1% of the nearly 400 papers released. The incident has sparked debate about the reliability of AI-generated proofs, the role of Lean formal verification, and the future of mathematical research. The video argues that AI-generated math is still a net positive, and that human error is just as common, if not more so, in traditional mathematics.

## Key Points

- OpenAI withdrew three papers due to a single sign error that invalidated a stabilization trace cancellation argument, affecting two dependent papers. [00:02:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kiwzc3e5SQI&t=164)
- The error was found by a human mathematician, not by the AI, and the withdrawal rate is less than 1% of the nearly 400 papers released. [00:16:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kiwzc3e5SQI&t=1012)
- The incident highlights the importance of Lean formal verification, but also the challenge of translating plain English theorems into formal statements. [00:12:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kiwzc3e5SQI&t=774)
