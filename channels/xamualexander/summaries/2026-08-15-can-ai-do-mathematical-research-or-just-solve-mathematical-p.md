# Can AI do mathematical research, or just solve mathematical problems? (LetsRead Morgan Jones)

## Executive Summary

The video reviews Morgan Jones's article on whether AI can conduct original mathematical research or merely solve well-defined problems. It argues that while AI can reproduce completed research, it struggles with the exploratory, iterative process of discovering false assumptions and building theories, which is central to human mathematical work. The author highlights the importance of long-term memory and adaptation, noting that current models fail at tasks like ARC AGI-3, and concludes that human theorists still have a role, though the distinction may blur as AI improves.

## Key Points

- The article distinguishes between reproducing completed research and conducting the research itself, noting that AI can likely reproduce aspects of graduate student papers but not the exploratory process behind them. [00:01:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MRelXi9TUic&t=93)
- Mathematical research involves iterative failure and learning from mistakes, as illustrated by the feedback linearization project where failures revealed hidden assumptions and led to new theoretical insights. [00:05:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MRelXi9TUic&t=308)
- Current AI models struggle with long-term context and cumulative errors, making them poor at tasks like ARC AGI-3 that require continuous adaptation, but the author sees no fundamental reason why AI couldn't eventually automate this process. [00:08:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MRelXi9TUic&t=520)
