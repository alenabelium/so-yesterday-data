# Jan von Plato used AI to decipher Kurt Gödel's handwritten notebooks

## Executive Summary

The video discusses a Guardian article claiming that AI, specifically Claude, helped decipher Kurt Gödel's handwritten notebooks, revealing he was close to proving the independence of the axiom of choice decades before Paul Cohen. The host, Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander, expresses skepticism about the novelty and sensationalism of the claims, noting that the notebooks were already transcribed by a couple and that the AI's findings may be overhyped. He highlights the potential significance of Gödel's unpublished work and the role of AI in historical mathematical research, while also questioning the lack of broader academic discussion.

## Key Points

- The Guardian article claims AI deciphered Gödel's notebooks, revealing he was close to proving the independence of the axiom of choice, but the host is skeptical of the sensationalism. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Bv24QVmnQU&t=0)
- A Hacker News commenter notes that the notebooks were already transcribed by a couple, John and Cheryl Dawson, casting doubt on the novelty of the AI's deciphering. [00:02:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Bv24QVmnQU&t=139)
- Von Plato used Claude to analyze Gödel's notebooks, producing a 650-page explanation in days, and the reconstructed proof of the axiom of choice's independence passed the proof assistant Coq. [00:05:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Bv24QVmnQU&t=349)
- Claude found 663 theorems in the notebooks, with about 400 verified, 200 repaired, and 50 false, showing Gödel's own critical annotations. [00:14:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Bv24QVmnQU&t=862)
- The host highlights Gödel's unexplored physics and cosmology notebooks as potentially more interesting than the logic findings, and notes the lack of broader academic discussion beyond the Guardian article. [00:16:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Bv24QVmnQU&t=963)
