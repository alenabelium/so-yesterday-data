# Math AI Advisory Group on OpenAI's ETH/YangMills/Smoothing breakthroughs

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander discusses OpenAI's three recent mathematical breakthroughs and the Math AI Advisory Group's recommendations. He supports the immediate public release of AI-generated mathematical output, criticizing OpenAI's practice of designating specific mathematicians or physicists to shepherd results. He argues this 'kingmaking' creates unequal access, denies community agency, and risks biased selection, while celebrating the organic, grassroots mathematical community that has emerged from open releases. He also notes the advisory group's statement was released a day before OpenAI's announcements, suggesting possible internal tensions.

## Key Points

- Dr. Alexander endorses the advisory group's call for OpenAI to immediately release the Yang-Mills and related physics outputs, arguing that mathematicians should share data openly rather than hide it. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=npfmLIXLRD4&t=0)
- He criticizes OpenAI's decision to designate specific mathematicians and physicists as 'shepherds' for their breakthroughs, arguing this practice creates unequal access, denies community agency, and risks biased selection. [00:04:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=npfmLIXLRD4&t=279)
- He highlights the advisory group's recommendation that AI labs should follow open release pathways, including posting on independent repositories, providing process information, and lean formalization, while noting the group's statement was released a day before OpenAI's announcements, suggesting possible infighting.
