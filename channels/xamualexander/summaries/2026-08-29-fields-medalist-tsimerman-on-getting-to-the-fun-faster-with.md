# Fields Medalist Tsimerman on "Getting to the Fun Faster with AI" in mathematics

## Executive Summary

Fields Medalist Jacob Tsimerman discusses how AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude have doubled his mathematical productivity by accelerating the 'boring parts'—literature searches, orientation, and proof cleanup—while leaving the creative core of research untouched. He warns that AI will soon surpass human mathematicians at all aspects of the discipline, potentially ending it as a profession, and urges the community to integrate AI now to avoid being caught off guard. Tsimerman also co-authored a taxonomy of AI-related omnicidal risks, emphasizing the need for cultural familiarity and proactive adaptation.

## Key Points

- Tsimerman reports that AI boosts his productivity by a factor of two, mainly by speeding up literature searches, orientation, and cleanup tasks, while the creative 'fun' parts of math remain human-driven. [00:09:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GZcD6BoJuas&t=576)
- He describes a practical workflow: using AI for 'dumb questions' in unfamiliar fields, asking for overviews of known results, and having it prove simple lemmas—though he always verifies outputs because AI often hallucinates and rarely recovers from errors. [00:07:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GZcD6BoJuas&t=467)
- Tsimerman predicts AI will surpass human mathematicians in all aspects within about five years, potentially ending mathematics as a profession, and stresses the importance of staying abreast of AI to avoid being left behind. [00:18:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GZcD6BoJuas&t=1108)
