# LetsRead "The Sesquicentennial of the Prime Number 2^127-1" (by Matthew Baker)

## Executive Summary

This video is a reading and commentary on Matthew Baker's article about the 150th anniversary of the discovery that 2^127-1 is prime. The host, Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander, explains the historical context, Édouard Lucas's innovative methods, and the modern Lucas-Lehmer test. He highlights how Lucas's manual chessboard calculations evolved into the algorithms used today in the Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search. The video concludes with reflections on the role of AI in accelerating mathematical discovery.

## Key Points

- Édouard Lucas proved 2^127-1 is prime in 1876, and it remained the largest known prime for 75 years. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_LtQyPQ98k4&t=0)
- Lucas used a 127x127 chessboard to perform the modular squaring calculations by hand, encoding binary numbers with pawns. [00:06:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_LtQyPQ98k4&t=365)
- The Lucas-Lehmer test, based on Lucas's ideas, is still used today to verify Mersenne primes, including the current record prime found in 2024. [00:08:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_LtQyPQ98k4&t=482)
