# LetsRead "Rate-Based Synaptic Plasticity Rules" (by Lawrence Udeigwe)

## Executive Summary

This video is a detailed walkthrough of Lawrence Udeigwe's article on rate-based synaptic plasticity rules, presented by Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander. The video explains foundational concepts like Hebb's rule, its instability, and subsequent stabilization methods such as Oja's rule and the BCM learning rule. It also discusses the role of homeostatic plasticity, the debate over its time scales, and recent models incorporating multiple firing rate set points. The presenter highlights the mathematical derivations and biological implications, while also pointing out potential typos and areas needing clarification.

## Key Points

- Hebb's rule states that synaptic strength increases when a presynaptic neuron repeatedly participates in firing a postsynaptic neuron, but this rule is unstable without compensatory mechanisms. [00:02:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F47kXPkZ6iQ&t=120)
- Oja's rule introduces weight decay to stabilize Hebbian learning, effectively making the neuron a principal component analyzer. [00:12:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F47kXPkZ6iQ&t=773)
- The BCM learning rule uses a sliding threshold to provide stability and has been used to model neuronal responses under different rearing conditions, including amblyopia recovery. [00:15:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F47kXPkZ6iQ&t=927)
- There is a debate between theoretical models requiring fast homeostatic plasticity and experimental observations showing slower time scales, leading to the concept of rapid compensatory processes (RCPs). [00:29:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F47kXPkZ6iQ&t=1790)
- Recent models propose multiple firing rate set points to allow for selective responses while maintaining stability, showing complex dynamics like bistability and chaos. [00:31:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F47kXPkZ6iQ&t=1883)
