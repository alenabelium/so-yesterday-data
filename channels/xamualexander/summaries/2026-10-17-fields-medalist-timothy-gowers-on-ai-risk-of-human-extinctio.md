# Fields medalist Timothy Gowers on AI risk of human extinction

## Executive Summary

Fields medalist Timothy Gowers discusses his evolving views on existential risk from AI, explaining why he joined the Mathematical AI Security Institute and why mathematicians are well-suited to contribute to AI safety. He walks through first-level arguments and their rebuttals, evaluates the probability of extinction scenarios, and concludes that while he cannot assign a specific probability, the risk is serious enough to warrant caution and further theoretical work. The video also features commentary from Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander, who critiques Gowers' reasoning and offers his own perspective on AI risks.

## Key Points

- Gowers explains why he joined the Mathematical AI Security Institute, citing the high stakes and the potential for mathematicians to reduce existential risk by even a small percentage. [00:01:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9ecnLsfltQ&t=75)
- Gowers discusses the hierarchy of arguments about AI risk, noting that many common objections are 'level one' arguments that have known rebuttals. [00:05:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9ecnLsfltQ&t=323)
- Gowers uses a probabilistic model to argue that while individual extinction scenarios may be unlikely, the cumulative risk could be significant if scenarios are independent. [00:23:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9ecnLsfltQ&t=1428)
- Gowers highlights the difficulty of verifying superintelligent AI and mentions 'debate' as a potential scalable supervision technique. [00:42:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9ecnLsfltQ&t=2551)
- Gowers concludes that while he cannot assign a specific probability to existential risk, the potential consequences are severe enough to warrant pausing development and investing in theoretical safety research. [00:46:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9ecnLsfltQ&t=2804)
