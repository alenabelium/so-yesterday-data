# Post-AI reform needed for math journals/conferences/hiring boards

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reviews Amit Harlove's article urging immediate reform in mathematics' incentive structures—journals, conferences, and hiring committees—in response to AI's rapid capability growth. The article highlights an exponential surge in paper submissions, a prisoner's dilemma where mathematicians must use AI to stay competitive, and proposes that value should shift from merely proving results to improving human understanding through clear presentation and proof refinement. Alexander agrees with the controversial stance that all results should be treated as AI-proven to prevent dishonesty, and emphasizes the urgency of systemic change to avoid a publication crisis.

## Key Points

- Amit Harlove's article argues that math organizations must immediately update incentives to encourage valuable activities in the post-AI era, such as clear presentation and proof refinement, rather than just proving complex results. [00:01:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iXnH3gDeZ5A&t=78)
- The number of math papers on arXiv has nearly doubled since 2025, especially in combinatorics and metric geometry, leading to a publication crisis and a prisoner's dilemma where mathematicians feel forced to use AI to stay competitive. [00:03:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iXnH3gDeZ5A&t=208)
- Harlove proposes that all results should be treated as AI-proven to avoid incentivizing dishonesty, and that credit should go to those who improve collective understanding through clear writing and canonization, not just first publication. [00:20:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iXnH3gDeZ5A&t=1236)
