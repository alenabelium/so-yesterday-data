# Everyone's wrong about 0^0 and it's hilarious

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander argues that 0^0 is universally equal to 1 by definition, not by proof, and criticizes mathematicians, software like Wolfram Alpha, and even Terence Tao for treating it as undefined or indeterminate. He uses the Maclaurin series for e^x and the Lean theorem prover as evidence, and traces historical confusion to Cauchy's use of 0^0 as a symbol for indeterminate forms. He concludes that the widespread disagreement stems from conflating the definition with limit behavior, and that the mathematical community's stubbornness on this point is both amusing and unfounded.

## Key Points

- Wolfram Alpha contradicts itself by accepting the Maclaurin series for e^x (which requires 0^0=1) while claiming the sum is undefined at x=0 due to 0^0 being undefined. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aEodSCgnuc8&t=0)
- The Lean theorem prover defines 0^0=1 for real, complex, natural, and integer numbers, making it the highest source of mathematical truth in 2026. [00:02:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aEodSCgnuc8&t=140)
- Terence Tao is wrong in his 2025 AMS article for suggesting 0^0 has different interpretations across number systems; it is always 1 by definition. [00:10:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aEodSCgnuc8&t=618)
- Historical confusion stems from Cauchy's use of 0^0 as a symbol for indeterminate forms, not as a statement about the value of 0^0 itself. [00:21:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aEodSCgnuc8&t=1260)
- Even AI models have been trained on this confusion, hallucinating that 0^0 is undefined despite Lean's definition, showing how deeply ingrained the error is. [00:25:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aEodSCgnuc8&t=1533)
