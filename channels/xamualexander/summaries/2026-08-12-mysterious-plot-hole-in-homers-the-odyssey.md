# Mysterious Plot Hole in Homer's "The Odyssey"

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander discusses a plot hole in Homer's Odyssey where Odysseus predicts the Cyclops Polyphemus's dialogue with his neighbors after blinding him, a feat requiring superhuman foresight. He connects this to his own philosophical work 'Meno 2,' a self-referential Socratic dialogue that encodes a computer program, arguing that both examples push the boundaries of narrative logic and self-reference.

## Key Points

- Odysseus's 'Nobody' trick relies on predicting the exact dialogue between Polyphemus and his neighbors, which seems impossible without superhuman knowledge. [00:05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o9KVgKC59vg&t=350)
- Dr. Alexander's article 'Meno 2' uses a similar plot hole, where Socrates crafts a dialogue that encodes a Python program, requiring foreknowledge of every word Meno will say. [00:08:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o9KVgKC59vg&t=507)
- The program in 'Meno 2' ultimately predicts Meno's response by using the password 'I don't know any passwords, Socrates,' echoing the self-referential nature of the Odyssey's plot hole. [00:13:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o9KVgKC59vg&t=818)
