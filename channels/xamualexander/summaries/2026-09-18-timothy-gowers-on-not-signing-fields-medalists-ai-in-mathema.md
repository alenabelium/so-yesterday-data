# Timothy Gowers on Not Signing Fields Medalists' AI-in-Mathematics Letter

## Executive Summary

Timothy Gowers explains why he did not sign the Fields Medalists' letter on AI in mathematics, despite agreeing with some of its concerns. He argues that the letter oversimplifies the relationship between problem-solving and conceptual understanding, and that the real crisis is not the volume of AI-generated results but the potential collapse of social structures that support mathematical digestion and training. Gowers advocates for adaptation and creative use of AI rather than resistance, warning against tribalism within the mathematical community.

## Key Points

- Gowers agrees with the letter's emphasis on learning from problem-solving but rejects its narrow view of mathematical motivation, advocating for a spectrum of attitudes between problem-solving and conceptual understanding. [00:08:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T3P_bxAFqvY&t=536)
- He argues that the flood of AI-generated results is not inherently harmful, comparing it to streaming services where more content allows selective digestion, and notes that human mathematicians already produce more papers than can be read. [00:22:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T3P_bxAFqvY&t=1359)
- Gowers identifies the primary risk as the loss of motivation for new mathematicians and the destruction of social structures supporting the mathematical tradition, urging the community to find imaginative ways to adapt. [00:32:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T3P_bxAFqvY&t=1927)
