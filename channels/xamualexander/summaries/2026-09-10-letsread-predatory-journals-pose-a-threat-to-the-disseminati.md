# LetsRead "Predatory Journals Pose a Threat to the Dissemination of Science" (by Moslehian & Peralta)

## Executive Summary

This video reviews an article on predatory journals, which are low-standard, fee-charging publications that undermine scientific integrity by bypassing rigorous peer review. The host, Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander, critiques academia's over-reliance on peer review and flawed incentive structures, which inadvertently encourage predatory practices. He discusses deceptive tactics used by these journals, the gaming of impact metrics, and the role of databases like Scopus and Web of Science. He advocates for a shift toward merit-based evaluation and draws parallels with big tech's open indexing approach.

## Key Points

- Predatory journals exploit the academic incentive system by offering quick, low-quality publication for a fee, misleading researchers and damaging scientific credibility. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GPKaVdOfGqk&t=0)
- Academia's blind trust in peer review as a magical quality guarantee creates a predictable market for predatory journals, which offer superficial review for pay. [00:01:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GPKaVdOfGqk&t=78)
- Predatory journals use deceptive tactics like fake editorial board invitations, guest editor offers, and tourist-friendly conferences to lure researchers. [00:03:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GPKaVdOfGqk&t=222)
- Impact factor metrics are easily gamed by predatory journals, leading to misleading rankings and wasted university incentives. [00:06:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GPKaVdOfGqk&t=394)
- The host argues that academia's gatekeeping model is unsustainable and contrasts it with big tech's open indexing approach, suggesting a need for merit-based evaluation over publication venue. [00:14:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GPKaVdOfGqk&t=891)
