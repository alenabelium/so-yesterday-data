# Will AI create demand for *more* mathematicians?

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander critiques Amrit Sahai's essay arguing that AI will create demand for more mathematicians. He challenges the premise that struggle is essential to understanding, questions the feasibility of a 'deployable intellectual reserve' of pure mathematicians, and argues that the rate of AI-generated breakthroughs would lead to an unsustainable need for mathematicians. He also discusses the changing prestige and career incentives in pure mathematics, ultimately concluding that while the vision is appealing, it is unrealistic.

## Key Points

- Alexander challenges Sahai's claim that struggle is essential to understanding, suggesting it may be a result of poor education infrastructure rather than a fundamental necessity. [00:04:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKHpkuPIp7o&t=282)
- He argues that increasing the number of mathematicians to understand AI breakthroughs would lead to an exponential demand, as those mathematicians would use AI to generate even more breakthroughs. [00:07:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKHpkuPIp7o&t=426)
- Alexander critiques the idea of a 'deployable intellectual reserve' of pure mathematicians, questioning why it would be limited to mathematicians and noting that pure mathematicians are like artists, not applied scientists. [00:14:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKHpkuPIp7o&t=865)
