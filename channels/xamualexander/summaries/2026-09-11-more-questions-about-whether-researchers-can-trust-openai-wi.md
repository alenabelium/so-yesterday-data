# More questions about whether researchers can trust OpenAI with unpublished math

## Executive Summary

The video discusses the controversy surrounding OpenAI's alleged use of unpublished mathematical research from Tristan Buckmaster and others to solve the Navier-Stokes problem, raising questions about trust and transparency. The host analyzes various community reactions, including concerns about data privacy, the ethics of AI training on user data, and the potential impact on mathematical collaboration. He highlights OpenAI's categorical denial of using specific user data, but notes the difficulty of verifying such claims and the lasting damage to trust. The video concludes by reflecting on the dilemma mathematicians face in using AI tools while risking their work being absorbed, and predicts that AI will become indispensable despite these concerns.

## Key Points

- Andreas Tom's email to OpenAI reveals a lack of transparency about whether conversations with ChatGPT entered training data or were accessible to the solving process, with OpenAI's categorical denial seen as addressing only direct access, not training data.
