# Selected prompts used by OpenAI to solve 372 open math problems

## Executive Summary

The video analyzes ten reasoning traces released by OpenAI for major mathematical breakthroughs, focusing on the prompts used to guide the AI. The speaker, Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander, highlights the brevity and simplicity of some prompts, the machine-generated LaTeX in others, and the inconsistency in labeling (prompt vs. problem). He questions whether the prompts themselves were AI-generated and ponders the recursive nature of prompt creation. The video concludes with reflections on the democratizing impact of AI on mathematics, making once-elite breakthroughs accessible to all.

## Key Points

- The first prompt is remarkably short, yet it led to a solution for a major open problem in arithmetic progressions, a feat that would have earned a PhD a year ago. [01:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=80)
- Some prompts, like the one for the binary corrected Elliott conjecture, are written in machine-generated LaTeX, making them nearly unreadable to humans and raising questions about their origin. [04:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=262)
- The prompts vary greatly in length and style, with some being extremely long and detailed, while others are short and direct, indicating possible different authors or AI generation. [16:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=973)
- The video highlights the absence of a prompt for the irrationality exponent of pi breakthrough, leaving the reason for its omission a mystery. [20:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=1253)
- The speaker repeatedly questions whether the prompts themselves were AI-generated, pondering the recursive nature of prompt creation and the implications for the field. [33:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=2019)
