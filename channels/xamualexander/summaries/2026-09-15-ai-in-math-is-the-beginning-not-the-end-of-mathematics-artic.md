# AI in Math is the *Beginning*, not the *End*, of mathematics (article by Daniel Litt)

## Executive Summary

Daniel Litt's essay argues that AI's rise in mathematics marks a beginning, not an end, for the field, envisioning a future where human mathematicians focus on understanding, asking interesting questions, and building community rather than merely proving theorems. The video's host, Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander, strongly agrees, emphasizing that academia must adapt or become obsolete, and that the production of mathematical text is becoming disconnected from human understanding. Litt proposes institutional changes like redefining PhD goals around deep understanding and communication, and rewarding talks and discussions over papers. The host concludes that this is an exciting time for mathematics, with AI enabling an explosion of new results while humans remain essential for interpretation and meaning.

## Key Points

- AI's rapid progress in mathematics, from failing basic arithmetic to solving open problems, necessitates a radical rethinking of the profession, not its end. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ssr88F-vAiw&t=0)
- The goal of mathematics is not merely proving theorems; it is producing and understanding high-quality mathematics and mathematicians, which AI cannot replace. [00:04:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ssr88F-vAiw&t=268)
- Institutions must shift from valuing papers to rewarding understanding, communication, and community, with PhDs focused on deep expertise and defense rather than thesis production. [00:10:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ssr88F-vAiw&t=655)
