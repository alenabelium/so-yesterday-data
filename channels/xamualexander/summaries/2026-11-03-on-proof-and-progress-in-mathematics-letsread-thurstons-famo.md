# On Proof and Progress in Mathematics (LetsRead Thurston's famous paper)

## Executive Summary

This video is a detailed reading and commentary on William Thurston's influential paper 'On Proof and Progress in Mathematics,' which argues that the true goal of mathematics is advancing human understanding, not merely proving theorems. The host connects Thurston's insights to the current AI and math revolution, noting how AI's ability to generate proofs challenges traditional views of mathematical progress and the role of human understanding. The video explores Thurston's critique of the 'definition-theorem-proof' model, the social and psychological dimensions of mathematical thinking, and the importance of communication and infrastructure in mathematical progress. The host also reflects on how AI and formal proof systems like Lean are changing the landscape, making Thurston's ideas more relevant than ever.

## Key Points

- Thurston's central thesis is that mathematics advances human understanding, not just theorem-proving, a view that becomes increasingly relevant with AI-generated proofs. [00:04:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gFf5sRxa3mg&t=245)
- The 'definition-theorem-proof' model fails to capture the social and psychological processes that drive mathematical progress, as illustrated by the multiple ways mathematicians understand concepts like the derivative. [00:10:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gFf5sRxa3mg&t=604)
- Mathematical knowledge is embedded in the social fabric of the community, not just in written documents, which explains why AI, relying solely on written sources, may miss crucial human insights. [00:30:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gFf5sRxa3mg&t=1843)
- Thurston's personal experiences with foliations and the geometrization conjecture demonstrate how focusing on building infrastructure and communicating ideas can be more valuable than accumulating theorem credits. [00:49:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gFf5sRxa3mg&t=2973)
- The video highlights the ongoing relevance of Thurston's ideas in the context of AI and formal proof systems, suggesting that the AI revolution may force mathematicians to finally value understanding over credit. [00:58:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gFf5sRxa3mg&t=3480)
