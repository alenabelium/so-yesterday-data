# LetsRead "Why Mathematics is Boring" (John Baez on how to write better math papers)

## Executive Summary

In this video, Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reads and discusses John Baez's article 'Why Mathematics is Boring,' which argues that math papers often fail to engage readers because they neglect the human dimension. Baez suggests applying storytelling techniques—such as catchy openings, setting context, developing characters, creating conflict, and providing resolution—to make mathematical writing more compelling. Alexander agrees, emphasizing that even niche papers should aim to be accessible and interesting to a broader mathematical audience.

## Key Points

- Baez argues that math papers become boring when they neglect the human dimension, and suggests looking to storytellers for techniques to keep readers engaged. [00:01:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W-wdfJfKh-g&t=82)
- A catchy first paragraph is crucial because most readers decide whether to continue based on the opening lines; Baez advises avoiding notation-heavy starts and instead framing the problem in an engaging way. [00:02:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W-wdfJfKh-g&t=152)
- Baez recommends structuring introductions to set the scene, introduce characters (mathematical entities) with personality, create conflict by highlighting difficulties, and provide a satisfying resolution that points to open problems. [00:07:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W-wdfJfKh-g&t=420)
