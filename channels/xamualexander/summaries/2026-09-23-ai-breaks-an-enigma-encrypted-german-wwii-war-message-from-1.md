# AI breaks an Enigma-encrypted German WWII war message from 1941

## Executive Summary

The video discusses a 2026 announcement that OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra successfully broke an unbroken Enigma-encrypted German WWII message from 1941, which had resisted decryption since 2005. The AI independently developed software, used a crib based on a repeated place name, and performed the break without human steering, sparking debate about AI autonomy and the role of prior work. The video also highlights the historical significance and the ongoing challenge of breaking remaining Enigma messages.

## Key Points

- GPT-6 Astra broke the Enigma message MVEH from 10 July 1941, which had been unbroken since 2005, using a crib based on the repeated place name 'Rosenau' [00:01:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=atD6HPS9_NE&t=91).
- The AI independently developed Python and C++ software for an Enigma simulator and bomb, and executed the break without human steering, completing in two days what would take humans weeks or months [00:04:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=atD6HPS9_NE&t=292).
- The decrypted message reads: 'Please specify the route of march. I am in Rosenau Rosenau. Immediate reply by radio. Washbush.' [00:10:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=atD6HPS9_NE&t=616).
- The video discusses the debate on whether the AI's achievement counts as 'on its own' given it used code and prior knowledge, with some arguing that building the necessary tools makes it more impressive [00:18:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=atD6HPS9_NE&t=1118).
