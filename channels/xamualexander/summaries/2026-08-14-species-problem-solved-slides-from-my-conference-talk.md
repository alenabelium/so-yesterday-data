# Species Problem solved?? Slides from my conference talk

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander presents his recent work published in the Journal of Mathematical Biology, proposing axioms to mathematically define biological species. He introduces the common ancestor point axiom and convexity axiom, showing that species satisfying these are almost unique, thus challenging the long-held view that species are subjective. The talk also addresses the problem of small species-like clusters by adding constraints to ensure each organism belongs to a maximal cluster. Alexander argues that this work exemplifies mathematics aimed at understanding reality, especially relevant in the post-AI era.

## Key Points

- Introduces the common ancestor point axiom and convexity axiom as necessary conditions for defining biological species, arguing these are biologically plausible. [00:03:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6NbrPN6_2jo&t=225)
- The objective species theorem shows that any two infinite species-like clusters are either almost disjoint or almost identical, implying species are objective under these axioms. [00:09:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6NbrPN6_2jo&t=559)
- By adding common ancestor and mapping properties, each organism belongs to a maximal species-like cluster, solving the problem of small clusters like singletons. [00:15:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6NbrPN6_2jo&t=900)
