# Reflections on the Millennium Problems. Are some of them overhyped?

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reflects on Lloyd N. Trefethen's article about the Millennium Problems, arguing that the Riemann hypothesis, P vs. NP, and Navier-Stokes equations have lost much of their original practical significance over time. He notes that the traditional journal system is outdated, as OpenAI's recent solutions to Navier-Stokes and progress on 3-SAT have already made parts of the article obsolete. Alexander agrees with Trefethen that these problems remain theoretically fascinating but their real-world impact is less than initially imagined, and he highlights the recent renaissance in mathematics driven by AI breakthroughs.

## Key Points

- Trefethen's article, written before OpenAI's solution to Navier-Stokes, illustrates how the slow journal system lags behind rapid mathematical progress. [00:00:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H2ukEXB2G6w&t=1)
- The Riemann hypothesis, while famous, has limited practical consequences; disproving it would have an imperceptible effect on prime distribution, and it cannot break cryptography. [00:10:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H2ukEXB2G6w&t=604)
- P vs. NP's practical importance has diminished because many NP-complete problems are solvable in practice via heuristics and approximations, despite worst-case exponential complexity. [00:17:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H2ukEXB2G6w&t=1052)
- The Navier-Stokes problem, though solved by OpenAI, turned out to have no real-world applications, as singularities likely don't occur in physical flows, making the problem more theoretical than practical. [00:28:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H2ukEXB2G6w&t=1681)
