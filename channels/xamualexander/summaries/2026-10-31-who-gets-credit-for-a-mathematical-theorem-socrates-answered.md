# Who gets credit for a mathematical theorem? Socrates answered this 2500 years ago

## Executive Summary

This video explores the question of who deserves credit for mathematical discoveries made with AI assistance, drawing on Socrates' dialogue 'Meno' to argue that knowledge is innate and merely recollected through questioning. The speaker demonstrates how Socrates guides a servant boy to discover the square root of 8 through Socratic questioning, without teaching him anything directly. The conclusion is that neither the questioner nor the answerer deserves credit, because the knowledge was already within the person all along. This ancient philosophical insight is applied to modern AI, suggesting that credit for AI-discovered theorems belongs to no one, as the knowledge was already latent in humanity.

## Key Points

- Socrates' dialogue 'Meno' demonstrates that learning is actually recollection, as he guides a servant boy to discover the square root of 8 through questioning alone, without teaching him anything. [00:02:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pJJKKIu2rxs&t=141)
- The servant boy initially gives wrong answers (e.g., doubling the side to double the area), but through Socratic questioning, he realizes his errors and eventually arrives at the correct geometric construction using the diagonal. [00:07:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pJJKKIu2rxs&t=421)
- The speaker applies this to AI: since neither the questioner nor the answerer creates the knowledge, credit for AI-discovered theorems belongs to no one—the knowledge was already latent within us, waiting for the right questions. [00:20:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pJJKKIu2rxs&t=1201)
