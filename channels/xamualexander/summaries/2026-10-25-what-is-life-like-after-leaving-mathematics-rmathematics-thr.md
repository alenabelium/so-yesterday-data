# What is life like after leaving mathematics? (r/mathematics thread)

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander reacts to a Reddit thread on r/mathematics about life after leaving mathematics, where a math graduate transitions to nursing and others share their own career pivots. He critiques the masochistic reasons people pursue math degrees, argues that math's real value lies in developing analytical thinking and problem-solving skills that transfer to any field, and reflects on academia's financial and ego traps. He also notes the thread's surprising lack of AI discussion, given the current AI revolution's impact on mathematics.

## Key Points

- The original poster, a math graduate, is transitioning to nursing, which Alexander calls a rare and strange trajectory, and he questions why people who hate math pursue it in the first place. [00:02:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCt56rsYXfM&t=150)
- A commenter shares how their math master's helped them get into computational data science and eventually a NASA job, highlighting that the logical and analytical thinking from math is invaluable beyond academia. [00:05:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCt56rsYXfM&t=316)
- Alexander argues that academia often underpays and exploits mathematicians, and that leaving for higher-paying jobs is wise, while also noting that AI is making math easier and more accessible than ever. [00:15:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCt56rsYXfM&t=931)
