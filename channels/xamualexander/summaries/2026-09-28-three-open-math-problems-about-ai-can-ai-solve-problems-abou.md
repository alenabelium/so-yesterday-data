# Three open math problems about AI (can AI solve problems about itself?)

## Executive Summary

Dr. Samuel Allen Alexander discusses three open mathematical problems about AI, specifically questions 311, 312, and 313 from his doctoral dissertation on hierarchies of knowing machines. These problems ask whether it is possible for AIs in a well-founded, computably enumerable hierarchy to know the truthfulness, codes, and mechanicalness of all agents, including superiors. He explains the background of Reinhardt's and Carlson's results, and his own contributions, and expresses hope that AI itself might solve these problems, which would deepen our understanding of AI.

## Key Points

- AI has made great strides in solving open math problems, but none about AI itself; three open problems from the speaker's dissertation remain unsolved. [00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuSKyTDwis8&t=0)
- Reinhardt proved a truthful machine cannot know its own code, while Carlson showed it can know it is a machine without knowing which one; the speaker generalized this to hierarchies of AIs. [00:02:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuSKyTDwis8&t=174)
- The three open problems (311, 312, 313) ask whether AIs in a hierarchy can know everyone's truthfulness, codes of subordinates, and mechanicalness of all, with 313 likely the hardest. [00:18:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuSKyTDwis8&t=1133)
