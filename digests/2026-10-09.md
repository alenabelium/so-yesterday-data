## 2. AI achieves 99%+ success in reverse engineering binary code

GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 can decompile software with over 99% accuracy, enabling anyone to mod, port, or mash up games and applications. This breakthrough blurs the line between source and binary, opening new opportunities for software innovation while raising legal and ethical questions.

<!--detail
The line between source and binary has effectively dissolved. Recent models like GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 now decompile compiled software with over 99% accuracy, transforming what was once a niche, expert-only activity into a prompt-driven operation. The demonstrations are striking: tools like Universal Modder and Ghidra MCP enable through-the-loop mods that bridge entire game worlds—dropping Minecraft mechanics into GTA—or port massive titles like World of Warcraft to entirely new engines such as Rust. Even more granular, AI agents can extract a single mechanic, like Mirror's Edge parkour, and transplant it into Skyrim. This is not incremental improvement; it is the commoditization of software archaeology.

This hedging reveals the uncomfortable gap between technical possibility and legal reality. The video does not resolve this tension—it simply presents the capability and then steps back, leaving practitioners to navigate a landscape where the technical barriers to reverse engineering have collapsed but the legal and ethical frameworks have not yet adapted.

For AI-transformation practitioners, this is a double-edged sword. On one side, it unlocks unprecedented opportunities for legacy system modernization—decompiling and re-platforming aging internal applications that lack documentation or original developers. On the other, it demands immediate governance. Organizations must establish clear policies on what can and cannot be reverse engineered, particularly for third-party dependencies and acquired software. The practical implication is urgent: the capability is here, the risk is real, and the competitive advantage will go to teams that build ethical review processes into their AI toolchains *before* the first decompilation prompt is run, not after.
