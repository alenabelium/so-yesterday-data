Running on 200x less infrastructure and making checkouts take seconds, Scion exemplifies the shift from human-centric tools to machine-first design, unlocking the productivity J-curve of agentic coding.

This is creating a two-tier internet—secure sites that block agents and less-protected hotspots—demanding new cybersecurity practices and signaling a shift where agents become primary internet users.

This is not a hypothetical future; it is a live operational environment. ## 4. AI lending collapses as lenders doubt AI's ability to repay debt

AI-related lending plummeted from $113 billion in June to $23 billion in September, while credit default swap costs for companies like Oracle and Coreweave hit record highs. This signals growing lender distrust in AI's profitability, suggesting that value may flow to users through lower prices rather than to AI companies or lenders, reshaping the economics of the AI boom.

This signals a fundamental shift in lender sentiment: the financial markets are increasingly betting that AI companies may not generate sufficient returns to service their debt obligations. The collapse is not merely a liquidity event but a repricing of risk across the entire AI value chain, with lenders now questioning the profitability assumptions that underpinned the previous lending boom.

## 5. Post-labor economics: Redesigning workflows for machines, not humans

Anthropic's Scion example illustrates the productivity J-curve and post-labor economics, where output grows while headcount stays flat. Companies must stop 'paving cow paths' and redesign workflows for machines first, as human-centric tools like Git limit the gains from agentic coding, and human value shifts to accountability, long-term goals, and undocumented knowhow.

<!--detail
Anthropic’s Scion is less a product announcement than a proof-of-concept for a new economic logic. The system, built by a team of seven with Claude’s assistance, was created to absorb a 12x surge in daily code merges—from 2,500 to over 30,000—that overwhelmed Git, a tool designed for human-scale collaboration. Scion runs on 200 times less infrastructure and reduces checkouts from minutes to seconds, but the deeper signal is the productivity J-curve: output scales while headcount stays flat. This is post-labor economics in miniature, where the binding constraint shifts from human throughput to system design.

The source video’s central provocation is that most organizations are “paving cow paths”—automating workflows built around human limitations rather than redesigning them for machine capabilities. Git’s merge conflicts, code review queues, and branch hierarchies encode a world where humans write code slowly and carefully. Agents write code fast and sloppily, and the bottleneck moves upstream. The tension here is real: the video celebrates Scion as a triumph of machine-first design, yet it was built by humans with Claude’s help, and the remaining human value is explicitly non-technical—accountability, long-term goals, relationships, undocumented knowhow. That’s a hedge worth naming: if humans are no longer the constraint on output, they become the constraint on direction, and the video doesn’t fully resolve how organizations transition from one mode to the other without a period of chaos.

For practitioners, the implication is uncomfortable but actionable. The first wave of AI transformation was about augmenting existing workflows; the second wave requires dismantling them. Before scaling agentic systems, audit your toolchain for human-centric assumptions—version control, ticketing, approval gates, status meetings. Each is a candidate for redesign, not automation. The organizations that win won’t be those with the best prompts, but those willing to let headcount stay flat while output triples, and to redefine human roles around judgment rather than execution.
