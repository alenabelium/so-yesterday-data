This signals a fundamental shift in software engineering infrastructure, with second-order bottlenecks now in CI/CD, code review, and repo design.

Git’s offline-first, branch-and-merge model assumes human-paced, deliberate change. The actionable move is to treat your CI/CD and review processes as the primary targets for AI augmentation, not just code generation. ## 5. Businesses must redesign around AI, not just add tools, to unlock productivity gains

David Shapiro argues that the Solow paradox and theory of constraints explain why AI hasn't yet boosted productivity: companies are adding AI to existing workflows instead of redesigning operations around it. His own 7x output increase after a full redesign illustrates the potential, but also the need for structural change to overcome bottlenecks.

<!--detail
The core claim from David Shapiro is that the productivity paradox surrounding AI is not a technology problem but an organizational design problem. His argument rests on a sharp distinction between augmentation and reinvention: bolting AI onto legacy processes merely accelerates the existing bottleneck, while redesigning the entire value stream around AI capabilities can unlock exponential gains. His personal case—moving from weekly to daily content production, a sevenfold output increase, and monthly views jumping from 200,000 to 1.8 million—is offered as proof that structural change, not tool adoption, is the real lever.

The tension in his analysis lies in the interplay between the Solow paradox and the theory of constraints. The Solow paradox suggests patience: like PCs in 1987, AI needs time for workflows and applications to mature before showing up in aggregate statistics. Yet the theory of constraints argues that time alone won't help—if you make every employee ten times more productive but the bottleneck remains unchanged, overall throughput stays flat. These two frameworks pull in opposite directions: one says "wait," the other says "restructure now." Shapiro resolves this by insisting that the waiting period is precisely when redesign should happen, not after the tools mature. The nuance practitioners should note is that his 7x result came from a solo operation, where he controlled the entire pipeline; in a large enterprise, the bottleneck is often cross-functional coordination, which is far harder to redesign than an individual workflow.

For AI-transformation leaders, the actionable implication is uncomfortable but clear: the unit of analysis must shift from the individual task to the entire value stream. Before deploying AI, map the constraint—whether it's approval chains, handoffs between teams, or legacy data infrastructure—and redesign around AI's ability to compress or eliminate that constraint. Adding AI to a broken process merely produces faster brokenness. The organizations that will see productivity gains are those willing to treat AI as the organizing principle of the business, not as an accessory to it.
