## 1. OpenAI's Yang-Mills proof data finally released, but validation reveals gaps

After a month-long embargo, the Yang-Mills Shepherd group released OpenAI's construction data and a 35-page validation paper. The paper confirms the proof establishes a quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms via a scaling limit of lattice gauge theory, but it does not prove uniqueness or a positive mass gap, leaving the Millennium Prize problem unresolved. This marks a significant milestone in AI-assisted mathematics, yet also highlights the limits of current AI proofs and the need for human verification.

## 2. Fable 5.2's cost-performance tradeoff creates a K-shaped economy for AI adoption

Fable 5.2 excels at complex, high-stakes tasks—completing a project that stumped the author for months—but its high cost (e.g., $250 for one API dialogue) makes it impractical for routine work. This forces businesses to carefully identify high-return problems, leading to a K-shaped economy where some workers and companies benefit greatly while others see little improvement. The shift from AI as an assistant to an intelligent partner requires humans to act as judges and focus on tasks where the model's capabilities provide outsized returns.
