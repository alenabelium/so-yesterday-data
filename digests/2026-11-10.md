This capital is flowing into Bitcoin for wealth preservation and into decentralized AI projects like Render, Bittensor, and Grass, signaling a major shift in investment strategy and the rise of crypto-AI as a serious asset class.

This shift could reshape the AI industry's geographic and economic landscape.

## 4. Groundbreaking math result: integers are Diophantine in the rationals, solved with AI assistance

A new proof shows that the integers are Diophantine in the rationals, refuting Mazur's conjecture and solving Hilbert's tenth problem for rational numbers. The result, initially with 300 billion variables, was rapidly improved to a quartic with 962 variables through collaborative, AI-assisted mathematics, demonstrating the accelerating pace of mathematical discovery and the growing role of AI in formal verification.

Concretely, this means there now exists a polynomial with integer coefficients whose rational-valued solutions project precisely onto the integers, a construction that directly refutes Mazur's conjecture and, when combined with the classical DPRM theorem, resolves Hilbert's tenth problem for the rationals. The initial proof, however, was a computational monstrosity—a polynomial involving roughly 300 billion variables with coefficient bounds so large they defied practical comprehension. Within days, a decentralized, collaborative effort among mathematicians reduced this to a quartic polynomial with just 962 variables, a compression of several orders of magnitude that signals a new tempo for pure mathematics.

The video does not flatten this into a triumphalist narrative—it openly acknowledges that the "AI did it" framing is premature, and that human mathematicians rapidly improved the unwieldy initial construction through classic collaborative intuition.

For practitioners driving AI transformation, this is a case study in human-AI complementarity rather than replacement. The lesson is that AI can generate valid but impractical solutions—massive, unreadable, and unverifiable—and the real value emerges when human experts use their judgment to refine, simplify, and validate. Organizations should not expect AI to deliver production-ready outputs; they should build workflows where AI proposes and humans dispose, with formal verification as the safety net. A Google DeepMind product manager shares insights into the unpredictable nature of frontier model development, emphasizing the importance of product managers who understand the 'vibe' of models and the 60-20-20 rule for building ahead of model capabilities. This reveals a shift in how AI products are designed and managed, with adaptability and evaluation becoming key skills in an AI-driven world.

<!--detail
The frontier of AI product development has quietly inverted the traditional relationship between engineering and product management. Tulsi, a product manager at Google DeepMind, describes model development as less like deterministic software engineering and more like a "Potions lesson"—a process where magic emerges unpredictably from the process itself. The concrete shift here is that product managers are no longer translating fixed capabilities into user needs; they are observing emergent behaviors and deciding which surprises to cultivate into value. This is codified in the "60-20-20 rule": build 60% of features that work reliably today, invest 20% in capabilities that will only become possible with the next model iteration, and reserve 20% for speculative bets on what the model might suddenly excel at.

The source material reveals a genuine tension beneath this confident framing. The 60-20-20 rule implies a level of strategic foresight that the "Potions lesson" metaphor directly contradicts. If model development is truly unpredictable, how can a product manager reliably allocate 20% of resources to future capabilities? The answer, hedged in the video, is that the rule is less a planning methodology and more a risk-management posture—a way to ensure teams are never fully exposed to either current limitations or future surprises. Similarly, the emphasis on "vibe" as a core PM competency sits uneasily alongside the call for rigorous evaluation and user feedback. The video does not resolve this; it simply asserts that both intuition and measurement are now required, without explaining how to reconcile them when they conflict.

For practitioners driving AI transformation, the actionable implication is that organizational planning cycles must be redesigned around adaptability rather than predictability. The old model of gathering requirements, building to spec, and shipping is structurally incompatible with frontier models. Instead, transformation leaders should institutionalize the 60-20-20 mindset: create portfolios of initiatives that mix guaranteed incremental wins with speculative bets, and—critically—build evaluation infrastructure that can rapidly identify where a model's emergent strengths lie. The PM who understands "vibe" is not a mystic; they are someone who has learned to read model behavior as a signal, and that skill is now as important as roadmap discipline.
