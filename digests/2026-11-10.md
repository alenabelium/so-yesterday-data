## 5. Groundbreaking math result: integers are Diophantine in rationals, rapidly improved via collaboration

A new proof shows the integers are Diophantine in the rationals, solving Hilbert's tenth problem for rational numbers and refuting Mazur's conjecture. The result, initially with 300 billion variables, was rapidly improved to a quartic with 962 variables through grassroots collaboration, highlighting the accelerating pace of AI-assisted mathematics and the shift toward collaborative proof development.

<!--detail
The mathematical community just witnessed something unprecedented: a proof that the integers are Diophantine in the rationals—meaning there exists a polynomial with integer coefficients whose rational zeros project exactly onto the integers—was announced, then radically improved within days. The initial construction, a monstrosity of roughly 300 billion variables with absurd coefficient bounds, was rapidly whittled down through grassroots collaboration to a quartic polynomial with just 962 variables. This result refutes Mazur's conjecture and, combined with the DPRM theorem, resolves Hilbert's tenth problem for rational numbers. The proof builds on OpenAI's earlier work in this area, marking a rare instance where an AI-generated line of attack became the foundation for a major human-verified breakthrough.

The presenter frames this as a feature of the new collaborative era, but it also highlights a risk: rapid iteration on unverified foundations could propagate subtle errors.

For practitioners driving AI transformation, this is a case study in how AI-assisted discovery reshapes organizational workflows. The key lesson is not that AI produces final answers, but that it generates starting points which human experts can rapidly refine through open collaboration. The 300-billion-variable proof was a scaffold, not a solution.
