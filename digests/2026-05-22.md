## 1. Financialization of Compute Infrastructure via ICE Futures

The launch of the first major exchange-listed compute futures on the Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) marks a pivotal shift in AI infrastructure financing. By establishing the OR Compute Price Index (OCPI) and allowing hedging of GPU costs (H100, B200, etc.), this development enables institutional capital to treat compute like traditional commodities, facilitating the $7 trillion buildout with established risk management tools.

## 4. Standardization of the Agent Protocol Stack (MCP, A2A, AGUI)

The industry is coalescing around a core agent protocol stack consisting of MCP for tool access, A2A for cross-agent delegation, and AGUI for human-in-the-loop control. Understanding these layers is now critical for developers, as they dictate the security, reliability, and customer experience of AI agents, moving beyond simple model selection to infrastructure governance.

Sources: [Google Spent a Year Stitching MCP, A2A, AG-UI Together. I/O Today.](/videos/zP6TnEiueEc)

## 5. Shift to Infrastructure Governance and Data Rooms

Two key practical insights are emerging: first, the 'kill switch' and governance of AI agents now reside in infrastructure layers (runtime, identity, payments) controlled by companies like Cloudflare and Stripe, not just model providers. Second, to prevent hallucinations, professionals must adopt 'data room' workflows where agents audit and organize source data before generating content, treating AI as a collaborative colleague rather than a simple tool.
