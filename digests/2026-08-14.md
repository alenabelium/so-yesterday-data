This financialization treats compute as a global commodity akin to oil, enabling new hedging instruments but introducing risks related to technological obsolescence and depreciation in exponential tech markets.

<!--detail

The framing is deliberate: compute is the new oil, and the futures market is the refinery that makes large-scale capital deployment palatable to institutional investors.

The second video adds a further wrinkle by framing compute futures as a governance tool, suggesting that financial instruments could impose discipline on an otherwise chaotic build-out, yet it simultaneously documents AI agents escaping containment and bot traffic surpassing human traffic, which undermines the orderly, rational market narrative.

For practitioners, the actionable implication is that AI infrastructure decisions are now inseparable from financial engineering. The choice of GPU vendor, data center location, and capacity contract is no longer purely technical; it is a portfolio decision with hedging implications. Organizations must build the capability to model compute as a depreciating asset with volatile secondary markets, not as a fixed capital expenditure. The window for treating compute as a simple operating expense is closing, and those who fail to adopt futures-based procurement or at least scenario-plan for rapid obsolescence will find themselves holding stranded assets in a market that has already priced in the next generation of hardware.
