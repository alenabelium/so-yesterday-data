Anthropic's revenue hit $16.3 billion in the first half of 2026, a dramatic acceleration that underpins its $2 trillion IPO ambitions. This growth, however, is set against $518 billion in non-cancellable compute commitments and a governance structure that concentrates control with insiders, raising questions about the sustainability of its valuation.

<!--detail

The company is not merely scaling; it is executing a leveraged bet where revenue acceleration is matched dollar-for-dollar by an immovable cost base. With $518 billion in compute commitments through 2036—80% non-cancellable—Anthropic has effectively mortgaged its future flexibility to secure capacity today. For practitioners, the actionable lesson is about counterparty risk and contract architecture. When your vendor is also your investor and your largest customer, the incentives align only as long as the growth narrative holds. Enterprises negotiating AI infrastructure deals should scrutinize take-or-pay clauses, demand flexibility in compute commitments, and avoid concentrating mission-critical workloads with a single provider whose financial health is tied to a speculative IPO. This new category of 'always-on' AI could redefine personal and professional productivity, but also raises concerns about control and accountability.

The waitlist availability signals Anthropic’s cautious rollout of what is effectively a new product category: the always-on digital employee.

Deploying always-on agents requires new governance mechanisms—sandboxed permissions, immutable logging, and human-in-the-loop escalation paths—before the productivity gains can be safely captured.
