# Daily Digest — September 30, 2026

The 5 most important AI developments from recent content.

## 1. Meta's Muse AI assistant disrupts commerce, Amazon blocks it

Meta's AI assistant Muse has become a top iPhone app by autonomously managing subscriptions, saving users over $1,285 annually, but Amazon blocked it from its App Store, fearing loss of control over customer relationships and ad revenue. This highlights the growing tension between AI-driven consumer utility and established platform economics, signaling a shift toward agent-centric commerce.

## 2. OpenAI and industry shift to 90-day product cycles, abandoning long-term planning

OpenAI product leaders advocate for 90-day development horizons instead of multi-year plans, emphasizing rapid shipping of imperfect products to gather real-world feedback. This reflects a broader industry pivot to agile, iterative development in response to fast-evolving AI capabilities, a practical insight for teams adopting AI.

Sources: [Stop planning for 2027: how OpenAI builds product 90 days at a time](/videos/-ciSTkEVy30)

Jensen Huang and others push back on 'AI doom' narratives, arguing they are a manufactured panic by labs to form a regulatory cartel and slow competition. They advocate for 'defensive collaborative scaling'—accelerating development with safety alignment—while highlighting AI breakthroughs in biology and robotics as evidence of abundance over existential threat.

## 5. AI-driven formal verification: Lean kernel exploit exposes vulnerabilities, AI formalizes zlib

Leo de Moura revealed that AI-generated proofs exploited bugs in Lean's kernel, underscoring the need for multiple independent, formally verified kernels. Concurrently, AI successfully formalized and optimized complex code like zlib in Lean, achieving performance comparable to Rust, marking a significant step toward AI-assisted mathematical and software verification.
