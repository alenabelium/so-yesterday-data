## 4. Anthropic's VP≠VNP proof formalized in Lean, offering weak evidence for P≠NP

Anthropic's internal Claude model discovered a proof that VP is not equal to VNP over every field, formalized in Lean, establishing a super-polynomial lower bound for permanent and Hamiltonian cycle polynomials. This is a significant milestone in algebraic complexity theory and provides weak abductive evidence that P is not equal to NP.

<!--detail
Anthropic’s internal Claude model has produced a formal proof, verified in the Lean theorem prover, that the algebraic complexity classes VP and VNP are distinct over every field. The proof establishes a super-polynomial lower bound on the circuit size required to compute the permanent and Hamiltonian cycle polynomials—specifically, more than \(m^{c \cdot \sqrt[3]{\log m}}\) gates. While this does not resolve the P vs. NP question, it is the first separation of these classes, a milestone that has long been considered a "baby version" of the famous problem.

The source video is careful to delineate the limits of the result. The proof does not settle P vs. NP because arithmetic circuits must operate over all field extensions, whereas Boolean circuits do not. This distinction is not a minor technicality—it means the lower bound, while super-polynomial, does not translate directly into the Boolean world. The video also notes that the result offers only "weak abductive evidence" for P≠NP, a hedge that reflects the community's cautious reception of AI-discovered proofs.

The tension here is between the genuine mathematical significance of the VP≠VNP separation and the temptation to overstate its implications for the broader P vs. NP conjecture. The video itself acknowledges this gap, framing the result as a significant step in algebraic complexity theory rather than a breakthrough in computational complexity more broadly.

For practitioners driving AI transformation, the actionable implication is twofold. First, the proof demonstrates that AI systems can now generate results that are not merely plausible but formally verified—a shift from AI as a pattern-finder to AI as a theorem-prover. Second, the careful framing around what the result does and does not prove is a model for how organizations should communicate AI-generated insights: with precision about scope, explicit acknowledgment of limitations, and a clear distinction between evidence and proof. The VP≠VNP result is a reminder that AI's value in technical domains often lies not in replacing human judgment but in extending it—provided the human side maintains rigorous standards of verification and honest assessment of what has actually been established.
