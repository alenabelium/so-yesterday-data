Employees and unions are pushing back, and the debate centers on what constitutes 'real work' versus imitation in the age of AI.

The purchase of emails, Teams messages, and internal documents represents a bet that the chaotic residue of corporate life—the meetings, the Slack threads, the half-finished drafts—contains enough signal to train agents capable of replicating knowledge work. Yet this bet rests on a fundamental confusion between the observable traces of work and the tacit judgment that actually produces value.

The source material highlights a sharp tension between the buyers' assumptions and the sellers' reality. The video's framing of "chaotic imitation" versus "true value" cuts to the heart of the matter, yet it also hedges on whether this distinction is even knowable in advance. Perhaps the agents trained on these archives will surprise us, or perhaps they will inherit the pathologies of a failing airline's culture.
