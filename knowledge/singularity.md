The singularity is the asymptote of AI discourse: the moment technological growth becomes self-sustaining via [recursive self-improvement](/knowledge/recursive-self-improvement), after which prediction fails. In the corpus it functions less as a forecast than as a sorting device — positions on near-term practical questions (automation, [post-labor economics](/knowledge/post-labor-economics)) correlate strongly with how much weight a speaker gives singularity scenarios, and the platform's accelerationist and skeptical clusters are largely mapped by it.

The useful middle position the pragmatic-operator cluster holds: treat singularity probability as uncertain but the *path* as graded. Every step toward it — better agents, partial self-improvement, automated research assistance — is individually mundane, employment-relevant, and decidable with normal engineering evidence ([scaling laws](/knowledge/scaling-laws), [benchmarks](/knowledge/benchmarks)). So the strategic posture is scenario-hedging: build the practice that wins in the mundane continuation (validation, governance, role redesign) while keeping optionality on the tail — which is also exactly what [alignment](/knowledge/alignment)-minded preparation asks for.
