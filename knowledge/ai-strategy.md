AI strategy is the organizational discipline of choosing where AI creates advantage and where it does not: which workflows to rebuild around agents, what to insource versus rent, how to sequence adoption, and how to redesign roles and governance around automated execution. The corpus's own trajectory tells the story — through mid-2026 the conversation was tools and agent-building; by the second half it was overwhelmingly strategy and careers, because the technical layer standardized faster than the organizational layer could absorb it.

The recurring frameworks: [AI-native economics](/knowledge/ai-native-economics) (revenue per employee as the headline metric), the [compounding gap](/knowledge/compounding-gap) (early adopters pull ahead non-linearly), [three layers of work](/knowledge/three-layers-of-work) (automatable execution vs. orchestration vs. judgment), and the honest counter-signal that most programs fail on leadership and process rather than technology. A defensible AI strategy in the corpus's terms is therefore mostly a *validation* strategy: specify well, measure outcomes, keep humans accountable for the consequential 30%.
