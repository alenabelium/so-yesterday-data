Physical AI is the umbrella for learned systems that sense and act in the real world: manipulation, locomotion, autonomous navigation, industrial control. The corpus tracks its inflection from [world models](/knowledge/world-model) — learned simulators that made sim-to-real training credible — and treats it as the slower, larger twin of software agents: same substitution logic, plus hardware cost and safety-case burdens (see [robotics](/knowledge/robotics)).

For strategists the relevant clock is deployment conservatism: physical systems certify slowly, so capability demos run years ahead of volume deployment. The investable near term is bounded autonomy in structured environments (warehouses, inspection, logistics) with humanoid generalists as the late-decade option (see [humanoid robotics](/knowledge/humanoid-robotics)).
