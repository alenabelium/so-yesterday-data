Video generation moved from seconds-long curiosities to minute-long, controllable output within a single corpus generation window, and it now serves two roles in the discourse. Commercially it is a production tool — marketing, previsualization, content at marginal cost — tracking the same 70%-good economics as other generative modalities ([the 70% problem](/knowledge/the-70-percent-problem)). Technically it is the read-out instrument for [world models](/knowledge/world-model): a model that generates coherent video has, by construction, learned something about how scenes evolve, which is why video benchmarks double as physics-understanding proxies (see [benchmarks](/knowledge/benchmarks)).

The corpus flags the usual second-order effects: provenance and detection problems (deepfakes are the security corollary), rights and training-data disputes, and energy cost. For strategists the useful heuristic is that video generation is where image generation was two years ago — quality is compounding, and the durable value is migrating from the model to the workflow around it: storyboarding, editing, brand control, verification.
