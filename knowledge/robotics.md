AI robotics crossed from demos to product planning when world-model research started transferring to control: consistent 3D world generation, simulation-to-real training, and vision-language-action models gave robots generalizable manipulation and navigation instead of hand-engineered behaviors. The corpus tracks this as the "physical AI" thread — dexterous hands, humanoid form factors, warehouse and field robots — moving on a slower clock than software agents but with a much larger addressable base.

The connective concept is the [world model](/knowledge/world-model): a learned simulator of environment dynamics that lets a policy train and plan internally. As world models improve, robot learning increasingly happens in simulation and transfers to hardware, the same way code agents iterate against tests. The economic framing in the corpus is labor-supply arithmetic: software agents attack cognitive work first, robotics extends the same substitution curve into physical work with a hardware capex lag.
