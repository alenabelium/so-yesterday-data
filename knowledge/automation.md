Automation is the general phenomenon AI accelerates: work performed by machines instead of people. The corpus's contribution is a sharpened taxonomy. Classical automation executes a *known* procedure (a script, a pipeline, an assembly line); agentic automation executes a *specified outcome* — the machine holds the procedure, the human holds the intent (see [three layers of work](/knowledge/three-layers-of-work)). That difference is why AI-era automation reaches work previously considered judgment-proof, and why [job-market bifurcation](/knowledge/job-market-bifurcation) concentrates risk in the middle layer: procedural expertise without outcome ownership.

For practitioners the corpus's automation playbook is consistent: inventory work by how fully success can be specified; automate the specifiable tail first with agents plus review; measure throughput and error rates honestly; and reinvest the freed capacity in the specification and validation layer, which is the scarce input. The personal-scale version — [solo operators](/knowledge/solo-operator) and HI-ICs running agent fleets — is the same playbook with one practitioner playing every role.
