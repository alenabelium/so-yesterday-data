AI data centers are the industrial fact underneath the capability curve: hundreds of megawatts of accelerators, cooling, and interconnect, amortized over inference and training demand. The corpus tracks them at three levels. Physically, power is the binding constraint — grid interconnects, generation deals, and siting politics now gate capacity more than chips. Financially, the capex wave is the bubble question: valuations of AI infrastructure depend on inference demand growing into the buildout, which is precisely where the ROI-gap critique bites (see [token economics](/knowledge/token-economics)). Strategically, data-center geography is becoming sovereignty policy — where compute lives determines whose law and whose access controls apply (see [AI infrastructure](/knowledge/ai-infrastructure)).

For operators the practical readings are cost and latency: [inference](/knowledge/inference) economics dominate running costs, driving model routing and distillation; for everyone else, data-center buildout is the leading indicator to watch — it is capacity that must be monetized, which shapes every vendor's pricing behavior downstream.
