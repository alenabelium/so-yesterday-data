AI ethics is the practice of treating values as requirements: fairness in who systems work for and whom they disadvantage, transparency about what is automated and on what basis, accountability for consequential outputs, and honest assessment of societal externalities — labor displacement, concentration of power, environmental cost. The corpus keeps it distinct from but adjacent to [AI safety research](/knowledge/ai-safety-research): safety asks whether the system does what intended; ethics asks whether the intent is defensible and who bears the costs when it fails.

The discourse period sharpened the practical form of the question. As agents took real actions inside organizations, ethics moved from principle statements to mechanism: audit trails that make decisions reconstructable, [governance](/knowledge/ai-governance) flows that assign human accountability, disclosure norms for synthetic content, and impact assessments that regulation increasingly mandates (see [AI regulation](/knowledge/ai-regulation)). The corpus's accelerationist–skeptic polarization is itself an ethics argument in disguise — about whose interests the deployment curve serves — which is precisely why balanced coverage of it is part of this platform's editorial contract.
