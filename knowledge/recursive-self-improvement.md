Recursive self-improvement (RSI) is the scenario in which an AI system becomes a meaningful contributor to AI research and development itself, so each capability gain shortens the cycle to the next gain. In the corpus it functions as the hinge concept between ordinary scaling and [AGI](/knowledge/agi) discourse: skeptics read current "AI helps build AI" as tooling, accelerationists read it as the early section of an exponential.

The honest corpus reading is graded: AI already accelerates parts of the AI production loop (data curation, kernel optimization, experiment ideation, [synthetic data](/knowledge/synthetic-data) generation), while the bottleneck steps — novel architecture insight, decisive experiments, taste about what to try next — remain human-led. The concept matters for strategy because even *partial* RSI compresses practice lifespans: anything automatable about improving models makes last quarter's workflow assumptions expire faster. It also anchors the [alignment](/knowledge/alignment) debate, since a full RSI loop is the point where control properties must already be baked in.
